## Grammar points

### Interjection: joh

**Joh** is described by the book as an almost untranslatable spoken-only interjection marking a
friendly relationship with no clear power difference between speaker and addressee — closest to
English "hey, man" but usable regardless of the gender of either party. It's a generally young usage,
though also common between older friends.

### Zelf vs. zelfs

**Zelf** ("myself/yourself/himself/herself/ourselves...") is a pronoun; **zelfs** ("even") expresses
surprise. They must not be confused: *Je moet het zelf niet doen* ("You shouldn't do it yourself")
vs. *Hij is zelfs begonnen te roken* ("He even started to smoke").

### Broadened sense of lekker

Beyond its literal "nice-tasting" sense, **lekker** is used very frequently and broadly to convey a
general sense of pleasure or satisfaction, not limited to taste or food: *Nou, dat is lekker
goedkoop* ("Well, that's nice and cheap"), *Ik ga even lekker zitten lezen* ("I'm just going to treat
myself and read for a while").

### Hoe ... hoe ("the more ... the more")

A causal comparison between two things is expressed with **hoe ... hoe**: *Hoe meer modules u neemt,
hoe duurder het wordt* ("The more modules you take, the more expensive it will become"). The
construction is built from **two sub-clauses**, each introduced by **hoe**, so the finite verb in
each half moves to clause-final position (per the general sub-clause rule from Unit 13). When no
adjective is available/given for one half of the comparison, **meer** substitutes as a default:
*Hoe meer ik op de computer werk, hoe leuker ik het vind* ("The more I work on the computer, the more
I enjoy it").
