### Idioom — full harvest, Lessons 1–20

Unlike `033`'s partial/representative Idioom sample from later in the book, **every Idioom entry in
Lessons 1–20 was captured**, since this file's systematic pass through the lesson spreads made full
coverage of this specific feature straightforward. Each lesson carries exactly one idiom (a fixed
expression, proverb, or in one case a set of colloquial tag-question particles), always in a small
boxed "Idioom" section at the foot of the theory page, thematically unconnected to that lesson's own
grammar point.

- **Les 1** (Letter, woord, zin, getal, cijfer, printed p. 12): *De puntjes op de i zetten* —
  literally "to put the dots on the i," idiomatically "to put the finishing touches on something /
  to be scrupulously precise about the last details."
- **Les 2** (Zinnen, printed p. 14): *Hij zit op zijn geld* — literally "he's sitting on his money,"
  idiomatically "he's stingy/tight-fisted."
- **Les 3** (Persoonswoorden (1), printed p. 16): *Zij zitten met hun handen in het haar* —
  "they're at their wits' end" (already captured in `033`'s own Idioom sample; not re-tabulated
  below to avoid a duplicate vocabulary row).
- **Les 4** (Hij/het/je/we/ze, printed p. 18): *Je kunt geen ijzer met handen breken* — literally
  "you can't break iron with your bare hands," idiomatically "there are limits to what anyone can
  do / you can't do the impossible."
- **Les 5** (Werkwoorden, printed p. 22): *Op de klok kijken* — literally "to watch the clock,"
  idiomatically "to clock-watch" (used both literally, of checking the time, and of impatiently
  waiting for a shift/task to end).
- **Les 6** (De persoonsvorm van het werkwoord, printed p. 24): *Tussen de regels door lezen* — "to
  read between the lines."
- **Les 7** (De, het, een, printed p. 26): *De kous op de kop krijgen* — literally "to get the sock
  on your head," idiomatically "to be turned down / rejected" (used of a request or proposal that
  fails).
- **Les 8** (Het meervoud (op -en, -s of -'s), printed p. 28): *Nieuwe bezems vegen schoon* — "new
  brooms sweep clean" (a direct cognate-idiom match with the English expression: a newly-appointed
  person starts out making vigorous changes).
- **Les 9** (De spelling van het meervoud, printed p. 32): *Geen rozen zonder doornen* — "no roses
  without thorns" (a direct cognate-idiom match: nothing good comes without some difficulty).
- **Les 10** (Voorzetsels (1), printed p. 34): *Achter de wolken schijnt de zon* — literally "behind
  the clouds the sun shines," idiomatically "every cloud has a silver lining."
- **Les 11** (Het werkwoord 'zijn', printed p. 36): *Waar een wil is, is een weg* — "where there's a
  will, there's a way" (a direct cognate-idiom match).
- **Les 12** (Bijvoeglijke naamwoorden (1), printed p. 38): *Hoge bomen vangen veel wind* —
  literally "tall trees catch a lot of wind," idiomatically "people of high status/prominence
  attract more criticism/trouble" (a functional cognate of the English "the bigger they are...").
- **Les 13** (Vraagwoorden; volgorde, printed p. 42): *Vragen staat vrij* — literally "asking stands
  free," idiomatically "there's no harm in asking / it never hurts to ask."
- **Les 14** (Het werkwoord 'hebben', printed p. 44): *Hebben is hebben!* — "having is having!" —
  used the way English "possession is nine-tenths of the law" is: what you actually hold/possess
  outweighs a mere promise or claim.
- **Les 15** (Vraagzinnen, printed p. 46): *Leuk, hè? Nietwaar? Ja toch?* — presented as this
  lesson's "Idioom" entry but is actually a small set of colloquial spoken-register **tag-question
  particles** rather than a fixed idiom: *hè* (~"isn't it?"/"right?", a general softening/checking
  tag), *nietwaar?* ("isn't that so?", more formal/literary than *hè*), and *ja toch?* ("yeah,
  right?" — seeking agreement). **Worth flagging distinctly for the mechanics-analysis phase**: this
  is a directly relevant precedent for how a derived colloquial register might mark
  conversational-checking/agreement-seeking function via clause-final particles, structurally
  different from the fixed-phrase idioms found in every other lesson in this range.
- **Les 16** (Dit/dat; deze/die, printed p. 48): *Over ditjes en datjes praten* — literally "to talk
  about this-lets and that-lets" (using the same diminutive-suffixed demonstratives just taught in
  this lesson's grammar point), idiomatically "to make small talk / chat about nothing in
  particular."
- **Les 17** (Niet/geen, printed p. 52): *Spreken is zilver, zwijgen is goud* — "speech is silver,
  silence is golden" (a direct cognate-idiom match).
- **Les 18** (Welk(e); elk(e); ieder(e), printed p. 54): *Elk huisje heeft zijn kruisje* — literally
  "every little house has its little cross [to bear]," idiomatically "every family/household has
  its own burdens/problems" (functional cognate of "every family has its skeletons").
- **Les 19** (Bezittelijke voornaamwoorden, printed p. 56): *Van je stokje gaan* — literally "to go
  from your little perch" (an image from a caged bird), idiomatically "to faint / pass out."
- **Les 20** (Persoonswoorden (2), printed p. 58): *Moet je mij hebben?!* — literally "do you need
  to have me?!"/"is it me you want?!", idiomatically a confrontational spoken-register challenge:
  "You looking to start something with me?!"/"You want a piece of me?!" — directly relevant to this
  project's interest in confrontational/aggressive register markers.
