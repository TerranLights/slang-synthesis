## Grammar points

### Passive voice (introduced, not drilled)

Unit 16 introduces the **passive voice** — where the sentence subject is acted upon rather than
acting — formed with a conjugated form of **worden** + past participle: *Dat zijn kranten die in een
bepaalde stad of regio worden gepubliceerd* ("These are newspapers which are published in a
particular town or region"), *Er wordt ook veel aandacht besteed aan plaatselijk nieuws* ("A lot of
attention is also given to local news"). The book explicitly scopes this down: at this stage learners
only need to **recognize** the passive, not actively produce it, and defers active mastery to a
companion Dutch grammar reference in the same series.

### Adverb categories

The book organizes common adverbs into functional categories rather than presenting them as an
undifferentiated list: **if/likelihood** (misschien, waarschijnlijk, mogelijk, zeker, natuurlijk,
blijkbaar, hopelijk, beslist), **degree** (vrij, redelijk, echt, compleet, helemaal, erg, absoluut,
hartstikke — hartstikke flagged as informal), **how often** (vaak, weinig, soms, meestal, regelmatig,
altijd, zelden, nooit), plus separate **how/manner**, **time**, and **place** columns. A "degree"
adverb is frequently followed by another adverb or adjective it modifies (*Ik ben redelijk goed in
Nederlands*; *Dit artikel is vrij moeilijk*).

### Linking adverbs and adverb-initial inversion

Certain adverbs link one sentence/clause to another, again grouped by relationship: **time** (daarna,
daarvoor, toen, dan, nu), **cause/reason** (daardoor, daarom), **summing up** (ook, verder,
bovendien), **conclusion** (dus). Do not confuse **toen** as this kind of linking adverb ("then")
with **toen** as the subordinating conjunction ("when") from Unit 13 — same word, different
grammatical role. When one of these linking adverbs opens a clause, the clause undergoes
**inversion** (verb immediately after the adverb, before the subject) — the same "first grammatical
slot" principle already seen with sub-clause-first sentences in Unit 13. A **fixed exception list**
of adverbs does *not* trigger inversion when clause-initial, instead simply being followed by a
comma and normal subject-verb order: **overigens, althans, trouwens, tenminste, integendeel**.

### Of zo, en zo

**Of zo** and **en zo** ("or that kind of thing," "and that kind of thing") are added after giving an
example, in spoken language, to signal there are other unstated examples too; **bijvoorbeeld** ("for
example") is the more formal equivalent for flagging that something is illustrative rather than
exhaustive.
