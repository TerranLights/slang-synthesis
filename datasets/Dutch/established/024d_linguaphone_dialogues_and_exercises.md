## Scope-check methodology and decision (read this before the tables below)

This file is the product of a scope check, not a blind extraction, per this dispatch's
instructions. 8 representative pages were vision-read from "Tekst van de Lessen" (PDF pp. 1, 5,
20, 40, 60, 70–80, 81–84) and 18 from "Schriftelijke Oefeningen" (PDF pp. 140, 142, 145, 148,
150, 153, 156, 158, 160, 163, 166, 170, 173, 176, 179, 180, 182, 183), spanning Introduction
through Lesson 49 in both booklets. Findings and resulting scope decision:

**"Tekst van de Lessen" (PDF pp. 1–84) — mostly redundant, confirmed by the source's own stated
design.** PDF pp. 1–5 and 81–84 are English-language front matter (title page, preface,
speaker credits, table of contents — not extractable vocabulary content). The lesson dialogues
themselves (sampled at Les. 10, 30, 46) are continuous-prose conversations built entirely from
vocabulary the per-lesson Woordenlijst already glosses at first occurrence — this is not an
assumption but the source's own explicit design claim (PDF p. 5/printed p.3: "Extra Reading
Practice... will show the student how, **without going beyond the scope of the lessons**, he can
adapt the material he has learned"). Per the coverage rule's drill-exercise exclusion, these
dialogues are skipped as redundant. **Exception, extracted below:** the "Aanvullende
Leesoefeningen" (Extra Reading Practice) supplement, printed pp. 139–159 (PDF pp. 70–80). This
is a distinct section: cumulative review dialogues recombining vocabulary from pairs/groups of
lessons (genuinely low marginal value, skipped in bulk — same redundancy logic), **but its closing
pages (printed pp. 158–159) contain a "Bekende Citaten" (quotations from classic Dutch writers)
collection and a "Spreekwoorden" (proverbs) collection that appear nowhere else in this source** —
extracted in full below as genuinely new content. A small curated set of idiomatic
collocations spotted in the review dialogues (not decomposable from the per-word Woordenlijst
glosses) is also extracted.

**"Schriftelijke Oefeningen" (PDF pp. 138–183) — genuinely new, extracted as Grammar points.**
This booklet is pattern-drill exercises (fill-in-the-blank, transformation, unscramble,
sentence-combining), organized in 4 numbered "Oefening" sets per lesson-pair. Per the coverage
rule, the drill *items themselves* (10 near-identical sentences per exercise) are low-information
padding and are not reproduced — but the drill *structure* of each Oefening reveals an explicit
grammatical paradigm that is not stated as prose anywhere else in this source (the Woordenlijst is
a word list with no conjugation tables or syntax rules). This matches exactly what `024`'s header
anticipated for a planned `024e`. 18 sampled pages across Lessons 1–49 surfaced **16 distinct
grammar points**, documented below with paraphrase and a single illustrative example per point
(never the full 10-item drill), per copyright discipline.

**Not in scope for this file:** the Woordenlijst's own remaining Lessons 34–50, its phonology
appendix ("Notes on the Dutch Sounds," printed p. 55), and its two closing alphabetical
dictionaries (printed pp. 66, 88) are unextracted — but they belong to the Woordenlijst booklet
(already `024`'s assigned source), not to the two booklets this dispatch covers, and are left for
a separate dispatch.

## Grammar points

*(All drawn from the "Schriftelijke Oefeningen" booklet's paradigm-drill structure, printed pp.
2–88 (PDF pp. 138–183), spanning the Introduction through Lesson 49. Each point paraphrases a
pattern the drills make explicit; only one or two illustrative sentences are given per point,
never a full 10-item exercise, per copyright discipline.)*

### 1. Perfect-tense auxiliary selection: hebben vs. zijn

Exercises repeatedly drill converting between simple past and perfect tense, revealing the
zijn/hebben split explicitly: verbs of motion/change-of-state or intransitives expressing a change
take **zijn** ("Wij zijn met de trein gekomen," "Mijn moeder is verleden week gestorven," "Jan is
gevallen in de tuin"), while transitive/activity verbs take **hebben** ("Ik heb U niet goed
verstaan," "Het dienstmeisje heeft de tafel gedekt"). Les. 11-12 Oef.3 and Les. 27-28 Oef.1 drill
this split directly against each other, sentence by sentence.

### 2. Passive voice with worden, across all tenses, with door-marked agent

Les. 11-12 Oef.2 drills the passive paradigm itself: **wordt** (present), **werd** (past), **zal
worden** (future), **zou worden** (conditional) — e.g. "Hoe wordt de lunch dikwijls genoemd?" /
"Hier worden sinaasappels gekweekt." Les. 41-42 Oef.2 and Les. 45 Oef.2 add explicit agent-marking
with **door** + noun phrase ("De koeien worden door mijn oom gemolken," "Zandkastelen werden door
de kinderen gebouwd") and drill active↔passive conversion directly against each other.

### 3. Inchoative/ingressive aspect: gaan / komen + infinitive

Les. 11-12 Oef.4 drills a construction using **gaan** or **komen** + bare infinitive to express the
onset of an action or state ("Marie gaat al aardig Nederlands spreken" — "Marie is starting to
speak Dutch quite well now"; "Hij komt te weten waar je woont" — "he'll come to find out where you
live"). Distinct from the simple present.

### 4. Durative aspect, two competing strategies

Two distinct constructions express an ongoing action, drilled in different lessons:
(a) **posture verb (staan/zitten/liggen) + te + infinitive**, encoding the subject's physical
posture as part of the aspect marking (Les. 27-28 Oef.3: "Het kind ligt op de grond te spelen,"
"Moeder zit een sigaret te roken"); (b) **aan het + infinitive**, posture-neutral (Les. 39-40
Oef.2: "Ze was aan het schrijven, toen hij haar zag" — "She was writing when he saw her"). The
source drills these as separate paradigms in separate lessons rather than presenting them as
interchangeable.

### 5. Diminutive formation with irregular stem alternation

Beyond the regular -je/-tje/-pje/-kje suffix rules (documented from the Woordenlijst in `024`/
`024b`), Les. 17-18 Oef.3 drills diminutives that insert an extra syllable or alter the stem:
**pudding → puddinkje**, **vruchtensla → vruchtenslaatje** (not *vruchtenslaje*). Confirms the
diminutive suffix's allomorphy is sensitive to the final consonant/vowel of the stem in ways the
regular rule alone doesn't predict.

### 6. Present participle as prenominal adjective (-end + inflectional -e)

Les. 17-18 Oef.2 drills converting a finite-verb clause into a present-participle noun phrase: "De
kinderen lachen" → "**de lachende kinderen**"; "Mijn nichtje rookt een sigaret" → "**het rokende
nichtje**." The participle is formed with **-end** and takes the regular adjectival **-e** ending
in attributive position.

### 7. Material-noun-to-adjective derivation

Les. 33-34 Oef.2 drills converting "van + material noun" into a single derived adjective, mostly
via **-en**: "van leer gemaakt" → "**leren** schoenen," "van hout" → "**houten** vloeren," "van
zilver" → "**van zilver**" stays periphrastic in one item but "van zijde" → "**zijden** jurk" is
drilled as the derived form — confirming -en as the productive material-adjective suffix.

### 8. Comparative and superlative: synthetic vs. periphrastic, plus irregulars

Les. 29-30 Oef.1-2 drills synthetic comparison (**-er**, **-st**: jongste, drukste, kleinste,
oudste, mooiste, grootste) and the "even ... als" / "... dan ..." equative/comparative frame
("Deze piano is even mooi als die vleugel," "De reis per auto is sneller dan per trein"). Les.
37-38 Oef.1 separately drills the **het + -st** superlative pattern for adjectives already ending
in a sibilant/consonant cluster (**het aardigst**, **het mooist**) — confirming two superlative
surface forms exist side by side depending on the adjective's phonological shape (this
supplements, rather than duplicates, the periphrastic "het meest + adjective" pattern for longer
adjectives already implicit in `024c`'s Les. 44 material).

### 9. Substantivized indefinite: iets/wat + adjective-s

Les. 37-38 Oef.2 drills a fixed construction where an adjective following **iets** or **wat**
("something") takes an obligatory **-s**: "Heb ik iets verkeerds gezegd?", "Koop je iets lekkers
voor ons?" — the adjective is nominalized/substantivized in this position, distinct from its
normal attributive inflection.

### 10. Imperative: bare stem/infinitive, plus negative imperative

Les. 48 Oef.3 (context of public-sign/instruction imperatives) drills a full paradigm of bare
imperative forms: "Diep inademen!", "Doorlopen!", "Niet roken hier!", "Stil zijn!" — confirming the
imperative is formed from the bare verb stem (or, for "zijn," a suppletive **wees**, also drilled
separately at Les. 37-38 Oef.3: "Wees stil, als ik praat"), and that the negative imperative is
simply **niet** + infinitive/stem, with no separate negative-imperative morphology.

### 11. Pronominal-adverb (er-compound) splitting with prepositional verbs

Les. 5-6 Oef.1 drills the basic er-compounds as locative adverbs (**ernaast**, **erop**,
**ertegenover**, **erbij**, **erin**, **ervan**). Les. 45 Oef.1 shows these same compounds
*splitting* around a subordinate clause when the verb governs a fixed preposition: "Denk je **er
aan** dat je morgen moet werken?" (denken aan, "to think of/remember"), "Bent U **er** zeker
**van**, dat ze U betalen?" (zeker zijn van, "to be sure of") — the pronominal element **er**
fronts to before the finite verb while its governing preposition stays in its clause-final-ish
position, straddling the subordinate clause.

### 12. Existential er with plural-verb agreement

Les. 41-42 Oef.3 drills existential-**er** sentences with the verb agreeing in number with the
following notional subject, not with **er** itself: "Er **zitten** al bladeren aan de boom," "Er
**wachten** een paar klanten op U," vs. singular "Er **is** iemand aan de deur."

### 13. Headless/free relative clauses with die

Les. 49 Oef.2 drills a "die + verb..." clause functioning as its own subject with no antecedent
noun, equivalent to "those who...": "**Die** niet hard werken, zullen niet veel leren" ("Those who
don't work hard won't learn much"), "**Die** een kaartje voor het balcon hebben, moeten hier de
trap op" ("Those who have a balcony ticket must go up these stairs here").

### 14. Weak past tense formation and the 't kofschip devoicing rule

Les. 27-28 Oef.4 explicitly drills weak-verb past-tense endings **-de(n)** vs. **-te(n)** by stem
type: "De kapitein **leidde** de werkzaamheden" (leiden, voiced stem → -de), "Wij **haastten** ons
naar de uitgang" (haasten, stem ends in a voiceless consonant → -te). Confirms the classic Dutch
"'t kofschip" (or "'t fokschaap") consonant-devoicing mnemonic rule is operative and drilled as a
real paradigm choice, not left implicit.

### 15. Verbal-noun (gerund) nominalization: het + infinitive + van

Les. 15-16 Oef.3 drills converting a finite clause into a nominalized "het + infinitive (+ van +
object)" construction, functioning as an abstract action-noun: "Het **bespreken van** een kamer"
("the booking of a room"), "Het **dekken van** de tafel" ("the laying of the table"). This is a
productive gerund-like nominalization strategy distinct from any dedicated deverbal noun suffix.

### 16. Dutch clock-time convention and ordinal-number usage

Les. 45 Oef.3 confirms the Dutch half-hour clock convention counts toward the *coming* hour, not
back from the last one: "half vier" = 3:30 ("half past three," lit. "half four"), drilled directly
against 24-hour-style cues (15.30 → "half vier"). Les. 29-30 Oef.3 separately drills ordinal
numbers in dates/references ("de achtste maart," "de zestiende les," "de vijfentwintigste les"),
confirming regular ordinal formation with **-de/-ste** is productive well past the small numbers
shown in `024`'s core vocabulary.
