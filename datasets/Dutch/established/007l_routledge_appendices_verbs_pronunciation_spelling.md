## Grammar points

### 1. Diphthong-style vowel combinations (Appendix 2, new relative to established/001)

Beyond the single/double-letter short and long vowels already covered in established/001, this
appendix tabulates a further set of vowel-letter combinations, each described as a straightforward
combination of two already-known vowel sounds: *aai* (combination of *aa* + *ie*, e.g. *saai*
"boring"), *eeuw* (*ee* + *oe*, e.g. *leeuw* "lion"), *ieuw* (*ie* + *oe*, e.g. *nieuw* "new"), *oei*
(*oe* + *ie*, e.g. *doei* "bye"), *ooi* (*oo* + *ie*, e.g. *mooi* "beautiful"), *uw* (*uu* + *oe*,
e.g. *ruw* "rough").

### 2. The schwa ("sjwa") (Appendix 2, new)

Dutch has one further vowel sound not covered by the letter-based system above: an unstressed,
neutral "sjwa" sound (similar to the vowel in English "sister"), produced simply by letting air
escape from the mouth without a distinct target vowel quality. Distinctively, this single sound is
spelled multiple different ways depending on the word: *e* (*de* "the"), *ee* (*een* "a/an"), *i*
(*bezig* "busy"), *ij* (*mogelijk* "possible").

### 3. Word-final devoicing spelling rule: z/v → s/f (Appendix 3, new)

Dutch *z* and *v* frequently become *s* and *f* respectively at the end of a word (reflecting
final-obstruent devoicing), which surfaces as a genuine spelling alternation between a word's
different inflected forms: *reizen* "to travel" → *ik reis* "I travel" (not *\*reiz*); *geloven* "to
believe" → *ik geloof* "I believe." The same alternation applies to nouns and adjectives, not just
verbs: *brieven* "letters" → *een brief* "a letter"; *poezen* "cats" → *een poes* "a cat."

### 4. No word-final double consonants (Appendix 3, new)

Unlike English, Dutch words cannot end in a double consonant — English "ball," "bell," "fall" have
single-consonant Dutch cognates/equivalents: *bal, bel, ik val*. (This connects to, but is distinct
from, the consonant-doubling-on-suffixation rule already fully covered in established/001 — that
rule governs what happens when a syllable is *added*; this rule governs the bare, unsuffixed word
form.)
