**Source:** Bruce Donaldson, *Colloquial Dutch* (Routledge, 1996), back-matter "Alphabetical list of
irregular verbs," pp. 249-254 (PDF pp. 130-133, image spreads 130-133 of 162). The book's "Key to

answer key to earlier lessons' exercises and introduces no content not already captured in

name Bois-le-Duc) noted here for completeness since they were visible in passing.

**Coverage note:** Vision-read, no text layer, `verified` confidence throughout (clean print, no
marginalia). This is a ~150-entry alphabetical reference table of principal parts (infinitive,
imperfect singular, imperfect plural, past participle, gloss) for Dutch's strong/irregular verbs — a
large, systematic reference box, not vocabulary prose. Per the coverage rule's copyright discipline
(never bulk-reproduce a vocabulary box verbatim) and following this Dutch corpus's established
representative/curated approach for large reference lists, this file **does not transcribe all ~150
entries**. Instead it documents the systematic vowel-gradation (ablaut) pattern classes with
representative examples from each, and separately flags every entry the source itself register-marks
or footnotes as an exception — these are the highest-value items for this project's purposes. The
full list remains available at the cited page range if exhaustive lookup is ever needed.

## Grammar points

### Alphabetical list of irregular verbs (pp. 249-254)

A ~150-entry principal-parts reference table (infinitive / imperfect singular / imperfect plural /
past participle / English gloss), marked throughout with `†` (always `zijn` in the perfect) and `+`
(either `zijn` or `hebben`, meaning-dependent). The list's own headline note (see vocabulary table's
`staan`/`opstaan` and `trekken`/`vertrekken` pairs) makes an important systematic point: a base
verb's auxiliary-selection behavior is **not** inherited by compounds formed from it, in either
direction — this must be checked per-verb rather than assumed from a known root form. Several
individual entries carry footnoted exceptions/traps (see vocabulary table) that are of more interest
to this project than the bulk of the regularly-patterned entries: two derivationally-related-looking
but actually-regular "impostor" verbs (`glimlachen`, `stofzuigen`), a homograph-with-different-
conjugation pair (`slaan`/`slagen`), free vs. meaning-conditioned auxiliary variation
(`verliezen` vs. `vergeten`), co-existing strong/weak imperfect doublets (`willen`'s `wou`/`wilde`,
`waaien`'s `woei`/`waaide`), and one informal-register gloss (`zuipen`, "to booze") standing out
against an otherwise neutral-register list.
