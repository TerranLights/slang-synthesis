# Dutch — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *Klare taal! Uitgebreide basisgrammatica NT2* (Deel 2, Lessons 63–70)

**Source:** Jenny van der Toorn-Schutte, *Klare taal! Uitgebreide basisgrammatica NT2* (Houten,
2006). Continues `033i` (Lessons 53–62). Covers **Lessons 63–70** (printed pp. 162–176), same
confirmed `PDF page = printed page − 1` offset, theory-page-only vision-reading.

**Coverage note:** Lessons 63–70 cover the imperative/subjunctive mood in practical use (recipe/
instruction registers, the archaic-flavored fixed subjunctive forms *leve, moge, gelieve*),
question-sentence pragmatics (information questions, confirmation-seeking questions, polite-request
questions, reproach/rebuke questions, proposal questions, disbelief/surprise questions, warning
questions), fixed-preposition verbs and meaning shift via preposition/prefix change (this lesson —
Les 66 — is the same lesson `033`'s own header note ties its "Werkwoorden met een prefix" appendix
to; already fully captured there, so no additional extraction here beyond the Idioom), clause
reduction/*samentrekking* (ellipsis in coordinate clauses, including word-level ellipsis like *op- en
aanmerkingen*), *beknopte bijzinnen* (infinitive-clause clause reduction, *om te*), compounding
(*samenstellingen* — noun+noun, verb-stem+noun, preposition+noun, adjective+adjective), and
derivation (*afleidingen* — deverbal nouns/adjectives via *-zaam/-baar/-lijk* and a wide survey of
other derivational suffixes: *-heid, -ier, -erij, -ing, -aar, -in, -esse, -lijk, -loos, -vol, -isch,
-ig, -achtig, -jes*). **All standard, already covered across established/001–017** (compounding,
derivation, and clause-reduction patterns are core content in comprehensive Dutch reference grammars,
e.g. the Comprehensive Grammar parts already in this project's baseline). None re-derived here.
**Every lesson's Idioom entry is captured in full**, including Lesson 67's genuinely interesting
two-item entry, one of which (*met kunst- en vliegwerk*) is itself a worked example of this lesson's
own *samentrekking* grammar point.

## Grammar points

No new grammar points beyond the coverage note above.

### Idioom — full harvest, Lessons 63–70

- **Les 63** (Het gebruik van de gebiedende en de aanvoegende wijs, printed p. 162): *Wie de schoen
  past, trekke hem aan* — "if the shoe fits, wear it" (direct cognate idiom, and itself an archaic
  subjunctive-mood form — *trekke* — illustrating this lesson's own grammar point).
- **Les 64** (Het gebruik van vraagzinnen (1), printed p. 164): *Wat heb ik nou aan mijn fiets
  hangen?* — literally "what do I have hanging on my bicycle now?", idiomatically "what on earth is
  going on now?!" — an exasperated-confusion interjection.
- **Les 65** (Het gebruik van vraagzinnen (2), printed p. 166): *Heb je je tong verloren?* —
  literally "did you lose your tongue?", functionally equivalent to English "cat got your tongue?"
  (said to someone unexpectedly silent).
- **Les 66** (Werkwoorden met een vast voorzetsel, printed p. 168): *Recht hebben op...* — "to have
  a right to..." — a fixed collocation rather than a figurative idiom. **This lesson's own grammar
  content (fixed-preposition/prefix verbs and their meaning shifts) is the lesson `033`'s "Werkwoorden
  met een prefix" appendix is explicitly tied to** — already fully captured in `033` and `033b`; no
  further extraction needed here.
- **Les 67** (Samentrekking, printed p. 170): **two** idioms given together:
  - *Zoals de ouden zongen, piepen de jongen* — literally "as the old ones sang, the young ones
    chirp," idiomatically "the apple doesn't fall far from the tree / children follow in their
    parents' footsteps (in habits, attitudes, or ways of speaking)."
  - *Met kunst- en vliegwerk* — literally "with art- and flying-work" (a word-level *samentrekking*
    of *kunstwerk* and *vliegwerk*, exactly this lesson's own grammar point), idiomatically "by the
    skin of one's teeth / through great effort and improvisation."
- **Les 68** (Beknopte bijzinnen, printed p. 172): *Te mooi om waar te zijn* — "too good to be true"
  (direct cognate idiom, also a worked example of this lesson's own *te ... om ... te* infinitive-
  clause construction).
- **Les 69** (Samenstellingen, printed p. 174): *Een olifantenhuid hebben* — literally "to have an
  elephant's hide," idiomatically "to be thick-skinned" (impervious to criticism/insults).
- **Les 70** (Afleidingen, printed p. 176): *Oefening baart kunst* — "practice makes perfect"
  (direct cognate idiom, literally "practice gives birth to skill/art").
