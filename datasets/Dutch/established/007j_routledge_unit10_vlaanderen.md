## Grammar points

### 1. Indirect questions

The finite verb in a Dutch subclause moves to clause-final position, with a fixed internal ordering
when multiple verbs stack up: (1) finite verb first, (2) modal verbs before other non-finite verbs,
(3) infinitives like *gaan, komen, laten* before the main verb but after any modal, (4) the main verb
last. Example: *Zij heeft nog niet beslist waar ze graag zou willen gaan wonen* (finite / modal /
other infinitive / main verb). In the perfect tenses, *hebben/zijn* and the participle can swap order
without a meaning change. The question word from the corresponding direct question is reused as the
subclause's linking conjunction (*Waar wil ze gaan wonen?* → *Ze weet niet waar ze wil gaan wonen*).
A preposition + *wat* becomes *waar* + preposition when forming the question word (*houden van iets*
→ *Waarvan houd je?*, never *\*Van wat houd je?*). Tense in the indirect question must match/reflect
the tense of the main clause, which can require shifting the embedded verb into the past tense (*Wie
gaat er mee?* → *Zij vroeg wie er mee ging*).

### 2. Use of *men*, *je*, *ze* as impersonal subjects

*Men* is used to talk about people in general and can only function as a subject; it's usually
replaceable by *ze* or by a passive construction (*Men fluistert dat er spoedig gezinsuitbreiding
komt* ≈ *Er wordt gefluisterd dat...* ≈ *Ze fluisteren dat...*). *Je* is used for general statements
that implicitly include the speaker and listener (*Het zal je maar overkomen, zo'n inbreker in je
slaapkamer* — "Imagine that happening to you"); using *je* creates some distance between speaker and
topic while also drawing the listener into the statement.

### 3. Position of direct and indirect object pronouns

The indirect object normally precedes the direct object, *unless* the direct object is a pronoun, in
which case the direct-object pronoun moves immediately after the finite verb, ahead of the indirect
object: *Caesar vertelt zijn senatoren dat...* (full NPs, indirect-before-direct order) vs. *Caesar
vertelt het zijn senatoren* / *Caesar vertelt het hen* (direct-object pronoun *het* jumps ahead of the
indirect object). Both direct and indirect object pronouns immediately follow the finite verb; the
book notes that spoken usage can vary this ordering somewhat.
