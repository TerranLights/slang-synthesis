# Dutch — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Teach Yourself Dutch Grammar, Units 1–23

**Source:** Teach Yourself Dutch Grammar (workbook/reference-and-practice format, explanation +
facing-page exercise per unit), 129-PDF-page scan, 100 numbered grammar units (pp. 2–198) plus
front/back matter. This file covers Units 1–23 (sentence composition through possession).

**Coverage note:** No text layer — vision-read page by page. Every PDF page is a two-printed-
page spread (confirmed empirically: PDF page 7 = book pp. 2–3, and the offset holds as
`PDF page = unit number + 5` for units 3–100, one full unit's explanation+exercise per PDF page).
Units 1–2 ("what's the use of grammar?", "double Dutch?") are meta-preface prose with no
grammar/vocabulary content and are skipped. No handwritten marginalia found on any page read for
this file — all content is clean typeset print.

**Non-redundant-supplement scope decision.** This book covers the same beginner/intermediate
territory as several already-extracted sources — `established/001` (Shetter & Van der Cruysse-
Van Antwerpen, spelling/plural), `established/010`/`011` (grammar workbooks), `established/012`/
`013`/`013b` (Comprehensive Grammar of Dutch). **Grammar-rule explanations are paraphrased only
where this source adds something not already captured** (a different framing, an exception, a
usage nuance) — where a unit's rule is pure restatement of already-covered ground (e.g. basic
plural -en/-s/-'s, basic present-tense conjugation), the explanation below is deliberately terse
with a pointer rather than a full re-derivation. **All vocabulary is kept** — every exercise in
this book pairs Dutch terms with an English gloss, and these are independent lexical data points
from an independently-authored source, per the coverage rule. Pure grammatical filler (bare
pronouns, single-letter drill variables, unglossed proper names used only as exercise subjects)
is not treated as a vocabulary item.

## Grammar points

### Unit 3 — Sentence composition (subject/verb/object)
Basic clause constituents. Fully standard, no new information beyond `established/001`/`010`.

### Unit 4/5 — Spelling: vowel length and consonant devoicing
Closed vs. open syllable spelling rule for long/short vowels (double vowel in closed syllable,
single vowel in open syllable, e.g. `maan`→`manen`), and consonant-doubling to keep a syllable
closed (`man`→`mannen`). Unit 5 adds the b/d/v/z final-devoicing rule (pronounced/written as
p/t/f/s at word end: `brieven`→`brief`, `huizen`→`huis`). This matches the phonology already
captured in `established/001` in more depth; kept here mainly for its distinct exercise vocabulary
set (p. 6–9).

### Unit 6/7 — Articles and nouns; compound nouns; feminine/derived nouns
`de`/`het`/`een` article system, countable/uncountable distinction, and the roughly 2:1 `de`:`het`
ratio — standard, matches prior coverage. Unit 7's productive derivational patterns (feminine
agent nouns via `-e`/`-es`/`-in`/`-ster`; adjective/participle→noun via `-e`, pluralized with `-n`)
are a genuinely useful compact summary of Dutch nominalization not laid out this concisely
elsewhere in this project's Dutch established/ files.

### Unit 8 — Demonstratives (deze/die/dit/dat)
Gender-conditioned demonstrative choice (`de`-words: deze/die; `het`-words: dit/dat), all-plural
default to deze/die, and the `dit is/dat is/het is` construction where deze/die are barred even
with a `de`-word referent — this last point (deze/die never used with `zijn` to introduce people/
things) is a specific exception worth flagging as it's easy to over-generalize.

### Unit 9/10/11 — Adjectives: form, derivation, and nominalized use
Standard -e inflection rule (post-nominal: no -e; pre-nominal: add -e; exception: indefinite
`het`-words and -en-final adjectives never take -e). Units 10–11 cover adjective derivation from
verbs/nouns (participle-as-adjective, -baar/-lijk/-loos suffixes) and adjective-to-noun conversion
(`de grote`, "the big one"; abstract sense forces `het`; quantity words like `velen`/`allen` make
person-referring plurals). This derivational-morphology material is a useful, fairly systematic
supplement — Dutch's mildly-fusional adjective-inflection profile (per this language's
morphological-typology note) is well illustrated by the -e/no-e alternation examples here.

### Unit 12/13 — Comparative and superlative
Regular `-er`/`-st` formation, r-stem `-d-` insertion (duur→duurder), irregular triad
goed/veel/weinig, and Unit 13's direct/negative/equative comparison patterns (dan/als, minder...
dan, niet zo...als, even...als, net zo...als, hoe...hoe..., steeds...). The `dan` vs. `als`
prescriptive-vs.-actual-usage note (Unit 13 §A) is a genuine, source-explicit register/usage-shift
observation worth flagging even though the grammatical mechanism itself is standard.

### Unit 14/15/16 — Plural nouns, compound nouns, diminutives
Standard -en/-s/-'s plural rules and common irregulars (stad/steden, kind/kinderen, -heid/-heden,
Latin -us/-i) — largely redundant with `established/001` and `012`/`013`'s fuller plural
paradigms; kept terse. Compound-noun formation (juxtaposition, -s-/-e-/-en- linking, verb-stem-
initial compounds, adjective/adjective compounds) is a clean, compact treatment. **Diminutives
(Unit 16) substantially overlap with `established/003` (Chapter 22, dedicated diminutive chapter)
— the four core allomorphs (-je/-tje/-pje/-etje/-kje) and phonological conditioning match that
file's more detailed treatment closely.** What's kept here as non-redundant: Unit 16 §C's explicit
three-way pragmatic breakdown (endearment / positive attitude / negative attitude-or-contempt)
with its own distinct illustrative examples (`kereltje`, `vreemd zaakje`, `in het zonnetje met een
biertje`) not found verbatim in `established/003`.

### Unit 17–20 — Numbers, fractions/percentages, weights/measurements/time
Cardinal and ordinal number formation, the eé/trema convention distinguishing "een" (a/an) from
"één" (one) and marking new-syllable starts in compound numbers, decimal-comma/thousands-point
convention (opposite of English), fractions, percentages and temperature phrasing, and Dutch-
specific weight/measure units (`ons`, `pond`) that lack direct English equivalents. Unit 20's
approximation idioms (`een week of twee`, `hij loopt tegen de 50`) and the `zoveelste`/`hoeveelste`
ordinal-interrogative pair (source-flagged as having no direct English equivalent) are worth
keeping as genuinely distinct lexical/idiomatic content.

### Unit 21/22/23 — Personal pronouns, addressing people, possession
Full subject/object pronoun paradigm with stressed/unstressed alternate forms. Unit 22 covers the
je/jij vs. u informal/formal address distinction (sociolinguistically significant, matches
register material already captured elsewhere) and gender-conditioned "it" pronoun choice (`het`
for het-words, `hij`/`hem` for de-words) plus `die` as a common spoken alternative to `hij`/`ze`
for de-word reference. Unit 23 covers possessive pronouns (stressed/unstressed forms), -s
possessive (rare, apostrophe generally omitted except where needed for pronunciation), and the
much more common `van`-construction for possession, plus the `die/dat van + object pronoun`
construction for "mine/yours/his/hers/theirs."
