## Grammar points

### Language point 1 — The indefinite pronouns "something/anything" and "someone/anyone"

Unlike English, which swaps "something"/"someone" for "anything"/"anyone" in questions and
negatives, Dutch uses **iets** for both "something" and "anything" in a question (**Heb je iets
voor me?**), with negation carried entirely by switching to **niets** ("not anything" = **Nee, ik
heb niets voor je**). Parallel system for **iemand** ("someone/anyone") vs. **niemand** ("no
one/not anyone"). "Something/someone else" = **iets anders/iemand anders**. Colloquially, **wat**
substitutes for **iets** and **niks** for **niets** (**Ik heb wat voor je**; **Ik weet er niks
van**). p.144.

### Language point 2 — Adjectival participial phrases

Adjectival constructions built on a past or present participle can precede a noun in place of a
(wordier) relative clause, and the participle construction typically also has an acceptable
post-nominal-clause English translation: **Hoe ziet het zojuist ingevoerde paspoort ... eruit?**
("What does the pet passport that has just been introduced look like?"); **Alle in dit gebied
voorkomende aardbevingen zijn boven de 6,5** ("All earthquakes which occur in this area..."). Less
commonly the pre-nominal phrase contains an infinitive rather than a participle instead: **Het nog
door u te betalen bedrag** ("The amount still to be paid by you"), contrasted with the participial
**Het al door u betaalde bedrag** ("The amount already paid by you"). In practice, spoken Dutch
almost always uses the simpler relative-clause version (**het bedrag dat nog door u betaald moet
worden**) — the participial-phrase construction mainly needs to be *recognized* when reading. pp.
148–149.

### Language point 3 — The indefinite pronouns iets, niets, alles, ergens, nergens and overal

When "something"/"nothing"/"everything" (**iets/niets/alles**) is governed by a preposition, Dutch
replaces them with **ergens/nergens/overal** respectively, with the adverb preceding the
preposition (**Hij heeft iets nodig** → **Hij heeft het ergens voor nodig**; **Ik zie niets** →
**Ik kijk nergens naar**; **Dat kind heeft alles** → **Dat kind speelt overal mee**). However, if the
pronoun is followed by a relative clause (even with a preceding preposition), **iets/niets/alles**
are used as normal, not the **ergens/nergens/overal** forms (**Ze keek naar iets wat ik geschilderd
had**; **Hij is met niets wat zijn dochter doet tevreden**). The source also cross-references the
parallel **er/hier/daar/waar** + preposition system for pronominal objects (**Hij speelt met de
bal** → **Hij speelt ermee**; **Met welke bal speelt hij?** → **Waarmee speelt hij?**). pp.149–150.
