## Grammar points

### *toen* vs. *dan* for past-tense "then": single occasion vs. repeated occasions

Noted under "then": the choice between *toen* and *dan* for 'then' depends not just on sense but
also on tense. Broadly, if the clause's verb is in the past tense, the word is *toen*; if present or
future, it is *dan*. But a further wrinkle applies within the past tense itself: *toen* specifically
means "then on that ONE occasion in the past" or "at a particular point in time in the past" — if
'then' occurs in a past-tense sentence but refers to REPEATED occasions, *dan* is used instead (e.g.
*Vaak duurden hun reizen erg lang en dan kwamen ze terug...* "Often their voyages took a long time
and then they [would] return..."). The same *als*/*toen* distinction applies to 'when' elsewhere in
the source.
