## Grammar points

### Interrogatives (pp. 91-92)

Standard wh-word inventory: `wanneer`, `hoe`, `hoeveel`, `waarom`, `wat`, `wat voor een...`, `wie`,
`van wie`, `waar`, `waar...vandaan`, `waar...naartoe`, `welk(e)`. `Welk` inflects like an adjective
(`-e` before common-gender/plural). With verbs of motion, `waar` is always translated `waarnaartoe`
even though English "where" alone often suffices — `naartoe` can either attach directly to `waar`
or, more commonly in speech, separate to the end of the clause.

### Dutch naming conventions (pp. 93-94)

Given names commonly abbreviated in colloquial/nickname use (`Jaap`←Jacob, `Ruud`←Rudolf, `Ria`←
Maria). Phonebook alphabetization skips tussenvoegsels (`van`, `de`, `van den`) when a surname
stands with its first name/initial, sorting instead by the following element — practical
consequence: you must know someone's actual first name to find them, since the `roepnaam` isn't
predictable from it. Capitalization rule: `van`/`de`/`van den` are lowercase when preceded by a
first name or initial, capitalized when the surname stands alone or after `meneer`/`mevrouw`.

### The future tense (pp. 94-96)

Three strategies, same inventory as English (present tense, `gaan` + infinitive, `zullen` +
infinitive) but **different distributional frequency**: Dutch defaults to the present tense far more
readily than English does even with an explicit future-time adverb (`Ik koop volgend jaar een
nieuwe auto` — present tense despite "next year"), reserving `zullen` for contexts with no
time-adverb or for the "shall we...?" suggestion construction. `Zou(den)` (historically `zullen`'s
past tense) is the ordinary way to render "would," including in the common request frame `Ik zou
graag... willen (hebben/gaan)`.

### TMP word order — time/manner/place (p. 97)

A fixed ordering constraint: adverbial expressions of Time, Manner, and Place must occur in that
relative order within a Dutch clause, regardless of how many of the three are present. Fronting a
time expression (very common in Dutch) forces subject-verb inversion per the general F-V-N-Rest
schema, and because time already precedes manner/place structurally, no further reordering is
needed once it's fronted.

### Position of objects relative to time/manner/place (p. 98)

A direct-object noun follows a time expression; a direct-object pronoun precedes it
(`Hij heeft gisteren zijn auto verkocht` vs. `Hij heeft hem gisteren verkocht`) — pronoun objects
are lighter/more topic-like and gravitate earlier in the clause, a general weight-based ordering
principle rather than a fixed positional slot.
