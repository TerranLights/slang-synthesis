## Grammar points

### Language point 1 — The conditional

The conditional ("would + infinitive") uses **zou(den)**, the past tense of **zal/zullen**, exactly
as English "would" is historically the past of "will": **Ik zou het niet kopen (als ik het me niet
kon veroorloven)**. Dutch has a second, fully equivalent stylistic option: the plain imperfect
(simple past) can stand in for the conditional whenever context makes the conditional sense clear,
so **Ik kocht het niet** can mean "I wouldn't buy it" (not "I didn't buy it") when the surrounding
clause supplies the unreal/hypothetical framing — **kocht** substitutes for **zou kopen** the same
way **kon** can substitute for **zou kunnen**. The source explicitly labels the two as "stylistic
variants," not a meaning distinction. pp.84-85.

### Language point 2 — Word formation

The source is explicit that Dutch derivational word-formation has no single hard-and-fast rule set
— it presents a large worked array of verb→noun derivations to be learned by exposure/pattern-
matching rather than by rule: bare-stem nominalizations with a vowel/consonant shift
(**piekeren→het gepieker**/**de piekeraar**; **tobben→het getob**/**de tobber**;
**zeuren→het gezeur**/**de zeurder**~**de zeurpiet**), the productive **ge-** + verb-stem pattern
for "the activity of X-ing" nominals seen across all three examples above, **-tenis**/**-enis**
abstract-noun formations (**gebeuren→de gebeurtenis**; **geschieden→de geschiedenis**;
**beleven→de belevenis**), bare-stem noun conversion (**bezoeken→het bezoek**;
**onderzoeken→het onderzoek**; **verzuimen→het verzuim**; **zorgen→de zorg**;
**arbeiden→de arbeid**), and **-ing** abstract nominals (**schatten→de schatting**;
**belasten→de belasting**; **veranderen→de verandering**). Separately, the **-heid** suffix
(cognate with English **-ness**) is presented as a genuinely productive, rule-governed process for
deriving abstract nouns from adjectives: **gezond→de gezondheid**, **ijdel→de ijdelheid**,
**bedrijvig→de bedrijvigheid**, **zuinig→de zuinigheid**. p.87-88.

### Language point 3 — The pluperfect and conditional perfect

The pluperfect ("had done") substitutes **had(den)**/**was(waren)** for **heeft/hebben** or
**is/zijn** in the perfect tense — critically, verbs taking **zijn** in the perfect (motion/change-
of-state verbs) take **was/waren**, *not* **had/hadden**, in the pluperfect: **Ik was al naar huis
gegaan** ("I had already gone home"), **Hij was gevallen** ("He had fallen"). Exactly parallel to
Language point 1's imperfect-for-conditional stylistic variant, the *conditional perfect* ("would
have done") has the same two-option system: the periphrastic **zou(den) + past participle +
hebben/zijn** (**ik zou verwacht hebben**; **hij zou gevallen zijn**) alternates with the plain
pluperfect standing in for it by context (**ik had verwacht** can mean "I would have expected," not
only "I had expected"). pp.89-90.
