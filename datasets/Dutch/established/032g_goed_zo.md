## Grammar points

### Separable verb prefixes (Глаголы с отделяемыми приставками)
A major new word-class distinction: Dutch verbal prefixes fall into two categories. **Unstressed
prefixes** (`be-, ge-, her-, ver-, ont-`, plus a few others already seen — `betalen, geloven,
herinneren, ontbijten, vertellen`) never separate from the verb stem. **Stressed prefixes** — a much
larger, more open class, often identical in form to a preposition, adverb, noun, or adjective
(`meebrengen, opstaan, dichtdoen, paardrijden, stilstaan`) — **do** separate: in a finite clause, the
separable prefix moves to the very end of the clause (`Ik sta laat op`, `Sta je nooit laat op?`). In
an infinitive construction with `te`, the `te` is inserted **between** the prefix and the verb stem,
and the whole unit is written as three separate words (`Ik probeer niet laat op te staan`). In the
perfect participle, the `ge-` prefix is inserted between the separable prefix and the stem, and the
whole is written solid as one word (`opgestaan`, `meegebracht`). A noun/adjective functioning as a
separable prefix follows the identical rule (`paardrijden→Ik rijd paard`; `stilstaan→Hij staat stil`).
Some verbs occur **only** with their prefix attached, never bare (`uitnodigen`, `zich aankleden` —
the bare `zich kleden` exists but is markedly rarer). A large reference list of separable-prefix verbs
is given (`aanbellen, aankijken, zich aankleden, aankomen(z), aannemen, achterblijven(z), afkomen(z),
zich afvragen, afwachten, binnenkomen(z), bovenkomen(z), buitenkomen(z), dichtdoen, dichtgaan(z),
doorbrengen, doorlezen, langsgaan bij(z), langskomen bij(z), meebrengen, meenemen, meegaan(z),
meevallen(z)/tegenvallen(z), nadenken, opbellen, opeten, opstaan(z), opendoen, opengaan(z),
overgaan(z), paardrijden, stilstaan, tegenkomen, teruggaan(z), uitnodigen, vasthouden, weggaan(z)`) —
kept as a reference set since these recur constantly through the rest of the book.

### `on-` negative prefix — restricted productivity (extends 032's `ongeduldig` note)
Parallels Russian "не-"/"без-" but is **not freely productive** the way Russian's negative prefix is:
it attaches to some adjectives (`onbelangrijk, oneerlijk, onprettig`) but not others, where a bare
`niet` + adjective must be used instead (`niet breed`, not *`onbreed`*; `niet zoet`, not *`onzoet`*).
Can also attach to nouns (`de onzin` = "nonsense").

### Dimension and age expressions
A fixed sentence-pattern is **obligatory** for stating a measurement or age — cannot be paraphrased
freely: `[subject] is [number] [unit] [dimension-adjective]` (`Het strand is honderd meter breed`,
`Deze deur is zes centimeter dik`, `Hij is twaalf (jaar) oud`). Measure nouns (`meter, kilometer,
centimeter`) stay singular after a numeral, same rule as `jaar`/`uur`/`liter` (032c/032f).

### `een paar` + noun — agreement rule
`een paar` (≈ "a few/several") is followed by a noun that stays **singular** if that noun doesn't
otherwise pluralize after a numeral (`een paar uur`), but the finite verb still agrees as **plural**
when `een paar + noun` is the subject (`Er staan een paar fietsen tegen het raam`).

### `zitten` — high-frequency verb, idiom cluster
Flagged as one of the most frequent Dutch verbs, entering many fixed expressions beyond its literal
"to sit": `Waar heb je gezeten?` ("where have you been?"), `Het zit namelijk zo` ("here's the thing/
the situation is this"), `Antwoord je nog, of hoe zit 't?` ("are you going to answer, or what?"), `Het
zit wel goed` ("don't worry, it's fine"), `Zit niet aan mijn boeken` ("don't touch my books" — `aan`
+ zitten = "to touch/handle"), `Hij zit op een goede school` ("he attends/is enrolled at a good
school"), `We zitten hier maar te zitten` ("we're just sitting around doing nothing"), `Het zit me tot
hier` ("I'm fed up to here with it"), `Ze zaten me op de hielen` ("they were hot on my heels"), `Die
zit!` ("that hit home!/bullseye!" — sports-derived idiom, per source note: originally "the ball hit
the target").

### `leven` / `wonen` / `logeren` — three-way near-synonym distinction
`leven` = "to live/be alive," typically paired with a time-span adverbial describing a lifespan or era
(`Ze leefden nog lang en gelukkig`). `wonen` = "to reside," typically paired with a place adverbial
(`Hij woont nog bij zijn ouders`). `logeren (bij)` = "to stay/lodge temporarily (with someone)," as a
guest (`We hebben bij oma gelogeerd`). `Leve de koningin!` = "Long live the Queen!" (subjunctive-like
fixed formula using bare `leve`, not conjugated `leeft`).

### Prepositions of place, continued: `af, binnen, boven, weg, open, dicht, vast`
These function as adjectives/adverbs but also double as prepositions and, per the separable-verb
section above, as separable prefixes:
- **`af`** ("finished/down"): `Is het boek af?` = "is the book finished?"
- **`binnen`**/**`boven`**: also prepositions ("inside [a place]"/"above"), and `binnen` can mean
  temporal "within" (`Ik doe dat binnen een week`).
- **`weg`**: adjective/adverb "away/gone" (`Kees is weg`), but also a noun, `de weg` ("the road" —
  diminutive `wegje/weggetje`); `ver weg` = "far away"; `onderweg` = "on the way/en route."
- **`open`**/**`dicht`**: "open"/"closed," used predicatively (`De deur is dicht, maar de ramen zijn
  open`); `dichtbij` = "nearby" (contrasted with `vlakbij`, 032b).
- **`vast`**: literally "firm/solid," but `vast (wel)` idiomatically means "probably" (`Piet weet dat
  vast wel/niet` = "Piet probably (doesn't) know that").
- `Dat spreekt vanzelf` = "that goes without saying."

### `achter, door, langs, na` — four more prepositions
`achter` = "behind"; `door` = "through" (motion penetrating a boundary — contrasted explicitly with
`over`, which marks motion **along/across a surface**: `over het strand` vs. `door het bos`); `langs`
= "along/past"; `na` = "after" (temporal). Fixed phrases: `een dag/week na...` ("a day/week after...")
paired with `een dag/week voor...` ("a day/week before...").

### Full cardinal numeral system (13 and up)
`dertien(13)...negentien(19)` stress the first syllable; compound tens (`twintig(20)` onward) carry
two stresses and are written solid with no spelling changes despite being phonologically two words
(`eenentwintig(21)`). In two-digit numbers, units are named before tens, joined by an unstressed-
pronounced `en` (`vierenvijftig` = 54, literally "four-and-fifty"). `honderd`(100) is never preceded
by `één`. `wel` before a numeral can mean "at least" (`Het strand is wel tweehonderd meter breed`).
Fixed idioms: `Hij is tegen de veertig` ("he's nearing 40"), `Hij is al over de veertig` ("he's past
40"), `boven de zestig` / `onder de twaalf` ("over 60"/"under 12," age brackets). Plural verb
agreement with an approximate time expression uses `tegen, bij, na, voor` (`Het is al tegen zessen`,
`Hij komt na vijven`, `Hij komt niet voor negenen` — the numeral itself takes a plural-like `-en`
ending in this specific time-approximation construction). Presence/absence of a numeral before a noun
doesn't change how negation is formed (`Er zijn nog geen tien mensen gekomen`).

### Indefinite and negative pronouns/adverbs
Full paired system: `iemand/niemand` ("someone/no one"), `iets/niets` ("something/nothing"),
`ergens/nergens` ("somewhere/nowhere"), plus `al/alles` ("all"), `allerlei` ("various/all sorts of"),
and `(de) een of ander(e)` ("some ... or other"). Key rules:
- A clause whose grammatical subject is one of these indefinite pronouns typically begins with
  existential `er` (`Er staat iemand voor de deur`).
- Unlike Russian, **only one** negative word may appear per clause (`Ik zie nergens iemand`, not
  *`nergens niemand`*).
- Dutch makes **no distinction** between "someone/anyone" or "something/anything" senses the way
  English (or Russian's -то/-нибудь/-либо system) does — `iemand`/`iets` cover all of these uniformly.
- `zoiemand`/`zoiets` (written solid) = "someone/something like that."
- `iemands`/`niemands` can function possessively (`Is dit iemands wijn?`).
- Colloquial register often substitutes `niks` for `niets`.
- `al` (indefinite pronoun, "all") is a homograph of the unrelated adverb `al` ("already") — not to be
  confused; the corresponding adjectival form is `alle`, not `al`, before a definite/specified noun
  (`Is dit alle rijst?`, `Dit zijn al mijn boeken`). Fixed idiom: `Ik heb alle tijd` = "I have plenty
  of time."
- `van allerlei` = "of all sorts" (`Er is van allerlei` = "there's a bit of everything").
- Diminutive/intensified forms: `ietsje meer` ("a tiny bit more"), `een niemendalletje` ("a trifle/
  nothing much").
