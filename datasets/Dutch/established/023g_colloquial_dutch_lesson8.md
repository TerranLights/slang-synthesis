## Grammar points

### Diminutives (pp. 116-119)

Six phonologically-conditioned allomorph rules (more granular than `established/003`'s four-way
split from a different source book):

1. **Default `-je`** for most nouns (`aap→aapje`, `huis→huisje`).
2. **`-tje`** after a long vowel/diphthong word-finally or before `l`/`n`/`r` (`ei→eitje`,
   `stoel→stoeltje`), and after unstressed `-el`/`-en`/`-er` (`tafel→tafeltje`,
   `kamer→kamertje`). Nouns ending `-a`/`-o`/`-u` (mostly loanwords) double the vowel before
   `-tje` (`oma→omaatje`, `auto→autootje`); note `café→cafeetje`, a single-`e` exception.
3. **`-etje`** after a short vowel + `l`/`m`/`n`/`ng`/`r` (`bel→belletje`, `ding→dingetje`,
   `pan→pannetje`); a few short-vowel + `b`/`g`/`p` nouns also take `-etje` though most belong to
   rule 1 (`rib→ribbetje`, `kip→kippetje`, `vlag→vlaggetje`).
4. **`-pje`** after `m` (`boom→boompje`, `bezem→bezempje`).
5. **`-etje`** for monosyllabic `-ing` nouns (`ring→ringetje`, `ding→dingetje`) and for nouns
   ending `-eling`/`-ening` (`wandeling→wandelingetje`); all other unstressed `-ing` nouns take
   plain `-je` with `g`→`k` (`koning→koninkje`, `regering→regerinkje`) — a genuinely intricate
   sub-rule not captured with this granularity in `established/003`.
6. A subset of nouns that lengthen their vowel in the plural (cross-reference `established/001`'s
   plural coverage) **also** lengthen it in the diminutive (`gat→gaatje`, `pad→paadje`,
   `glas→glaasje`, `schip→scheepje`) — a systematic three-way correspondence (singular/plural/
   diminutive vowel length) worth flagging for the morphological-typology profile.

**All diminutives are grammatically neuter** regardless of the base noun's own gender
(`de brug`→`het bruggetje`), and pluralize with `-s` without exception (`de bruggetjes`).

**Pragmatic functions beyond literal smallness** (source-flagged as "one of the most unique and
charming aspects of Dutch," "seldom translatable into English," and explicitly contrasted with
English *doggy*/*bickie* as childish-register-only — the Dutch diminutive is not restricted to
childish register): (1) **gezelligheid/coziness** (`een leuk avondje` "a nice night"), (2)
**affection** (`wat een lief huisje` "what a sweet house"), (3) **contempt** (`een burgermannetje`
"a petit-bourgeois"). This pragmatic breadth — a single morphological process spanning affection
through contempt — is a strong candidate mechanism for slang-derivation synthesis work later in
the pipeline.

### Colours (pp. 118-119)

Closed set of basic color adjectives; compound colors formed by direct concatenation of two color
names (`blauwgroen`) or by `licht-`/`donker-` prefixation (`lichtgroen`, `donkerblauw`) — both
fully productive processes.

### Expressions of time — periods of the day (pp. 119-120)

Systematic template: `{van/morgen/gisteren/overmorgen/eergisteren}` + `{ochtend/middag/avond}`
compounds, entirely productive/predictable once the template is known, with `vannacht` as the one
genuinely ambiguous exception (past or future reference depending on context) and `gisteravond` as
the fixed idiom for "last night" specifically in the sense of "before I went to bed."
