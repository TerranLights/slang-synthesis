### Idioom — full harvest, Lessons 29–36

- **Les 29** (Scheidbare werkwoorden, printed p. 82): *Dat doet de deur dicht!* — literally "that
  closes the door!", idiomatically "that's the final straw / that settles it" (usually said of a
  last negative event that closes off a possibility or ends someone's patience).
- **Les 30** (Werkwoorden met 'te', printed p. 84): *Zit niet zo te zeuren!* — "Stop whining like
  that!" — a colloquial imperative, notable as an example sentence built from the lesson's own
  *zitten te + infinitief* durative-aspect construction (literally "don't sit whining," i.e. "stop
  going on and on").
- **Les 31** (Volgorde, printed p. 86): *Je moet je plaats kennen!* — "You must know your place!"
- **Les 32** (Voltooide tijd, printed p. 88): *Het ijs is gebroken* — "the ice is broken" (direct
  cognate idiom).
- **Les 33** (De spelling van het voltooid deelwoord (1), printed p. 92): *Snel op je tenen getrapt
  zijn* — literally "to quickly get your toes stepped on," idiomatically "to be thin-skinned/easily
  offended."
- **Les 34** (De spelling van het voltooid deelwoord (2), printed p. 94): *Tot in de puntjes
  verzorgd zijn* — literally "to be taken care of down to the last little dots," idiomatically "to
  be immaculately/meticulously groomed or arranged" (echoes Les 1's *de puntjes op de i zetten*
  idiom — the "dot/detail" image recurs across this book's idiom set).
- **Les 35** (Onregelmatige voltooide deelwoorden, printed p. 96): *De bom is gebarsten* — literally
  "the bomb has burst," idiomatically "the crisis has finally broken out / the situation has come to
  a head."
- **Les 36** (Verleden tijd, printed p. 98): *En zij leefden nog lang en gelukkig...* — "And they
  lived happily ever after..." — the standard Dutch fairy-tale closing formula (direct cognate of
  the English convention), chosen here specifically because it showcases the lesson's own
  weak-verb past-tense plural ending *-den* (*leefden*).
