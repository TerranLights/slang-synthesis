# Dutch — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *Klare taal! Uitgebreide basisgrammatica NT2* (Deel 1, Lessons 37–44)

**Source:** Jenny van der Toorn-Schutte, *Klare taal! Uitgebreide basisgrammatica NT2* (Houten,
2006). Continues `033f` (Lessons 29–36). Covers **Lessons 37–44** (printed pp. 102–118), same
confirmed `PDF page = printed page − 1` offset, theory-page-only vision-reading.

**Coverage note:** Lessons 37–44 cover irregular past-tense vowel-change verbs, `hebben`-vs-`zijn`
auxiliary selection (including verbs that can take either depending on whether the sentence
expresses a goal/direction), *staan/zitten/liggen/lopen* + *te* durative constructions and their
perfect-tense formation (infinitive-cluster "double infinitive" effect — no `ge-` participle used),
the *aan het ___ zijn* / *bezig zijn met ___* progressive-aspect periphrases, the imperative mood
(including the bare-infinitive imperative used on signage), fixed reflexive verbs (*zich
herinneren/haasten/vergissen/schamen*, etc.) and the *zichzelf* vs. *zelf* vs. *elkaar* distinction,
and indefinite pronouns/adverbs (*iemand/niemand/iets/niets*, *men*, *ergens/nergens/ooit/nooit*).
**All of this is standard A2 grammar already covered across established/001–017.** None re-derived
here. As with prior files in this series, **every lesson's Idioom entry is captured in full** — and
this range is unusually rich, surfacing two genuine multi-item idiom clusters (Les 41's disbelief-
interjection set and Les 43's *elkaar*+preposition idiom list) worth flagging distinctly.

## Grammar points

### Idioom — full harvest, Lessons 37–44

- **Les 37** (Onregelmatige verleden tijdsvormen, printed p. 102): *Hij kwam, zag en overwon...* —
  "He came, he saw, and he conquered..." (a direct Dutch rendering of the Latin *veni, vidi, vici*,
  chosen to showcase this lesson's irregular past-tense forms *kwam/zag*). This lesson's own theory
  text is itself a short fable excerpt cited from Svend Otto, *De vos en de ooievaar: 20 fabels van
  Aesopus* (Lemniscaat, Rotterdam, 1985) — noted for provenance but not separately extracted, being
  the lesson's illustrative reading passage rather idiom content.
- **Les 38** (Vervoeging met 'hebben' of met 'zijn', printed p. 104): *Hij is met zijn neus in de
  boter gevallen* — literally "he fell nose-first into the butter," idiomatically "he lucked into
  good fortune / things fell into his lap by sheer luck."
- **Les 39** (Hebben staan wachten, printed p. 106): *Op je stuk blijven staan* — "to stand your
  ground / stick to your position."
- **Les 40** (Aan het ___ zijn; bezig zijn met ___, printed p. 108): *Aan het dwalen zijn* —
  literally "to be in the process of wandering" (built from this lesson's own progressive-aspect
  construction), used both literally (wandering/roaming) and figuratively (rambling, losing one's
  train of thought).
- **Les 41** (Gebiedende wijs, printed p. 112): a cluster of colloquial **disbelief/protest
  interjections** rather than a single fixed idiom — *Ga weg!* ("Get out of here!"/"No way!"), *Is
  dat echt waar?* ("Is that really true?"), *Kom nou!* ("Come on!"), *Ik geloof er niets van!* ("I
  don't believe a word of it!"). Presented together as this lesson's Idioom entry, deliberately
  built from imperative-mood forms (the lesson's own grammar point) used in their pragmatic
  disbelief-marking function rather than literal commands — a genuinely useful register cluster for
  the mechanics-analysis phase.
- **Les 42** (Zich herinneren, printed p. 114): *Haast je langzaam!* — "Make haste slowly" (a direct
  cognate of the Latin *festina lente* / English proverb, built from this lesson's reflexive-verb
  grammar point, *zich haasten*).
- **Les 43** (Zichzelf, zelf, elkaar, printed p. 116): the source itself frames this lesson's Idioom
  entry as a **cluster of *elkaar* + preposition idioms**, not a single expression — six distinct
  items, all built on this lesson's own reciprocal-pronoun grammar point:
  - *de eindjes aan elkaar knopen* — literally "to tie the little ends to each other,"
    idiomatically "to make ends meet" (financially).
  - *achter elkaar lopen* — literally "to walk behind each other," i.e. "to walk in single file/in
    a row" (illustrated with geese).
  - *door elkaar halen* — "to mix up/confuse (two things)."
  - *het is voor elkaar* — "it's all arranged/settled" (everything is in order).
  - *in elkaar zetten* — "to assemble (something)."
  - *uit elkaar houden* — "to tell (two things/people) apart, to distinguish."
- **Les 44** (Onbepaalde woorden, printed p. 118): *Nooit van z'n leven!* — literally "never in his
  life!", idiomatically "no way!/not a chance!" — an emphatic denial/refusal interjection.
