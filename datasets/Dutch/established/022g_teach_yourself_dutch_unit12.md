## Grammar points

### Zou/zouden — four distinct functions

**Zou** (singular) / **zouden** (plural) is grammatically the simple-past (imperfect) of **zullen**,
but functionally covers four distinct uses that must each be learned:
1. **Polite requests**, always combined with **kunnen** or **willen** (never alone): *Zou je dit
   voor me kunnen doen?* ("Could you do this for me?"). Because the construction is inherently
   polite, **alstublieft** is not additionally needed in the same sentence.
2. **Reminding/stating what was planned**: *Je zou nog bellen* ("You were going to phone").
3. **Giving advice**, typically with **moeten**: *Je zou wat vroeger naar bed moeten gaan* ("You
   should go to bed a bit earlier").
4. **The conditional** — expressing what would happen under different conditions, typically paired
   with an **als**-clause: *Als ik de loterij zou winnen, zou ik stoppen met werken* ("Were I to
   win the lottery, I would give up work").

### Prepositions and fixed-preposition verb phrases

Dutch prepositions (op, om, in, door, bij, etc.) mostly mark place/location/direction/means, but
many verbs and expressions take a **fixed preposition** that must simply be memorized alongside the
verb (e.g. *afhangen van* "to depend on", *een afspraakje maken met* "to make a date with", *iets
niet bij de hand hebben* "to not have something to hand").

### Prepositions in relative clauses: wie vs. waar

Unit 10 introduced **die/dat** relative clauses. When the relative clause needs a **preposition**,
neither die nor dat can be used — instead:
- **Referring to a person**: the preposition + **wie** (*de man met wie ik samenwerk*, "the man
  with whom I work"). The preposition precedes wie.
- **Referring to a thing/idea**: **waar** + the preposition, most correctly fused in writing
  (*waarover*, *waaraan*) but commonly split in speech, with the preposition moving to the end of
  the clause, right before the verb (*de zaak waar we aan hebben gewerkt*, "the project we worked
  on"). The preposition follows waar (opposite order from the wie construction).

### Er/daar + preposition (replacing an object pronoun)

Dutch object pronouns (*hem, het, ze*) cannot combine with a preposition when referring to a thing
or idea (not a person) — **er** must be used instead, generally fused with the preposition in
writing (*ervan, eraan, erover, ernaar*) though commonly split in speech with an adverb, **niet**,
or a time word intervening (*Ik werk er hard aan*, "I'm working hard on it"). **Note the irregular
fusion**: **met** becomes **mee** in combination with er (*ermee*, not *ermet*). **Daar** can
substitute for er in this function with added emphasis, and — unlike er — daar can also open the
sentence (*Daar geloof ik in*). Referring to a **person** in the same construction still uses the
ordinary object pronoun, not er/daar.
