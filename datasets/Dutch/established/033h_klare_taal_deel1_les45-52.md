### Idioom — full harvest, Lessons 45–52 (Deel 1 complete)

- **Les 45** (En, maar, want, of, printed p. 122): *Een vos verliest wel zijn haren, maar niet zijn
  streken* — literally "a fox loses its fur, but not its tricks," idiomatically "a leopard can't
  change its spots" (functional cognate).
- **Les 46** (Omdat, als, printed p. 124): *Je moet het ijzer smeden als het heet is* — "strike
  while the iron is hot" (direct cognate idiom).
- **Les 47** (Toen, nadat, voordat, zodat, doordat, printed p. 126): *Gooi geen oude schoenen weg
  voordat je nieuwe hebt* — literally "don't throw away old shoes before you have new ones,"
  idiomatically "don't give up something (a job, a relationship, a plan) until you have a secure
  replacement" (a Dutch-specific formulation of the "don't burn bridges" caution).
- **Les 48** (Toen, als, sinds, terwijl, zodra, printed p. 128): *Als de kat van huis is, dansen de
  muizen* — "when the cat's away, the mice will play" (direct cognate idiom).
- **Les 49** (Opdat, hoewel, ofschoon, mits, tenzij, printed p. 132): *Ja, mits... Nee, tenzij...* —
  not a fixed idiom but a formulaic **conditional-answer frame** ("Yes, provided that.../No,
  unless..."), deliberately built from this lesson's own two conditional conjunctions to model how
  they're used to hedge a yes/no answer in conversation.
- **Les 50** (Vragen of; zeggen dat, printed p. 134): *Men zegt dat...* — "It is said that..." /
  "Rumor has it that..." — a hearsay-marking discourse formula, built from this lesson's own
  *zeggen dat* construction.
- **Les 51** (Verbindende bijwoorden, printed p. 136): *Pas op, anders...!* — "Watch out, or
  else...!" — a warning/threat formula built from this lesson's own consequence-adverb *anders*.
- **Les 52** (Die, dat, wie, wat, waar, printed p. 138): **two** idioms given together as this
  lesson's Idioom entry:
  - *Wie voor een dubbeltje geboren is, wordt nooit een kwartje* — literally "whoever is born a
    [10-cent] dime will never become a [25-cent] quarter," idiomatically "you can't rise above your
    original station/nature" — an old pre-euro-coinage class-fatalism idiom (a *dubbeltje* and
    *kwartje* were historical Dutch guilder-era coin denominations), worth flagging as a concrete
    example of currency-based idiom formation for any in-universe economic/class-register slang
    work.
  - *Wat de boer niet kent dat eet hij niet* — literally "what the farmer doesn't know, he doesn't
    eat," idiomatically describes reflexive wariness/rejection of anything unfamiliar (roughly the
    inverse framing of English "don't knock it till you try it" — here framed as a critical
    observation of closed-mindedness rather than folk wisdom).
