# Dutch — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *Klare taal!* — De meestgebruikte het-woorden

**Source:** Jenny van der Toorn-Schutte, *Klare taal! Uitgebreide basisgrammatica NT2* (Houten,
2006), Appendix "De meestgebruikte het-woorden," printed pp. 224–226 (PDF pp. 222–224). DJVU scan,
vision-read. See `033_klare_taal_nt2_basisgrammatica.md` for full source/page-offset notes and
this book's overall non-redundant-supplement scoping rationale.

**Coverage note:** this is a flat alphabetical list of the *het*-woorden (neuter-gender nouns,
i.e. nouns that take the definite article *het* rather than *de*) the source judges most useful
for a learner to memorize — the back-cover blurb for this edition specifically markets the
*het-woordenlijst* as new to this (revised) edition and a headline feature. Dutch grammatical
gender for nouns is largely unpredictable from form and must be memorized per word (the general
default article is *de*; a substantial minority of nouns are *het*-words, and this appendix is
exactly that minority list). No prior established/ file for this language tabulates *het*-gender
nouns at this scale, so the full list is captured here — this is genuinely useful reference data
for any later derivation work involving Dutch-style noun-phrase agreement (diminutives, which are
always *het*-words regardless of their base noun's own gender, being the clearest existing
connection — see established/003). The source gives **no glosses or example sentences** for this
appendix, just the bare noun list with its article; the Gloss column below is supplied by this
extraction (a concise English gloss for each headword, based on standard Dutch vocabulary, not
copied from the source). Two nouns are flagged by the source itself as needing a parenthetical
sense-disambiguation (*het blik (verpakking)* "tin/can," as opposed to the metal *blik*; *het
portier (van een auto)* "car door," as opposed to *portier* "doorman/porter" as a person) — these
are preserved below.

**Vision-reading guard:** clean, well-lit, crisply typeset four-column scan throughout this
appendix (dense small print in four columns per page); no handwritten marginalia encountered.
Confidence is marked `plausible_unverified` throughout rather than `verified`, since individual
words were not independently cross-checked against a second occurrence elsewhere in the source —
the print itself was uniformly legible with no blur, faded ink, or gutter-shadow obstruction on any
of the three pages read.
