# Dutch — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Kosmos Mini-Woordenboek (Dutch–English section)

**Source:** *Engels-Nederlands, Nederlands-Engels* (Wolters' Mini-woordenboek), Wolters-Noordhoff,
Groningen, 1987 (this PDF: Acrobat-distilled 2003 printing). Extracted from the **Dutch→English**
half of the mini-dictionary, pp. 195–352 (printed page numbers; the book's English→Dutch half runs
pp. 1–188 and was not sampled here — this file's Term/Gloss direction is Dutch headword → English
gloss, matching the project's usual established-vocabulary convention). 366 pages total; clean text
layer, extracted with `pdftotext -layout` (two-column dictionary layout reconstructed correctly by
`-layout` on spot-checks — no column-interleaving corruption observed, unlike some other sources
flagged in `00_Reference_Extraction_Spec.md`).

**Coverage note — representative sampling, not exhaustive.** Per dispatch instructions, this is a
366-page specialist pocket dictionary (the Dutch→English half alone runs ~158 pages, an estimated
6,000+ headwords) — the same "representative sampling across the alphabet" discipline used on this
project's other oversized dictionaries applies here rather than full transcription. Sampled 22 pages
spread across every letter of the alphabet (one page cluster per letter for A–Z, checking neighboring
pages where a letter's own page was thin), pulling a broadly representative cross-section of parts of
speech (noun/verb/adjective/adverb/numeral) and semantic domains (body, household, travel, law,
nature, abstract/emotion vocabulary) rather than every single entry on each sampled page. **No
register/slang annotation exists anywhere in this source to flag**: the book's own abbreviations key
(p. 191) lists only grammatical tags (`adj`, `adv`, `Am`, `art`, `c`, `conj`, `n`, `nAm`, `nt`, `num`,
`p`, `pl`, `plAm`, `pp`, `pr`, `pref`, `prep`, `pron`, `v`, `vAm`) — no `fig.`, `coll.`, `sl.`, `vulg.`,
or dialect-region tags of any kind. A few headwords translate *as* "vulgar"/"familiar"/"informal" (Dutch
`vulgair`, `familiaar`, `informeel`) but these are ordinary lexical glosses, not register markers on
*other* entries — confirmed by scanning the full extracted 195–352 page range for any register-tag
abbreviation pattern. This is worth recording precisely because it contrasts with `002_chapter16_
colloquial_speech.md` and `003_chapter22_diminutives.md`, which found genuine source-marked register
diversity in Dutch grammar-reference material — a plain bilingual pocket dictionary, by contrast, does
not annotate register at all. Nearly every sampled entry is accordingly tagged `core` below; see the
few exceptions noted in the table.

**Letters with little or no native headword content:** `Q` (only two entries in the whole
dictionary — `quarantaine`, `quota`, both loanwords, embedded at the very end of the P/start of R
page span rather than getting their own letter-section header); `X` (no entries found at all — Dutch
orthography does not use word-initial x); `Y` (no entries found — Dutch merged historical y into
`ij`, so native y-initial headwords don't exist in this dictionary; the only stand-alone `y`-adjacent
content found was the pronoun forms `je`/`jou` etc. under `J`). This gap is a genuine property of
Dutch orthography, not a sampling miss — confirmed by scanning the full 195–352 range for any
`q`/`x`/`y`-initial headword before concluding sparsity.

**Morpheme breakdown examples (Dutch productive compounding — illustrative, not exhaustive).**
Dutch nouns compound freely and transparently; per `00_Word_Concept_and_Morphological_Typology_Guide.md`,
an atomic gloss can hide this structure for entries where the mechanics-analysis phase will likely
want it later. A representative handful from the sample above:

> **aardbeving** = aarde ("earth") + beving ("shaking/trembling") — "earthquake," fully transparent
> N+N compound.

> **flessenhals** = fles-en ("bottle," linking -en-) + hals ("neck") — "bottleneck," concrete-noun
> compound with a common Dutch linking-morpheme pattern (-en- between the two nouns).

> **schijnheilig** = schijn ("appearance/semblance") + heilig ("holy") — "hypocritical," an
> adjective compound where the literal sense ("holy-seeming") diverges only mildly from the
> figurative gloss.

> **pandjesbaas** = pandje (diminutive of pand, "pawned item") + baas ("boss") — "pawnbroker," shows
> the diminutive suffix `-je` (see `003_chapter22_diminutives.md`) embedded inside a further
> compound, not standing alone.

> **vanzelfsprekend** = van ("of") + zelf ("self") + sprekend (present participle of spreken, "to
> speak") — "self-evident," a three-morpheme compound built from a preposition + reflexive pronoun +
> verb participle, illustrating that Dutch compounding isn't limited to noun+noun.

## Grammar points

This is a pure headword-list dictionary with no grammar exposition of its own; the only
"grammar points" it supplies are the conventions stated in its own front-matter (pp. 190–193),
paraphrased here rather than covered by other extraction files:

### Plural-notation convention

The dictionary abbreviates repeated plural material with a tilde (`~`) standing in for the full
singular headword (e.g. `beker (pl ~s)` → plural `bekers`), and for longer words marks only the
changed portion with a hyphen prefix (e.g. `leslokaal (pl -kalen)` → plural `leslokalen`). This is a
lexicographic shorthand, not a grammatical rule of Dutch itself, but worth recording since it
explains the `(pl ...)` notation preserved verbatim in the Term column above.

### Reflexive-verb alphabetization

Reflexive verbs are alphabetized under their main verb, not under `zich`, e.g. *zich afvragen*
("to wonder") is listed under `a`. Several entries above (`zich *afvragen`, `zich indenken`, `zich
uitkleden`, `zich *scheren`) follow this convention.

### Irregular-verb asterisk marking

An asterisk (`*`) prefixed to a verb entry indicates an irregular verb, cross-referenced to a
separate irregular-verb table at the back of the book (pp. 353–356, strong/ablaut verbs — infinitive,
past tense, past participle, English gloss; not reproduced here as vocabulary rows since it's a
grammar-paradigm table, not a set of new lexical items, and overlaps with irregular-verb material
likely already covered by `25 Dutch An Essential Grammar`'s own chapters — flagged here for a future
non-redundant-supplement check against that source's irregular-verb chapter rather than duplicated).

### American-English variant tagging

The source explicitly tags American-English divergent forms with `Am`/`nAm`/`vAm`/`plAm` (e.g.
*claxonneren* "hoot; toot `vAm`, honk `vAm`"; *flat* n "flat; apartment `nAm`"). This is the one place
the dictionary marks any kind of variety distinction at all — but it marks *English*-side regional
variation (British vs. American English glosses), never Dutch-side dialectal/regional variation. No
Belgian-Dutch vs. Netherlandic-Dutch distinction is marked anywhere in the sampled pages, despite the
book's own back-matter abbreviation list (p. 357) covering both Belgian (`Bfr.`, `BRT`, `VAB`, `VTB`)
and Netherlandic (`NS`, `NBT`, `KNAC`) institutional abbreviations — those are administrative/currency
abbreviations, not headword-level dialect tags.
