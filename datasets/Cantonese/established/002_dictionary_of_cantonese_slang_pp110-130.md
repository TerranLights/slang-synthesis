# Cantonese — Established Vocabulary: *A Dictionary of Cantonese Slang* (Hutton & Bolton), PDF pp. 110–130

**Source:** Christopher Hutton & Kingsley Bolton, *A Dictionary of Cantonese Slang: The Language of
Hong Kong Movies, Street Gangs and City Life* (Hurst & Co., 2005). This dictionary explicitly
targets social workers, police officers, film censors, and academics/learners, and covers
obscene/vulgar slang, triad argot, drug jargon, and street/youth slang drawn from Hong Kong films,
newspapers, and novels.

**Page range:** PDF pages 110–130, corresponding to printed book pages 92–112 — the tail of the
"D" headword section (dīn… through dyun gú, the end of "D") plus the complete short "E" section
(ē… through ēn jī) and the opening third of the "F" section (fāa… through faht). Printed book page
106 (first page of "F") render showed no page-number stamp but sits contiguously between verified
pages 105 and 107.

**Format/reading method:** This PDF has no text layer (image-only scan, 509 pages total). All 21
pages in this range were rendered at 150 DPI and read via vision — no OCR or transcript was used.
Every page was checked for handwritten marginalia before extraction; none was found on any page in
this range (all content below is from clean printed text only). A few pages show faint bleed-through
of the opposite side's printed text (visible as ghosted mirror-text in the lower portion of some
scans); that bleed-through was never used as source content, only the sharp foreground print.

**Coverage approach:** This is a representative sample, not an exhaustive transcription. The
dictionary's entries are densely organized under headword clusters (a bold-face headword followed
by many compound/phrasal sub-entries). Given the density, this extraction prioritizes breadth of
distinct headwords and sub-entries across the full page range, selecting diverse examples from
each cluster (ordinary colloquialisms, taboo/obscene terms, triad argot, drug jargon, gambling
jargon, and acronym-based slang) rather than reproducing every sub-entry verbatim. The content
explicitly includes the dictionary's obscene/taboo material faithfully, per project instructions —
nothing here is sanitized.

**Abbreviation note:** Dictionary citation codes in Notes (e.g. "snch", "gwdl", "ad", "ty", "hsw",
"OS") are the source dictionary's own citation abbreviations for newspapers, films, novels, or "OS"
= oral source; they are preserved only where quoted directly from the page, not expanded (the
dictionary's own abbreviations key was not in this page range).
