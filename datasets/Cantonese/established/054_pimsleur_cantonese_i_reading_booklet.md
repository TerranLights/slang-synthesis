**Source:** `/media/kuroskalacs/Home-Theater/w-lang/Cantonese/00.Cantonese I Reading Booklet.pdf`
(Recorded Books/Pimsleur Language Programs, *Cantonese* — Supplemental Reading Booklet accompanying
the Pimsleur "Cantonese I" audio course). 38-page PDF, rendered at 150dpi and read via vision; a
second copy of the same booklet exists as a `.doc` container (`00.Cantonese I Booklet.doc`) and was

**Triage correction.** The project's triage flagged this PDF as "mostly blank/image, effectively
zero real text." That flag was produced by `pdftotext`-style extraction, which correctly found no
text layer — but the PDF is in fact a clean, fully legible scanned-image booklet of genuine running
prose, not a blank or cover-only document. Every one of the 38 rendered pages was reviewed via
vision. Content breakdown: p.1 is the front cover (title/compass photo); p.2 is the table of
contents; pp.3-37 are substantive prose essays on Cantonese language and culture, organized under
12 named sections (listed below); p.38 is the Pimsleur back-cover language-course list (not
Cantonese content, not extracted as vocabulary). Several interior pages show faint bleed-through
ghosting from the facing page's text (an artifact of scanning a bound booklet, not handwriting);
no handwritten marginalia of any kind was found anywhere in the booklet, and none is reflected in
the table below. The triage's "mostly blank" read is corrected here: this is a content-rich,
text-dense booklet, not a low-content item — low priority (Tier 5) proved to be about source
*type* (a short course supplement), not about actual content density.

**What the booklet actually contains.** Twelve sections of cultural/linguistic commentary aimed at
Pimsleur course users: *The Cantonese Language* (Yue-family background, Guangzhou/Hong Kong dialect
comparison, the course's explicit choice of the Hong Kong dialect), *Tonality* (the six-tone system
with minimal-pair examples), *Traditional Language Beliefs* (lucky/unlucky numbers via homophony),
*About Drinking*, *Chinese Names*, *Dim Sum and Tea Houses*, *Cantonese and English* (English
loanwords in Hong Kong Cantonese), *The Concept of Min* ("face"), *Compliments*, *Rice and Meals*,
*The Chinese Zodiac*, *Hospitality* (dinner-table etiquette and toasts), *Chinese Festivals* (New
Year, Ching Ming, Dün Ng/Dragon Boat, Mid-Autumn), *The Chinese Calendar* (months, weekdays), and
*Written Chinese*. Content is almost entirely cultural-linguistic commentary and everyday/core
vocabulary rather than slang proper — the booklet is explicitly a course companion for beginners,
not a slang source. It is nonetheless useful to the project as a vetted source of core vocabulary,
idioms built on number/food/face homophony, politeness formulas, and English-loanword data for
Hong Kong Cantonese specifically.

**Geographic Scope convention for this entry.** The booklet states explicitly (p.5): "It is the
Hong Kong dialect which is taught and practiced in this course." Following the same convention used
for other single-scope sources in this dataset, `Geographic Scope` is filled "Hong Kong" by default
across the table, except for the two entries that explicitly describe the contrasting Guangzhou
pronunciation/name, which are marked "Guangzhou" instead. `Attested Era` and `Attested Region` are
left "—" except where an individual entry states a specific date/place (e.g., the 1997 Hong Kong
handover, Kowloon). Several zodiac/calendar/festival terms are pan-Chinese cultural content carried
in Cantonese pronunciation rather than Hong-Kong-specific content as such; they are still marked
"Hong Kong" per the uniform convention above, since that is the booklet's stated scope, not because
the customs themselves are HK-exclusive.

**A note on homophony/polysemy in the table.** Cantonese's small syllable inventory means several
entries below share an identical (untoned) romanization across the booklet's different sections but
denote unrelated words — e.g. *geo* = "nine" / "rescue" / Year-of-the-Dog, and *séi* = "four" /
"death," *xü* = "loss" (Chinese Festivals) / Year-of-the-Rat (Zodiac). Each sense is listed as its
own row with a disambiguating note; the booklet's own romanization does not mark tones consistently
with diacritics, so some of these pairs are genuinely homophonous in the booklet's transcription
even where the spoken tones differ (the booklet explains several such pairs directly, e.g. séi "four"
vs. séi "death").
