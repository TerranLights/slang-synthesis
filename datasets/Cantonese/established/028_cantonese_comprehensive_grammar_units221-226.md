## Overview — this range is entirely back matter

This range contains no running prose/grammar exposition. It consists of two sections:

**1. References (pp. 418-421, units 221-222).** A standard alphabetized academic bibliography (Au 1983 through Zhan 1993) of scholarly sources cited throughout the grammar — PhD dissertations, journal articles (*Journal of Chinese Linguistics*, *Linguistics*, etc.), and books on Cantonese/Chinese linguistics, sociolinguistics, and general linguistic theory. This is pure citation-list material: author names, years, titles, publishers/journals. It contains no Cantonese vocabulary or example forms of its own (aside from bibliographic titles that are themselves *about* Cantonese, e.g. Kwan's dictionaries, Huang's *Cantonese Dictionary*) and is not extractable as vocabulary content. No table rows are drawn from this section.

**2. Index (pp. 422-429, units 223-226, the book's final page).** An alphabetical subject/word index covering both (a) English grammatical terminology used in the book (e.g. "topic", "passives", "reduplication") and (b) Cantonese words and particles that have their own entries because they are discussed as specific grammatical items, each typically glossed inline with a short quoted English translation and a cross-reference to the relevant chapter section(s) (e.g. *bōng* 'help' 3.1.3, 8.3). The index's own prefatory note (p. 422) explains its scope: grammatical terms, English grammatical words, and Cantonese words with grammatical function or notable syntactic properties; idioms are not listed individually (they appear under their component grammatical words); alphabetization of Cantonese forms ignores the *h* that marks low tones.

This index is a genuine (if incidental) glossary: it surfaces roughly 180 distinct Cantonese words/particles/phrases with authoritative inline English glosses, spanning pronouns, demonstratives, numerals, classifiers, aspect/sentence particles, coverbs/prepositions, conjunctions, modal verbs, directional verbs, and common adverbs — i.e., the book's own core-grammar lexicon rather than slang. These are tabulated below. Pure English-only index entries (e.g. "topic 4.2", "word order 4.1") and Cantonese entries that are indexed without an explicit inline gloss (e.g. bare particle references like "lā particle 18.2.1" or "jyuh continuous aspect marker 11.1") are not tabulated as vocabulary rows, since inventing a gloss for them would not be a faithful extraction — the discussion of what those particles actually mean lives in the body chapters already covered by earlier extraction waves (units 171-220 territory), not in this index itself.
