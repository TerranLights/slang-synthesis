# Lonely Planet Cantonese Phrasebook (Kam Y Lau, 3rd ed., 1999) — pp. 1-40

**Source**: `08.Lonely Planet Cantonese Phrasebook.pdf` (image-only scan, 160 pages total). This
extraction covers pages 1-40 of the PDF render (front matter through the start of the "Getting
Around" chapter, roughly book pages 1-77 given front-matter/cover pages). Vision-read via
150dpi PNG renders; no text layer in source.

## Coverage of this range

Pages 1-40 of the PDF span: front/back cover, publication info, author bio and acknowledgements,
table of contents, the Introduction, the full Pronunciation chapter (tones, consonants, vowels,
comparison tables across six romanization systems), the full Grammar chapter (sentence structure,
articles, nouns, adjectives, pronouns, verbs, tense, commands, negatives, yes/no and question
types, possession, modals, classifiers), and the first half of the "Meeting People" chapter
(greetings, goodbyes, body language, forms of address, first encounters, nationalities, age,
zodiac, occupations, studying, feelings, cultural differences, language difficulties, family,
useful phrases) through the opening page of "Getting Around" (ticket buying, customs, air travel,
bus/tram/trolley bus, train, taxi, car, bicycle, boat — all through page ~77).

**Slang/colloquialism section check (explicit open item from triage):** This range does **not**
contain a single dedicated "Slang" or "Colloquialisms" chapter/section comparable to the Japanese
Rough Guide precedent. However, the book does something structurally similar in spirit: it
scatters short highlighted callout boxes (titled "DID YOU KNOW...", "BOSS", "SIDE-KICK",
"SUPERIOR", "HUMILITY") through the Meeting People chapter that specifically flag colloquial,
slangy, or informal terms outside the main phrase tables, and the main dictionary-style word lists
tag several entries "(col)" for colloquial variants (e.g. husband/wife/younger-sibling terms).
The Introduction (p.9) also states outright: "Many of the words used are slang and cannot be
written. However the language that is written is written in the same script as Mandarin" —
confirming the author's awareness that spoken Cantonese slang exceeds what a phrasebook can
capture in characters. So: partial positive — colloquial material is present and flagged, but as
scattered sidebars/tags rather than one consolidated slang section. It is possible a dedicated
slang/colloquialism section exists later in the book (outside this page range); that is for the
subagent(s) covering later pages to confirm.
