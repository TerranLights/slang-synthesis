## Coverage summary

This range opens mid-book and runs to the very back cover, covering (in printed-page order):
Film/TV/Media vocabulary tail-end (238) → Pilgrimage & Religion (239-242) → Times, Dates &
Festivals, including Telling the Time, Days of the Week, Months, Dates, Traditional Festivals,
and Chinese Dynasties (243-254) → Numbers & Amounts, including cardinal/ordinal numbers,
measure words, fractions/percentages, and currency reading conventions (255-262) → Emergencies,
including Medical Attention, Dealing with the Police, and the "Police May Say" box (263-268) →
the full **English-Cantonese dictionary** (269-290) and reverse **Cantonese-English dictionary**
(291-305) → **Index** (307-311) → **Cantonese Finder** (Chinese-language table of contents,
313-315) → back-cover Lonely Planet publisher boilerplate and a traffic/public-signs chart
(316/inside back cover).

This is the book's back matter: roughly three-quarters of the range (pp. 269-305, i.e. PDF pages
137-156) is the bidirectional dictionary, which is explicitly non-extractable as running prose —
it is already word-list format, and its content is captured selectively below only where an entry
carries a register marker or cultural/etymological note worth preserving.

**Did we find a dedicated slang/colloquialism section? No.** The Index (pp. 310-311, checked in
full, A-Z) and the Chinese-language Cantonese Finder (pp. 314-315) contain no "Slang,"
"Colloquialisms," or equivalent heading. The dictionary does, however, mark a small number of
entries `(col)` for colloquial register, and one "Did You Know" sidebar (p. 268) explicitly
surfaces a genuine piece of Hong Kong slang (a joke term for a parking ticket). Those are the
real finds in this range and are captured in the table below; everything else in the 40-page
