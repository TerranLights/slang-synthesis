## Section coverage

This range runs from the tail end of the **Food** chapter (Banquets, Self-Catering: measurements,
market vocabulary, meat, poultry, seafood, vegetables, fruit & nuts, dairy, spices & condiments,
other foods, cooking methods) through **Beverages**, a cutlery/restaurant "Some Useful Words"
block, then the entire **In The Country** chapter (weather, seasons, hiking, camping, geographical
terms, the stars, the environment, fauna — mammals/birds/aquatic/insects & reptiles, flora —
trees/flowers, waterside/aquatic sports, sports & games), the **Health** chapter in full (hospital/
clinic phrases, ailments, doctor dialogue, women's health, allergies, alternative/Chinese medicine,
the chemist, parts of the body, dentist, optometrist, general health phrases and Traditional
Chinese Health Practices), and the opening of **Specific Needs** (disabled travellers, **gay
travellers**, travelling with a family, education, mid-term accommodation, on business, on tour,
and film/TV/media).

**Yes — this range contains a dedicated slang/colloquialism callout.** The "GAY TRAVELLERS"
subsection (book pp.222–225) is the notable find, directly comparable to the Japanese Rough Guide
precedent. The book's own prose explicitly flags register: *"In Hong Kong, some gay people call
each other `tung jī`, meaning the person with same hobbies or interests; and the lesbians `nũi
tung jī`, 'female comrade'. The words `kāi dāi`, denote a homosexual partner or 'gay boy'."* The
facing vocabulary list then marks `gèi`/`gèi ló` explicitly with the abbreviation **"(col)"** —
the book's own colloquial-register flag — set against the neutral/clinical `tung sīng lüen`
("homosexual") elsewhere in the same list. This is genuine in-group self-reference vocabulary
(euphemistic borrowing from the political sense of "comrade," a parallel pattern to Mandarin
tóngzhì usage) rather than ordinary dictionary vocabulary, and is flagged as such below with
`Usage Tier = slang`. No other dedicated slang/colloquialism section appears in this page range;
the remaining ~750 terms are standard phrasebook glossary vocabulary (food, nature, sports, body
parts, medical terms, business/travel administrivia) at `core`/`technical`/`formal` register.

omitted from the table below in favor of the underlying vocabulary items (per established
