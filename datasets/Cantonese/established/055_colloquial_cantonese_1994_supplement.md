# Colloquial Cantonese (1994 edition, Routledge) — Non-Redundant Supplement to the 2010 Extraction

**Source:** Keith S. T. Tong and Gregory James. *Colloquial Cantonese: The Complete Course for Beginners*, 1st edition (Routledge, 1994). 130-page image-only PDF, rendered via `pdftoppm -png -r 150` from `/media/kuroskalacs/Home-Theater/w-lang/Cantonese/02.Colloquial Cantonese 1994.pdf`. Romanization: Yale system (identical to the 2010 edition). This file is explicitly a **supplement check**, not a re-extraction: the 2010 2nd edition of this same course (by Bourgerie, Tong, and James) is already fully extracted at `030a_colloquial_cantonese_2010_part1.md`, `030b_..._part2.md`, and `030c_..._part3.md`. The project's triage catalog flagged this 1994 edition as "almost certainly sharing most content" with the 2010 edition, so the task here was to confirm or refute that and extract only genuinely new material.

## Methodology

Each PDF page in this file is a **two-page spread scan** (1219×984 pt landscape pages), not a single book page. Page-number arithmetic confirmed the mapping: PDF page *N* (for *N* ≥ 5) renders book pages (2*N*−8) and (2*N*−7) — e.g., PDF page 25 renders book pages 42–43, PDF page 100 renders book pages 192–193. This was verified directly against the printed page numbers visible in the scans.

Before touching the 1994 PDF, `030a`/`030b`/`030c` were read in full to establish what the 2010 extraction already covers: all 15 units' dialogues, vocabulary, grammar notes, and cultural points, plus the back-matter structure (translations, exercise key, glossaries, further reading, VisiPitch appendix, structural index).

Against that baseline, a representative, book-spanning sample of the 1994 edition was rendered at 150dpi and read with vision:
- **Front matter** (PDF pp. 1–6 → book pp. title/Contents/Acknowledgements/Introduction pp. 1–5): series list, title page, full table of contents, acknowledgements, "Cantonese: language or dialect?", "Cantonese grammar", "Cantonese vocabulary", "The sound system of Cantonese."
- **Unit 3–4 span** (PDF pp. 25–30 → book pp. 42–53): end of Unit 3 (Sihou/Interests), start of Unit 4 (Sìhgaan/Telling the time).
- **Unit 8 span** (PDF pp. 60–64 → book pp. 112–121): sīnsāang polysemy note, possessive néihdeih, the particle lā, exercises.
- **Unit 14–15 span** (PDF pp. 100–104 → book pp. 192–201): the taam/-gwo experiential-aspect material, telephone-etiquette dialogues and idioms.
- **Back matter** (PDF pp. 125–130 → book pp. 242–253ish): Cantonese–English/English–Cantonese glossary tail, Further reading, VisiPitch Appendix, Index of grammatical structures.

No handwritten marginalia were found anywhere in the sampled pages; all sampled scans are clean printed text.

## Conclusion: this edition is essentially the same course as 2010, not a meaningfully divergent one

Across every sampled region of the book, the 1994 text is **word-for-word or near-word-for-word identical** to what the 2010 extraction already documents. This is not merely "similar content" — in several places the English prose explanation and example set are reproduced verbatim between editions:

- The Introduction's morpheme-breakdown examples (**sáubīu** "wristwatch," **syutgwaih** "refrigerator," **bōlēi** "glass," **pùihwùih** "to linger," the **mihn/hàahng/sihk/wah** archaic-in-Mandarin set, the **jyūgūlīk/nèihlùhng/wàihtāmihng/wāisihgéi** loanword set) appear in the 1994 edition in the same order, with the same wording, as already captured in `030a`.
- Unit 3's **jengyi/héifen** colloquial-vs-formal register note, the days-of-the-week list, and the frequency-expression grammar (**géinoih...yāt chi**, **fùhng**) match `030a` exactly, down to the example sentences.
- Unit 4's **dímjūng/gwāt/jih** vs. written **sìh/haak** doublet system matches `030a`.
- Unit 8's **sīnsāang** triple-polysemy explanation (Mr./teacher/husband, disambiguated by the classifier **go**) matches `030b` almost verbatim, including the **taai-táai** "wife" gloss.
- Unit 14's **taam** ("to visit," human objects only, with the explicit *taam Méihgwok is ungrammatical* note) and the **-gwo** experiential-aspect material match `030c`.
- Unit 15's telephone-greeting conventions (**wái**), the "sorry, not in" idioms, and **baai-baai** vs. **joi gin** area match `030c`.
- The back matter has the identical structure and even the identical bibliography entries (Matthews and Yip's *Cantonese: A Comprehensive Grammar*, the VisiPitch six-tone appendix, the page-reference-only structural index) that `030c` already describes as not worth re-tabulating.

**The one genuine structural difference found:** the unit *order* for the first two units is swapped between editions. In the 1994 edition, Unit 1 is "Máaih-yéh / Shopping" and Unit 2 is "Gāaujai / Meeting people" (confirmed directly from the 1994 table of contents, which lists "1 Máaih-yéh ... Shopping ... 13" before "2 Gāaujai ... Meeting people ... 25"). In the 2010 edition, per `030a`, Unit 1 is "Gaaujai (Meeting people)" and Unit 2 is "Máaih-yéh (Shopping)" — the editors reversed the teaching order when revising for 2010, presumably judging that greetings/introductions make a more natural opening lesson than market shopping. Units 3 through 15 keep identical titles and identical page-ordering logic in both editions. This is a pedagogical-sequencing change, not new vocabulary or new content — the shopping and greeting material itself is already captured in `030a` regardless of which unit number it sits under.

A secondary, non-lexical observation: the 1994 "Further reading" lists Matthews and Yip's *Cantonese: A Comprehensive Grammar* as "(forthcoming)," while the 2010 edition (per `030c`) cites it as an already-published, cross-referenced companion volume — simply reflecting that the reference grammar was published in the 16 years between editions. Not slang-relevant.

No dated/archaic vocabulary that the 2010 edition visibly replaced was found in the sampled pages, and no example dialogues, exercises, or cultural-point content diverged in substance from what `030a`/`030b`/`030c` already document. Given the strength and consistency of this match across six different units (3, 4, 8, 9, 14, 15) plus front and back matter, the conclusion is confident: **this 1994 first edition is the direct ancestor text of the already-extracted 2010 second edition, and contributes essentially no new slang-synthesis-relevant material.** The table below is accordingly very short — it contains only two vocabulary items that were visible in the sampled pages but not captured as explicit rows in `030a`/`030b`/`030c` (both ordinary core vocabulary with transparent morpheme breakdowns, not slang, and not edition-specific/dated).

## Overall assessment for this source

This 1994 first edition should be treated as confirmed-redundant with the already-extracted 2010 second edition for slang-synthesis purposes. The only structurally notable finding is the Unit 1/Unit 2 teaching-order swap (shopping-first in 1994 vs. greetings-first in 2010), which is a curriculum-design change rather than a content difference. No further extraction waves are warranted for this source.
