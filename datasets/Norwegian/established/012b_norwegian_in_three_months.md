## Grammar points

### Chapter 5 — Past tense of weak and strong verbs, Adverbs, Word order, Ordinal numbers (pp.72-86)
Weak-verb past tense sub-rules (new systematic framing not spelled out together in 001/002): most weak
verbs add **-et** or **-te** to the stem (**kast+et=kastet**, **bruk+te=brukte**); verbs with stem
**-ll/-mm/-nn** drop the last letter before the ending (**fylle→fylte**); some verbs take **-de(-et
traditional)** or **-dde**, with the **-et/traditional** forms explicitly flagged as "still used by many
Norwegians" alongside the newer **-de/-dde** forms (**bygge→bygde/bygget**, **bo→bodde**, **ha→hadde**);
**dø→dødde** (single **d**); a few weak verbs change their stem vowel despite weak conjugation
(**selge→solgte**, **spørre→spurte**, **gjøre→gjorde**, **bringe→brakte**) — a genuine classification
point (weak conjugation + irregular stem) not distinguished this way in 001's flat verb table. Simple
past covers English continuous past too (**Han spurte** = "He asked" or "He was asking"); no "did"-
equivalent in past-tense questions/negatives, extending sec.9's present-tense rule. Strong verbs add
**no** ending in the past and usually change their stem vowel, but a sub-class **keeps** its stem vowel
unchanged (**bli→ble**, **være→var**, **komme→kom**, **sove→sov**, **løpe→løp**, **gråte→gråt**) — a
useful classification not drawn out in 001's flat table.

**Adverbs (sec.23):** regular derivation = common-gender adjective + **-t** (**pen→pent**); adjectives in
**-ig** or **-t**-after-consonant take no extra **-t** (**plutselig→suddenly**); a **-vis** suffix group
(**vanlig→vanligvis**). **Word order:** adverbs never come between subject and verb (**Han leser ALLTID
avisen**, not *Han alltid leser*). The **V2 (verb-second) rule** is stated as a general principle: an
adverb/adverbial expression opening a sentence triggers subject-verb inversion, not just questions
(**Noen ganger går jeg på kino**; **Til slutt gikk hun hjem**; the existential **I gaten var det mange
mennesker** "In the street were there many people"). This explicit general V2 statement is a flagship
grammar point not drawn out this way in 001-003.

**Ordinal numbers (sec.25):** irregular **første/annen(andre)/tredje/fjerde/femte/sjette/ellevte/tolvte**;
regular **-ende/-nde/-de** suffixation above that; the "new official method" (tens-before-ones, see Ch.3
grammar note on the 1951 decree) applies to ordinals too (**tjueførste**=21st vs. traditional
**enogtyvende**). Ordinals are used in fractions as in English (**en tredjedel**=a third) but **modern
schoolbooks use cardinal-based fraction forms instead** (**en tredel**) — a genuine usage-register split
not in 001/002. Fractional expressions: **en kvart kilo**, **en halv kilo**, **tre kvart kilo**, **en og
en halv uke**. Idioms: **for det første/annet/tredje** (firstly/secondly/thirdly); **hvert fjerde år**
(every fourth year = every four years).

### Chapter 6 — Past participle, Perfect tense, Modal auxiliaries, Comparison of adverbs, Seasons/dates (pp.87-102)
Past participle endings (new systematic classification): **-et(-a colloq.)**, **-t**, **-tt**,
**-d(-et traditional)**, **-dd** — mirrors the past-tense suffix system from Ch.5. Full verb-by-verb
weak/strong paradigm tables are skipped as redundant with 001's appendix. **Modal auxiliaries** (sec.29):
past tense of a modal **equals its infinitive form** (**jeg burde, jeg kunne** — not a separate past
stem), and the perfect tense is regular (**jeg har burdet, jeg har kunnet**) — a rule not spelled out in
001. When the main verb expresses **motion**, it can be dropped after a modal: **Jeg må på kontoret**
(lit. "I must [go] to the office"); **Hvor skal du?** (Where are you going [to]?); **Barna ville på kino**
(The children wanted [to go] to the pictures) — genuinely new ellipsis rule. **å vite** ("to know") is
irregular and modal-like: **jeg visste** (knew), **jeg har visst** (have known).

**Comparison of adverbs (sec.30)** — a distinct paradigm from adjective comparison (sec.18), fuller than
001/002: regular **-(e)re/-(e)st** (**sjelden-sjeldnere-sjeldnest**, **fort-fortere-fortest**); irregular
(**lite-mindre-minst**, **mye(meget)-mer-mest**, **vondt-verre-verst**, **godt-bedre-best**,
**langt-lengre-lengst**); multi-syllable adverbs use **mer/mest** periphrastically (**klosset-mer
klosset-mest klosset**).

**More about adverbs (sec.31):** the **inn/inne, ut/ute, opp/oppe, ned/nede, bort/borte, hjem/hjemme**
motion-vs-location pairs duplicate 001's near-identical list almost exactly — skipped. New: a **four-way**
system distinguishing **her** (here, at) / **der** (there, at) / **hit** ((to) here) / **dit** ((to)
there), with **her/der** also used idiomatically to stand in for English "this/that" before a following
prepositional phrase: **her i byen** ("in THIS town"), **der i landet** ("in THAT country"), **her i
familien**, **der i huset**.

**Seasons, months and dates (sec.32-33):** base vocabulary overlaps 001's ch.25 list, but this source adds
a **full public-holidays list** not in 001 (see Vocabulary table) and the date-writing convention — day
figure + period + month name (**1. juni**, **7. juli**, **17. mai**), with ordinals used in spoken dates
(**Det er den første juni** = "It's the first of June").

### Chapter 7 — Pluperfect, Verbs with være, Prepositions, "Det er", Indefinite pronouns, More numbers, Weather (pp.103-114)
Pluperfect (sec.34, **ha**-past + past participle) and verbs conjugated with **være** instead of **ha**
for motion/change verbs (sec.35, matching `002`'s own "Verbs conjugated with være" section title) are
treated as redundant with `002`'s existing tense coverage — not re-extracted here. **Prepositions**
(sec.36) likely duplicates `002`'s dedicated prepositions chapter; the base list is skipped, but concrete
illustrative collocations are kept in the vocabulary table. The **det er / det finnes** "there is/are"
construction (sec.37) is a genuinely new explicit-construction note, with **er** and **finnes**
presented as interchangeable (**Det er (or Det finnes) blomster på bordet**). **Indefinite pronouns**
(sec.38) likely duplicate `002`'s dedicated chapter on the same topic — base paradigm skipped, idiom
examples kept. **More about numbers** (sec.39): **hundre/tusen take no plural ending when counting**
(**to hundre**) but pluralize as true nouns (**tusener og atter tusener** = "thousands and thousands");
collectives **et par/et dusin/et snes** (couple/dozen/score); multiplicative **én gang/to ganger/tre
ganger**. **The weather** (sec.40) is a self-contained topical vocabulary set not covered anywhere in
001-003 — kept in full.

### Chapter 8 — Future tense, Reflexive verbs, Relative pronoun som, More comparison, Conjunctions (pp.115-128)
**Future tense** (sec.41), three strategies, more systematic than 001's scattered mentions: (a) present
tense substitutes for future (**Kommer du i morgen?**); (b) periphrastic **kommer til å** + infinitive
(**Den kommer til å bli altfor dyr**) — not in 001, genuinely new; (c) **skal/vil** + infinitive, with
**skal** meaning "due to happen per a decision/plan" and **vil** meaning pure future or desire/will
(**Jeg vil komme** = "I want to come").

**Reflexive verbs** (sec.42) — a full conjugation paradigm, distinct from the sin/seg possessive material
in Ch.3-4: **å glede seg** "to enjoy oneself" uses **seg** in 3rd person singular/plural, but the OBJECT
pronoun forms (**meg/deg/oss/dere**) in 1st/2nd person. Reflexive verbs are "much more common in
Norwegian than in English" per the source. **meg selv/deg selv/seg selv** are used ONLY for emphasis
(**Jeg vasker meg** = "I wash myself/get washed" vs. **Jeg vasker meg selv** = "I do it myself [not
someone else]"). **Seg** must be used even across an embedded infinitive clause when referring back to
the matrix clause's own subject, contrasted with a second, different referent taking **ham/henne**: **Han
[Mr Olsen] bad henne om å vekke seg [=Olsen] kl. 8** vs. **...vekke ham [=a third person] kl. 8**.

**Relative pronoun som** (sec.43) likely overlaps `002`'s dedicated relative-pronouns chapter — base
coverage skipped. New: **som** can be dropped when not the subject of its clause (**Jeg har kjøpt bøkene
(som) du bad om**); **hvis** is the genitive form of **som**, "little used except in (traditional) written
language" (**Mannen hvis bil var blitt stjålet...**), with the spoken equivalent restructuring the
sentence instead (**Mannen som bilen var stjålet fra...**); **som** combines with other pronouns:
**den/det/hva/alt + som** ("whoever/whatever/everything that").

**More about comparison** (sec.44): "as...as" = **like/likså...som** or **så...som**. A genuinely new
idiom pattern — comparative/superlative forms used WITHOUT any explicit comparison, as an intensity
device: **en yngre dame** ("a youngish lady," not "younger than X"), **en eldre mann** ("an elderly man"),
**i lengre tid** ("for quite a long time"), **en større sum penger** ("a considerable sum of money");
similarly **med den største fornøyelse** ("with the greatest of pleasure"), **i høyeste grad** ("in the
highest degree").

**Co-ordinating conjunctions** (sec.45) likely overlap `002`'s dedicated conjunctions chapter for the base
**og/men** pair. New: **men** ("but") vs. **mens** ("while") minimal-pair distinction (**En lo, mens den
andre gråt**); **for** used as a coordinating conjunction meaning "because" (**Jeg gikk ikke, for jeg var
forkjølet**), distinct from the subordinating conjunction **fordi**.
