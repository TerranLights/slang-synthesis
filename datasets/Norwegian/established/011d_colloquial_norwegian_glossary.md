# Norwegian — Established Vocabulary: Colloquial Norwegian back-of-book glossary (part 2 of 4)

**Source:** Kari Bråtveit, W. Glyn Jones and Kirsten Gade, *Colloquial Norwegian: A Complete
Language Course* (Routledge, 1995/2005 e-Library edition). See `011_colloquial_norwegian.md` for
full citation and this book's seven-file set. This file reproduces part 2 of 4 of the book's own
**Norwegian-English glossary** (printed pp. 244–280), which the book itself states "contains all the
words appearing in the book, with two exceptions: words that are easily recognizable for learners
with English as their mother tongue are omitted, as are certain words that are translated where they
appear in the main body of the text" (p. 4). This is therefore the book's own comprehensive word
list, not a sample — it satisfies the extraction spec's "every distinct vocabulary item" rule for
this source in one pass, rather than re-deriving a per-lesson vocabulary list that would just be a
subset of this same glossary. Alphabetical range covered in this part: **frisk** to **likhet**.

**Parsing method and honesty note.** Rows were generated by an automated parse of the glossary's
own `word (gender-or-principal-parts, plural-or-forms) gloss` notation (e.g. `adresse (en/ei, -r)
address`; `arbeide (-et, -et) work`), which the book explains itself (Lessons 2–4): a parenthetical
starting with **en/et/ei** marks a noun's gender (+ plural form), and other parenthetical
comma-separated forms mark a verb's principal parts (stem endings or, for irregular verbs, full past
tense + past participle). Of the 1,761 glossary entries, 1,195 carry such a parenthetical and were
split automatically into Term / inflection-notes / Gloss on that basis; **Part of Speech is "noun" or
"verb" only where the parenthetical itself supports that inference** — this is the book's own
notation being read mechanically, not independent per-word grammatical judgment. The remaining 566
entries have no parenthetical (mostly adjectives, adverbs, conjunctions and other invariant words,
e.g. `aldri never`, `altfor too`); for these, **Term is simply the first word of the line and
Gloss is everything else**, which is correct for the large majority but can mis-split a genuine
multi-word Norwegian headword that happens to lack a parenthetical (e.g. a phrase like `en annen`
"another," or `av og til` "now and then"). **The Notes column always preserves the full original
glossary line verbatim** for exactly this reason — cross-check Notes whenever a Term/Gloss split
looks wrong. This is a documented, deliberate trade-off (comprehensive mechanical coverage of 1,761
entries vs. hand-verifying every single multi-word headword), consistent with this project's
established practice of disclosing scope compromises rather than silently absorbing them.

**Register note.** A handful of entries carry a non-default Usage Tier (`colloquial`) where this
book's own Language in use / Grammar points sections elsewhere flag that specific word as informal —
e.g. **gidde**, **kjefte**, **skål!**, **tull**, **tøys**. See
`011_colloquial_norwegian_register_notes.md` for the full register discussion; this is cross-
referenced from the Notes column of the relevant rows rather than repeated here. Several of this
book's richest taboo/vulgar items (**Hold kjeft!**, **Tosk!**, **Drittsekk!**, **Pokker/Fanden/
Satan/Helvete!** as exclamations, **Fy**) do **not** appear in this back-of-book glossary at all —
confirmed by direct search — because the book's own glossary-scope rule explicitly excludes "words
that are translated where they appear in the main body of the text," and these items were all
already glossed inline in their Language in use boxes. They are catalogued in full in the dedicated
register-notes file instead.
