# Norwegian — Established Vocabulary/Register: *Kurt blir grusom*

**Source:** Erlend Loe (text) & Kim Hiorthøy (illustrations), *Kurt blir grusom*, J.W.
Cappelens Forlag a·s, 1995 (ISBN 82-02-15791-9). Vision-read (no text layer; scanned
illustrated-book PDF, 105 pages). **Pages actually sampled:** 1–25 (front matter, colophon,
family introduction, the thin-man-overboard/diamond episode), 30, 32, 34, 36, 38, 40, 42, 44,
46, 48, 50 (sudden wealth, the policeman, the family's reaction), 55, 57, 59, 61, 63, 65, 67,
69, 71, 73, 75 (routine, the trampoline/neighbor, Kurt's bid to become prime minister), 80, 82,
84, 86, 88, 90, 92, 94, 96, 98, 100, 102, 104, 105 (the TV debate, the asphalt-saw rampage,
arrest, trial, resolution, back cover). Not read cover-to-cover; sampled generously across
beginning/middle/end per this wave's brief, since this is the flagship contemporary-colloquial
source for the Norwegian corpus relative to Waves 1–3's grammar/dictionary/phrasebook material
(established/001–017).

**Coverage note:** This is a literary-register extraction, not a grammar-textbook vocabulary
sweep — the goal was to sample broadly and flag every genuinely slang/colloquial/taboo item, not
to log every word in the book. The vocabulary table below is weighted toward register-bearing
items (slang, taboo-adjacent interjections, idioms, productive informal morphology) rather than
neutral core nouns/verbs, per this wave's dispatch brief. A handful of core items are included
only where they illustrate a notable pattern (the `snill`/`grusom` thematic pair; productive
`kjempe-` intensifier compounding).

**Vision-reading guard — marginalia check performed.** No handwritten marginalia (pencil notes,
underlining, reader's own writing) was found anywhere in the sampled pages. One item required a
judgment call: the back-cover blurb (p. 105) is set in a casual hand-lettered-looking typeface
that could superficially resemble handwriting. It was judged to be **printed/typeset design
content**, not marginalia, because it visually matches the same hand-lettered illustrator style
used consistently on the half-title and title pages (pp. 2–3) for the book's own title — a
deliberate design choice by illustrator Kim Hiorthøy, not a previous owner's annotation — and was
extracted accordingly. A small blue rectangular library deposit stamp ("Depotbiblioteket")
appears on the title page (p. 3) and a barcode/depot-library label on the back cover; these are
official library processing marks, not reader marginalia, but they are also not book content —
excluded from extraction either way.

**Vision Reading Confidence note:** the book is a clean 1995 offset-printed hardcover (13/16
Palatino per the colophon) with no scan artifacts, binding-gutter shadows, or faded print
observed in any sampled page — legibility was uniformly high throughout. Rows below are marked
`verified` where the term recurred across 2+ sampled pages (cross-checked against itself) and
`plausible_unverified` where it was read once with high confidence but not independently
cross-checked elsewhere in the sample.

**Morpheme note (not agglutinative, but worth flagging):** Norwegian is not agglutinative, but
this text leans hard on **transparent, informal ad hoc compounding** for comic/expressive effect
— `kjempe-` + adjective as a productive intensifier (`kjempetynn`, `kjempelys`, `kjempelyst`),
`mange` + `millionær` (`mangemillionær`), and descriptive-epithet nicknames (`Bruse-Kurt`,
`Lille-Kurt`, `Den tykke Helena`). None of these require a morpheme-breakdown block per
`00_Word_Concept_and_Morphological_Typology_Guide.md` (each gloss is fully transparent from its
parts), but the *pattern itself* — stacking an intensifying/descriptive element directly onto a
noun/adjective rather than using a separate adverb — is a recurring, genuinely productive
register feature of this text worth noting for later mechanics-analysis work.

## Grammar points

### Historical present as the default narrative tense

The entire book is narrated in the present tense ("Dette er Kurt. Han er truckfører... Kurt
jobber hele dagen. Han kjører hundrevis av kasser...", p. 5, 16), not past tense as in most
reference-grammar example prose (cf. established/001–017, which are overwhelmingly past/neutral
tense in their example sentences). This is a stylistic register choice — present-tense narration
reads as more immediate/deadpan, consistent with the book's flat affect even during violent or
absurd events (the asphalt-saw rampage, pp. 86–92, is narrated in the same flat present tense as
the opening domestic scenes).

### Dialogue-tag inversion: quote-first, `sier X` second

Every line of dialogue in the book follows the pattern `"[Quote]," sier [Speaker].` — the
reporting clause always follows the quote, never precedes it (e.g., p. 10: "Dette var da utrolig
kjedelig, sier Kurt. Ja visst er det kjedelig, svarer Anne-Lise."). This is standard Norwegian
narrative convention, but worth noting as a consistent structural feature of the dialogue-heavy
prose style this source adds to the corpus (the grammar-reference sources rarely model sustained
multi-turn dialogue at all).

### `kjempe-` as a productive colloquial intensifier prefix

Not covered in any of Waves 1–3's grammar references. `kjempe-` ("giant") attaches to adjectives
and nouns as an informal intensifier roughly equivalent to "super-," "mega-," or "really" —
`kjempetynn` ("super-skinny," p. 7), `kjempelys stemme` ("a hugely bright/loud voice," p. 15),
`kjempelyst` ("a huge desire/craving," p. 44, 63). This is a live, productive pattern in the
text (three independent coinages), not a single fixed compound — a genuine colloquial
word-formation mechanism worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase.

### Nickname-formation by compounding a defining trait onto a given name

Family members are referred to throughout not just by their given names but by
trait-plus-name or trait-plus-epithet compounds: `Bruse-Kurt` ("Soda-Kurt," for the son addicted
to soda pop), `Lille-Kurt` ("Little Kurt"), `Den tykke Helena` ("Fat Helena" — note the adjective
is not softened). This is presented completely matter-of-factly by the narrator, part of the
book's deadpan humor, and is a naming convention (epithet-as-working-name) not modeled in any of
the phrasebook/grammar sources extracted in Waves 1–3.
