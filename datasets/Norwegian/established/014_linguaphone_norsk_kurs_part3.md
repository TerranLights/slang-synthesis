# Norwegian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Linguaphone Norwegian Course — Kurs + Handbook (Part 3 of 4)

**Source.** Same matched pair as Parts 1–2 (The Linguaphone Institute, *Norwegian Course* +
*Norwegian Course Handbook*, 1st ed. 1964, 3rd rev. ed. 1986). This file covers **Leksjon 41 through
Leksjon 50**, including the two literary poem texts that close the Vocabularies section ("Søfuglen"
and "Forviklinger") — Handbook pp.49–60. See Part 1 for the full source/methodology header. One
addition specific to this part: **the two poems are the only point in this Handbook's vocab section
where the informal pronoun `du` appears as the default address form**, consistent with their being
literary/lyric register rather than the course's own De-default conversational register (see Part 1
register note) — a genuine register-vocabulary data point, not just a word list.

## Grammar points

### Spelling-reform (pre-1938/1959) archaic forms in the two closing poems

The two poems "Søfuglen" and "Forviklinger" that close the Handbook's vocabulary section (Leksjon 50)
are set in a visibly older orthography than the rest of the course — spellings like *æbletræ* (modern
*epletre*), *sig* (modern *seg*), *fik* (modern *fikk*), *faldt* (modern *falt*), and *høie* (modern
*høye*) all predate the 1938/1959 Bokmål spelling reforms the Handbook's own "The Norwegian Language"
essay describes (see Part 4). This is a live, textual example of exactly the older orthography that
essay describes abstractly, and is worth keeping as a concrete data point: a 20th-century Bokmål
learner course deliberately preserved an un-modernized literary text for register/period flavor rather
than normalizing it. The poems' consistent **ei**-for-**ikke** negator is also archaic/poetic and does
not appear anywhere else in this course's ~2,500-entry vocabulary.
