### 2.3 Morphological formation of nouns — nominal derivation

Ilocano is highly productive at deriving nominals from roots via prefixes, circumfixes, infixes,
and reduplication. Any lexeme in nominal position (after an article/demonstrative) functions as a
noun without further marking (*immáy* "came" → *dagití immáy* "the ones who came").

### 3. Articles and Demonstratives

#### 3.1 Articles

Ilocano articles encode number (singular/plural), two cases (core/oblique), and distinguish
personal nouns (names, vocatives) from non-personal nouns.

**Table — Article paradigm (p. lii):**

| Class | Case | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| Non-personal | Core | ti | dagití |
| Non-personal | Oblique | ití | kadagití |

**Grammar point — 4.1 Placement.** Adjectives may precede or follow the noun, linked by ligature
*(ng)a*; contrastive adjectives (distinguishing from other possible referents) usually follow: *ti
nasingpét nga ubíng* "the well-behaved child" (neutral) vs. *ti ubíng a nasingpét* "the well-behaved
child [as opposed to the naughty ones]." Adjectives of composition/material always follow (*ti
tulbék a balitók* "the gold key").

**Grammar point — 4.2 Intensification and derivation.** Intensification devices: prefix *nag-* +
enclitic *=(e)n* for wonder/admiration (*adú* "much" → *nagadún* "so many!"; *napintás* "beautiful" →
*nagpintásen* "how beautiful!"); prefix *nakaCVC-* or adverb *unay* for strong intensification
(*naángot* "smelly" → *nakaang-ángot* "stinking very much"); affix *paN- -en* to **moderate**
(soften) the degree (*nalukmég* "fat" → *panglukmegén* "rather stout"); affix *nain- -an* to derive
abstract adjectives (*kappiá* "peace" → *nainkappiáan* "peaceful").

**Grammar point — 4.3 Comparatives and superlatives.** Comparative degree: reduplicate the root's
first CVC (*dákes* "bad" → *dakdákes* "worse"; *nakuttóng* "thin" → *nakutkuttóng* "thinner"),
linked across two nouns by disjunctive conjunction *ngem* "but" (*Naluklukmégka ngem ni Sergio*
"You are fatter than Sergio"). A **conditional superlative** uses prefix *sumangka-* ("if X, Y is
even more so"): *No napúdaw ni Gloria, sumangkapúdaw ni Maria* "If Gloria is fair-complexioned,
Maria is even more so." True superlative: circumfix *ka- -an* (*nasadút* "lazy" → *kasadután*
"laziest"; *dakkél* "large" → *kadak(ke)lán* "largest, biggest").

**Grammar point — 6.3.1 Recent past.** Prefix *ka-* + CV reduplication of the root's initial
syllable; actor in ergative case: *Kasasangpétko* "I just arrived" (root *sangpet* "arrive"). Also
formed by prefix *kapag-* or *kaCVC-/kaC-*: *itlóg* "egg" → *kait-itlóg* "freshly laid (egg); just
laid"; *lúto* "cook" → *kal(ut)lúto* "freshly cooked (food); just cooked"; *asáwa* "spouse" →
*kapagas-asáwa* "recent marriage; just married"; *agasáwa* "marry" → *kapagasawáan* "newlywed
couple, recently married"; *serrék* "enter" → *kasserrék* "newcomer; just entered"; *purós* "pick"
→ *kappurós* "just picked"; *raép* "transplant" → *karraép* "just transplanted (rice)"; *tugáw*
"chair" → *kattugáw* "just appointed"; *sibét* "depart" → *kassibét* "just departed."

**Grammar point — 6.3.2 Inchoativity.** Infix *-um-* ("becoming"), perfective *-imm-* (historically
from *-inum-): *táyag* "tall" → *tumáyag* "to grow tall" → *timmáyag* "grew taller"; *baknáng*
"wealth" → *bumaknáng* "to become wealthy" → *bimmaknáng* "became wealthy"; *guápo* "handsome" →
*gumuápo* "to become handsome" → *gimmuápo* "became handsome." The verb *agbalín* "become" is also
used, usually for more-controllable/energetic becoming (*Nagbalínda a nabaknáng* "They became
rich").

pronouns, those pronouns attach to the negative adverbs themselves: *Ay-ayaténka* "I love you" →

Existential negation substitutes *awán* for *addá* (*Addá ások* "I have a dog" → *Awán ások* "I
don't have a dog").

#### 13.2 Complement clauses

Three main types: nominal *ti* complementation, ligature-like *ta* complementation, and ligature
*nga* complementation.

**Grammar point — ti complementation.** Used when the matrix verb is transitive; the pivot is the
ergative actor, shared between both clauses and overt only in the main clause: *Masápulko ti
mangán* "I need to eat"; *Kayátmo ti aglangóy?* "Do you want to swim?" The embedded verb may take
perfective morphology for completed actions: *Inan-ánatna ti timmakdér* "He carefully stood up."

**Grammar point — ta complementation.** Finite clauses preceded by ligature-like *ta*;
co-referential arguments are not deleted. Predicators requiring *ta* function as lexicalized
case-frame expressions:

**Grammar point — nga complementation.** The most versatile complementation type; both main-clause
and complement-clause verbs can be personal or impersonal, transitive or intransitive, and there
are no restrictions on the co-referential argument's grammatical role in the main clause (it may be
ergative or absolutive). Example (shared argument): *Padásekto a kasaríta ni Anching* "I'll try to
speak with Anching." Example (non-shared argument): *Kayátko a patayém ni Bérning* "I want you to
kill Berning."

#### 13.3 Adverbial clauses

Adverbial clauses are sentential/phrasal adjuncts, formed with an adverb or overt subordinator,
covering a range of types (listed per the book's own enumeration):

| Type | Example (Ilocano) | English gloss |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal | Nalúton idí sumangpét ti asáwana. | It was already opened when his wife arrived. |
| Locational | Inggasanggásatda iti dagá a sinaldáda. | They tried their luck in the land they mortgaged. |
| Manner | Saánmo nga aramíden a kas iti inarámidna. | Don't do it like he did. |
| Reason/purpose | Agsangsángitda ta nabáutda. | They are crying because they were whipped. |
| Circumstantial | Naduáyaka ití panagkantána. | You were lulled to sleep by her singing. |
| Simultaneous | Saának a nakatúrog báyat ti kaaddáda. | I wasn't able to sleep while they were here. |
| Conditional | No umáyka ditóy, agpadayátayonto. | If you come here, we'll have a party. |
| Concessive | Úray agluáka iti dára, díkanto taliáwen. | Even if you cry blood, I will not look back at you! |
| Substitutive | Isú ti maanupán imbés nga isú ti aganúp. | He will be the hunted instead of the one who hunts. |
| Additive | Abús ta sadút, naalás. | Aside from being lazy, she is ugly. |
| Resultative | Natuduán ngamín, isú nga natáy. | It got rained on, so it died. |
| Optative | Báreng no umáy. | I hope he comes. |

#### 13.4 Coordination

Three coordinators: *ken* "and", *ngem* "but", *wennó* "or". Clauses may also combine with no overt
coordinator at all. Numbers are traditionally linked with *ket*, though *ken* is now used by most
speakers for that purpose; *ket* most often marks inversed predicates or resultative/reason
