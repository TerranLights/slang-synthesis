instruction to prioritize paradigm completeness over exhaustive example vocabulary — though
notable or recurring ones are captured.

**Flagship finding flagged for this project:** The **fused ergative/absolutive pronoun matrix**
(Section 4.4.1, p. 58/PDF 84) is the single richest piece of grammatical-system content in this
chunk — an 8×8 table showing how the actor (`=ko` series, ergative) and patient (`=ak` series,
absolutive) enclitics of a transitive verb have **phonologically fused into unique portmanteau
forms** over the history of the language (e.g. *siak→sika* 'I...you' = `=ka`, *sika→siak*
'you...me' = `=nak`, *isuda→siak* 'they...me' = `=dak`), rather than simply concatenating the two
independently-attested series. This is a strong candidate for conlang slang-derivation interest:
a synthetic language building compressed pronoun-pair clitics for its own in-group transitive
"who-did-what-to-whom" shorthand would have a real-world typological precedent here.

## Chapter 3 — Articles, Demonstratives and Case

### 3.2–3.3 — Core/oblique case distinction; other functions of the oblique

The core/oblique distinction is Ilocano's basic case system: core noun phrases (replaceable by an
absolutive `=ak` or ergative `=ko` enclitic) take the core article; all other core arguments of a
transitive verb and all non-argument/oblique nominals take the oblique article or an oblique
preposition.

### 3.10.1 — Spatial demonstratives (full paradigm)

= near addressee/slightly far from both, distal = far from the speech-event setting) crossed with
core/oblique case and singular/plural number. Contracted (clitic) forms are given in parentheses.

## Chapter 4 — Pronouns

Ilocano has five pronoun paradigms: independent absolutives, independent possessives, enclitic
absolutives, enclitic ergatives, and obliques.
