## Glossary of Linguistics Terms (printed pp. 499–510, PDF pp. 524–535)

Reproduced in full (112 entries, alphabetical A–W). This is the dissertation's own glossary,
defining the linguistic metalanguage used throughout the grammar (not Ilocano vocabulary itself,
but the terminological apparatus applied to it) — kept here verbatim as a standing cross-reference
for this project's grammar-logic analysis work.

entries *absolutive* through *affix*) was originally skipped due to a 1-page offset gap between
this file's assigned range (524-549) and sibling file 011's assigned range (470-523): both
agents' self-reports described the boundary as "PDF p.524" when the true Glossary start is PDF
p.523. Directly vision-read and inserted below by the orchestrating session after the wave

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| absolutive | The grammatical category that includes subjects of intransitive verbs and objects of transitive verbs. The *=ak* pronouns in Ilocano are absolutive. (See also *ergative*) |
| abstract noun | A noun whose meaning is an abstract concept, *truth*, *patience*, *virtue* or a noun denoting an event, *departure*, *realization*. Contrast *concrete noun*. |
| active voice | Grammatical construction with an active verb where the argument performing the action is in the ergative case (with transitive verbs) or the absolutive case (with intransitive verbs). Contrast with *passive*. |
| actor | Extension of the semantic notion of *agent*, referring to the performer or experiencer of the action or state of a verb (see Schachter 1976); argument of a predicate which performs, effects, instigates or controls the situation of a predicate (Foley and Van Valin 1984:29). |
| adjective | A lexical class of words that expresses permanent or temporary attributes of nouns. *dakkel* 'big', *bassit* 'small', and *naulpit* 'cruel' are adjectives. |
| adverb | A lexical category whose members are usually grammatical adjuncts of verbs and typically express such semantic notions as time, manner, place, instrument or circumstance. |
| adverbial | Any category with function and distribution similar to that of a lexical adverb. |
| adverbial clause | A subordinate clause which bears to its main clause any of a range of semantic relations similar to those shared by adverbs, such as time, manner, place, instrument, circumstance, concession, purpose, result, cause, or condition, i.e. *idiay padaya* 'at the party', *intono rabii* 'this evening' are adverbial phrases (see Thompson and Longacre 1985). |
| affirmative | An utterance in which no negative element is present. |
| affix | A type of bound morpheme that is attached to a stem. The four affix types in Ilocano are prefix, infix, suffix, and reduplication. |
| affixation | The process of attaching an *affix* to a stem. |
| affricate | A type of consonant which consists of a stop and a fricative, i.e. *ts*. |
| agent | The semantic role of a noun which is perceived as the conscious instigator of an action. |
| agglutinating language | A language whose morphology is characterized predominantly by agglutination. |
| agglutination | A type of morphological structure in which words can be readily divided into a sequence of distinct morphemes of fairly consistent shapes and readily identifiable functions. |
| allomorph | A variant of a morpheme which occurs in a specific environment, i.e. the future enclitic *=(n)to* has two allomorphs, *=to* after consonants, and *=nto* after vowels. |
| allophone | A variant sound of a phoneme, which is not contrastive with other variant sounds of the phoneme. |
| alveolar | Pertaining to the inner gum ridge just above the upper teeth, the *s* is an alveolar fricative. |
| alveo-palatal | Pertaining to the region of the gum ridge and the hard palate behind the inner gum ridge. The *ts* and *y* are alveo-palatal consonants in Ilocano. |
| animate | Referring to a living being. |
| antipassive | An intransitive construction whose absolutive argument is an agent and which contains an oblique argument representing a patientive argument or object. Antipassives commonly appear in *ergative* languages. |
| argument | A noun bearing a specific grammatical relationship to a verb. |
| article | A determiner used to mark nouns. The distinctions that an article may provide vary from language to language. |
| aspect | A grammatical category of verbs which indicates the duration or nature of the verbal action. |
| Austronesian | The language family to which all Philippine languages belong. |
| benefactive | The semantic relation indicating for whom the action of the verb is performed. |
| bilabial | A sound produced with both lips, the *b* and *p* are bilabial stops. |
| bound morpheme | A morpheme that must be attached to another morpheme; it may not occur separately. |
| canonical | Denoting a pattern or structure that is most typical, most frequent, or least marked among competing possibilities. |
| case | The grammatical relationship of a nominal referent to its verb. Ilocano has three cases, ergative, absolutive, and oblique. |
| causative | A transitive construction in which the action of a verb is indirectly caused or performed. Ilocano has a causative prefix *pa-*. |
| circumfix | An affix combination of a prefix and suffix. |
| clitic | An item that exhibits behavior intermediate between that of a word and that of an affix. Typically, clitics attach phonologically to constituents, whereas affixes attach to words. |
| clause | A construction containing a predicative element. |
| closed syllable | A syllable that ends in a consonant. |
| comparative | A construction in which two entities are compared. *Nataytayagak ngem sika* 'I am taller than you' is a comparative construction. |
| complement clause | A type of subordinate clause which functions as a noun phrase. The syntactic situation that arises when a notional sentence or predication is an argument of a predicate. See Noonan 1985. |
| complementizer | Grammatical morpheme which marks a complement clause. |
| complex sentence | Any sentence that contains one or more subordinate clauses. |
| compound sentence | A sentence that contains two or more main clauses. |
| concessive clause | A type of adverbial clause which implies that the proposition it expresses might have been expected to exclude the proposition expressed in the main clause, but in fact does not. *uray no* 'although' and *nupay* 'although' introduce concessive clauses in Ilocano. |
| concrete noun | A noun whose meaning is perceived as a physical entity, i.e. tree, ladder, jar, flower. Contrast abstract noun. |
| conditional clause | A type of adverbial clause which expresses a condition upon whose fulfillment the proposition of the main clause depends. *No* 'if' usually marks conditional clauses in Ilocano. |
| conjunct | Any one of the constituents which are conjoined in a coordinate structure. |
| conjunction | A closed lexical category or item of the category, whose members serve to construct coordinate structures. *ken* 'and', *wenno* 'or' and *ngem* 'but' are conjunctions in Ilocano. |
| consonant cluster | Two or more consonants that occur together without an intervening vowel. |
| constituent | Any part of an utterance which forms a distinct syntactic unit (in terms of its behavior and semantics). |
| context | The linguistic environment; linguistic situation in which an utterance is made. |
| contraction | A single phonological word representing a sequence of two or more separate word forms, i.e. *maykadtoy* is a contraction for *umayka ditoy* 'come here.' |
| contrastive sounds | Sounds that contrast (may change meaning) in a given language. In Ilocano, *p* and *b* are contrastive because *pala* 'shovel' ≠ *bala* 'bullet'. |
| coordination | The linking of two or more elements as conjuncts. |
| core case | The cases which represent required arguments of the verb. In Ilocano, the ergative and the absolutive are core cases. |
| declarative | A statement which the speaker believes to be true. |
| defective verbs | Verbs that do not conform to the general rules of other verbs in the language. |
| deictic | Anything which serves to express a distinction in terms of orientation within the immediate context of an utterance. Among the deictic categories in Ilocano are person, tense, and position. |
| demonstrative | A determiner with a clear deictic function. |
| derivational affix | An affix whose primary function is to change the word class or meaning of the stem to which it attaches. *ag-* is a derivational prefix because with *tugaw* 'chair' it forms a verb *agtugaw* 'to sit.' |
| determiner | A lexical category consisting of words that introduce noun phrases. In Ilocano *daytoy* 'this' and *ti* 'article' are among the many determiners. |
| dialect | A variety of a language spoken by a particular speech community. Dialects of a given language are mutually intelligible. |
| diphthong | A vowel-like sound consisting of a vowel followed by a glide: *aw*, *ay*, *iw*, *ey*, etc. |
| distribution | The set of environments in which a particular linguistic form occurs. |
| dual number | Category used to express two of a particular entity. In Ilocano the dual inclusive pronoun 'you and I' is *data*. |
| enclitic | A clitic which is phonologically bound after a host. The Ilocano ergative pronouns are enclitics. |
| equational sentence | A sentence in which one noun phrase is identified with another without a copula. *Lalaki ni Andoy.* 'Andoy is a boy' is an equational sentence. |
| ergative | The grammatical category that distinguishes the agents of transitive verbs from the absolutive category. In Ilocano, the *=ko* pronouns are ergative. |
| etymology | The history and derivation of a word. |
| exclamation | An utterance serving to express emotion. |
| exclusive pronoun | A form of the first person plural that excludes the person being spoken to. *Dakami* is an exclusive pronoun. |
| existential | A type of word class that expresses existence of an entity. In Ilocano the existentials may denote existence, location, or possession. The existential in Ilocano is *adda*, with a negative counterpart *awan*. |
| experiencer | The semantic role of an animate referent who is the passive recipient of a sensation or mental experience. |
| expletive | An exclamatory word or interjection. |
| feminine | A traditional label in gender languages which shows some correlation with female sex. In Ilocano, certain Spanish adjective and noun borrowings may express feminine gender. |
| focus | A system characteristic of Philippine languages whereby a verbal affix designates the semantic and grammatical relationship between the verb and the absolutive category. |
| fossilized prefix | A prefix that is no longer productive and has become an inseparable part of a word. |
| free morpheme | A morpheme that may form a word. |
| frequentative | Aspect which expresses the frequent repetition of an action. |
| fricative | A type of consonant formed by a continuing release of air through a partial obstruction in the mouth, *s* is a fricative. |
| future | Tense correlated with future time. |
| geminate | A sequence of identical consonants. In Ilocano orthography, they are written as double consonants, i.e. *lallaki* 'boys.' |
| gender | Grammatical category found in certain Spanish loanwords that indicates the sex (masculine or feminine) of the referent. |
| genitive | The possessive case. |
| gerund | Verbal noun, verb with nominal morphology. |
| glide | A vowel that takes on consonantal properties when in contact with another vowel or the consonants *y* and *w*. |
| gloss | Translation. |
| glottal | Referring to the glottis (lower region of the vocal tract near the vocal cords), *h* is a glottal fricative. |
| habitual | Aspect category which expresses an action which is regularly or consistently performed. |
| head word | Word that is at the center of a phrase and is modified by another constituent. |
| homonym | A word that sounds identical to another word, but contrasts in meaning. |
| host | A stem to which an affix attaches. |
| idiom | An expression consisting of two or more words whose meanings cannot be predicted from the meanings of its constituent parts. |
| imperfective | Aspectual category which contrasts with perfective. |
| inanimate | Denoting a noun which is lifeless, not animate. |
| inchoative | An aspectual form which expresses the beginning of a state or the becoming of a state. |
| inclusive pronoun | A pronoun that includes the addressee, i.e. *datayo*, *data* are inclusive pronouns. |
| infix | An affix that is attached internally within another morpheme, i.e. *surat* 'write' → *sinurat* 'wrote'. |
| inflectional affix | An affix whose primary function is grammatical, rather than semantic. It has no independent meaning by itself and does not change the lexical category of its host. |
| initial position | The beginning of a linguistic unit, such as a syllable, word, or utterance. |
| instrumental | The case form which expresses the means by which something is done. |
| interrogative | Pertaining to questions. |
| intonation | The rise and fall in pitch and the differing degrees of loudness in a speaker's articulation. |
| intransitive verb | A verb that takes one argument at the most. Ilocano intransitive verbs may take an absolutive argument. |
| involuntary | Action not done on purpose, in which the performer generally does not exhibit control or volition. |
| irregular | Not conforming to the general rule of a language. |
| iterative | Aspectual form which expresses the repetition of an action, a subclass of the imperfective aspect. |
| labial | Sounds produced with the lips, i.e. *p*, *b*, *m*. |
| lexical category | A class which contains certain items that share morphological and syntactic behavior. Lexical classes may include: noun, verb, adjective, determiner, etc. |
| ligature | A certain word, common in Austronesian languages, that is used to link constituents. *Nga* is the Ilocano ligature. Also called linker. |
| linker | See *ligature*. |
| liquid | A type of sound made with partial closure but without friction, i.e. *l*, *r*. |
| loan | Word or morpheme borrowed from another language, i.e. *kalesa* 'horse drawn carriage' is borrowed from Spanish. |
| locative | Case or word which expresses where something exists or occurs. |
| main clause | A clause that may stand alone. |
| masculine | A traditional label in gender languages which shows some correlation with male sex. In Ilocano, certain borrowed nouns and adjectives indicate gender. |
| medial position | In the middle of a linguistic unit, i.e. in the middle of a word. |
| metathesis | The transposition of sounds, i.e. *in-* often becomes *ni-* before *l*. |
| minimal pair | Two words that are distinguished by a single contrast, i.e. *dugi* 'large sewing needle' vs. *duri* 'spine'. |
| modifier | A word, phrase, or clause that qualifies another word, phrase or clause. Modifiers provide additional semantic information about the constituents they modify. |
| morpheme | The smallest unit of speech that has meaning, *basa* 'reading' consists of one morpheme, while *agbasa* 'to read' consists of two, the stem *basa*, and the verbalizing prefix *ag-*. |
| morphological | Pertaining to the morphology. |
| morphology | The study of morphemes and their combination in word formation. |
| nasal | A sound produced with a lowered uvula, forcing the air to escape through the nose, *m*, *n*, *ng* are nasal sounds. |
| nominalization | The process in which a word or phrase is converted into a noun-like unit. In Ilocano the verb *agsurat* 'to write' may be nominalized to *panagsurat* 'writing.' |
| noun | A lexical category whose members denote physical entities and other words that behave grammatically equivalently to the physical entities. Examples of Ilocano nouns are *kalding* 'goat', *ima* 'hand', and *ayat* 'love.' |
| noun phrase (NP) | A noun phrase which minimally consists of a head noun, but may contain modifiers. Noun phrases in Ilocano include *aso* 'dog', *ti aso* 'the dog', and *ti aso nga nagtaul* 'the dog that barked.' |
| oblique | Denoting a non-core case in a language. Oblique phrases or nouns are typically marked with prepositions in Ilocano. |
| open syllable | A syllable that ends in a vowel. |
| optative | The mood category which expresses wishes or hopes. The particle *kuma* in Ilocano expresses the optative. |
| orthography | A system of spelling in a given language. |
| particle | A lexical item that is invariant in form. It does not exhibit lexical or derivational morphology. |
| passive voice | A term used to describe a verb form in which the absolutive argument receives or undergoes the action, and the actor is not specified, i.e. *Tinawaganak.* 'I was called.' |
| patient | The semantic role of a noun which expresses the entity undergoing an action. |
| penultimate | Second to last. In the word *súrat* the stress is on the penultimate syllable. |
| perfective | An aspect category which expresses an action or state which is confined to a single instant in time. |
| person | The deictic category which distinguishes the entities in a speech context. First person refers to the speaker, second person refers to the addressee, and third person refers to everyone or everything else. |
| phoneme | A significant or contrastive sound in a given language. In Ilocano *p* and *b* are separate phonemes because they contrast as in the following minimal pair *apay* 'why' vs. *abay* 'beside.' |
| phonology | The study of the nature of sounds and their distributions, the study of the nature of phonemes. |
| pitch | The highness or lowness of the tone in an individual's speech. |
| point of articulation | The point where the speech articulators (such as the tongue and lips) meet the immovable speech organs (teeth, alveolar ridge, palate). |
| potential | Mood category expressing ability or possibility. |
| prefix | An affix that is attached to the beginning of a stem. |
| preposition | A term used in grammatical classification of words to refer to a set of items which precede noun phrases to form a single constituent structure. |
| presentative | A construction that is used to introduce a new referent in the discourse. |
| productive | Referring to a grammatical or morphological pattern which can be freely applied to any item meeting its requirements. An affix that may be added to new words recently adopted by a language is still productive. (compare *fossilized*) |
| progressive | The aspect category which refers to an action or event which is in progress at the moment of time serving as a reference point for the utterance. |
| pronoun | A morpheme class whose members may substitute full noun phrases. *Isu* 'he/she' may substitute *Ni Cristy* 'Cristy'. |
| recipient | The referent which receives an object. In the sentence *Intedko ti kuarta kenni Maria* 'I gave the money to Maria', *Maria* is the recipient. |
| reciprocal | Construction in which two entities perform an action onto each other. |
| reduplication | The repetition of all or part of a syllable or word, *agbasa* 'read' may reduplicate to *agbasbasa* 'reading'. |
| referent | Entity in the real or conceptual world which is associated with a noun phrase. |
| reflexive | Construction in which two noun phrases are understood as being co-referential (having the same referent), i.e. *Nakitak ti bagik* 'I saw myself.' |
| relative clause | Type of subordinate clause which functions as a modifier to a noun. (See Keenan 1985). |
| relativizer | Morpheme that introduces a relative clause, or links a relative clause to the noun it modifies. |
| resultative | An aspectual form representing a state resulting from an earlier event. |
| root | A word which cannot be reduced any further in terms of its morpheme structure. It may take other affixes. |
| semantics | The study of meaning. |
| semelfactive | Aspectual category which expresses actions that happen exactly once. |
| sentence | Utterance which may stand alone to express a coherent thought. |
| simple sentence | Sentence which minimally contains one verb phrase or non-verbal predicate. |
| singular | In languages with a number distinction, the category of number which expresses a single entity. (contrast with plural) |
| stative verb | A verb which expresses a state of affairs rather than an event, i.e. *ammo* 'know', *maawatan* 'understand', *kayat* 'like.' |
| stem | Any word to which another morpheme may attach. Stems, unlike roots, may be poly-morphemic. |
| stop | A type of consonant produced by creating an instantaneous release of air immediately following a complete obstruction of the vocal tract, *p*, *b*, *t*, *d*, *k*, *g* are stops. |
| stress | The degree of loudness of a syllable. An alternate term is accent. |
| subordinate clause | A clause that cannot stand alone and is dependent upon a main clause. |
| subordinator | A word used to introduce a subordinate clause. |
| suffix | Any affix that is attached to the end of a stem. |
| superlative | Form used with adjectives to indicate the highest degree of comparison, i.e. *kabassitan* 'smallest' is the superlative of *bassit* 'small.' |
| syllable | A unit in the sound system of a language that has a vowel as its peak. |
| syntax | The study of the relationship of words and phrases in grammatical constructions. |
| tag question | A question which immediately follows a statement and serves to request confirmation. |
| tense | A grammatical category which indicates the location of an event or action in time. |
| topicalization | The phenomenon in which some element is singled out as the most prominent element by use of a marked construction. |
| transitive verb | A verb that takes two or more core arguments. |
| typology | The classification of languages in terms of their structural features. |
| undergoer | Argument which expresses the participant which does not perform, initiate or control the situation of the predicate, but is affected by it (Foley and Van Valin 1984:29). |
| unproductive | Denoting an affix which may not be used with new words introduced in the language. |
| velar | Referring to the velum, the back part of the roof of the mouth, *k*, *g*, and *ng* are velar sounds. |
| velum | The back of the roof of the mouth. |
| verb | A member of the lexical class of verbs which generally refer to 'actions' and sometimes states. Verbs may generally display morphological contrasts of tense, aspect, voice, mood, person, and number. They may be the minimal predicates in a sentence. Verbs in Ilocano include *agbasa* 'read', *maawatan* 'understand', *ibelleng* 'throw out', and *mapan* 'go'. |
| verb phrase | A phrase consisting of a verb and its modifiers. |
| verbalizing affix | An affix that may change a non-verb into a word that functions like a verb. |
| voice | The grammatical category which expresses the relationship between participant roles of the NP and their grammatical relations. |
| voiced | Sounds produced while the vocal chords are vibrating, i.e. vowels, *b*, *d*, *g*, *m*, *n*, *ng* are voiced sounds. |
| voiceless | Sounds produced without the vibration of the vocal chords, *p*, *t*, *k*, *s* are voiceless sounds. |
| volitional | Mood category which expresses wish or desire. |
| Wackernagel's position | The second position in an utterance or sentence in which many clitics tend to occur. |

## References / Bibliography (printed pp. 511–512, PDF pp. 537–538) — scope note

Two pages of alphabetized bibliography, roughly 45 entries, running from Benton (1976, "Pluralization
in Ilocano") through Woods (1971, "First language interference with English verbs and personal
pronouns by Ilocano students"). Not transcribed as vocabulary per the dispatch instructions; its
scope is recorded here for reference. Notable entries of potential future interest to this project
include several works specifically on Ilocano slang/intensive-course register (McKaughan 1952,
*Ilocano: an intensive language course*; Espiritu 1984, *Let's Speak Ilokano*), the foundational
descriptive grammars (Bloomfield 1942; Constantino 1971, *Ilokano reference grammar*; Vanoverbergh
1955, *Iloko grammar*), several PhD/MA theses on specific subsystems (Clausen 1995 on adverbial
clauses; Wimbish 1987, *A relational grammar of Ilocano*; Rubino 1994, the author's own MA thesis
on the Ilocano mental lexicon), and typological/ergativity literature (Dixon 1979/1994; Mithun 1995;
Gerdts 1988, "Antipassives and Causatives in Ilokano: Evidence for an Ergative analysis") that is
directly relevant to the ergative/absolutive case system this dissertation documents. Also present
is a short, separate 6-item reference list (Foley & Van Valin 1984, Keenan 1985, Noonan 1985,
Schachter 1976, Thompson & Longacre 1985, Trask 1993) specific to citations used inside the
Glossary's own definitions — see the preamble above for its page location.

## Map Appendix (printed pp. 513–516, PDF pp. 539–542)

A 4-page appendix (1 title/contents page + 3 maps), explicitly credited as reproductions from
Curtis D. McFarland, *A Linguistic Atlas of the Philippines* (1980), Study of Languages and
Cultures of Asia and Africa Monograph Series No. 15, Tokyo University of Foreign Studies.

- **Title/contents page** (PDF p. 539): lists the three maps and their page numbers (Ilocano
  Speakers in the Philippines — p. 514; Region I — p. 515; Region II — p. 516, in the printed
  book's own pagination).
- **Map 1, "Ilocano Speakers in the Philippines"** (PDF p. 540): a national outline map of the
  Philippine archipelago with a 5-tier shaded legend indicating the proportion of Ilocano speakers
  per province (from a solid-black "highest" tier, concentrated on the northwestern Luzon coast —
  the historical Ilocos homeland — down through cross-hatched and lined tiers of lighter density
  extending into Mindanao and other islands, reflecting post-19th-century Ilocano out-migration).
  The exact wording of each legend tier's percentage threshold is too small/blurred in this scan to
  transcribe reliably and is not guessed here.
- **Map 2, "Region I"** (PDF p. 541): a detailed province-level map of the Ilocos Region (the
  Ilocano-speaking heartland: Ilocos Norte, Ilocos Sur, La Union, and Pangasinan, per the region's
  standard composition — though individual province/municipality labels on the map itself are below
  this scan's legible resolution). Carries its own inset, clearly legible: **"BATANES AND BABUYAN
  ISLANDS"**, a separate inset map of the small island groups north of mainland Luzon.
- **Map 3, "Region II"** (PDF p. 542): a detailed province-level map of the Cagayan Valley region
  (immediately east/southeast of the Ilocos Region, a documented secondary area of Ilocano
  settlement via internal migration), again with individual place-name labels too small to read
  reliably in this scan.

**Flagged for future reference (geographic-scope value, not fully legible here):** the two maps
directly visualize the real-world geographic core (Region I / Ilocos) versus a documented secondary
settlement zone (Region II / Cagayan Valley) for Ilocano, which could usefully anchor "Geographic
Scope" and "Attested Region" fields in other Ilocano `established/` files going forward (e.g.
distinguishing "Ilocos heartland" vocabulary/register from "diaspora/Cagayan Valley" or other
out-migration-zone vocabulary). A higher-resolution scan or the original McFarland (1980) atlas
would be needed to extract specific province/town labels; none are invented here.
