### §6 Chapter opening — two lexical classes of nominal modifier

**Patch note (2026-10-30):** this chapter's own opening page (PDF p.146, printed p.121 — the Ch.6
intro paragraph and its three illustrative sentences) fell into a 1-page gap between this file's
assigned range and sibling file 003's, and was originally skipped. Directly vision-read and
inserted below by the orchestrating session after the wave landed.

Ilocano nominal modifiers occur as two separate lexical classes: fully inflected verbs that appear
in apposition to the nominal, linked by the ligature *(ng)a* (e.g. a continuative-aspect verb
modifying a noun); and monomorphemic or derived adjectives, also linked to the modified noun by
*(ng)a*. Nominal modifiers can thus be distinguished from verbs only by their use, not by form —
though inherent qualities like comparative/superlative affixation and extensive Spanish-loan
adjective vocabulary set the dedicated adjective class apart, discussed through the rest of this
chapter.
