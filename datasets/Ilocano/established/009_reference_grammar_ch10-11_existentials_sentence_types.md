### 10.1 Possessive use (absolute vs. current possession)

Absolute possession: *adda* + possessed nominal + possessor in ergative/possessive case (possessor
always follows possessed). Negated with *awan*. Current possession (actual possession at time of
utterance): *adda* + possessor in **oblique** case (commonly fronted for emphasis) + possessed
nominal in absolutive. Current-possession *adda*-phrases are also used idiomatically for innate
traits/characteristics.

information from the predicate, signaling an upcoming predicate and often marking contrastive focus

#### 11.4.2 *Ne*, *ala* — the inciting exclamations

*Ne* [ni:] and *ala* are the two most common phrase-initial exclamations in Ilocano; they most often
project a following utterance. Both are uttered with higher pitch and volume and occur in their own
intonation unit, as with other interjections. **Register note: *ne* is used only informally, with
one's social equals or inferiors** — an explicit formality constraint given in the source. *Ne* is
also used, with surprised intonation, in exclamatory greetings. *Ala* is the phrase-initial
exclamatory particle used (with or without the enclitic *=(e)n*) as a summation particle, summing up
a situation before the speaker expresses themselves with a reason or resultative clause; like *ne*,
it occurs in its own intonation contour.
