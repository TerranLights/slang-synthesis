**Coverage note — relation to parallel 1997-dissertation extraction (files 001–012):** This is a
different book by the same author (Rubino's 2000 PALI dictionary/grammar vs. his 1997 UCSB
dissertation, extracted in parallel in this same dataset). Expect topical overlap — both cover
Ilocano phonology, pronoun paradigms, and noun pluralization — but the framing, examples, and depth
differ considerably: this dictionary's grammar outline is a compressed, pedagogically-oriented
synopsis "aimed primarily at introducing the fundamentals... that may aid readers in using this
dictionary," explicitly *not* "a thorough representation of the grammar" (the author refers readers
to the 1997 dissertation for that). Per project convention this chunk is extracted in full
regardless of the redundancy; places where content closely echoes what the parallel dissertation
files likely also cover are flagged briefly in the Notes column rather than skipped.

**Coverage note — density:** Like the Hawaiian grammar chapters previously extracted, this
dictionary's Phonology section is extremely vocabulary-dense: nearly every phonological rule and
exception is illustrated with multiple glossed example words (frequently given with IPA
transcription), making it read almost like an illustrated mini-lexicon. Every distinct glossed item
is captured below, organized by the book's own section numbering for navigability. Compact
grammatical paradigm tables (pronoun sets, the actor×patient portmanteau-enclitic grid, etc.) are
reproduced in their own non-12-column tables immediately after the relevant subsection, following
the convention used for Hawaiian's numbered Tables 1–7. Fully bare affix lists with no glosses
(the alphabetized Prefix/Suffix/Infix/Enclitic Cross-Reference List, printed pp. xviii–xxi, several
hundred unglossed forms) are **not** reproduced as vocabulary rows, since the 12-column format
requires a real gloss and this list supplies none (it is a pure word-search index into the main
dictionary) — it is instead summarized narratively with a short representative sample, per the
no-placeholder-residue guardrail. Repeated re-use of an already-glossed word for a different
illustrative point (e.g. *siák* 'I' appears under three different phonological rules) is kept as
separate rows, since each occurrence illustrates a distinct rule, matching the Hawaiian convention.

### Front Matter — Dedication, Acknowledgments, Introduction, Orthography, Dictionary Methodology (printed pp. [unfolioed]–xv)

The dedication page (unfolioed, between the copyright and contents pages) gives an English
dedication followed by a parallel Ilocano dedication naming the same people; a handful of glossable
words are drawn from it below. The Acknowledgments page (p. ix) is almost entirely in English,
except for one isolated interjection of thanks rendered in Tagalog rather than Ilocano — *"Maraming
salamat"* ('thank you very much') — a small code-switching data point (the author, a UC Santa
Barbara/ANU-trained linguist writing primarily in English, reaches for the national-language
Tagalog phrase rather than an Ilocano equivalent at that one spot); not included as a vocabulary row
since it is not Ilocano. The Introduction (pp. xi–xii) is historiographic/sociolinguistic prose (the
Cordilleran language family tree, Ilocano's ~9 million speakers, its role as a lingua franca in
northern Luzon, and its diaspora communities including Hawaiʻi) with no new glossed vocabulary.
"The Dictionary" section (pp. xiii–xiv) discusses the book's root-based methodology and incidentally
glosses two morphologically related items. "A Note on the Orthography" (pp. xiii–xv) is the richest
vein in the front matter: it walks through the old Spanish-based vs. modern standardized spelling
systems with two runs of paired example words.

### Phonology — §1 The Consonants (printed pp. xxiii–xxvii)

Ilocano has fifteen original contrastive consonant phonemes, one loan consonant /h/, and two complex
affricates that arise from palatalization or borrowing, set out in a stops/fricatives/affricates/
lateral/tap-trill/glides/nasals chart by place of articulation (labial, dental, alveolar,
alveopalatal, palatal, velar, glottal) — reproduced below as a compact grammar table, followed by
the glossed example words for each subsection (§1.1 Stops through §1.7 Palatalization).

### Phonology — §4 Glottal Stop (printed pp. xxxv–xxxvi)

The glottal stop /ʔ/ occurs syllable-initially or as an onset variant of /k/ or /t/ before another
consonant, and may also occur in coda position (unlike in Tagalog, which bars word-final glottal
stop). It is never written word-initially (every vowel-initial word has an implicit glottal onset);
word-medially it is written with a hyphen.

### Grammar Outline — §1 Pronouns (printed pp. xli–xlv)

The Grammar Outline (p.xli) opens with a synopsis for linguists summarizing Ilocano as a
predicate-initial, head-marking, highly prefixing Philippine-type language with ergative, absolutive,
and oblique pronoun case, a first-person-plural inclusive/exclusive distinction, enclitic core
pronouns and a ligature particle *nga/a* (§12, not reached in this chunk). §1 Pronouns covers five
pronoun sets: independent absolutive (§1.1), enclitic absolutive =ak-series (§1.2), ergative enclitic
=ko-series (§1.3), oblique (§1.4), independent possessive (§1.5), and reflexive (§1.6).
