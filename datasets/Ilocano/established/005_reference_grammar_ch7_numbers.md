**Coverage note:** Chapter 7 is Ilocano's complete numeral system: cardinals, the decimal group-noun
system, ordinals, the *sanga-* 'one unit of' prefix (with its *-an* nominalizing-measurement

verbalized and multiplicative sub-forms), the *ka- -an* 'increase to N' affix, grouping numbers,

chapter is captured below, organized by section to mirror the source's own structure (all tables
share the same 12-column format). Full interlinear example sentences are included as rows (Part of
Speech = sentence/phrase) since Rubino uses them to demonstrate each affix in context.

**Flagship finding — Spanish-numeral layering (§7.11–7.16):** Ilocano maintains **two parallel,
functionally-specialized numeral systems** rather than one with the other as a marginal variant.
Native Ilocano numerals (*maysa, dua, tallo...*) are obligatory for forming multiplicatives,
ordinals, distributives, and other numeral-derivational morphology, and are preferred for small
round prices. Spanish-derived numerals (*uno, dos, tres...*, from the Spanish colonial period) are
obligatory for telling time (*alas* + Spanish number), used for the date and year, and preferred
for ages over ten and large quantities/prices — with even the connecting ligature rules differing
by which system is used (native numbers require the ligature *(ng)a* before the counted noun or
monetary unit; Spanish numbers take no ligature). Spanish ordinals additionally retain **Spanish
grammatical gender agreement** when modifying Spanish-loan nouns (*segunda mano*, *primera klase*,
*tersera klase*) even though no gender-agreement system exists natively in Ilocano — a borrowed
agreement-pattern fragment, not just borrowed vocabulary. This is a clean, register-stratified
contact-linguistics case: the colonial-source system was not merely added to the lexicon but
captured specific semantic domains (time, date, large currency) wholesale, in parallel with a fully
intact native numeral-derivation system used everywhere else.

### 7.7 — Multiplicatives (pp.176–178)

Formed by adding the prefix *mami(n)-* (past *nami(n)-*) to the numerical stem (same phonological
alternations as *maika-*). *Mami-/mamin-* become *maminpi-/maminpin-* for limiting multiplicatives
('only N times'; perfective *naminpi-/naminpin-*). The multiplicative ordinal uses *kapami(n)-*
(nominalizing, takes the *=ko* pronoun series); a regional variant is *pakamin-*. *Nakapamin-* is
used with the same numeric roots but yields an adjectival-type meaning, linked with ligature *(ng)a*
rather than nominalized.
