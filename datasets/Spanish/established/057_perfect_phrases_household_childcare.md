# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Phrases: Perfect Phrases in Spanish for Household Maintenance and Child Care (full book)

**Source:** Jean Yates, *Perfect Phrases in Spanish for Household Maintenance and Child Care: 500+
Essential Words and Phrases for Communicating with Spanish-Speakers* (McGraw-Hill, 2008), 178 pp.
Employer-facing phrasebook for US/Canadian households hiring Spanish-speaking domestic help and
child-care workers. Five chapters (1. Spanish Basics — greetings, hiring/firing, wages, schedules;
2. Establishing Basic Policies — home rules, transportation, emergencies, meals, breaks; 3. General
Cleaning Instructions — rooms, priorities, pests; 4. Specific Projects Around the House — kitchen,
bathroom, living room, dining room, bedrooms, closets, laundry, home office; 5. Child Care — safety,
discipline, babies, toddlers/older children) plus a numbers appendix and bidirectional
English-Spanish/Spanish-English glossary (pp.121-178).

**Text layer:** confirmed via `pdftotext`, clean extraction, no OCR artifacts. Transcription
Confidence: verified.

**Page-count/scope reasoning:** 178 pages / ~23,100 words. Unlike the narrative "Perfect Phrases ...
Travel to Mexico" title in this same series, this book is a dense, list-format vocabulary reference
(explicitly subtitled "500+ Essential Words and Phrases") with minimal narrative cultural commentary
— most content is substitutable-word lists under topic headings (e.g., a base sentence with a
column of swappable nouns/verbs) rather than extended prose. This format is highly tractable for
full coverage: every distinct headword/phrase across all five chapters and the glossary's unique
terms (beyond what chapters 1-5 already cover) is captured below. Phonetic transcriptions are
omitted (mechanical pronunciation aids, not lexical content); terms are grouped by the book's own
subsection headings.

**Domestic-register framing — this entire book is explicitly domestic/employer-employee register
content** (housekeeping, childcare, employment-of-domestic-staff), addressed from the US employer's
side to a Spanish-speaking employee, in the formal usted register throughout (the book explicitly
states this is a deliberate respect-signaling choice). Usage Tier marks the relevant technical
register (technical.household / technical.childcare / technical.employment) rather than flattening
to "core."
