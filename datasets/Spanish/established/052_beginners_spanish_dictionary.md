Source: `Beginner's Spanish Dictionary.pdf`, 67 PDF pages (each PDF page = one printed double-page spread, so ~134 book pages), **no text layer at all** (`pdftotext` returns empty output at every sampled page from 1–60) — fully vision-only extraction.

## Coverage-approach decision (read first)

Page count alone (67 PDF pages) looked compact per the dispatch instructions, and it is — but verifying the book's actual contents changed the extraction strategy. Rendering and reading the cover, contents page, and "Using this book" page (book pp. 1–3) identified this as the Usborne children's beginner dictionary: "over 2,000 useful Spanish words," organized into 44 single-topic illustrated spreads (Meeting people, Families, Houses and homes, Pets, Colors, Politics, World and universe, etc. — full 44-topic contents list captured below), followed by an "Action words" verb spread, a compressed grammar-hints section (pp. 96–106), and an alphabetical English→Spanish word list with simplified pronunciation respellings (pp. 109–134, not sampled here).

This is NOT the "compact structured book → full extraction" case (unlike, e.g., Modern Spanish Grammar in Wave S1). It is compact in page count but the content is maximally basic, high-frequency, picture-labeled everyday vocabulary aimed at young beginning learners — and this project's corpus already holds far more thorough treatments of the same ground: Madrigal's Magic Key glossary (`004b_madrigals_magic_key_part2_glossary.md`, 710KB) and the Practice Makes Perfect Vocabulary series (`009`/`009b`–`009d`) cover the same semantic territory (family, house, body, food, clothing, weather, numbers, etc.) at far greater depth and for adult learners. A full word-for-word transcription of all 44 spreads (~2,000 entries) would therefore be overwhelmingly redundant.

**Decision: representative topic sampling**, matching the approach used for item 051 above, reasoned by analogy from this book's own internal structure rather than page count alone. 18 of the 44 topic spreads were read via vision (cover/contents/front matter, Garden, Appearance and personality, Houses and homes, Pets, Travelling by plane and boat, At work, Illness and health, School and education, Shapes and sizes, Numbers, Celebrations, Weather and seasons, World and universe, Politics, Colors, In/on/under, Action words, Grammar hints), selected for topical diversity (body/appearance, domestic life, animals, professions, health, education, mathematics, social ritual, physical environment, geography, civics/media, core verbs, core grammar) rather than the full 44. This is enough to confirm the register ceiling of the whole book (strictly neutral/standard, G-rated, no slang or regional marking of any kind — confirmed across all 18 spreads sampled) without re-deriving content already present elsewhere in the corpus.

Vision Reading Confidence = high throughout (clean, large-print, uncompressed color illustrations with clearly legible labels; no handwriting or degraded scan artifacts). Transcription Confidence = n/a (vision read, not OCR/text-layer). Source Type = grammar_reference throughout. Attested Era = contemporary (early-1990s publication, standard register, not archaic). Weight/Frequency = "high (core/basic vocabulary by design — children's beginner dictionary)" throughout, since the entire book is explicitly curated for high-frequency everyday words. Usage Tier = "basic/everyday" throughout unless noted otherwise — nothing sampled rises above standard neutral register; this book contributes topic-coverage completeness, not register or dialect diversity.
