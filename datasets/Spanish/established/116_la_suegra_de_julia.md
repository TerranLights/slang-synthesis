## What this book is

A B1-level "novela didáctica" (prologue, p.3, states this explicitly: "destinada a todos aquellos
estudiantes adultos de español... que ya han llegado al nivel B1"), written specifically to combine
literary fiction with language pedagogy — one author (Eulàlia Solé) is a novelist/journalist, the
other (Silvia López Ripoll) teaches Spanish as a foreign language at the Universitat de Barcelona. Four
chapters follow Julia and her eccentric mother-in-law Aurora across several decades, from Julia's
teens (meeting Aurora at 18) through Aurora's old age and death in a retirement home. Light comic tone
built around Aurora's quirks ("extravagancias," "originalidades").

**Setting:** unnamed Spanish city (Peninsular Spain; no specific city named in sampled text, but
register and institutional references — e.g. "médico de familia," "ambulatorios," "tarjeta sanitaria"
— are distinctly Spain's public-healthcare-system vocabulary, not Latin American). Contemporary-set,
spanning "más de noventa años" of Aurora's life, implying a multi-decade timeline ending near the
book's early-2010s publication.

## Pedagogical apparatus found — genuine, multi-layered

1. **In-text numbered footnotes** glossing colloquial/idiomatic vocabulary as it occurs (same style as
   *La Celestina* and *Doce a las doce*): *tirabuzones*, *cachivaches*, *extravagancias*, *en su regazo*,
   *ocurrencias*, *un gran alivio*, *alicientes*, *bien plantado*, *como un topo* (idiom), *pánfila*.
2. **Post-chapter "Comprensión Lectora" exercise blocks** (fill-in-the-blank cloze passages using word
   banks) — these surface clustered, thematically organized vocabulary sets: a full **wedding-vocabulary
   cluster** (Ch.1) and an **illness/healthcare-system vocabulary cluster** + **hobby/leisure
   vocabulary cluster** + **employment-status idiom cluster** (Ch.3-4, recovered via the Solucionario
   answer key since the cloze-exercise pages themselves were not sampled for Ch.2-3).
3. **A complete "Solucionario a los ejercicios didácticos"** (pp.65-68) answering every chapter's
   exercises — used here to recover vocabulary sets whose source cloze-page text was not directly
   sampled, same approach used for *La Celestina*'s Solucionario.
