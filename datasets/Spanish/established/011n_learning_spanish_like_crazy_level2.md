## Grammar points

### "lo importante/lo mejor/lo necesario es que" + subjunctive (continued from Lección 56)

Lección 57 extends Lección 56's infinitive-vs-subjunctive contrast one step further: the neuter
"lo importante/lo mejor/lo necesario es que..." construction takes the **subjunctive** when a
specific person's action is named ("lo importante es que comas saludable" — the important thing is
that you eat healthy), completing the pattern only partially shown in Lección 56 (which drilled the
plain infinitive form of the same neuter construction, "lo mejor es vivir en el exterior," with no
named subject). Together the two lessons give the full four-way contrast: es importante/es mejor/es
necesario + infinitive (general); es importante/es mejor/es necesario + que + subjunctive (specific
subject); lo importante/lo mejor/lo necesario + es + infinitive (general, neuter-noun-phrase variant);
lo importante/lo mejor/lo necesario + es que + subjunctive (specific subject, neuter-noun-phrase
variant). (Lección 57, pp. 1-4.)

### Subjunctive triggered by temporal/adverbial conjunctions: hasta que, antes de que, cuando, después de que

Lección 58 introduces the "adverbial clause" subjunctive trigger: when a temporal conjunction (hasta
que — until, antes de que — before, cuando — when, después de que — after) introduces a clause
describing an action that hasn't yet happened / is pending relative to the main clause, the
subordinate verb takes the subjunctive: "esperamos hasta que reparen el carro" (we wait until they
fix the car), "no puedo depositar el cheque hasta que él lo firme" (I can't deposit the check until
he signs it), "antes de que tenga dieciocho años" (before he turns eighteen). The source does not
explicitly contrast this against a completed/habitual-time indicative use of the same conjunctions
(e.g. "cuando llega, como" — indicative, a routine event) — every example drilled in this lesson
describes a pending/future-oriented action, so the indicative-vs-subjunctive split for "cuando" itself
is implicit in the data rather than stated as a rule. (Lección 58, throughout.)
