## Grammar points

### Condicional perfecto (conditional perfect) — party/event vocabulary — Lecciones 61–62

Same tense as documented in `012b` for the `Leccion##-final` series (`habría/habrías/habría/
habríamos/habrían` + past participle, "would have done X"), but this series introduces it through an
entirely different dialogue (Isabel/Pedro, about a disappointing quinceañera party) and a different
vocabulary set (party/event planning: quinceañera, discoteca, pista de baile, vestido, adorno) rather
than the alcohol/food themes used in the `Leccion##-final` series' conditional-perfect lessons. Confirms
this really is separate lesson content, not a duplicate recording of the same script.

### Presente perfecto de subjuntivo (present perfect subjunctive), -ar verbs — university vocabulary — Lección 63

Paraphrase (p.4–8): a new tense, the present perfect subjunctive, formed with the present subjunctive of
`haber` (haya/hayas/haya/hayamos/hayan) + past participle, used after subjunctive-triggering expressions
of doubt, hope, surprise, or value judgment ("espero que hayas terminado," "dudo que ella haya
descansado," "es necesario que usted haya estudiado," "me sorprende que me hayas llamado"). Introduced
via a new René/María dialogue about reuniting after medical school, and drilled with `-ar`-class verbs
(llamar, descansar, estudiar) plus university-application vocabulary (carrera, notas, maestría, beca).

### Presente perfecto de subjuntivo — continued, more subjunctive triggers — Lección 64

Continues the same tense with more `-ar` verbs and subjunctive triggers (`es bueno que`, `es increíble
que`, `me alegro que`, `es posible que`), plus vocabulary for scholarship applications and academic
achievement (solicitar, graduarse, lograr, colegio, memorizar).

### Presente perfecto de subjuntivo — circus/leisure vocabulary — Lección 65

Continues the same tense into a new thematic dialogue (Diana/Juan at the circus), still using the
`haya/hayas/haya/hayamos/hayan` + participle pattern with subjunctive triggers (`ojalá que`, `es
probable que`, `es imposible que`, `espero que`), now applied to reflexive-form participles too (`se
haya comido`, `se haya caído`) — the lesson explicitly calls out the reflexive "comer" (`se haya comido`,
lit. "has eaten UP") as paralleling the English intensifying particle "up."
