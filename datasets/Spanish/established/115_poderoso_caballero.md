## What this book is

A detective novella in diary format, "Serie Lola Lago, detective" — first in a series by the same
author team (Lourdes Miquel / Neus Sans) responsible for *Doce a las doce* (established/119), published
by the same house (Difusión, with Instituto Cervantes backing). Title is a quotation from Francisco de
Quevedo's famous satirical poem "Poderoso caballero es don Dinero" ("A powerful gentleman is Sir
Money"), epigraph given on the title page — a literary allusion signaling the novel's money/greed theme
(inheritance fraud) before the story even starts.

**Setting: Madrid, Spain** (Plaza de la Paja, Casa de Campo, Paseo de la Castellana, calle de Serrano,
La Moraleja, El Rastro — all named, real Madrid locations). Protagonist Lola Lago runs a small detective
agency with two male partners/employees, Paco and Miguel, plus a secretary (Margarita) and errand boy
(Feliciano). Plot: a multimillionaire's apparent suicide is actually murder-by-fraud engineered by his
son to seize the inheritance. Diary-entry dates run March-June (unspecified year, consistent with an
early-1990s first edition per the copyright page: 1st ed. 1991).

## Pedagogical apparatus found

**40 numbered "Notas explicativas"** (pp.47-51) — the richest apparatus feature, explaining Madrid
geography, Spanish cultural institutions, and — unusually for this wave — **colloquial/slang
expressions explicitly glossed as slang register** (*pasta* = money, *ligar* = to pick up/flirt,
*estar como una cabra* = to be crazy, *mecachis* = a euphemistic swear, *niño bien* = spoiled rich kid,
*frío/caliente* as a children's guessing-game convention). This is a genuine slang-relevant find
distinct from most of this wave's thinner readers. No in-text footnote markers were observed on sampled
body pages (unlike *La Celestina*'s per-page footnotes) — all 40 notes are keyed by superscript number
only and collected at the back.

Setting/register: contemporary (early-1990s) Peninsular Spanish, conversational/informal diary-prose
register with substantial direct-speech dialogue carrying additional colloquialisms (terms of address
like *nena*, *muñeca*, *hija* used ironically/affectionately between the female detective and her male
colleagues).
