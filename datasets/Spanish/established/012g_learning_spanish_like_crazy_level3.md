## Grammar points

### "Para" — its seven core uses (Clase 009)

Paraphrase: the instructor opens by warning that `por` and `para` both often translate as English "for,"
but choosing the wrong one changes meaning and can cause real misunderstanding — illustrated with the
minimal pair `yo cocino para mi hijo` ("I cook for my son," i.e., he receives the food) vs. `yo cocino
por mi hijo` ("I cook in his place/instead of him," e.g., because he's a sick chef) and `el libro fue
escrito por Juan` ("Juan is the author") vs. `...para Juan` ("someone else wrote it, for Juan/at Juan's
request"). `Para`'s uses taught here:

1. **Destination** (literal or figurative) — "this rag is for the kitchen" → `este trapo es para la
   cocina`.
2. **Recipient of an action** — "these chocolates are for my wife" → `estos chocolates son para mi
   esposa`.
3. **Final destination** vs. an intermediate stop (which instead takes `por`, covered in Clase 012
   below) — "we are going to Colombia [as our destination]" → `vamos para Colombia`.
4. **Objective of one's studies/profession** — "she is studying to be a doctor" → `ella estudia para ser
   médica` (Clase 011 adds: it's actually more natural to just name the program of study, e.g. `ella
   estudia medicina`, than to use this construction).
5. **Deadline/time limit** — `para` + infinitive-adjacent noun phrase, translatable as "by," "on," "for,"
   or "before" — "I need this by Monday" → `necesito esto para el lunes`.
6. **Purpose**, formula `para` + infinitive, translatable as "for" — "food is for eating" → `la comida es
   para comer` (Clase 011 adds: Spanish never conjugates a verb after a preposition, which is precisely
   why this construction always uses the infinitive rather than a gerund/progressive form the way English
   "for eating" might suggest).
7. **Purpose of an action ("in order to")**, same `para` + infinitive formula — "I sleep in order to
   rest" → `yo duermo para descansar`.
8. **Comparison to a standard** — "it's cold for June" → `hace frío para ser junio`; "the boy is smart
   for his age" → `el niño es inteligente para su edad`.
9. **Expressing an opinion** — "for me, Colombia is a beautiful country" → `para mí, Colombia es un país
   hermoso`.

### "Por" — its uses (Clases 011–012, drilled against "para" as contrast pairs)

Paraphrase, building the contrast case-by-case against the `para` uses above:

1. **Duration of time** — "we are going to practice for 2 hours" → `vamos a practicar por dos horas`.
2. **An unspecified span within a 24-hour period** — "on Sunday mornings" → `los domingos por la
   mañana`; "during the afternoon" → `por la tarde`.
3. **Rate/frequency expressions** (times per day/month, or a percentage) — "four books per month" →
   `cuatro libros por mes`.
4. **Exchange or trade** — "we paid $3 for that magazine" → `pagamos tres dólares por esa revista`; "she
   exchanged her dress for a blouse" → `cambió su vestido por una blusa`.
5. **"On behalf of" / "in place of" / "instead of"** — contrasted directly with the `para` reading of the
   same sentence: `Miguel va a cantar por mí` ("Miguel will sing in my place," e.g. because illness has
   cost the speaker their voice) vs. `Miguel va a cantar para mí` ("Miguel will sing for my enjoyment").
6. **Gratitude/thanks** — "thanks for the chocolates" → `gracias por los chocolates`.
7. **"Because of" / "due to"** — "I didn't study this morning because of being sick" → `no estudié esta
   mañana por estar enfermo`.
8. **Means of transportation or how one travels** — "we are going to Cuba by sea" → `vamos a Cuba por
   mar` (Natalia adds a further set: `por tierra`, `por aire` for "by land"/"by air" — but not `por taxi`,
   which would be wrong; a specific vehicle takes a different construction).
9. **Means of sending a message/information** — "write me by email" → `escríbeme por correo` (the source
   notes `por correo`, `por correo electrónico`, and the loanword `por email`/`por mail` are all heard);
   "call me by cellphone" → `llámame por celular`.
10. **Intermediate/temporary stop en route to a final destination** — directly contrasted with `para`'s
    final-destination sense: "we pass through Colombia on the trip to Argentina" → `pasamos por Colombia
    en el viaje a Argentina` (Colombia is a layover, not the destination) vs. `vamos para Colombia`
    (Colombia itself is the destination).
11. **Expressing an emotion directed at someone/something** — "Carolina has a lot of affection for her
    dog" → `Carolina tiene mucho cariño por su perro`; "respect for her friends" → `respeto por sus
    amigos` — explicitly contrasted with `para`'s "opinion" use (#9 in the `para` list above): an
    *emotion* about someone/something takes `por`, an *opinion* about something takes `para`.
12. A closing list of fixed idiomatic `por`-phrases the source calls out as common beyond the
    grammatical rules above: `por aquí` ("around here"), `por lo general` ("generally"), `por lo menos`
    ("at least"), `por primera vez` ("for the first time"), `por todas partes` ("everywhere"), plus `por
    supuesto`, `por ejemplo`, `por eso`, `por favor`.

This three-file unit is the single most systematic piece of pure-grammar exposition found anywhere in
this Level III set — most other lessons teach grammar incidentally alongside a vocabulary theme, whereas
these three are dedicated entirely to one contrastive grammatical distinction, drilled with matched
minimal-pair examples throughout.
