## Grammar points

### Irregular future stems: tener, venir, salir, decir, hacer, saber

Lección 45 catalogs six high-frequency verbs whose future tense is built on a modified stem (rather
than the bare infinitive drilled in Lección 44), each still taking the regular future endings
(-é/-ás/-á/-emos/-án): tener→tendr- (tendré), venir→vendr- (vendré), salir→saldr- (saldré),
decir→dir- (diré), hacer→har- (haré), saber→sabr- (sabré). This is a clean, complete single-lesson
catalog of the most common irregular future stems, directly complementing Lección 44's regular
paradigm. (Lección 45, throughout.)

### "Para" + infinitive to express purpose ("in order to")

Lección 46's first half drills "para" + infinitive as a purpose clause answering "para qué" (what
for): "fui al aeropuerto para tomar el avión" (I went to the airport to take the plane), "vamos a ir
al bar para beber una cerveza" (we're going to go to the bar to drink a beer). One example
("fuimos a la peluquería para que nos peinen," we went to the hair salon to get our hair blow-dried)
shows "para que" + subjunctive rather than plain "para" + infinitive, used specifically because the
purpose-action's subject differs from the main clause's subject (the stylists, not "we," do the
styling) — the source flags this construction's literal sense ("for them to blow-dry our hair")
without formally naming the subject-switch rule, but the contrast with the plain "para + infinitive"
examples earlier in the same lesson is clear from the data itself. (Lección 46, pp. 1-6.)

### "Hay que" + infinitive: impersonal obligation ("one must / it is necessary to")

The lesson's second half introduces "hay que" + infinitive as an impersonal, subject-less obligation
construction, contrasted directly against the personal "tener que" + infinitive already known from
earlier lessons: "tengo que cancelar la reunión" (I have to cancel the meeting, personal) vs. "hay
que cancelar la reunión" (one must cancel the meeting / it is necessary to cancel the meeting,
impersonal). Drilled across a range of topics (rent, passports, driver's licenses, fitness,
scheduling appointments) with the interrogative "¿qué hay que hacer para...?" (what do you have to
do to...?) as the recurring question frame. (Lección 46, pp. 6-12.)
