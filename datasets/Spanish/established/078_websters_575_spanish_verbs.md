# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Webster's New World 575+ Spanish Verbs (REPRESENTATIVE SAMPLE, not exhaustive)

**Source:** Elsa Pittman, *Webster's New World™ 575+ Spanish Verbs* (Wiley Publishing, 2004), 722
pages. Full structure: Introduction (pp. 1–4); Verb Usage Review (pp. 5–64: subject pronouns, verb
basics, the 5 simple + 5 compound indicative tenses, the 2 simple + 2 compound subjunctive tenses
and trigger verbs, imperative mood, progressive tenses, reflexive verbs, special-construction verbs
[gustar/doler/faltar], infinitives, passive voice); alphabetical Verb Charts A–Z (pp. 65–651: 575
verbs, each with full conjugation table across all moods/tenses + an EXAMPLES section + an
IDIOMATIC EXAMPLE(S) section); Appendix A, "Appendix of Additional Verbs" (pp. 653–690: ~1,500
more verbs, each given only an English gloss plus a page cross-reference to the model verb it
conjugates like — no full conjugation or examples); Appendix B, "Appendix of Irregular Verb Forms"
(pp. 691–700); English-Spanish Verb Index (pp. 701–722). This source has a genuine clean text
layer (`pdftotext -layout`, confirmed via direct sampling — no font-substitution or scrambling
artifacts detected); no vision-reading was required.

**REPRESENTATIVE SAMPLING METHODOLOGY (per dispatch instructions — this is NOT an exhaustive
extraction):** This book is, as expected, a large reference-style "fully conjugated verbs"
dictionary of exactly the kind this project already handles via non-exhaustive sampling for its
other large dictionaries (Korean's 962pp, Hawaiian's 3,695pp, and this language's own
`established/047_mcgraw_hill_diccionario_enciclopedico.md`-scale Wave 2 dictionary). Bulk
transcription of all 575 full conjugation tables would be almost entirely redundant with
conjugation-paradigm material already established in `007_...`, `008_...`, `074_...`, `075_...`,
and `076_...`. Per the dispatch brief, sampling was targeted at exactly the three things a bulk
dictionary like this can uniquely contribute: **(1) irregular/high-frequency verbs** — sampled 15
of the highest-frequency, most idiomatically rich verb entries across the alphabet (tener, hacer,
dar, echar, pedir, preguntar, tocar, poner, ir, venir, decir, llevar, volver, ser, estar) for their
IDIOMATIC EXAMPLE(S) sections, which this book attaches to nearly every entry but which no other
Spanish `established/` file has captured at this granularity; **(2) usage notes distinguishing
near-synonym verbs** — the Verb Usage Review's explicit saber/conocer/querer/poder
preterit-vs-imperfect meaning-shift table, a crisp, well-organized treatment of a classic Spanish
learner confusion point; **(3) regional/register annotations** — the book's vosotros-vs-Uds. usage
notes (consistent with, not contradicting, established/075's fuller tuteo/voseo treatment). Full
conjugation tables, the ~1,500-entry Appendix A gloss list, and the English-Spanish index were
sampled only to confirm their format/content type and are NOT tabulated as vocabulary — they are
structurally identical in kind to material already fully established elsewhere in this corpus
(conjugation paradigms) or are bare gloss lists without grammar/usage content worth capturing
individually. The book's "special-construction verbs" section (gustar, doler, faltar) duplicates
`established/076_...`'s fuller reverse-construction-verb treatment and was not re-tabulated; see
that file instead.

**Entry count for this sampled file:** 4 cross-cutting Verb Usage Review grammar points + 15
sampled high-frequency verbs' idiomatic expressions (several verbs contributing 2 idioms each) =
19 vocabulary/grammar rows total, drawn from a 575-entry (+1,500 Appendix A) source.

**Relationship to other Spanish `established/` files:** This file supplements, rather than
duplicates, the conjugation-paradigm coverage already in `007_...`/`008_...`/`074_...`/`075_...`.
Its saber/conocer/querer/poder table is the first explicit treatment of this specific
four-verb meaning-shift phenomenon in the Spanish corpus (the irregular-verbs book, 007, lists
these verbs' paradigms but does not discuss the preterit/imperfect meaning contrast). Its sampled
idiomatic expressions are drawn from real example sentences rather than a bare phrase list, giving
them full-sentence context unlike a phrasebook entry.
