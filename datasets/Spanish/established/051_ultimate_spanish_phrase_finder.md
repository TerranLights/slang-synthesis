## Coverage-approach decision (read first)

**Structure check performed before committing to an approach, per dispatch instructions.** The front matter (Contents, pp. iii–xi) shows this is a true bidirectional dictionary, not a topic/chapter phrasebook: Spanish–English runs pp. 1–475, English–Spanish runs pp. 475–960. It is alphabetically ordered within each direction, dictionary-scale (roughly 960 pages of raw entries), with no chapter/situational organization comparable to what made "Modern Spanish Grammar" (Wave S1) compact enough for full extraction. This is exactly the "960pp of raw entries, true alphabetical/dictionary-scale reference" case flagged in the dispatch — **representative sampling, not full extraction, is the correct approach here.**

However, unlike a plain word-for-word bilingual dictionary, this book is unusually rich in register-marked idiomatic material. The preface (p. x) states the author deliberately uses field-label annotations — `coloquial`, `jerga`, `argot juvenil`, `despectivo`, `arcaico`, `subido de tono` ("risqué/off-color"), plus regional tags (`Esp.`, `Mex.`, `Arg.`, `Chi.`, `Uru.`, `Ven.`, `Bol.`, `L.am.`) — specifically to flag register and regional variation (field-label key on p. xi). A full-text pass (`pdftotext -layout`, ~1.22M words extracted) found roughly: 365 `coloquial`-tagged entries, 199 `subido de tono` (vulgar/risqué), 38 `jerga`, 28 `arcaico`, 616 `(Sp.)`/457 `(Esp.)`, 130 `(Mex.)`/59 `(Méx.)`, 61 `(Arg.)`, 7 `(Chi.)`, 4 `(Uru.)`, 2 `(Ven.)`, 1 `(Bol.)`, and 6 `despectivo`. This is precisely the non-redundant content this project needs (register tiers, regional minimal pairs, vulgarity gradients) and it is concentrated in a small, greppable fraction of the book's raw bulk — so the sampling strategy used below is **targeted at register/regional-marked entries specifically**, rather than a blind every-Nth-page sample. This captures the distinctive signal at a small fraction of the reading cost of linear extraction, while deliberately skipping the large mass of plain denotative translation pairs (which duplicate content already covered by the Madrigal's Magic Key glossary at `004b`, 710KB, and the Practice Makes Perfect vocabulary series).

**Caveat on transcription fidelity:** this PDF uses a two-column-per-page layout. `pdftotext -layout` occasionally interleaves text from adjacent columns on the same output line when columns don't align cleanly, and the plain (non `-layout`) full-text dump reflows multi-line entries further. Entries below were selected where the grep context was self-contained and unambiguous; a handful of entries with visibly truncated or cross-column-contaminated context were discarded rather than guessed at. Where an entry's full gloss could not be confirmed with certainty, Transcription Confidence is marked "moderate" with the reason noted.

Source Type = grammar_reference for all rows. Vision Reading Confidence = n/a throughout (text-layer extraction only). Weight/Frequency is "unspecified (dictionary entry, no corpus frequency data in source)" throughout since this is a reference dictionary, not a corpus.
