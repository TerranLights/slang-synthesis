## STRUCTURAL FINDING: the shape of "advanced" is re-practice + 7 genuinely new topics, not more subjunctive triggers

Where established/066 (Intermedio) spent roughly two-thirds of its 32 temas building the subjunctive system outward trigger-by-trigger, this Avanzado volume's 22 temas split cleanly into two kinds of unit:

**(a) "Práctica de..." re-drill units (temas 5, 6, 10, 11, 13, 15)** that take constructions already fully taught in Intermedio (oraciones finales, de relativo, condicionales, comparación, consecutivas, temporales) and give them harder, longer, more idiomatic practice sentences — without introducing new rules for most of them. The one exception is tema 10 (oraciones condicionales), which *does* add new grammar (see below).

**(b) Seven genuinely new topics not covered at either prior level:**
1. **Tema 1, la pasiva y la impersonalidad** — passive voice (SER+participio+POR) and two impersonal alternatives (SE+active verb; bare 3rd-person-plural verb), explicitly framed as register-conditioned: "La forma pasiva se emplea principalmente en el lenguaje periodístico y en narraciones históricas" — i.e., the book explicitly marks true passive as a written/journalistic register feature rather than a spoken-Spanish default, with SE-impersonal/3pl-impersonal as the ordinary conversational alternatives for the same communicative need.
2. **Tema 8, VOLVERSE/QUEDARSE/PONERSE/HACERSE/LLEGAR A SER/CONVERTIRSE EN** — a six-term "become"-verb system, graded by type and radicality of change (volverse=qualitative transformation, quedarse=post-process result/often negative, ponerse=momentary mood/health shift, hacerse=gradual ideological/professional/religious change via the subject's agency, llegar a ser=achievement-scale upward change, convertirse en=radical transformation, used with nouns).
3. **Tema 10 extension, Pretérito Pluscuamperfecto de Subjuntivo + Condicional Compuesto** — hubiera/hubiese+participio and habría+participio, completing the third/counterfactual-past conditional (*Si hubiera ahorrado lo suficiente, no tendría que pedir un préstamo* / *Si hubiera tenido tiempo, habría ido a tu casa*) — this is exactly the gap flagged in established/066's cross-reference note as missing from Intermedio, confirming the predicted 3-volume escalation (Elemental: no subjunctive at all → Intermedio: present + imperfect subjunctive → Avanzado: + pluperfect subjunctive, completing the full conditional-tense paradigm).
4. **Tema 12, perífrasis verbales** — a systematic catalog of 10+ aspectual periphrases organized by the non-finite form they govern (infinitive/gerund/participle): ponerse a, empezar a, echar a (inceptive); volver a (repetitive); venir a, llegar a, acabar de, dejar de (terminative/resultative); deber (obligation) vs. deber de (epistemic hypothesis — a lexical minimal pair distinguished only by the preposition); ir/venir+gerundio (gradual/frequent); llevar/seguir+gerundio (durative continuity); llevar/tener+participio (accumulated-result constructions, e.g. *llevo tres temas hechos*).
5. **Tema 17 extension, dativo de involuntariedad (se+le-type "accidental event" construction)** — the SE+indirect-object-pronoun construction for presenting an event as happening TO the experiencer rather than being caused BY them (*Se me rompió el jarrón* = "the vase broke on me," foregrounding non-culpability), illustrated via minimal pairs against the plain reflexive (*Ayer rompí el jarrón* = I broke it / *El jarrón se rompió en el traslado* = it broke / *Se me rompió el jarrón cuando le quitaba el polvo* = it broke on me). Also notes a list of verbs whose meaning changes with vs. without a reflexive/dative pronoun (quedar/quedarse, sentir/sentirse, faltar/faltarle, parecer/parecerse), and the exceptional non-reflexive pronoun consigo and tú-after-según.
6. **Tema 20, formación de palabras (derivational morphology)** — the only explicit word-formation unit in the entire 3-volume series: adjective-forming suffixes (-ble, -oso, -ivo, -al/-ales, -ico, -nte), the negative-prefix allomorph selection rule (in-/im-/in-/ir- conditioned by the initial sound of the base: lógico→ilógico, posible→imposible, responsable→irresponsable, cierto→incierto; des- as an alternative negating prefix for many verbs/adjectives: honesto→deshonesto), and abstract-noun-forming suffixes (-cia, -dad/-tad, -ismo, -eza, -ción, -encia, -miento). **This is the single most directly relevant unit in the whole series to the slang-synthesis project's conlang-derivation goals**, since it is the only place any of the three volumes makes Spanish derivational morphology (as opposed to inflectional paradigms) an explicit, systematic object of study.
7. **Tema 22, acentuación** — orthographic stress rules (agudas/llanas/esdrújulas, diptongo/hiato syllabification, compound-word accentuation) plus a compact closed-list of monosyllable diacritic-accent minimal pairs distinguishing homophonous grammatical categories (tú/tu, él/el, sí/si, dé/de, sé/se, mí/mi, té/te) and the rule that all interrogative/exclamative pronouns-adjectives-adverbs carry a tilde even in indirect questions (*No sé cuánto vale*).

## Apéndice (p. 128): the series' most complete sequence-of-tenses table

Where established/066 tema 32 gave a single binary correlation (present-family main clause → present/perfect subjunctive; past-family main clause → imperfect subjunctive), this volume's appendix splits the correlation by **four separate subordinate-clause types**, each with its own timing table:
1. **Regla general** (si-conditionals): Condicional simple → Pretérito Imperfecto de Subjuntivo.
2. **Oraciones finales** (para que...): (a) Presente/Pret.Perfecto/Futuro → Presente de Subjuntivo; (b) Pret.Perfecto/Pret.Indefinido/Pret.Imperfecto/Pret.Pluscuamperfecto → Pretérito Imperfecto de Subjuntivo.
3. **Oraciones adjetivas de relativo**: same two-way split as (2).
4. **Oraciones sustantivas** (me gusta que..., es mejor que..., no creo que...): (a) Presente/Pret.Perfecto → ANY of Presente/Pret.Perfecto/Pret.Imperfecto/Pret.Pluscuamperfecto de Subjuntivo (all four subjunctive tenses are explicitly listed as compatible, unlike the other three clause types); (b) Pret.Perfecto/Pret.Imperfecto/Pret.Indefinido/Pret.Pluscuamperfecto → Pret.Imperfecto/Pret.Pluscuamperfecto de Subjuntivo; (c) Futuro Imperfecto → Presente de Subjuntivo.
This four-way split — rather than one universal rule — is itself a structural finding: the book is implicitly teaching that sequence-of-tenses constraints are tighter for finalidad/relativo clauses (exactly one subjunctive tense fits each main-clause tense) than for sustantiva/opinion clauses (where a Presente main clause tolerates all four subjunctive tenses, since the subordinate event's own time reference is independently meaningful — "no creo que haya llegado" vs. "no creo que llegara" vs. "no creo que hubiera llegado" are all compatible with a present-tense "no creo").

## Cross-level progression summary (across established/065, 066, 067)

Reading the three volumes in sequence documents a legible, deliberately staged curriculum for Spanish's hardest grammatical-logic cluster — mood and tense in subordinate clauses — that this project's grammar-logic-diagrams methodology may find directly useful as a model:

- **Subjunctive mood**: absent (065) → introduced via 2 fixed desiderative/epistemic triggers, then built out across ~20 further trigger classes via two reusable mechanisms (same-subject/different-subject; affirmative/negated polarity), present+imperfect tenses only (066) → pluperfect subjunctive added, completing the full counterfactual-conditional paradigm; re-practice of all prior triggers at higher syntactic complexity (067).
- **SER/ESTAR**: 3-way SER/ESTAR/TENER grouping, no adjective lists (065) → closed ser-only/estar-only/meaning-shift adjective lists introduced (066) → much larger adjective inventory + SER/ESTAR+QUE+indicative/subjunctive clause-taking extension + colloquial ser+noun personality idioms (067).
- **Past narrative aspect** (imperfecto/indefinido): full 5-rule baseline including the circumstance/main-action narrative discourse function (065) → 3-way estuve/estaba/he estado+gerundio progressive refinement, pluscuamperfecto added (066) → not separately re-taught (067 assumes mastery, uses it only in narrative cloze exercises).
- **Word formation**: not addressed at all in 065 or 066 → first and only dedicated unit in 067 (tema 20), suggesting the series treats derivational morphology as a genuinely advanced-level skill rather than foundational vocabulary-building, in contrast to how an English-medium learner grammar might front-load prefixes/suffixes much earlier.
- **Idiomatic/colloquial register**: 065 tema 20 "reacciones" (yo también/tampoco, ¡qué pena!) is the only register-marked unit at elementary level; 066 has none dedicated (though preposition-government idioms creep in at tema 8); 067 has two dedicated units (tema 7's locuciones preposicionales sub-section, tema 21's full idiom inventory) plus scattered ser+noun colloquialisms in tema 2 — confirming that this series, like the overall grammar-to-idiom progression pattern, reserves concentrated idiomatic content for its most advanced volume.
