# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Phrases: Perfect Phrases in Spanish for Confident Travel to Mexico (full book)

**Source:** Eric Vogt, *Perfect Phrases in Spanish for Confident Travel to Mexico: The No Faux-Pas
Phrasebook for the Perfect Trip* (McGraw-Hill, 2009), 208 pp. Situational travel phrasebook, 20
chapters (Good Manners, Money, Public Transportation, Shopping, Shopping for Food, Friendship and
Romance, Hotels, Eating Out, Entertainment, Museums, Common Warning Signs, Health Issues,
Emergencies, Making Plans, Keeping in Touch, Weather, Sports and Gambling, Pyramids and Ruins,
Seaside, Conversational Phrases).

**Text layer:** confirmed via `pdftotext` (a handful of benign `Syntax Error: Dictionary key must be
a name object` warnings on isolated pages — these are PDF-structure quirks, not OCR/text-quality
problems; the extracted text itself is clean, accented characters intact). Extracted in full with
`pdftotext -layout`. Transcription Confidence: verified.

**Page-count/scope reasoning:** 208 pages / ~43,100 words of raw extracted text. This is a
situational phrasebook (not a reference grammar or dictionary) — the actual content per chapter is a
handful of "main entries" (a headline phrase with phonetic transcription + English gloss, followed
by an explanatory cultural-context paragraph, followed by 2-4 reinforcing phrases in the same
format). There is no drill/exercise padding to skip (unlike the task-based courses in this corpus).
Given this structure, full coverage of every distinct phrase/term across all 20 chapters is
tractable and is what's done below — phonetic transcriptions themselves are not reproduced (they are
mechanical pronunciation aids, not lexical/grammatical content for this project's purposes), but
every distinct Spanish phrase/term and its gloss is captured.

**Mexico-specific framing — this entire book is explicitly Mexico-dialect content.** The author
repeatedly flags forms as uniquely Mexican (e.g., ¿Mande? as "the uniquely Mexican way of asking
someone to repeat what they just said") in contrast to general/Peninsular Spanish or other Latin
American varieties documented elsewhere in this corpus (e.g., 037's Spain-vs-Latin-America
footnotes). Per dispatch instruction, **every row below is implicitly Mexico-flagged** via the
Attested Region/Geographic Scope columns; rows where the source explicitly calls out a form as
distinctively Mexican (vs. generic Latin American or Spain usage) are further flagged in Notes.
