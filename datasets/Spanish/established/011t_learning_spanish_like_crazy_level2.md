## Grammar points

### Passive voice: ser + past participle + por, across all major tenses

Bonus Lesson I introduces the Spanish passive voice systematically, always paired with the same
active-voice sentence for direct contrast, and drilled across present ("la Biblia es leída por
muchas personas"), preterite ("la Biblia fue leída..."), future ("la Biblia será leída..."), and
imperfect ("el libro era leído..."). The past participle agrees in gender/number with the grammatical
subject of the passive sentence (leída/leído, puesta/puesto/puestas/puestos, recibido/recibidos,
aplaudida/aplaudido) — this agreement is drilled consistently but not explicitly named as a rule by
the instructor; it is evident throughout the paradigm. The closing exercise requires transforming
active sentences into passive across mixed tenses, testing that the participle-agreement and
tense-shifting rules both transfer correctly. (Bonus Lesson I, throughout.)

### Reported/indirect speech ("estilo indirecto") and its tense-backshift rule

Bonus Lesson J introduces indirect/reported speech, built around a single systematic backshift rule:
a present-tense reporting verb ("dice que," she says that) keeps the embedded clause in the **future**
or **present**, while a past-tense reporting verb ("dijo que," she said that) shifts the embedded
clause to the **conditional** — mirroring English "will → would" but applied as a genuine grammatical
tense shift rather than a lexical substitution: "dice que vendrá mañana" (she says he will come
tomorrow) vs. "dijo que vendría mañana" (she said he would come tomorrow). The same backshift is
drilled inside indirect **questions** introduced by "si" (whether): "pregunta si le comprarán un carro"
(she's asking if they will buy her a car) vs. "preguntó si le comprarían un carro" (she asked if they
would buy her a car) — future→conditional under the identical present→past reporting-verb shift. The
lesson's closing drill is entirely built around this single transformation, applied across a wide set
of verbs (venir, vender, hacer, cuidar, pagar, mentir, engañar), making it a clean, self-contained
reference for the mechanism. (Bonus Lesson J, throughout.)
