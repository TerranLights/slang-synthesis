## Grammar points

### Imperfect progressive ("estaba + gerund") interrupted by a preterite "cuando" clause

Bonus Lesson A drills the classic Spanish "interrupted past action" frame: an ongoing background
action in the imperfect progressive ("estaba tomando café," "estaban durmiendo," "estaba sirviendo el
almuerzo") is interrupted by a single completed preterite event introduced by "cuando" ("cuando sonó
el teléfono," "cuando se fue la luz," "cuando sonó la alarma") — the aspectual contrast (ongoing
imperfect vs. punctual preterite) is the organizing point of the entire lesson, extended across a
family scene where every member (father, mother, son, daughter, grandparents, even the cat) is doing
something different when the lights go off. (Bonus Lesson A, throughout.)

### Present perfect indicative ("he/has/ha/hemos/han + past participle")

Bonus Lesson B introduces the present perfect for actions completed at an unspecified past time with
present relevance: "he buscado," "has empezado," "ha estudiado," "hemos trabajado," drilled across
regular -ar/-er/-ir participles (buscado, comprado, hablado, encontrado, estudiado, cenado, cocinado,
almorzado, hecho — the last being irregular, "hacer" → "hecho"). The instructor explicitly flags a
related but distinct construction earlier in the same lesson: Spanish sometimes uses the **simple
present** where English would use a present-perfect-continuous ("busco un apartamento hace una
semana" — lit. "I look for an apartment [since] a week ago," equivalent to English "I've been looking
for an apartment for a week") — an explicit cross-linguistic tense-mapping note distinguishing this
from the true present perfect drilled afterward. (Bonus Lesson B, pp. 1-8.)
