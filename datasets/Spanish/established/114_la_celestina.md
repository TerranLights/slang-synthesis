# La Celestina — Adapted Student Edition (Fernando de Rojas, orig. c. 1499; this edition modernized/abridged)

Source: "La Celestina.pdf", 115pp, no text layer (confirmed via `pdftotext` sampling on pp.1-2, 20-25 —
blank output both times; triage's vision-required classification upheld). Read via vision on pp.1-15
(front matter + Acto I) and pp.55-70 (Acto IX-XII) plus the full back-matter apparatus (pp.108-115).

## What this book is

A pedagogically adapted student edition of Fernando de Rojas's *Tragicomedia de Calisto y Melibea*
(better known by the name of its most memorable character, *La Celestina*), one of the foundational
texts of Spanish literature — a transitional work between the medieval and Renaissance periods,
originally written in the late 15th century. The front matter (pp.5-7, "El autor y la obra" and
"Criterios de adaptación de esta obra") states explicitly that this edition: (1) updated the original
spelling to modern orthography, (2) removed potentially confusing cultural references, (3) replaced
complex/low-frequency vocabulary with higher-frequency synonyms **except** where the editors chose to
retain a period/archaic term and gloss it with a footnote instead — meaning every footnoted word below
represents a deliberate editorial decision to preserve archaic/register-specific vocabulary rather than
simplify it away. All 21 original *actos* were kept, split further into scenes for readability.

**Confirmed: this IS an annotated edition with genuine historical/archaic-register content**, distinct
from the modern-Spanish material found elsewhere in this corpus. Two independent apparatus layers:
1. **Numbered in-text footnotes** glossing archaic, period-specific, or euphemistic vocabulary as it
   occurs (e.g. *jubón*, *sayo*, *alcahueta*, *punta de la barriga* as a genital euphemism).
2. **A multilingual appendix glossary** ("Glosario", pp.114-115) translating ~60 harder words from the
   text into English, French, German, Italian, and Portuguese — the richest pedagogical apparatus found
   in this wave. Only the Spanish and English columns are extracted below (project scope); the other
   four languages are out of scope for this corpus.

The back matter also includes a "Solucionario" (answer key) for comprehension questions, true/false
items, a synonym/antonym exercise, and a "words grouped by category" exercise (food/trades/body
parts/family/animals) — the underlying vocabulary from the synonym/antonym and category tables is
folded into the table below since it duplicates/extends the glossary's coverage.

## Setting / register note

Setting is unspecified/non-localized late-15th-century Castile (no specific city named in the adapted
text sampled). Register spans two extremes characteristic of the original work: elevated courtly-love
rhetoric (Calisto and Melibea's dialogue) and crude, insult-laden lower-class speech (Celestina,
Sempronio, Pármeno, Elicia) — the "contraste" the front matter explicitly flags as one of the book's
defining literary features. Several insult/taboo terms below (*puta*, *cabrón*-adjacent material,
*tetas*) are notable because they are still fully current in modern Spanish slang/insult registers,
giving this 500-year-old text unexpected continuity with the project's synthesis goals.
