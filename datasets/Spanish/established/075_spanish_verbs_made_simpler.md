# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Spanish Verbs Made Simple(r) (non-redundant supplement)

**Source:** David Brodsky, *Spanish Verbs Made Simple(r)* (University of Texas Press, 1st edition,
2005), 287 pages. Full structure: Preface, Introduction, Part I "Forms of Verbs" (Ch. 1–9: present/
past/imperfect/participles, present-tense irregularities, simple-past irregularities, future/
conditional, subjunctive/imperative forms, compound forms, orthographic modifications,
**Vosotros/Ustedes and Tuteo/Voseo**, summary of the 35 verb classes), Part II "Uses of Verbs"
(Ch. 10–12: indicative uses, **Ser versus Estar**, **Subjunctive uses**), Part III "Annexes" (Annex A:
full conjugations of the 35 model verbs and sub-classes; Annex B: alphabetical index of ~4,818
verbs by class/sub-class; Selected References). This source has a genuine clean text layer
(`pdftotext -layout`, confirmed via direct sampling — no font-substitution or OCR artifacts
detected); no vision-reading was required.

**Coverage decision (non-redundant supplement):** Part I, Chapters 1–7 and 9 cover regular/
irregular verb-form mechanics (present, simple past, imperfect, future, conditional, subjunctive
and imperative morphology, compound forms, orthographic spelling-change rules) that are already
thoroughly established in this project's `007_practice_makes_perfect_spanish_irregular_verbs.md`
(principal-parts method) and `008_...spanish_verb_tenses.md` (full present-tense paradigm +
irregular-verb groups), as well as in the general grammars (014–018, 022). Those chapters were
read but **not** re-tabulated here, per the coverage rule. This file instead captures the material
genuinely new to the Spanish corpus: **Chapter 8** (vosotros/ustedes and tuteo/voseo — the
fullest dialectal/historical treatment of 2nd-person pronoun variation found in this corpus so
far), **Chapter 9**'s 35-class verb taxonomy (a structurally distinct organizing scheme from 007's
six-form principal-parts method), a condensed pass on **Chapter 11** (ser/estar — included only
for its etymological framing and ambiguous-adjective list, since general ser/estar coverage is
likely present elsewhere in the corpus), and selected **Chapter 12** subjunctive material not
covered by `established/074_...`: the cross-linguistic (Spanish/French/Italian) subjunctive-usage
comparison, the historical -ra vs. -se imperfect-subjunctive distinction (more detailed than 074's
"fully interchangeable" simplification), the full "aunque" (concessive) clause mood system, and
additional conditional/excluding conjunctions and tense-correspondence tables beyond those in
074. Annex A (conjugation tables) and Annex B (the ~4,818-verb class index) are reference tables
redundant with conjugation paradigms already established elsewhere and were not tabulated;
Annex B's own structure (verb → class/sub-class number) was sampled only to confirm the Chapter
9 taxonomy is internally consistent.

**Relationship to other Spanish `established/` files:** Chapter 8 (tuteo/voseo) is this book's
flagship, standalone contribution — no other Spanish `established/` file to date gives comparable
historical depth (Latin vos/tu origins, the vuestra merced→usted evolution, the post-Franco
tú-normalization note) or the specific voseo present-subjunctive/present-indicative/imperative
conjugation table. The Chapter 12 material should be read as a supplement to, not a replacement
for, `established/074_...`'s trigger-expression catalog — this book adds concessive clauses
(aunque) which 074 does not cover at all, plus a more explicit if/then tense-correspondence table.
