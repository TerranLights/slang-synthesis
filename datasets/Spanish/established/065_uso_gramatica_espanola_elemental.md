# Uso de la Gramática Española — Nivel Elemental (Francisca Castro, Edelsa, 1996)

Source: `/media/kuroskalacs/Home-Theater/w-lang/Spanish/02.Grammar Workbooks/Uso de La Gramatica Espanola Elemental.pdf` (88 scanned PDF-page spreads = 175 numbered book pages). No text layer (confirmed via `pdftotext`); vision-read in full via page-spread montages rendered at 130dpi.

This is volume 1 of a 3-volume Spanish-medium graded grammar-practice series (Elemental → Intermedio → Avanzado), paired with established/066 (Intermedio) and established/067 (Avanzado). Unlike the English-medium workbooks in this corpus (Practice Makes Perfect, Schaum's, etc.), **all instruction, rule explanation, and metalanguage in this series is itself in Spanish** — it is written for classroom/self-study use by learners already literate enough in Spanish to read grammar explanations in the target language, a distinct pedagogical register from translation-based English-medium grammars. Author: Francisca Castro Viudez. Publisher: Edelsa Grupo Didascalia, Madrid. ISBN 84-7711-133-2.

## Pedagogical structure (applies uniformly to all 35 temas)

Each of the book's 35 **temas** (units) follows a fixed four-part micro-structure, stated explicitly in the book's own prologue:
1. **Observe** — a cartoon-dialogue scene (numbered speech bubbles) that contextualizes the grammar point before any rule is given — meaning-first, form-second sequencing.
2. **Forma** — a boxed paradigm/conjugation table, for quick visual reference.
3. **Uso** — numbered prose rules in Spanish, each illustrated with 2-4 bolded example sentences. This is the section of actual grammatical-logic content.
4. **Ejercicios** — graded fill-in/transform drills with a self-scoring "aciertos__/N" tally and a "Tema N. Puntuación total__/N" cumulative score — explicitly designed, per the prologue, for classroom use OR autonomous self-study (autoaprendizaje).

At the end of the book (pp. 167-175) is a separate "Ejercicios complementarios -libres y semilibres- de expresión estrita" section: open-ended writing prompts (write a diary page, a business itinerary report, a cooking recipe, compare two eras) with no fixed answer, explicitly excluded from the scoring system because they test productive competence rather than drilled forms.

## STRUCTURAL FINDING: zero subjunctive content at Elemental level

Across all 35 temas and the full 175-page body, **the subjunctive mood is never introduced, named, or used as a target form.** Tema 31 (Oraciones condicionales) — the natural place a subjunctive would appear — restricts itself entirely to real/open conditionals: *"Si" + Presente de Indicativo* in the subordinate clause, with Presente, Futuro, or Imperativo in the main clause (*Si podemos, salimos todas las noches*; *Si viene a mi casa, le enseñaré el vídeo nuevo*). No counterfactual (*si + imperfecto de subjuntivo*) or hypothetical construction appears anywhere in the book.

The book's own Imperativo unit (tema 14) comes closest to touching the subjunctive without naming it: its "Uso" section states outright that "Las demás personas [of the imperative paradigm, i.e. everyone except tú] son realmente del Presente de Subjuntivo" (the other persons [usted, nosotros, vosotros, ustedes imperative forms] are really from the Present Subjunctive) — but the book never develops this into an actual subjunctive lesson; it is mentioned only to explain irregular imperative morphology, and the word "subjuntivo" does not recur as a taught category elsewhere in the volume.

This is a deliberate pedagogical scope decision, not an oversight — the elemental level is built entirely on indicative-mood competence (presente, pretérito perfecto, pretérito indefinido, pretérito imperfecto, futuro imperfecto, estar+gerundio) plus imperative, and defers subjunctive entirely to a later volume. This makes it the explicit baseline against which 066 (Intermedio) and 067 (Avanzado)'s subjunctive coverage should be read as a *progression*, not a repetition.

## Cross-reference note

This volume's pretérito perfecto/indefinido split (tema 13, 19) and imperfecto/indefinido split (temas 25-26, 30) represent the **elemental baseline** for two of Spanish's hardest aspectual contrasts for learners. Established/066 (Intermedio) and established/067 (Avanzado) should be checked against this baseline specifically for: (a) whether they add exception classes or verbs whose meaning shifts between the two aspects (saber/conocer/querer/poder-type meaning-change verbs are notably ABSENT from this elemental treatment — a likely intermedio/avanzado addition); (b) whether the perfect/preterite split is maintained as Peninsular-default or gets geographically qualified at higher levels; (c) how/whether and at what level the subjunctive mood (entirely absent here) is finally introduced and connected to conditionals, obligation, and reactions already taught in indicative form at this level.
