# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: The Ultimate Spanish Verb Review and Practice (non-redundant supplement)

**Source:** Ronni L. Gordon, Ph.D. and David M. Stillman, Ph.D., *The Ultimate Spanish Verb Review
and Practice: Mastering Verbs and Sentence Building for Confident Communication* (McGraw-Hill),
321 pages. Full structure: Preface, Introduction, 15 chapters (Ch. 1 Present tense of regular
verbs; Ch. 2 Stem-changing verbs + -iar/-uar verbs; Ch. 3–4 Irregular verbs Parts I–II; Ch. 5 Ser
and estar; Ch. 6 The preterit; Ch. 7 The imperfect vs. preterit; Ch. 8 Past participle, present
perfect, pluperfect; Ch. 9 The imperative; Ch. 10 Future/conditional + future perfect/conditional
perfect; Ch. 11–12 Subjunctive Parts I–II (present; present perfect/imperfect/pluperfect); Ch. 13
Reflexive verbs; Ch. 14 Present participle + progressive tenses; Ch. 15 Passive constructions +
**reverse construction verbs** (gustar-type) + **se le construction for unplanned occurrences**),
each chapter with a "BUILDING SENTENCES" sentence-construction supplement, Answer Key
(pp. 280–304), Index. This source has a genuine clean text layer (`pdftotext -layout`, confirmed
via direct sampling — no scrambling or font-substitution artifacts detected); no vision-reading
was required.

**Coverage decision (non-redundant supplement):** This book's core content — regular/irregular
verb-form conjugation drills across all tenses and moods — substantially duplicates ground already
covered by `established/007_...`, `008_...`, `074_...`, and `075_...` (stem-changing, irregular
verb groups, preterit/imperfect, present/perfect/pluperfect subjunctive, etc.), and the bulk of the
book is fill-in-the-blank drill exercises reusing a small working vocabulary already captured
elsewhere. Per the coverage rule, this file does **not** re-tabulate standard conjugation
paradigms. It instead captures what is genuinely new to the Spanish corpus: (1) four **"LA
LENGUA ESPAÑOLA" etymological/historical language boxes** scattered through the book — short
sidebars on Latin-to-Spanish verb-system history, found nowhere else in this corpus so far; (2)
the **"Reverse Construction Verbs" chapter (Ch. 15)** — the fullest gustar-type-verb list found in
the Spanish corpus to date, including less-common members of the class; (3) the **"Se le
construction for unplanned occurrences"** supplement — accidental/involuntary-action se
constructions (se me rompió, se le olvidó, etc.), a distinctive Spanish grammatical feature not
previously captured in this corpus.

**Relationship to other Spanish `established/` files:** The ojalá/Arabic-borrowings language box
(Ch. 11) pairs directly with the subjunctive material in `established/074_...` (which also covers
ojalá as a trigger expression but without the etymological/historical detail given here). The
ser/estar language box (Ch. 5) supplements the etymological framing in `established/075_...`
(Chapter 11, Brodsky's book) by adding that the ser/estar essere/stare split is shared with
Portuguese and "especially southern Italian dialects," and dates to attested 4th-century Late
Latin — detail absent from 075.
