## Grammar points

### Present perfect of -ar verbs with plural subjects, zoo-animal domain

Bonus Lesson C extends Bonus Lesson B's present-perfect pattern specifically to plural-subject forms
("hemos visitado," "han caminado," "han cantado," "se han bañado" — note the reflexive "bañarse" for
animals bathing themselves) across an entire zoo-visit narrative frame, giving a dense,
single-domain drill of "hemos/han + -ado" participles. (Bonus Lesson C, throughout.)

### Present perfect of -er/-ir verbs, with irregular past participles

Bonus Lesson D completes the present-perfect paradigm by drilling -er/-ir verbs, most of them
irregular participles that don't follow the regular "-ido" pattern predictably from the infinitive:
salir→salido (regular), leer→leído (regular but with an accent to break the diphthong), comer→comido,
vivir→vivido, subir→subido, aprender→aprendido (all regular), but crucially **ir→ido** and **ser→sido**
— both highly irregular verbs whose participles the lesson drills explicitly ("han ido a Europa,"
"hemos sido estudiantes de español durante un año"). This lesson, paired with Bonus Lesson B and C,
completes a full three-lesson arc covering the present perfect across all three conjugation classes
plus its two most irregular participles. (Bonus Lesson D, throughout.)
