full "Índice de contenidos" (read in full across pp. 4–10 of the PDF, covering all 12 units) shows

## Grammar points

### Formal/informal address (`tú` vs. `usted`) — explicit social framing

Unit 2.4's "Formal e informal" box states the rule cleanly: `tú` for informal situations, `usted`
for formal ones, conjugated identically to the 3rd person singular. What this book adds beyond the
bare paradigm (already covered extensively elsewhere in this corpus) is the **social dynamics**
around the choice — demonstrated via a scripted scene where an adult explicitly grants a younger
guest permission to drop `usted` in her favor ("háblame de tú"), which is a live pragmatic
convention in Peninsular Spanish (the act of "tutear" someone, or being invited to) worth having
as a concrete illustration alongside the grammar.

### Telephone-interaction register set

A tight, self-contained functional-phrase cluster worth keeping together: answering (`¿Dígame?`),
asking who's calling (`¿De parte de quién?`), confirming identity (`Sí, soy yo`), and passing the
phone along (`Sí, ahora mismo se pone`). This is a genuinely new functional domain for this
corpus — no prior established/ file had telephone-etiquette phrases specifically.
