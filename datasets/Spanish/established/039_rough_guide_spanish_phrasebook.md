## Cross-dialect comparison trio — read this note first

This file is one of three coded **consistently** so the same concept can be compared across Spain,
Mexico, and Latin America at a glance:
- **039 (this file)** — The Rough Guide Spanish Phrasebook → Spain
- **040** — The Rough Guide Mexican Spanish Phrasebook → Mexico
- **041** — The Rough Guide Latin American Spanish Phrasebook → pan-regional Latin America, with
  internal country tagging (Arg, Mex, Col, Rpl, Ven, Ch, Pe, Cu, CAm, etc.)

Shared-concept rows carry a parallel entry in the sibling file(s), cross-referenced by file number
in the Notes column. The headline contrasts found between this book and its two siblings:

| Concept | Spain (039) | Mexico (040) | Latin America (041) |
|---|---|---|---|
| "swearword" (the noun) | **palabrota** | grosería | grosería |
| answering the phone | **¿Dígame?/¿Diga?** ("tell me") | **¿Bueno?** ("good") | **¿Aló?** (SAm) / bueno elsewhere |
| "to fuck" (taboo) | *not this verb* — `coger` = neutral "to catch/take" | **coger** = taboo "to fuck" | **coger** = taboo "to fuck" (same taboo shift as Mexico) |
| flagship taboo "to fuck" verb | joder (excl. "fuck!"); vete a tomar por culo | **chingar** (flagship, highly productive) | chingar *and* tirar both attested |
| "zero" pronunciation | cero **[thairo]** (distinción, "th") | cero **[sairo]** (seseo, "s") | cero **[sairo]** (seseo, "s") |
| 2nd-person plural familiar | **vosotros** (fam) vs ustedes (pol) — a 4-way tú/usted/vosotros/ustedes split | **ustedes** only (no vosotros; voseo/2nd-pl-fam distinction collapses) | ustedes generally; **vos** (voseo) also productive in Rpl/Central America zone — see established/002 for the full voseo paradigm, not re-extracted here |

The `coger` taboo-shift is the single most striking find in this trio: the identical verb is a
completely neutral everyday word in Spain ("coger el autobús" = to catch the bus) and a vulgar
sexual verb in Mexico/Latin America — a textbook case of regional semantic/taboo divergence from
one common Latin root, directly useful for this project's `taboo_inversion`/`semantic_shift`
slang-mechanics categories.

**Non-redundant-supplement note:** This project's general Spanish grammar corpus (001-015) already
covers tú/usted/vosotros/ustedes basic paradigms, and 002 (*Colloquial Spanish 2*) already has a
flagship, fully-worked voseo section (vos/sos conjugation paradigm, Argentina/Uruguay/Paraguay/Chile
+ parts of Central America/southern Mexico). This file therefore does **not** re-extract those
paradigms in depth — it only flags the specific lexical/phonological items a phrasebook adds that
general grammars don't: the "Colloquialisms" slang list, the phone-greeting idiom, the
distinción-vs-seseo transliteration difference as it surfaces concretely in this book's own IPA-ish
respelling system, and a handful of Spain-specific menu/culture terms. Basic numbers, dates, and
core A-Z vocabulary already well covered elsewhere are skipped.
