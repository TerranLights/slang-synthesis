## Grammar points

### Demonstrative pronouns vs. demonstrative adjectives, and neutral vs. gendered forms

Lección 47 draws an explicit three-way demonstrative contrast: (1) the **neutral pronouns** "esto"
(this) and "eso" (that), used specifically when the object's gender/identity is unknown to the
speaker ("¿Qué es esto?" — what is this [unidentified thing]?); (2) the **gendered/numbered
demonstrative adjectives** "este/esta/estos/estas" (this/these, proximal) and "ese/esa/esos/esas"
(that/those, medial), which must agree with the noun they modify, e.g. "esta corbata," "esos
zapatos"; and (3) the same forms used as **pronouns standing alone** (éstos, esos), which the source
notes traditionally carry a written accent when used pronominally rather than adjectivally — a
distinction some modern Spanish orthography has since dropped, but which this source's era applies.
(Lección 47, pp. 1, 6.)

### Distal demonstrative "aquel" family — three-way distance contrast

Lección 48 introduces "aquel/aquella/aquellos/aquellas," used for an object far from *both* speaker
and listener — completing a three-way Spanish demonstrative distance system explicitly drilled
across all three degrees in this pair of lessons: este (near speaker) / ese (near listener, medial) /
aquel (far from both). The lesson explicitly drills "¿Éstas o aquellas?" (these or those-over-there?)
and "¿Ese o aquel?" (that-near-you or that-over-there?) as direct three-way contrast questions.
(Lección 48, pp. 1-5.)

### Gustar-type verbs: agreement with the thing liked, not the person who likes it

Lección 48's second half introduces "gustar" (to like/to please) and its close relatives "encantar"
(to love/delight), "fascinar" (to fascinate), and "interesar" (to interest) — all verbs whose subject
is grammatically the thing liked, with the person doing the liking expressed as an indirect object:
"me gusta la playa" (lit. "the beach pleases me"), "me gustan todas las playas" (plural verb
agreement with the plural liked-item). The source explicitly states the verb "matches the second
noun and not the pronoun" (i.e., agrees with the liked thing, not the liking person), and notes that
"a mí," "a ti," "a él," etc. are optional clarifying/emphasis phrases layered onto the required
indirect-object clitic (me/te/le/nos/les), not the core grammatical subject. (Lección 48, pp. 5-9.)

### Explicit regional lexical variation and one false-friend warning

Two source-marked regional items and one explicit false-friend flag appear across these two lessons:
"medias" vs. "calcetines" for socks (Spain/Mexico use "calcetines"; "medias" means only "stockings"
in Spain specifically), "marrón" vs. "café" for brown (Colombia and Chile use "café" — one of the few
cases in this set where specific countries, not a vague "some countries," are named), and the
explicit warning that "granate" (maroon/deep red) is **not** interchangeable with "marrón" (brown)
despite superficial similarity to the English "maroon" — a genuine false-friend trap the instructor
calls out directly. (Lección 47, pp. 5, 8-9.)
