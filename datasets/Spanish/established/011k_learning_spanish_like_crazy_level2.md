## Grammar points

### Present subjunctive triggered by verbs of volition/desire: querer que, desear que, preferir que, esperar que

Both lessons together form a single connected presentation of the classic Spanish "volition"
subjunctive trigger pattern: when a main clause expresses a wish, desire, or preference directed at
someone else's action, the subordinate clause's verb must be in the subjunctive rather than the
indicative, joined by "que." Lección 51 introduces this with "querer que" ("quiero que hable español"
— I want you to speak Spanish, lit. "I want that you speak Spanish") and explicitly frames it as the
subjunctive transformation of an ordinary imperative ("hable español" as a command vs. "quiero que
hable español" as an expressed wish about someone else's action). Lección 52 extends the identical
pattern to three more triggers — "desear que" (deseo que no cene tan tarde — I wish you wouldn't eat
dinner so late), "preferir que" (prefiero que no fume en la clase — I prefer that you not smoke in
class), and "esperar que" (esperamos que se siente en el suelo — we hope you sit on the floor) —
drilled across all persons of both the trigger verb and the subordinate subjunctive verb (hable/
hables/hablen/hablemos; cene/cenes; fume/fumes; sienten/sentemos/siente). A key structural constraint
demonstrated throughout: the subjunctive construction is used specifically when the wisher and the
wished-for actor are different people/subjects — when they're the same, a plain infinitive is used
instead ("prefiero hablar inglés con mis amigos," I prefer to speak English — no "que," no
subjunctive, because the preferrer and the speaker are the same person), directly contrasted in
Lección 52's closing dialogue. (Lección 51, throughout; Lección 52, throughout, esp. the closing
teacher/student dialogue pp. 6-8.)
