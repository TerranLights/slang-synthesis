# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Reference: Amnesia (Lecturas de Español, Nivel Elemental I)

**Source:** *Amnesia* (José Luis Ocasar Ariza, Edinumen, "Lecturas de Español" series, Nivel
Elemental I, 41 pp., vision-required PDF — confirmed: `pdftotext` extraction is essentially empty,
~1 char/page). Read in full via vision (41/41 pages).

**Series/apparatus:** this is a genuine **graded reader with built-in pedagogical apparatus** — a
bolded in-text term paired with a **left-margin gloss box** on nearly every page (definition given
in simple monolingual Spanish, e.g. "bailones: personas que bailan"). Per the back cover, the book
is written **exclusively in the present indicative tense** (a deliberate grammar-scoping choice for
Nivel Elemental learners) and belongs to a 6-book collection spanning Elemental through Superior
levels: *Amnesia* and *Paisaje de Otoño* (Elemental I/II), *Muerte entre Muñecos* and *Memorias de
Septiembre* (Intermedio I/II — confirming `112_muerte_entre_munecos_nivel1.md` is a sibling book in
this same apparatus-bearing series), and *Los labios de Bárbara* / *Una música tan triste*
(Superior I/II). All ~69 margin-gloss terms found in the book were extracted below.

**Plot/setting:** a man (revealed only at the very end to be named Carlos) wakes in a hospital in
**Santander** (Cantabria, northern Spain) with no memory, two broken bones, and amnesia from a car
accident. A nurse, Ainoa (a Basque name — flagged in-text as such), and police inspector Pedro
Herrero investigate: the accident is linked to a jewelry-store robbery (Paseo de Pereda) via the
same red Seat Toledo, and the amnesiac recalls jewel-related memories, making him the prime
suspect. He is given the placeholder name "Javier" during the investigation. The twist: he recalls
a Mecano concert flyer from the night of the accident, and it emerges he is actually an innocent
pedestrian — a foreign exchange student (named Carlos) at the Universidad Internacional Menéndez
Pelayo (a real institution, housed in the real Palacio de la Magdalena in Santander) — while the
actual unconscious second victim is the real thief. Genre: light detective/suspense story.

**Register note:** overwhelmingly neutral/standard conversational Castilian Spanish (tú/usted
distinction, vosotros implicit in register though not heavily used in dialogue), consistent with
the book's A1–A2 pedagogical target. No slang beyond mild colloquialisms already glossed below
(e.g. "estar hecho polvo," "vale," "tirando").
