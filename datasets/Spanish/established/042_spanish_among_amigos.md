# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Spanish Among Amigos Phrasebook

**Source:** Nuria Agulló, *Spanish Among Amigos Phrasebook* (McGraw-Hill, 2006), full 208-page
volume — 12 topical units (Meeting and Greeting; Same Time, Same Place?; Breaking the Ice; Asking
for Help or Info; Likes and Dislikes; Wishing and Wanting; Offering Help and Advice; Speaking Your
Mind; Giving Descriptions; Relaying News and Gossip; Saying How You Feel; Special Occasions) plus a
Grammar Bank, a 20-category Word Bank, and a dedicated "Common Idioms and Expressions" glossary.
PDF has a genuine, clean text layer; `pdftotext -layout` extracted cleanly (~7,000 lines), no OCR or
vision-reading required, no font-cipher corruption detected. Transcription Confidence = n/a,
Vision Reading Confidence = n/a for every row below.

**Why this book is exceptionally high-value for this project:** unlike a standard phrasebook, this
one is explicitly built around a native three-tier register-flagging system the author applies to
nearly every colloquial phrase in the book — a superscript digit directly after the term:
`1` = colloquial expression/idiom (all ages), `2` = "soft" slang (popular with young people, broadly
accepted), `3` = "hard" slang (potentially offensive, "restricted to use among amigos" per the
book's own front-matter legend, p. xiii). The book separately tags regional scope inline:
`[SP]` = Spain-only, `[LA]` = most of Latin America, plus a handful of narrower country tags
(`[MX]` = Mexico) seen in the running text. One Word Bank entry ("apartment") also carries an
`[ACU]` tag for `departamento` that is **not defined anywhere in the book's own abbreviations
legend** — kept as-is below with a Notes flag rather than guessed at, per this project's
never-guess-fill rule for uncertain regional provenance.

**Mapping to this project's taxonomy:** book tier `1` → Usage Tier `colloquial`; tier `2` → Usage
Tier `slang`; tier `3` → Usage Tier `taboo` (the book's own description — "potentially offensive,"
"vulgar" — matches this project's `taboo` tier rather than plain `slang`). Untagged everyday phrases
used for comparison/context are marked `core`.

**Coverage approach:** every digit-flagged (register-marked) term and every entry in the standalone
"Common Idioms and Expressions" glossary (pp. 183–186, ~70 entries, A–Z) was captured below — this
is close to exhaustive for the book's own slang/idiom content, since the digit flags are the book's
own curation of what counts as colloquial/slang/taboo. Untagged core vocabulary (plain greetings,
grammar-point drills, standard Word Bank categories like Zodiac signs, banking terms, body parts,
professions) was **not** re-extracted — it duplicates material already well covered by this
language's general-purpose established files (001–022). A handful of untagged items are included
only where needed as contrastive context for a tagged item in the same list (e.g., the neutral
synonym next to a flagged slang synonym for the same concept).

**Non-redundant-supplement note:** *Learning Spanish Like Crazy* (010–012) already has dedicated
insult/vulgar-language supplement files; this book's content is almost entirely non-overlapping
with that — it's a different genre (social-conversation idiom phrasebook vs. spoken-drill insult
lists) and different specific terms, with only incidental overlap (e.g., `vale`/`OK` as a filler
word appears in many sources). No duplicate terms were skipped as a result; this book's curated
idiom list and mood/feeling/gossip vocabulary are new content for this language's corpus.
