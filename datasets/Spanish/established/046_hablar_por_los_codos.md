# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Hablar por los codos — Frases para un español cotidiano

**Source:** Gordana Vranic, *Hablar por los codos: Frases para un español cotidiano* (Edelsa, Grupo
Didascalia, Madrid), full 128-page spiral-bound illustrated manual — per its own back-cover summary,
"175 frases hechas y 45 refranes, con sus correspondientes ejercicios" (175 set phrases/idioms and 45
proverbs, with matching exercises). Structure: Frases hechas y dichos (pp. 6-82, entries 1-175),
Ejercicios de frases hechas (pp. 83-99), Refranes (pp. 100-115, entries 1-45), Ejercicios de refranes
(pp. 116-120), Soluciones (p. 121), Lista de frases hechas / Lista de refranes (alphabetical headword
indices, pp. 122-127, no glosses — index only). PDF has **no text layer at all** (`pdftotext` returned
0 bytes; `pdfimages`/`pdfinfo` confirm every page is a scanned JPEG, 945x1283px). All content below
was read directly via vision (rendered to JPEG at 150 DPI with `pdftoppm`). Transcription Confidence =
n/a for every row (no OCR involved). Vision Reading Confidence = `verified` for every row — print
quality throughout is clean, large, color-coded typeset text with no handwriting or degraded-scan
ambiguity on any page read.

**Format of each entry (per the book's own back-cover legend):** a numbered, alphabetized headword in
caps; a plain-language meaning gloss; an example sentence (sometimes a short dialogue); an "Origen"
sidebar explaining the phrase's historical/etymological origin where known (a distinctive feature of
this book — many entries trace back to specific historical customs, biblical passages, Greek myths,
or documented folk anecdotes); an "Otras expresiones similares" box listing synonym idioms; and, for
syntactically tricky entries, a "Para utilizarla" box giving the exact grammatical frame (color-coded
in the original: verb ending/subject/pronouns in blue, direct/indirect object in red, other
prepositional complements in green — flattened to plain text below since color isn't preserved through
vision transcription, but the grammatical pattern itself is captured in the Notes column).

**Register note — this book's content skews colloquial, not slang/taboo:** unlike the other books in
this wave (*Spanish in Your Face!*, *Hide This Spanish CD*, which explicitly flag off-color/vulgar
content), this is a general-purpose "everyday fixed expressions and proverbs" manual aimed at
intermediate/advanced learners — it does not use a register-flagging system and contains essentially
no vulgar/taboo material in the sampled content (one mild exception noted below: "mandar a hacer
puñetas" as a synonym listed for "mandar al quinto pino"). Nearly every entry captured below is
tagged Usage Tier `colloquial` (fixed idiomatic expressions in everyday use) or, for the refranes
section, `colloquial` with a Notes flag identifying it as a proverb — this is an accurate reflection
of the source's actual register profile, not an extraction gap. The book's value to this project is
its large stock of fully-worked idiom entries with documented etymological origins, which is valuable
raw material for this project's metaphor-productivity and idiom-derivation analysis even where the
current register sits at "colloquial" rather than "slang"/"taboo."

**Coverage approach — dictionary-scale, deliberately sampled (documented per dispatch instruction):**
with 220 total entries (175 frases hechas + 45 refranes) each requiring a full-page vision read, full
exhaustive extraction of all 128 pages was not attempted within this wave's scope. A systematic
every-4th-page sample was taken spanning the full A-Z range of both sections: **44 of the 175 frases
hechas** (entries 1, 2, 5, 6, 13, 14, 21, 22, 29, 30, 37, 38, 46, 47, 54, 55, 62, 63, 71, 72, 79, 80,
81, 88, 89, 98, 99, 107, 108, 118, 119, 120, 128, 129, 137, 138, 146, 147, 148, 158, 159, 160, 169,
170 — roughly 25% coverage, spread evenly from A to U) and **22 of the 45 refranes** (entries 1, 2, 6,
7, 8, 12, 13, 14, 18, 19, 20, 24, 25, 26, 30, 31, 32, 36, 37, 38, 42, 43 — roughly half coverage,
spread from A to U). The front-matter alphabetical index pages (122-127, headwords only, no glosses)
were skimmed to confirm the full 220-entry inventory exists and to spot-check the sampling spread
but were not separately transcribed as vocabulary rows since they carry no content beyond the
headword already captured (or not captured) from the content pages. The exercise sections (pp. 83-99,
116-120) are cloze/matching drills reusing already-captured headwords — skipped per this project's
coverage rule for drill material.

**Non-redundant-supplement note:** several entries captured here independently confirm idioms already
documented from other sources in this wave, which is a useful cross-source corroboration rather than
redundancy: "Ir por lana y volver trasquilado" (entry 88) matches "Fui por lana y volví trasquilado(-a)"
from *The Big Red Book of Spanish Idioms* (established/045); "Unos nacen con estrella y otros nacen
estrellados" (refrán 43) matches the identical proverb documented in established/045 and already
cross-referenced there to established/043. Entry 80, "HABLAR POR LOS CODOS," is this book's own title
phrase, included below for completeness. The large majority of the 66 entries captured are new content
for this corpus — fixed expressions not previously documented in any of this language's established
files.
