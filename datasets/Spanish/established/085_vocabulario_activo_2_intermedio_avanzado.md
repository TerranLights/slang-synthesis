## Graded-series comparison: 084 (Elemental) vs. 085 (Intermedio-Avanzado)

Both volumes share the same 60-unit, one-page-per-topic photocopiable-worksheet format from the same author and publisher (ELI), making this an unusually clean controlled comparison of how a single pedagogical series escalates difficulty across two levels.

**1. New unit *types* appear, not just harder words.** 084's 60-unit TOC contains zero units dedicated to idiom, proverb, false-friend, or colloquial-register content — every unit is a flat topic-vocabulary page (clothing, house, numbers, body parts...), with the closest it comes to "nuance" being grammar-adjacent units like Contrarios (antonyms) and Masculino o femenino (gender exceptions). 085 adds, with no 084 counterpart at all: **Refranes** (14 complete traditional proverbs), **Expresiones equivalentes** (register/paraphrase-equivalence judgment), **Expresiones coloquiales** (9 explicit contemporary slang items — majo, cabrearse, borde, rollo, irse de marcha, chupito, chulo, harta, traer sin cuidado), **Parecidos, pero diferentes** (gender-determines-meaning minimal pairs), **Reacciones** (11 fixed social reaction-formula phrases), **Busca la palabra** (synonym-discrimination puzzles, 2 units), and **¿Con "ser" o "estar"?** (6 adjectives whose meaning flips entirely depending on copula choice). This is the single clearest piece of evidence for the register/complexity escalation requested for this comparison: **the elementary level teaches words; the intermediate-advanced level teaches how words relate to each other idiomatically, connotatively, and grammatically-by-nuance.**

**2. The same topic, when it recurs, gains either depth or register (not always both).** Three directly-matched topics were sampled in both books: **La televisión** (084: one flat parts-list unit → 085: two units, the second of which adds explicit colloquial media-register vocabulary — rollo, hacer zapping, culebrón — and a full program-genre taxonomy); **El coche** (084: one static parts-diagram unit → 085: one unit reframed as a full procedural action-sequence — quitar el freno de mano → poner la llave de contacto y arrancar → ajustar el espejo → ponerse el cinturón → meter la marcha → repostar → aparcar — teaching the *operational script* of driving, not just naming the parts); **Animales** (084: one unit of plain animal-name vocabulary → 085: two units, the first entirely composed of ten zoomorphic idioms — estar como una cabra, trabajar como un burro, etc. — a register leap from "name the animal" to "use the animal metaphorically"). By contrast, **La familia** showed no real escalation — both books treat kinship vocabulary at a comparably basic level — indicating the series' difficulty curve is driven primarily by *adding whole new unit categories* (see point 1) rather than by uniformly deepening every recurring topic.

**3. Internal evidence of a one-volume vocabulary-complexity split even within a single word.** 085's own "Antiguamente... hoy en día" unit (p.57) uses "carro" to mean an old-fashioned horse-drawn cart (opposed to modern "coche"), which is the *reverse* of the semantic value "carro" carries in 083's dialectal car-vocabulary chapter, where Latin American varieties (Argentina, Mexico) have generalized exactly this root to mean "car" instead of peninsular "coche." Read together, the two sources sketch a plausible diachronic bridge for a single root's meaning drifting along a wheeled-vehicle conceptual axis (cart → car) that took different outcomes in different dialect regions — a nice cross-source finding that neither book states explicitly but which this extraction surfaces by comparing them.
