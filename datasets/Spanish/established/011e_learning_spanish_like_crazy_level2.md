## Grammar points

### Contrary-to-fact conditional with irregular imperfect subjunctives: ser (fuera) and poder (pudiera)

Lección 39 extends Lección 36-38's "si + imperfect subjunctive, ...+conditional" pattern to two
additional high-frequency irregular verbs: "si fuera rico" (if I were rich, from ser) and "si pudiera
ir a cualquier país" (if I could go to any country, from poder), each paired with a conditional main
clause across all persons (fuera/fueras/fuera/fuéramos/fueran; pudiera/pudieras/pudiéramos/pudieran).
The lesson is essentially a further systematic person/number drill of this same construction
introduced in Lección 36, applied to a fresh vocabulary domain (world travel, countries). (Lección
39, throughout.)

### The imperative mood: four-way paradigm (formal/informal × affirmative/negative)

Lección 40 is a systematic presentation of the Spanish imperative across seven verbs (entrar, salir,
abrir, cerrar, poner, tomar, hablar), each drilled in all four imperative combinations:
- **Formal affirmative** (usted): regular -ar verbs take an -e ending (entre, hable), matching the
  present-subjunctive-derived formal-command pattern; irregular poner → ponga.
- **Formal negative**: identical form to the formal affirmative, just preceded by "no" (e.g. "cierre
  la puerta" / "no cierre la puerta") — the source explicitly flags that the verb form itself does
  **not** change between formal affirmative and formal negative, unlike the informal register.
- **Informal affirmative** (tú): typically a special "tú-command" form distinct from the ordinary
  present tense in the irregular verbs (poner → "pon," salir → "sal"), regular for others (abre,
  cierra, habla, entra, toma).
- **Informal negative**: **does** change form from the informal affirmative — built off the
  present-subjunctive tú-form with an -s ending (no entres, no salgas, no abras, no cierres, no
  pongas, no tomes, no hables) — the source explicitly draws attention to this shift as the key
  contrast with the (unchanging) formal pair above.
This is the clearest single-lesson presentation of the full formal/informal, affirmative/negative
imperative paradigm in the set so far, and a directly reusable reference table for any future
mechanics-analysis work on Spanish command forms. (Lección 40, throughout.)
