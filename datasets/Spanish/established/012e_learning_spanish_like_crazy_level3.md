## Grammar points

### "Podría haber + participio" — continued, medical vocabulary — Lecciones 76–77

Continues the "could have done X" construction from `012d`'s Lección 75 with a full medical-vocabulary
arc: covering wounds, treatment, and injury (cubrir, curar/sanar, inyección, cirujano, enfermero/-a,
fractura, pomada, gotas). Confirms the pattern seen throughout this course: a grammar point introduced in
one lesson gets 2–3 more lessons of vocabulary-only continuation before the course moves to the next new
structure.

### Voz pasiva (passive voice) — sports vocabulary — Lecciones 78–79

Paraphrase (p.6, Lección 78): the Spanish passive voice is formed with `ser` (conjugated for tense) +
past participle (agreeing in gender/number with the subject) + optionally `por` + agent — e.g. `el balón
fue agarrado por un jugador` ("the ball was caught by a player"), `los equipos fueron vencidos` ("the
teams were defeated"). Introduced via a new Juan/Diana dialogue about a lost soccer game and a boxing
match, and drilled across two lessons with sports vocabulary (fútbol, equipo, partido, balón/pelota,
boxeo, golpear).

### Mandatos indirectos (indirect commands) with "que" + subjunctive — Lección 80

Paraphrase (p.1): indirect commands — "have someone do something" or "let someone do something," given
by a third party rather than addressed directly to the person acting — are formed in Spanish with `que` +
the present subjunctive form of the verb (`que entre él` — "let him enter"/"have him enter"; `que coman
ellos` — "let them eat"). The pronoun subject is optional (`que entren ellos` = `que entren`). This is the
final lesson in the numbered `Spanish_Lesson_##` series (it runs only to 80; the `Leccion##-final` series
stops at 75).
