## Grammar points

### Superlative: el/la (+ noun) más/menos + adjective + de

Lección 35 drills the superlative construction "el más fuerte de la clase" (the strongest in the
class), "la más vieja de la familia" (the oldest in the family) — the definite article + más/menos +
adjective + "de" (not "en") introducing the comparison group. Contrasted directly against the
comparative "más...que" from Lección 32-34 in the same lesson (e.g. "Carmen es más alta que Silvia"
vs. "Carmen es la más alta de la clase"). (Lección 35, pp. 1-7.)

### Conditional tense, introduced via "would" statements

Lección 35's second half introduces the simple conditional ("compraría," "viajaría," "escribiría") to
express hypothetical "would" actions, drilled first without an explicit "if"-clause (e.g. "¿Qué
comprarías?" — "What would you buy?") — establishing the conditional's form before Lección 36
attaches it to a full contrary-to-fact "si" clause. (Lección 35, pp. 7-10.)

### Contrary-to-fact conditional sentences: si + imperfect subjunctive, ... + conditional

Lección 36 is built entirely around the full two-clause hypothetical construction: "si" + imperfect
subjunctive ("si me ganara la lotería," "si no tuviera que trabajar," "si tuviera un carro") paired
with the main-clause conditional ("me iría," "trabajaría menos," "escribiríamos"). Drilled
systematically across persons/numbers of both the imperfect subjunctive (tuviera, tuviéramos,
ganaras, ganara) and the conditional (compraría, viajaríamos, escribirían, iría/iríamos/irían — note
"irse" reflexive conditional). This is the clearest, most systematic single-lesson presentation of
the Spanish contrary-to-fact conditional construction in the set so far. (Lección 36, throughout.)
