## Grammar points

### Optional reflexive use of "comprar" for personal-benefit emphasis

The instructor explicitly notes that "comprar" (to buy) can be used reflexively across all persons
("yo me compraría," "tú te comprarías," "nosotros nos compraríamos," "ellos se comprarían") to
emphasize buying something for oneself, alongside the plain transitive use — both grammatical, the
reflexive form foregrounding the beneficiary. (Lección 37, p. 4.)

### Conditional of poder ("podría/podrías/podríamos/podrían") for hypothetical ability/permission

Lección 37 introduces the conditional of "poder" inside "si"-conditional chains ("si vamos a la
fiesta, no podrías beber mucho" — if we go to the party, you won't be able to drink much), continuing
the present contrary-to-fact pattern from Lección 36 but shifting the apodosis verb to express
hypothetical capability rather than a simple action. (Lección 37, pp. 8-10.)

### Pluperfect subjunctive ("si hubiera(s)/hubiéramos + past participle") for past contrary-to-fact conditions

Lección 38 introduces the past-conditional pairing: "si" + pluperfect subjunctive describes an
unrealized past condition ("si hubieras ido a la frutería," if you had gone to the fruit shop; "si tú
los hubieras invitado," if you had invited them), paired with the conditional perfect in the main
clause (see next point) — this is the past-tense counterpart of Lección 36's present contrary-to-fact
"si tuviera...compraría" pattern. (Lección 38, throughout.)

### Conditional perfect ("habría/habrías/habríamos/habrían + past participle") for unrealized past results

The main-clause counterpart of the pluperfect-subjunctive "if" clause above: "habría comprado" (I
would have bought), "habría contratado" (she would have hired), "habríamos invitado" (we would have
invited), "habrían comprado" (they would have bought). The lesson also drills "habría" as the
conditional of the existential haber ("there would be" — "habría más ensalada en la fiesta") and
"sería" as the conditional of ser ("I would be" — "sería rico/rica"), rounding out the conditional
forms of Spanish's two most irregular high-frequency verbs alongside the regular pattern. Some rows
also mix a present contrary-to-fact ("si tuviera dinero," present imperfect subjunctive) with a past
conditional-perfect main clause ("habría contratado músicos... si tuviera dinero") — a looser mixed
conditional the lesson does not flag explicitly as distinct from the "pure" past/past pairing, worth
noting as a real attested mixed form rather than a strict textbook-clean two-tense pairing. (Lección
38, throughout, esp. pp. 1-6.)
