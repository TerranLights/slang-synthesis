## Grammar points

### Impersonal expressions (es importante/es mejor/es necesario) + que + subjunctive, with a specific subject

Lección 55 systematically drills "es importante que," "es mejor que," and "es necesario que" each
followed by a subjunctive verb whose subject is a specific named person: "es importante que se quede
en casa" (it's important that she stay home), "es mejor que el doctor lo examine" (it's better that
the doctor examine him), "es necesario que se queden en casa" (it's necessary that they stay home) —
drilled across every grammatical person (me/te/se/nos/se) of the reflexive verbs involved
(quedarse, tomarse). (Lección 55, throughout.)

### The same impersonal expressions + bare infinitive, when no specific subject is expressed

Lección 56 draws an explicit structural contrast: when the impersonal expression is *not* directed at
a particular person's action (a general statement, "in order to get better, one must rest" rather
than "it's necessary that *she* rests"), Spanish uses the **bare infinitive** instead of the
subjunctive: "para mejorarse es necesario descansar" (to get better, it's necessary to rest) rather
than "es necesario que descanse." The two lessons are explicitly designed as a contrasting pair —
Lección 56 opens by having the instructor state directly that "besides the subjunctive you can also
use the infinitive after es importante/es mejor/es necesario," making the subject-presence-vs-absence
distinction the organizing grammar point of the pair. The lesson also introduces two further
impersonal-superlative variants using the same infinitive pattern: "lo mejor es..." (the best thing
is to...) and "lo más importante es..." (the most important thing is to...). (Lección 56, throughout,
esp. pp. 1-2.)
