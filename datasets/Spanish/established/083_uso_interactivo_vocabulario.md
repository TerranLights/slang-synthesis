# Uso Interactivo del Vocabulario — Thematic Vocabulary Extraction

**Source:** *Uso Interactivo del Vocabulario* (PDFClerk/Quartz PDF, 163pp, image-only/vision-read). A thematic Spanish vocabulary workbook organized into 20 chapters, each built around a core topic. Each chapter opens with a 2-3 page "léxico" glossary spread (nouns grouped by sub-topic, then adjectives, then verbs, each with a blank column for the learner's own L1 translation — "¿Cómo se dice en tu lengua?"), followed by fill-in exercises ("ejercicios"), conversation prompts ("¡vamos a hablar!"), and situational prompts ("situaciones") that are workbook scaffolding rather than new lexical content. The book's stated selling point (per its "presentación" front matter, p.5) is systematic regional-variant coverage: native-speaker consultants from Argentina, Mexico, and Spain supplied alternate regional terms, marked in-text with a ➤ arrow pointing from the neutral/Spain headword to the regional synonym(s). This extraction focuses on those regional-variant chains plus thematic vocabulary not well covered by the broader-purpose vocabulary books already in this corpus (009 Practice Makes Perfect Spanish Vocabulary, 049 Visual Dictionary, 054 Spanish Around the House).

**Chapters (20):** 1 El cuerpo humano (the body) · 2 Los alimentos (food) · 3 La familia (family) · 4 La ropa (clothing) · 5 El tiempo y la tierra (weather/land) · 6 La vivienda (housing) · 7 La música (music) · 8 El coche (the car) · 9 Las profesiones (professions) · 10 Los viajes (travel) · 11 La ciudad (the city) · 12 Los deportes (sports) · 13 Los animales (animals) · 14 La salud y la enfermedad (health/illness) · 15 Las vacaciones en el mar (seaside vacations) · 16 La televisión (television) · 17 Los bancos (banks) · 18 Las fiestas (festivities) · 19 El orden público (public order/law) · 20 La enseñanza (education).

**Extraction approach:** Vision-read of the léxico spread for each chapter (the dense word-list pages); exercise/conversation pages were skimmed for standalone idiomatic content (refranes/proverbs appearing in "¡vamos a hablar!" prompts) but their blank-answer exercise content was not transcribed (no new lexical data). Regional variants are recorded as separate rows with Geographic Scope narrowed to the attested region(s) and a cross-reference in Notes to the neutral/Spain headword they vary from.

## Overall summary for this source

Across the 20 chapters sampled, **household/appliance vocabulary (ch.6) and automotive vocabulary (ch.8) showed the densest three-way Spain/Argentina/Mexico splits**, followed closely by food (ch.2) and clothing (ch.4) — consistent with the real-world pattern that culturally/technologically mediated domains (consumer goods, appliances, cars) fragment regionally far more than physically/biologically grounded domains (body parts, weather, animals, basic medicine), which stayed close to pan-Hispanic uniformity. Two chapters (food, ch.2, and festivities, ch.18) contained an unusual and valuable sub-category beyond simple naming variation: **explicit referential gaps**, where the source states a product or cultural practice simply does not exist in Argentina (certain food items; Spain-specific festival traditions — encierro, pregón, romería, saeta, verbena, torear). Chapter 10 (travel) independently reproduced the taboo-avoidance "coger→tomar" substitution already flagged as a flagship finding elsewhere in this corpus's Spanish Phase 1 work, cross-validating it from an unrelated source. Chapter 20 (education) surfaced a genuine institutional-semantics finding (Mexican colegio=private school vs. escuela=public school) distinct from ordinary lexical substitution. No other chapters of this 163-page book were sampled beyond the 20 covered above (the book's content ends at the chapters; pp.161-163 are an answer key ["claves"] for the fill-in exercises, not new vocabulary, and were not transcribed).
