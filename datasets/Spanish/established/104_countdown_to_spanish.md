# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *Countdown to Spanish: Learn to Communicate in 24 Hours*

**Source:** Gail Stein, *Countdown to Spanish* (McGraw-Hill, 2004). 337-page PDF, confirmed clean
text layer via `pdftotext` (no OCR corruption observed). Structured as 24 one-hour lessons counting
down from "24:00" to "00:00," each built around a Master-These-Skills topic list and ending with a
"Time's Up!" quiz. Units 1-11ish are grammar-forward (pronunciation, nouns, present/past/future
tenses, adjectives/adverbs); units 10-01 pivot to situational/topic vocabulary (making
acquaintances, invitations, directions, specialty shops, opinions, comparisons, hotels, food,
medical needs, clothing, travel, banking, business). Appendix A is "24 Important Words and
Phrases," Appendix B a verb glossary.

**Redundancy assessment:** this is a comprehensive 24-chapter course and the large majority of its
grammar content (pronunciation, noun gender, present/preterite/imperfect/future/conditional
tenses, subjunctive mood across multiple chapters, adjective/adverb agreement, comparatives,
passive voice, object pronouns) duplicates ground already extensively covered by this corpus's
grammar cluster (005-022, 060-078, etc.) — **not re-extracted**. The 24-basic-phrases appendix
duplicates existing phrasebooks (039-041, etc.) and is likewise skipped. What follows is this
book's genuinely distinctive content: its specialty-shop situational vocabulary (optician, camera
shop, jeweler, dry cleaner — a practical-errands cluster not well covered elsewhere in this
corpus), its indifference/indecision phrase bank, modern office/business vocabulary (photocopies,
faxes, computers, stock market), and a couple of nuanced usage points (pero vs. sino; the neuter
lo) that are easy to state briefly and genuinely useful.

**Entry count:** 24 rows, sampled from the topic-vocabulary chapters (11:00 "Seeking Help" through
01:00 "The Language of Business"), not an exhaustive pass of all 24 hours.
