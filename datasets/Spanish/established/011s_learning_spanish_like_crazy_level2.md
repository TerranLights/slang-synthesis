## Grammar points

### Pluperfect combined with "antes de que" + imperfect subjunctive

Bonus Lesson G extends the pluperfect ("había visto," "había escrito," "habíamos ido") into
combination with "antes de que" (before) taking the **imperfect subjunctive** in its subordinate
clause — not the pluperfect subjunctive — e.g. "habíamos ido a ese teatro antes de que se quemara" (we
had gone to that theater before it burned down) and "había vuelto a casa antes de que su padre
falleciera" (she had returned home before her father passed away). This combines two constructions
drilled separately earlier in the course (Lección 58's "antes de que" + subjunctive, and Bonus Lessons
F-G's pluperfect indicative) into a single compound sentence type, worth noting as a synthesis point
for later mechanics analysis. (Bonus Lesson G, pp. 3-6.)

### Pluperfect continued: robbery/wedding domain, plus a closing preterite-negative → pluperfect transformation drill

Bonus Lesson H continues the same pluperfect + "antes de que"-subjunctive pattern across a
thief/break-in narrative ("habían abierto la tienda antes de que los ladrones forzaran la entrada")
and a wedding/honeymoon narrative ("habían hecho un viaje a Canadá antes de la boda"). Its closing
exercise is a distinct drill type: each prompt is a preterite **negative** statement ("no viste la
película ayer" — you didn't see the movie yesterday), and the correct response reframes it as a
pluperfect **affirmative** describing an earlier occasion when the action *had* in fact happened ("ya
la había visto la semana anterior" — I had already seen it the week before) — a useful worked contrast
between "hasn't happened yet" (simple negative past) and "already happened earlier" (pluperfect).
(Bonus Lesson H, pp. 7-8.)
