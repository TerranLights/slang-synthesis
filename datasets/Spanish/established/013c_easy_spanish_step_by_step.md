## Grammar points

### Time expressions with *hacer* (how long)

To ask how long someone has been doing something, Spanish uses the **present** tense (unlike
English, which uses present perfect): **¿Cuánto tiempo hace que** + verb in present tense? — "How
long has ___ been ___ing?" (literally "How much time does it make that ___"). To answer: **Hace** +
length of time + **que** + verb in present tense — e.g. **Hace quince años que yo vivo aquí** ("I
have been living here for fifteen years," lit. "It makes fifteen years that I live here"). The
book's own pedagogical note flags this as a common point of confusion precisely because the
underlying tense doesn't match the English translation's tense (p. 94).

### Possessive-vs-definite-article convention with body parts

Spanish strongly prefers the definite article (**el**, **la**, **los**, **las**) over a possessive
adjective when referring to parts of the body, where English requires a possessive: **Ella tiene un
problema en el pie** = "She has a problem with **her foot**" (not *su pie*); **El niño tiene las
manos sucias** = "The boy has dirty hands" (i.e., "his hands," not *sus manos*) (p. 92). Ownership is
understood from the subject of the sentence rather than marked on the noun.

### *Que* as relative pronoun vs. conjunction

**Que** (English "that/which/who") is Spanish's most common relative pronoun, usable for persons or
things, singular or plural (**El programa que miro los viernes es interesante**). As the object of a
preposition, **que** can refer only to things, never persons (**El libro en que escribimos es
viejo**; **Ella tiene un bastón con que caminar**). As a conjunction joining a main clause to a
dependent clause (**El maestro sabe que los estudiantes entienden**), **que** is **never omitted** in
Spanish — unlike English "that," which is frequently dropped ("the program I watch" / "the program
that I watch" are both correct in English, but Spanish requires **que** in the equivalent
construction) (p. 89).
