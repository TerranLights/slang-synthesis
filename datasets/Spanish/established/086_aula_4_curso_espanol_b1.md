**Source:** Jaime Corpas, Agustín Garmendia, Carmen Soriano (coord. pedagógica Neus Sans Baulenas),

note, full table of contents) was read in full, and a representative sample of content pages was

## This is explicitly a Peninsular-Spanish, Spain-immersion course

Confirmed from the publisher's own methodology note (p. 4): **Aula** was designed specifically for
**intensive/semi-intensive Spanish courses taught in Spain to short-stay immersion students**
("cursos intensivos o semiintensivos en situación de inmersión" — students "en su breve estancia"
with "un contacto diario con la realidad española"), published by Difusión (Barcelona). This is
the second vision-required book in this Wave 4 dispatch and, together with established/087 and
089, is part of this dispatch's push to correct the corpus's existing Latin-America lean. Level B1
per the Common European Framework (Marco de Referencia Europeo).

## Book structure (10 units + appendices)

Each of the 10 units follows a fixed 5-block structure: **1. Comprender** (reading/listening
comprehension texts), **2. Explorar y reflexionar** (guided grammar-discovery from corpus
samples), **3. Practicar y comunicar** (communicative production tasks), **4. Viajar** (cultural
content: songs, poetry, games), **5. Más** (extra formal-practice exercises); plus a shared
**Verbos** appendix (full regular/irregular conjugation tables) and an **Agenda del estudiante**
appendix (practical per-country reference directory for Latin American countries — population,
phone codes, broadcasters, newspapers, and a "Te sugerimos" book/film/album recommendation per
country; this directory-style reference content, being phone codes and media-outlet URLs rather
than vocabulary or grammar, was not tabulated below as it carries no linguistic content of its
own).

## Full B1 grammar/functional syllabus (from the table of contents, pp. 6–7)

1. **¿Se te dan bien las lenguas?** — talking about abilities/emotions (`dar vergüenza/miedo`,
   `ponerse nervioso/a`); connecting clauses with `aunque`; pronominal verbs.
2. **El turista accidental** — narrating past events; anecdote-telling devices; expressing
   interest while listening; cause/consequence (`como`, `porque`, `así que`); Pretérito Indefinido
   vs. Imperfecto contrast; Pretérito Pluscuamperfecto.
3. **¡Basta ya!** — expressing demands/complaints/needs; `me parece muy bien/mal... que` +
   present subjunctive; `querer/pedir/exigir/necesitar` + infinitive vs. `+ que` + subjunctive;
   proposing solutions (`deberíamos/deberían/se debería/habría que`); `cuando` + subjunctive;
   present subjunctive.
4. **Tenemos que hablar** — expressing interests/feelings; talking about relationships; showing
   disagreement across registers; softening disagreement; counter-arguing.
5. **De diseño** — describing object features/function; superlatives in `-ísimo/a/os/as`;
   adjective modifiers (`excesivamente`, `demasiado`); exclamatives (`¡qué…!`, `¡qué… tan/más…!`);
   relative clauses with preposition; indicative/subjunctive in relative clauses.
6. **Misterios y enigmas** — hypothesizing/conjecturing; relating mysterious events; Futuro
   Simple/Compuesto for epistemic hypothesis; indicative/subjunctive constructions for degrees of
   certainty; `creer`/`creerse`.
7. **Buenas noticias** — writing/discussing a news item; passive voice; verbs of information
   transmission (`manifestar`, `declarar`); media vocabulary.
8. **Yo nunca lo haría** — giving advice; evoking imaginary situations (`si fuera/estuviera...` +
   conditional); opining on behavior; expressing prior ignorance (`no sabía que...`); conditional
   form/uses; Pretérito Imperfecto de Subjuntivo of `ser`/`estar`/`poder`.
9. **¿Y qué te dijo?** — transmitting orders/requests/advice; direct vs. indirect speech;
   Pretérito Imperfecto de Subjuntivo form/uses.
10. **América** — a Latin-America-knowledge review quiz unit; past-narration recap; reported
    speech recap; Presente de Subjuntivo recap; expressing knowledge/lack of knowledge about a
    topic.

## Grammar points

### Pretérito Imperfecto de Subjuntivo — full morphology, both `-ra` and `-se` paradigms

Unit 9's Grammar reference confirms this book teaches **both** historically parallel forms as
equally valid and interchangeable (common in Peninsular pedagogy, where both forms remain in
everyday use, unlike some Latin American varieties that favor `-ra` almost exclusively in speech):
root = 3rd-person-plural Pretérito Indefinido minus `-on` (e.g. `compraron` → `compra-`), plus
endings `-a/-as/-a/-amos/-ais/-an` (comprara, compraras, comprara, compráramos, comprarais,
compraran) **or equivalently** `-se/-ses/-se/-semos/-seis/-sen` (comprase, comprases, comprase,
comprásemos, compraseis, comprasen) — book explicitly cross-lists both for `hablar`, `comprar`,
`comer`, `saber`, `hacer`, `vivir`, `decir`, `producir`. Governed the same way as the present
subjunctive (triggered after `querer/exigir/encantar`-type verbs + `que`, when the governing verb
is in a past tense, conditional, or imperfect).

### Reported speech (estilo indirecto) — full deictic-shift table

Unit 9 gives a clean reference table for how time/space/person deictics shift when reporting
someone else's words indirectly rather than quoting them directly (estilo directo): `hoy` → `ese/
aquel día`; `mañana` → `el día siguiente`; `esta tarde` → `esa/aquella tarde`; `dentro de tres
días` → `al cabo de tres días`; `la semana que viene` → `la semana siguiente`; `aquí` → `allí`
(when the reporting speaker is no longer in that location); `esto` → `eso/aquello`; `mi hermana`
→ `su hermana`. Also covers the governing-verb-tense shift: present → `que` + present (if report
is immediate) or `que` + imperfect indicative (if more removed); imperative → `que` + imperative
(if order still stands) or `que` + imperfect subjunctive (if order has lapsed/been fulfilled
already) — paired with the "orden vigente vs. orden ya no vigente" (order still standing vs. no
longer standing) distinction, which determines present subjunctive vs. imperfect subjunctive when
transmitting a still-open vs. already-resolved order/request/recommendation.

## Relationship to other Spanish `established/` files

This file's colloquial-register cluster (`¡Anda ya!`, `hombre` as discourse particle, `igual` as
epistemic hedge, `¡Qué rollo!`, `chico para todo`) is new to this corpus and specifically
Peninsular — none of it overlaps with existing LatAm-leaning sources. `¡Qué va!` is now
cross-confirmed from three independent sources within this single Wave 4 dispatch alone
(established/087, 089, and this file), making it one of the most solidly attested colloquialisms
in the entire Spanish corpus. The reverse-construction (`gustar`-type) verb list here is treated
as a supplement to, not a replacement for, established/076's fuller treatment of the same
construction family — the "usage note on `mucho`-rejection" and the explicit `que`+subjunctive
case are this book's own distinct contribution.
