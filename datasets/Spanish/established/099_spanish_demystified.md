## Scope note and redundancy assessment

This is a large (20-chapter), standard-progression, Latin-American-focused beginner-to-intermediate
course (alphabet/pronunciation → nouns/articles → ser/estar → present tense → subjunctive → preterite/
imperfect → para/por). The grammar sequence itself is heavily redundant with material already
extracted many times over in this corpus (ser/estar, present/preterite/imperfect, subjunctive
formation, direct/indirect object pronouns, etc.) and was NOT re-extracted here.

Distinctive content that IS extracted below, found by targeted sampling of sections that stood out
from the TOC as less commonly covered:
1. An explicit three-way **regional pronunciation overview** (Ch. 1) naming specific phenomena by
   region — notably rare to see this compactly stated in a beginner course rather than scattered.
2. **Augmentative suffixes** (-ón/-ona, -ote/-ota, -azo/-aza) with an explicit warning that most
   augmentative-suffixed forms are slangy/pejorative (worked example: cabrón) — directly relevant to
   this project's register-mapping goals, and augmentatives are less commonly taught than diminutives.
3. A **"saying no/yes/maybe" colloquial-register phrase set** (Ch. 20-adjacent oral practice).
4. A **"Not-So-Simple Expressions for Simple Concepts"** chapter section — a genuinely distinctive
   pedagogical device: it tackles English concepts that don't map 1:1 onto a single Spanish verb
   (to become, to happen, to take, to miss, to have fun), laying out the competing Spanish verbs for
   each with register and dialect notes. The "to take" entry's coger note (neutral in Spain, vulgar/
   sexual in most of Latin America) is a notable taboo-avoidance/register-split finding consistent
   with this project's established interest in such splits.
5. A **Spanish-speaking-world currency vocabulary list** (country-by-country) plus a note on Spain's
   peseta→euro transition and Peru's sol→inti→nuevo sol hyperinflation history — distinctive
   historical/geographic vocabulary content not seen elsewhere in the corpus.
6. **Expressions with por** — a fixed-phrase idiom list.

Overall: moderate-to-good distinctive value, concentrated in a handful of sections scattered across
an otherwise heavily redundant standard course.
