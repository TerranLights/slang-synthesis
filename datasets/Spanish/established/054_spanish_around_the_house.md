# Spanish Around the House: The Quick Guide to Communicating with Your Spanish-Speaking Employees — José M. Díaz and María F. Nadel (McGraw-Hill, 2005)

Source: `Spanish Around the House.pdf`, 300 pages, clean text-layer PDF, `pdftotext -layout` extraction (PDF-page-to-book-page offset confirmed as +20: book page 1 = PDF page 21).

## Coverage-approach decision (read first)

**The dispatch instructions characterized this book as narrow-scope ("domestic/household-topic vocabulary — likely full coverage appropriate given its narrow scope"), but reading the full table of contents shows this assumption does not hold.** The book is explicitly subtitled "The Quick Guide to Communicating with Your Spanish-Speaking Employees" and its own introduction states it is "divided into fourteen chapters" meant to equip an English-speaking employer to communicate with Spanish-speaking domestic staff across effectively the entire domain of household and family life: Job Application and Interview, The Family, The House, The Kitchen, The Garden/Patio/Pets/Farm Animals, Fixing the House (trades), Places Around Town, The Service/Gas Station, Shopping, Clothing, Family Health, Taking Care of the Children, Social/Cultural Activities, and Numbers/Days/Time — plus three large appendices (pronunciation, grammar essentials, and a 70-page bidirectional dictionary, pp. 165–272). This is not narrow at all; it is a near-comprehensive domestic-life vocabulary book, and the bulk of its content (family relations, food, clothing, body parts, numbers) duplicates ground already covered in much greater depth elsewhere in this corpus (Madrigal's Magic Key glossary `004b`, the Practice Makes Perfect series `005`–`009d`).

**Decision: targeted chapter sampling, not full extraction**, following the same representative-sampling logic used for items 051–053 above, but targeted specifically at this book's one genuinely distinctive contribution: **it is the only source in this corpus written in the employer-to-domestic-employee command/instruction register.** Every other Spanish source in this project teaches the learner to speak *as* a Spanish speaker (conversational, touristic, literary, or academic registers); this book instead teaches an English-speaking employer how to *direct* a Spanish-speaking worker — formal (usted) imperative and periphrastic-obligation constructions, HR/interview vocabulary, contractor-negotiation phrasing, and specialized occupational-tool vocabulary (cleaning supplies, garden tools, pool-maintenance equipment, childcare items) not found in any other established file for this language. That register is this book's real non-redundant contribution, so extraction below concentrates on the chapters and subsections that carry it: **Chapter 1 (Job Application and Interview)**, **Chapter 3's "Chores and Cleaning the House" and "Laundry Room" sections**, **Chapter 5's "Taking Care of the Garden/Pool/Pets" sections**, **Chapter 6's "Trades/Explaining Problems/Finalizing the Contract" sections**, and **Chapter 12's "Daily Routine/Other Obligations" sections**. Chapters/sections covering ground this project's corpus already covers thoroughly (The Family's relative-term list, food vocabulary, clothing, body parts/health terminology, numbers/days/time, flower/tree botanical lists) were read for context but not transcribed in full, per this project's non-redundant-supplement convention; a brief summary note marks what was skipped and why.

It would be impossible to cover all the regionalisms," and in practice almost nothing in the sampled chapters is regionally marked, a notable contrast with items 051 and 053 above.
