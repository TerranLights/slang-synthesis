folder has two overlapping-numbered PDF series covering lessons 61+: `Leccion##-final.pdf` (61–75) and
`Spanish_Lesson_##.pdf` (61–80). A matching-number spot check (Lección 61: Rubén/Pamela dialogue about

series are **not duplicates of the same lesson** — they are two genuinely different lessons that happen

bundled together in this course package. **Decision: extract both series in full**, each as its own
numbered lesson within this file group (labeled `Leccion NN (final series)` vs. `Lesson NN
(Spanish_Lesson series)` in each Grammar Points header) rather than picking one as a duplicate to skip.

## Grammar points

### Futuro hipotético perfecto (future perfect), -ar verbs — Lección 61

Paraphrase (p.1): the "futuro hipotético perfecto"/future perfect tense expresses an event that will be
completed in the future, before another future event or a stated future point — "will have eaten," "will
have spoken," "will have written." Lección 61 restricts drilling to `-ar` verbs (e.g. `habrá llegado`,
`habrán practicado`, `habrán rezado`) using `haber` (future: he/habrás/habrá/habremos/habréis/habrán) +
past participle.

### Futuro hipotético perfecto, -er verbs — Lección 63

Same tense, now drilled with `-er` verbs (e.g. `habrá atraído`, `habrán leído`, `habrá bebido`, `habrá
comido`). Structurally identical construction (`haber` future conjugation + participle), only the
participle-formation rule differs (`-er` → `-ido`).

### Futuro hipotético perfecto, -ir verbs — Lección 65

Same tense again, now with `-ir` verbs (e.g. `habrá dicho` [irregular participle of `decir`], `habrá
escrito` [irregular participle of `escribir`], `habrá compartido`, `se habrá arrepentido`). Confirms the
course's three-lesson pattern of introducing one new tense across three consecutive lessons, one verb
conjugation class per lesson, reusing the same thematic vocabulary set (religion in 61–64, then
pregnancy/family news in 65) as the drilling context.
