# Spanish — Established Vocabulary: Must-Know Spanish 4000 Words (Nissenberg)

**Source:** Gilda Nissenberg, *Must-Know Spanish: Essential Words for a Successful Vocabulary*
(McGraw-Hill, 2006), 274 pp. Clean text-layer PDF (`pdftotext`/`pdftotext -layout` cross-checked,
no vision reading needed). Twelve thematic units (Communicating with Others; The Human Body,
Health, and Medicine; Education; Shopping; A Place to Live; Work and the Business World; Leisure
Time; Getting Around; Society and Government; Nature and the Environment; Measurements, Numbers,
and Time) plus 100 drill exercises and an answer key.

**Coverage decision (non-redundant-supplement rule applied):** This book's general thematic
word lists (clothing, food, furniture, basic travel, numbers, days/months, everyday body/health
vocabulary) heavily overlap with material already captured across this language's large existing
corpus — the Practice Makes Perfect Spanish Vocabulary series (`009`/`009b`-`009d`), the visual
dictionary (`049`), the student dictionary (`050`), the four phrasebooks (`039`-`041`, `051`), and
the around-the-house/business glossaries (`054`, `059`). Re-extracting those lists would be pure
duplication. Instead this file captures only what is genuinely new or distinctive:

1. **All 50 "Must-Know Tips"** — the book's sidebar annotations on false cognates, regional
   lexical splits, taboo/vulgar-connotation warnings, slang, and idioms. These are exactly the
   register- and region-marked content this project needs, and nothing elsewhere in the corpus
   has systematically collected this book's specific set of regionalisms (e.g., *coger* as vulgar
   in Argentina, *concha* as vulgar in the Southern Cone, *bicho* as vulgar in Puerto Rico, the
   bus-word map *colectivo*/*camión*/*guagua*, *la mota* as Mexican/Central American slang for
   marijuana). Pure-grammar tips (e.g., *ser* vs. *estar*, double negatives, demonstrative accents)
   are still logged with their example lexical item, since each tip anchors on at least one
   concrete word or phrase.
2. **Computers and Software vocabulary** (Education unit) — a technology-register word field not
   previously captured in this corpus's older/grammar-oriented sources.
3. **Government and Politics, Law and Order, and International Politics/Military/Terrorism
   vocabulary** (Society and Government unit, in full) — specialized civic/legal/military
   terminology not systematically covered elsewhere in the established corpus (prior sources
   mention these domains only in passing, not as dedicated word fields).
4. **Ecology and Pollution vocabulary** (Nature and the Environment unit) — an environmental
   register field, likewise new to this corpus.

Skipped as redundant: Education (general schooling/degrees), Shopping, A Place to Live, Work
(general business terms — largely covered by `034`-`037`, `059`), Leisure Time (arts/sports —
largely covered by phrasebooks), Getting Around (transportation/travel — covered by `039`-`041`),
general Nature/Farming/Animals lists, and Measurements/Numbers/Time (covered by `009`/grammar
texts). Exercise and answer-key pages are skipped throughout (drill content, not new vocabulary).

Source Type throughout: `grammar_reference`. Transcription Confidence: `verified` (clean digital
text layer; ligature artifacts like "oﬁcina"/"ﬁrmar" normalized to standard spelling during
extraction). Vision Reading Confidence: `n/a` (text-layer read). Attested Era throughout:
"contemporary (source published 2006)". Attested Region/Geographic Scope: `—` unless the tip
itself names a country or region, in which case that region is recorded (never guess-filled).
