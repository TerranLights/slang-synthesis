# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *En Activo: Practical Business Spanish* (sampled, non-redundant supplement)

**Source:** Esther Santamaría Iglesias and Helen Jones, *En Activo: Practical Business Spanish*
(Routledge, London and New York, first published 2008; Taylor & Francis e-Library edition).
305 PDF pages. A task-based business-Spanish course structured as 17 thematic "Unidades," each
with six lettered sections (A–F) that mix a reading/interview, a "Recuerde que..." grammar box,
a vocabulary/register point, a professional situation, a document-creation task, and an
Internet-research task. Units cover: bureaucracy/protocol, telecommunications/mobile phones,
business expansion, equal-opportunity employment, job-hunting/employment law, banking, the stock
market, health-sector work, customer complaints, import/export, media, tourism, real
estate/construction, and more (17 units total — this book is noticeably broader in sector
coverage than any single business-Spanish title already in this corpus).

**Text layer:** confirmed via `pdftotext`, clean extraction throughout the sampled pages (no
font-cipher or garbling issues — standard embedded subset fonts). Transcription Confidence: verified.

**Redundancy check against already-extracted business-Spanish files** (`034_espanol_comercial.md`,
`035_manual_of_business_spanish.md` + glossary scratch files, `036_spanish_business_correspondence.md`,
`037_spanish_business_situations.md`, `059_spanish_english_business_glossary.md` [confirmed duplicate
of 035]): **this is NOT a duplicate.** `034`'s units (Empresa y empresarios, Productos y mercado,
Recursos Humanos, Márketing, El sistema fiscal, Bancos y finanzas, Mercados financieros,
Negociaciones) and `036`/`037`'s correspondence/situations focus do not overlap with *En Activo*'s
distinctive content: (1) this book explicitly foregrounds **Spain-vs-Latin-America regional
vocabulary variation** as a recurring pedagogical feature (the back-cover blurb specifically
praises its "different varieties and registers of Spanish") — a dimension none of the other four
business titles treat systematically; (2) it has a dedicated **SMS/texting-abbreviation register**
section not found anywhere else in the business cluster or, so far, elsewhere in the whole Spanish
corpus; (3) it threads **Latin legal/business set phrases, paronym pairs, and acronym/abbreviation
inventories** through its units in a way the glossary-style books (035/059) don't, since those are
bare bilingual word lists rather than usage-in-context presentations. Genuine overlap exists only
at the shallow level of generic business-letter vocabulary (already fully covered by 035/036) and
basic banking terms (covered by 034) — that overlapping material is skipped here.

**Coverage note.** Per the project's coverage rule, this extraction skips the book's heavy
pedagogical padding (fill-in-the-blank verb/preposition drills, dialogue-completion exercises,
biography comprehension questionnaires, role-play prompts) wholesale. What's extracted below is a
representative sample — not an exhaustive page-by-page pass of all 305 pages/17 units — focused on
the genuinely new content flagged above: the SMS register, Latin set phrases, paronym pairs,
acronym inventories, the Spain/Latin-America regional house-vocabulary comparison, a handful of
idiomatic expressions drawn from the book's biography/interview texts, and a few grammar points
given with distinctive business-register example sentences (adjective-position meaning shift,
irregular comparative/superlative forms, numeral-writing conventions, subjunctive trigger phrases).
Sampled from Unidades 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 11, 12, 13, and 15 (of 17).

**Entry count:** 47 vocabulary/grammar/idiom rows, drawn from a sampled subset of a 305-page,
17-unit course.
