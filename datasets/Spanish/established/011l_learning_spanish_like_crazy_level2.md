## Grammar points

### Subjunctive of -er/-ir verbs and irregular "ir" in the volition-subjunctive pattern

Lección 53 extends Lessons 51-52's volition-triggered subjunctive to -er/-ir verbs, explicitly
stating the rule that these verbs take an "-a" vowel marker in the subjunctive (the mirror image of
-ar verbs taking "-e," already seen in the imperative lessons) — "quiero que vengas" (venir),
"deseamos que vaya" / "desea que vayamos" (ir, highly irregular stem "vay-"), "prefiere que coma"
(comer). The irregular subjunctive stem of "ir" (vaya/vayas/vaya/vayamos/vayan) is drilled
extensively across every person. (Lección 53, throughout.)

### Subjunctive triggered by decir que and pedir que (reported commands/requests)

Lección 54 adds two further triggers to the same subjunctive-of-volition family: "decir que" used in
its command sense (not its reporting-a-fact sense) — "le digo que coma vegetales" (I tell him to eat
vegetables, not "I tell him he eats vegetables") — and "pedir que" (to ask/request that) — "te pido
que abras la tienda" (I ask you to open the store). The lesson implicitly contrasts "decir que +
subjunctive" (a command) against a hypothetical "decir que + indicative" (a simple statement of
fact), though the source does not state this contrast explicitly — it is evident from context, since
every drilled example here uses "decir" to convey an instruction. (Lección 54, pp. 2-7.)
