## Coverage-approach decision (read first)

The book's 14 chapters split cleanly into two groups. **Chapters 1–6** (Pronunciation Guide, Numbers, Time, Days/Months/Dates/Seasons/Holidays/Weather, and — by chapter-number inference — a greetings/introductions chapter plus "Good Wishes/Words of Encouragement/Words of Praise") are generic, general-audience survival-Spanish content that heavily duplicates this corpus's existing, far more thorough grammar references (017, 019, 021, 022) and vocabulary lists; these were not re-extracted. **Chapters 7–14** (Day Care and Pre-K; Registration; In/Around/After School; Medical — including an "Abuse" subsection; Content Areas/school subjects; **Expectations and Consequences** — dress codes, lateness, absence, discipline, grades, conferences; **The Special-Needs Student** — IEP, parents' rights, developmental/medical-condition vocabulary; Planning for the Future — graduation, job and college applications) carry this book's one genuinely distinctive contribution: **a professional, institutional, school-specific register that exists nowhere else in this project's Spanish corpus.** Per the dispatch instructions, this register (professional/educational, "technical.education") is one this project's corpus likely lacks, so extraction below concentrates there, with **Chapter 12 ("Expectations and Consequences") and Chapter 13 ("The Special-Needs Student") sampled most heavily**, since they contain this book's richest and most load-bearing unique material: formal school-discipline directive language and special-education/legal-rights terminology, respectively.

**A structural feature worth flagging before the tables below:** essentially every phrase chapters 7–14 is built on a **three-way addressee-register template**, explicitly explained in a note at the start of each relevant chapter: (1) talking *about* a student in the third person to that student's parent, (2) addressing the student directly using formal *usted*, and (3) addressing the student directly using informal *tú* (marked in brackets). A single Spanish sentence is given with the verb form for register (1)/(2) in the main line and the *tú*-register verb ending or pronoun swapped in via a bracketed alternative, e.g., "Necesita[s] dos bolígrafos rojos" = "Necesita dos bolígrafos rojos" (about the student / to the student formally) vs. "Necesitas dos bolígrafos rojos" (to the student informally). This is a clean, systematically-documented three-tier formality/addressee system distinct from the simpler tú/usted binary taught in this corpus's general grammar references — genuinely useful as a worked example of a language teaching a *third* party-reference register (talking about someone to a third party) stacked onto the usual two-person formality axis, relevant to any conlang register system that wants more than a binary formal/informal split.
