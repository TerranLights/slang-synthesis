# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Hide This Spanish CD (Berlitz)

**Source:** Isabel Mendoza & Mariam Pérez Roch (writers), *Hide This Spanish CD* (Berlitz
Publishing/APA Publications, first printing October 2006, published 2007), full 26-page companion
booklet to an audio CD — 14 short tracks/chapters: intro, the basics, hi and bye, gettin' around,
money, hotel, food, drinks, havin' fun, sports/games/gambling, makin' friends, shopping, internet
and phone, damn it! PDF has **no text layer at all** (`pdftotext` returned 0 lines; `pdfinfo`/
`pdfimages` confirm every page is a scanned JPEG image, 1410x1028px at 204 DPI for the body pages).
All 26 pages were read directly via vision (rendered to JPEG at 150 DPI with `pdftoppm`, then read
page-by-page). Transcription Confidence = n/a for every row (no OCR/text-layer transcription was
involved — all content below is a direct vision read). Vision Reading Confidence = `verified` for
every row unless noted otherwise — the booklet's print is large, clean, typewriter-style display
font for headers and clean sans-serif body text throughout; no handwriting, no degraded scan
quality, no ambiguous characters were encountered on any of the 26 pages.

**Why this book is high-value despite its small size:** it is explicitly marketed as teaching "cool
Spanish lingo" that "can get you into trouble" (per its own intro), aimed at bar/club/dating/party
scenarios. It uses its own two-level register-flagging system via thermometer icons placed directly
after a term: a single-thermometer icon = "hot language," a double-thermometer icon = "very hot
language" (per the book's own "Key" page, p. 4) — mapped below to `slang` and `taboo` respectively.
It also uses ♂/♀ gender icons to mark male-speaker-only vs. female-speaker-only phrasing. A dedicated
final chapter, "damn it!," is entirely devoted to curses/profanity.

**Coverage approach:** this is a very short, focused booklet (26 pages, ~14 short chapters) — every
chapter was read page-by-page via vision and essentially all distinctive/colloquial content was
captured below (this is near-exhaustive for this source, unlike the larger dictionary-style books in
this wave). Deliberately thin/skipped: large stretches of completely neutral tourist-phrasebook
content with no register value (basic numbers, "where's the bathroom," credit-card/payment phrases,
basic transportation/hotel/shopping/internet vocabulary) — these are standard survival-Spanish
phrases this project's corpus already covers extensively and the booklet itself treats as filler
between its "hot language" call-outs.

**Non-redundant-supplement note:** this booklet's "pickup line," "breakup insult," and "cursing"
content is largely new relative to *Spanish Among Amigos* (established/042) and *Spanish in Your
Face!* (established/043, this same wave) — it is explicitly a flirting/partying/dating phrasebook
rather than a general-conversation or personality-description dictionary, so its headline content
(pickup lines, sexual-health vocabulary, breakup insults, swearing) does not duplicate either prior
book's focus, though a few individual swear words naturally recur across all three (joder, puta,
cabrón) given how small the core Spanish profanity vocabulary is.
