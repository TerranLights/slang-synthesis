## Grammar points

### Feminine nouns forced to the masculine singular article (el agua rule)

Spanish nouns that are grammatically feminine but begin with a **stressed** `a` or `ha` sound take
the masculine singular definite article **el** (not **la**) purely to avoid two stressed
low-vowel sounds colliding at the word boundary; the plural reverts to the expected feminine **las**.
Confirmed with four examples: **el agua** "the water" / **las aguas**, **el águila** "the eagle" /
**las águilas**, **el alma** "the soul" / **las almas**, **el arma** "the weapon" / **las armas**,
**el hacha** "the axe" / **las hachas** (p. 52). Adjectives modifying these nouns still agree as
feminine: **el agua fría** "the cold water," not *el agua frío*. This is a common beginner
stumbling point (the noun "looks" masculine only in the singular article) and is worth flagging for
mechanics-analysis work on gender-agreement exceptions, though it is core standard grammar rather
than a novel finding.
