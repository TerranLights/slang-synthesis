## Grammar points

### Plural formal imperative (ustedes) as the single form for group commands

Lección 43 systematically drills the "ustedes" imperative form (identical for both formal and
informal group address in most of Latin America, per the source's own usage pattern here — the
lesson does not distinguish a plural "vosotros" form at all): "digan," "cierren," "abran," "salgan,"
"saquen," "pongan," "metan," "escuchen," "hagan," "traigan," "conduzcan," "fumen," each shown in both
affirmative and negative forms within a single extended teacher-to-students dialogue frame. This
continues and completes the imperative paradigm begun in Lección 40-42 (singular formal/informal) by
supplying its plural counterpart. (Lección 43, throughout.)

### Simple future tense: regular formation and irregular high-frequency verbs

Lección 44 introduces the simple future by attaching a single set of endings (-é, -ás, -á, -emos,
-án) directly onto the full infinitive, regular across all three conjugation classes (tomaré,
beberé, comeré, escribiré) — contrasted with the conditional's identical suffix set but different
attachment behavior already drilled in Lessons 35-39. The lesson's closing transformation drill
(preterite prompt → future response) additionally surfaces the irregular futures of estar (estaré),
ir (iré), ver (veré), dar (darán), and traer (traeré) alongside regular forms, giving a compact
contrastive preterite/future pairing across ten verbs in a row. (Lección 44, throughout, esp. pp.
8-10.)

### Explicit regional lexical variant: jugo vs. sumo

The instructor explicitly attributes "sumo" (juice) to Spain as the regional counterpart of the
general Latin American "jugo" — one of relatively few instances in this lesson set where a regional
variant is tied to a specific, named country rather than a vague "some countries." (Lección 44, p.
4.)
