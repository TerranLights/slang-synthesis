# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Reference: Carnaval (Lecturas de Español, Nivel Elemental 1)

**Source:** *Carnaval* (Ramón Fernández Numen, Edinumen, "Lecturas de Español" series, Nivel
Elemental 1, 37 PDF pages — each PDF page is a scanned double-page spread, so the book's own
printed pagination runs to p. 74/75; "p. N" references below use the book's own printed page
numbers, visible in the scans — vision-required PDF, confirmed: `pdftotext` extraction is
essentially empty). Read via vision across the full narrative (pp. 1–58 of the book's own
numbering) plus a sample of the pre/post-reading apparatus to confirm its content.

**Series/apparatus:** sibling book to `110_amnesia_nivel1.md` in the same Edinumen "Lecturas de
Español" collection (different author — Ramón Fernández Numen here vs. José Luis Ocasar Ariza for
*Amnesia*), same format: bolded in-text term/expression paired with a **left-margin gloss box** in
simple monolingual Spanish. This book's apparatus is unusually rich in **idioms and colloquial
expressions** (as opposed to *Amnesia*'s more single-word vocabulary focus) — most margin glosses
here explain multi-word idiomatic phrases, interjections, and cultural-gesture descriptions rather
than isolated nouns. The book additionally has a full pedagogical frame absent from *Amnesia*: a
pre-reading "Antes de empezar a leer" section (word-association/prediction activities, pp. 4–8), a
mid-book "Párate un momento" reflection section (pp. 32–37, comprehension/opinion prompts), and a
substantial post-reading "Explotación Didáctica" ("Ejercicios para el Alumno," starting p. 61) with
reading-comprehension and grammar/lexicon drills (e.g., gerund-formation exercises built from verbs
pulled straight out of the story). These structural/exercise sections contain no new glossed
vocabulary of their own (the gerund-drill verb list, e.g., is recombination of words already in the
narrative) and were not transcribed line-by-line; all ~68 margin-gloss terms from the narrative
itself (pp. 5–58) are captured below.

**Plot/setting:** narrated in diary/journal form by **Natacha**, a young French exchange student
from **Burdeos (Bordeaux)** whose parents are Spanish exiles from the Spanish Civil War — she grew
up bilingual. She travels overnight by train to visit her best friend **Philippe**, who is doing an
Erasmus year in **Cádiz** (a real city in Andalusia, explicitly called "the oldest city in Europe"
in the text) to experience the famous **Carnaval de Cádiz**. Philippe's flatmates (Paco, Lucía, and
others) help disguise her for the carnival; she gets separated from the group in the crowds, is
"rescued"/accompanied for several hours by a stranger named **Martín** who initially seems
suspicious but turns out to be trustworthy, and the night ends with a reconciliation with Philippe
and a romantic turn with Martín at dawn on the beach (La Caleta). Real Cádiz locations are named
throughout (Plaza de la Catedral, Plaza de las Flores, Plaza de Mina, Barrio de la Viña, Plaza Tío
de la Tiza, Plaza de la Reina, La Caleta beach) — the book functions as a loose travelogue of
Carnaval-season Cádiz alongside the narrative.

**Register/dialect note:** this is the most colloquially/regionally marked reader in this wave —
several glosses are explicitly flagged as Andalusian-regional or colloquial register (chiquillo/a,
fino, gaditano/a, hace un frío que pela, chirigota, tablao), making it a genuine register/dialect
find distinct from the neutral-register `110_amnesia_nivel1.md`.
