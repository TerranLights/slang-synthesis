# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Reference: El Principito

**Source:** *El Principito* (38 pp., text-layer PDF, ~13,700 words), the Spanish translation of
Antoine de Saint-Exupéry's *Le Petit Prince*. Clean, reliable text layer confirmed via
`pdftotext`/`pdfinfo` (triage's "TEXT" classification holds).

**Pedagogical-apparatus check:** no translator or edition credit appears on the title page or
anywhere searched in the text, no footnotes, no in-text glosses, no appendix glossary, and no
comprehension questions. `grep` for common apparatus markers ("nota," "glos," "vocabulario,"
bracketed footnote numbers) turned up nothing beyond two incidental, non-apparatus uses of the
ordinary verb phrase "tomar nota" (to take note) inside the story itself (the king's dialogue on
his planet). This is a **plain, unannotated literary translation** — not a graded/annotated
learner edition — despite sitting in the "Graded Readers for Learners" source folder.

**Per the wave's methodology for readers with no pedagogical apparatus:** this is narrative
fiction with no built-in teaching aids, so it belongs to this project's **usage-corpus** category
(searched, not exhaustively extracted — see `00_Usage_Corpus_Catalog.md`), not Phase 1 reference
material. No exhaustive vocabulary table was built. Below is the book's premise/setting and a
small handful of notable words that surfaced while confirming the apparatus-absence.

**Premise/setting:** a pilot stranded in the Sahara desert meets a young prince from a tiny
asteroid (Asteroide B-612) who recounts his travels across several small planets — each inhabited
by a single adult representing a human folly (a king who rules nothing, a vain man, a drunkard, a
businessman counting stars, a lamplighter, a geographer) — before arriving on Earth, where he
befriends a fox and learns about love and loss through a rose he left behind. No real-world
country/region is named as the setting; the desert frame story and the prince's home asteroid are
both fictional/allegorical, so no dialect or regional-Spanish signal was found — the translation
reads as a neutral, standard literary register throughout.
