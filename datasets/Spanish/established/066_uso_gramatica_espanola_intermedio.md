## STRUCTURAL/PROGRESSION FINDING: this is where the subjunctive mood is introduced and systematically built out

Established/065 (Elemental) documented a deliberate zero-subjunctive scope for the first volume. This volume is where that gap is filled, and it is filled *fast and structurally*: subjunctive-trigger content begins at tema 11 (unit 11 of 32, roughly a third of the way in) and from there recurs as the throughline for the rest of the book. Reading the 32 temas in sequence shows a clear escalation:

1. **Tema 11** formally introduces the Presente de Subjuntivo paradigm for the first time in the series, framed generically: *"El Modo Subjuntivo tiene muchos valores; se utiliza para expresar inseguridad, futuro, deseo u otros sentimientos."* Anchored to three fixed triggers: OJALÁ (deseo), QUIZÁ(S)/TAL VEZ (posibilidad/hipótesis).
2. **Temas 15, 16, 18, 20** establish what becomes the book's single recurring master mechanism for mood selection: **same grammatical subject in both clauses → infinitive; different subject → QUE + subjuntivo.** This exact same-subject/different-subject test is restated nearly verbatim at estilo indirecto-orden (15, via imperativo→subjuntivo), oraciones finales (16, PARA+inf vs PARA QUE+subj), oraciones temporales (18, ANTES DE+inf vs ANTES DE QUE+subj), and desire verbs (20, ESPERO/QUIERO/PREFIERO/NECESITO+inf vs +QUE+subj). The book teaches this as one portable rule applied to many surface triggers rather than N independent idioms to memorize — a notably different pedagogical strategy from rote trigger-list approaches.
3. **Temas 21-22, 26-27** shift to a second mechanism: **polarity/epistemic-stance-conditioned mood selection** — value judgments and reactions (ES UNA PENA/QUÉ RARO, tema 21) always take subjunctive regardless of subject-matching; "knowledge/certainty" predicates (ESTÁ CLARO, ES OBVIO, ES CIERTO) take indicative when the main clause is affirmative but flip to subjunctive when the main clause is negated or interrogative-negative (tema 22); "opinion" verbs (CREO, PIENSO, ESTOY SEGURO) take indicative when affirmative, subjunctive when negated (tema 27) — explicitly presented as the same polarity-flip logic as tema 22.
4. **Temas 24-25** extend mood-selection into relative clauses: known/specific antecedent → indicative; unknown, non-specific, or explicitly negated/non-existent antecedent → subjunctive (*Tengo un ordenador que no funciona bien* [indicative, real, known] vs. *Estoy buscando un ordenador que funcione bien* [subjunctive, any such computer] vs. *Aquí no hay nadie que sepa inglés* [subjunctive, negated existence]).
5. **Temas 15, 28, 30-32** introduce the **Pretérito Imperfecto de Subjuntivo** (-ara/-iera forms) as a second subjunctive paradigm, layered onto the first: used when the governing verb is in a past tense (estilo indirecto de órdenes, tema 15), when expressing a polite/hypothetical wish with a conditional-mood main verb (ME GUSTARÍA QUE..., tema 28), and — critically — in **unreal/improbable conditional sentences** (tema 30: *Si tuviera tiempo, haría algún deporte*), which is the exact construction established/065 flagged as entirely absent from the Elemental volume. Tema 31 (oraciones concesivas) shows the same three-way aunque+real-indicative / aunque+subjunctive-uncertain-future / aunque+imperfect-subjunctive-counterfactual distinction, paralleling the conditional system.
6. **Tema 32 (Concordancia de tiempos)** is the volume's capstone: an explicit correlation table mapping every possible main-clause tense (Presente, Pret. Perfecto, Futuro, Imperativo vs. Pret. Imperfecto, Pret. Indefinido, Condicional, Pret. Pluscuamperfecto) to the corresponding subordinate subjunctive tense (Presente/Pret. Perfecto de Subjuntivo vs. Pret. Imperfecto de Subjuntivo) — unifying everything taught piecemeal across temas 11-31 into one sequence-of-tenses system.

Net effect: the Elemental volume's complete silence on subjunctive and the Intermedio volume's two-thirds-of-the-book build-out of it, culminating in an explicit tense-correlation table, together give this series a legible, deliberately staged subjunctive curriculum — directly useful as a model for how to sequence/scaffold a comparable modal distinction in the conlang's own slang-register grammar if the pipeline ever needs graded difficulty tiers.

## Other genuinely new content vs. established/065 (non-subjunctive)

- **Tema 3, Pretérito Pluscuamperfecto**: habia+participio, entirely new tense not in Elemental (anteriority-to-a-past-reference-point: *Cuando llegaron los bomberos la casa ya había ardido*).
- **Tema 4, CONOCER/SABER/PODER**: a 3-way contrast absent from Elemental — conocer = acquaintance with an entity (book/film/place/person) and, when the Direct Object is a person, obligatorily takes the "a personal" (*¿Conoces al marido de Concha?*); saber = learned skills/know-how (*¿Sabes hablar árabe?*) and also "to be aware of information" (*¿Sabes que María se ha casado?*); poder = physical/circumstantial capability or (with infinitive) requesting permission. Notes conocer/saber overlap specifically for "to find out/be aware of a piece of news."
- **Tema 5, ESTUVE/ESTABA/HE ESTADO + gerundio**: a three-way past-progressive contrast beyond Elemental's single estaba+gerundio — estuve+ger. for a delimited/completed stretch of ongoing action (*Anoche estuvimos hablando hasta las 3*), estaba+ger. for backgrounded ongoing action (*Cuando estaba esperando el autobús, me robaron*), he estado+ger. for a perfect-tense-style ongoing-duration-up-to-now reading (*Esta mañana la niña ha estado llorando un buen rato*). Reconfirms the same ir/tener/venir/volver exclusion list from Elemental tema 30.
- **Tema 6, A + objeto directo de persona**: explicit rule — obligatory personal "a" before a human direct object, optional before an animal DO depending on the verb's semantics (alimentar/pasear/querer optional; cosas inanimate objects never take it), and omitted when the referent is non-specific/indefinite (*busca un fontanero cualquiera*, no "a") or after the verb tener (*Gloria tiene tres hijos*, never "a tres hijos").
- **Tema 7, imperativo negativo**: the negative imperative paradigm (missing from Elemental, which covered only affirmative) is formed transparently from the Present Subjunctive stem for every person — a second explicit imperative/subjunctive morphological bridge, reinforcing Elemental tema 14's note that "the other persons [of the affirmative imperative] are really from the Present Subjunctive."
- **Tema 8, Preposiciones — régimen preposicional**: introduces verb-government ("rigen una preposición concreta") as an organizing concept — lists of verbs grouped by the preposition they obligatorily take (A: invitar, parecerse; CON: soñar, casarse; DE: tratar, enamorarse, depender; EN: confiar, insistir; POR: preocuparse, luchar, optar) plus fixed prepositional idioms (a máquina, de rodillas, por correo, a cántaros, de memoria, por los codos, por casualidad) — this concept of lexically-governed prepositions, as opposed to meaning-driven preposition choice, is new relative to Elemental's purely semantic preposition units (temas 23-24).
- **Tema 9-10, futuro/condicional modal (non-temporal) uses**: beyond simple future/conditional time reference, explicitly teaches the epistemic-probability use of Futuro Imperfecto (*¿A cuánto están hoy las naranjas? — Estarán a dos euros* = "they're probably at two euros," a guess about the present) and Futuro Perfecto for guessing about a completed-by-now event (*¿Dónde habré puesto las gafas?*), plus Condicional for softened suggestions/formal requests (*Yo, en tu lugar, no me pondría esa falda*; *¿Podrías traer el periódico?*) and as the reported-speech counterpart to direct-speech Futuro (estilo indirecto: *Mañana llegaré* → *dijo que llegaría*).
- **Tema 12, el género de los nombres**: systematic gender-assignment rules by noun ending (-tad/-dad, -ción/-sión/-zón, -tud feminine; -o masculine with listed exceptions la mano/la radio/la moto/la foto; -ista/-ante dual-gender distinguished only by article) plus a list of words whose masculine and feminine forms mean entirely different things (el cólera "cholera" / la cólera "anger"; el pendiente "earring" / la pendiente "slope"; el policía "policeman" / la policía "the police force"; el manzano "apple tree" / la manzana "apple") — not present in Elemental's simpler tema 1 agreement unit.
- **Tema 13, pronombres personales + verbs that pattern like GUSTAR**: beyond basic OD/OI pronoun forms, explicitly generalizes the gustar-construction to a broader verb class ("Hay muchos verbos que funcionan con la misma estructura que gustar... con los pronombres me/te/le/nos/os/les"), citing tocar (la lotería), importar, molestar as members of the same syntactic class — a useful structural generalization not made explicit in Elemental's tema 11 (GUSTAR/PARECER/DOLER/PASAR/QUEDAR), which listed the same verbs without naming the shared argument-structure pattern.
- **Tema 19, artículo neutro LO**: introduces the neuter article LO (nominalizing an adjective: *lo importante*; an adverb: *lo bien que está*; or a relative clause: *no se me ha olvidado lo que dijiste*) and LO DE + noun meaning "the matter of" (*lo de Aurora*) — entirely new category not in Elemental's article unit (which covered only el/la/los/las and un/una/unos/unas).
- **Tema 23, SER/ESTAR expanded**: goes well beyond Elemental's 3-way SER/ESTAR/TENER grouping with explicit closed lists — adjectives that occur only with ser (lógico, justo, importante, inteligente, conveniente, culpable, necesario, útil), only with estar (de buen/mal humor, enamorado, enfadado, harto, preocupado), and both with a meaning shift (listo=smart vs. ready; abierto/cerrado=personality trait vs. physical state; bueno/malo=inherent quality vs. a passing state/taste).
- **Tema 29, comparativos y superlativos**: full comparative/superlative system (regular más/menos+adj+que, irregular mejor/peor/mayor/menor, equality tan...como, absolute superlative -ísimo, relative superlative el/la/los/las+más/menos+adj+de/que) — not covered at all in Elemental (whose tema 16 "Comparación" this project has not yet cross-checked for overlap, but no superlative content appears there based on the TOC).

## Cross-reference note

This volume should be read as the direct continuation of established/065's subjunctive-absence finding: it is where the mood is introduced (tema 11) and built out across roughly two-thirds of the book via two recurring mechanisms (same-subject/different-subject, and affirmative/negated polarity-flip), culminating in a formal sequence-of-tenses table (tema 32). Established/067 (Avanzado) should be checked for whether it (a) introduces additional subjunctive tenses not covered here (Pretérito Pluscuamperfecto de Subjuntivo, hubiera/hubiese+participio, needed for third-conditional-type counterfactuals about the past — notably absent from this Intermedio volume, which only reaches Pretérito Imperfecto de Subjuntivo), (b) adds further trigger classes or exceptions to the same-subject/polarity-flip mechanisms, or (c) addresses register/dialectal variation in the -ara/-iese imperfect-subjunctive alternation that this volume left unglossed.
