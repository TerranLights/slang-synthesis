## Grammar points

### Adjectives whose meaning depends on position (preceding vs. following the noun)

A closed set of Spanish adjectives shift meaning depending on whether they precede or follow the
noun they modify (pp. 110-111):

- **antiguo**: *su antiguo novio* "her **former** boyfriend" (preceding) vs. *una civilización
  antigua* "an **ancient** civilization" (following)
- **cierto**: *cierto día* "a **certain** day" (preceding) vs. *una cosa cierta* "a **sure** thing"
  (following)
- **grande**: *el gran hombre* "the **great** man" vs. *el hombre grande* "the **big** man" — note
  **grande** shortens to **gran** before any singular noun (masc. or fem.) when preceding
- **pobre**: *el pobre niño* "the poor, **unfortunate** little boy" (preceding) vs. *el hombre
  pobre* "the poor man (**without money**)" (following)
- **mismo**: *el mismo libro* "the **same** book" (preceding) vs. *el profesor mismo* "the professor
  **himself**" (following, intensifying) — also intensifies pronouns/adverbs when following: *yo
  mismo* "I myself," *ahora mismo* "right now," *esta casa misma* "this very house"
  (p. 111)
- **viejo**: *los viejos amigos* "the **longtime** friends" (preceding, i.e. friends for a long
  time) vs. *los amigos viejos* "the friends who are **old in years**" (following)

This is a compact, well-organized version of a pattern also touched on more diffusely in prior
established files (e.g. `006`'s diminutive-suffix discussion); worth flagging together as a single
closed list for the mechanics-analysis phase, since semantic-shift-via-position is itself a
mechanism slang could plausibly extend.

### Adjectives expressing "next," "only," "last"

**próximo** ("next"), **único** ("only"), **último** ("last, final") — all standard prenominal
adjectives, agreeing in gender/number (p. 110). Not novel but recorded for completeness alongside
the position-dependent set above since they're presented in the same subsection.

### Irregular comparatives and superlatives

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative (sing.) | Superlative (pl.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| bueno (good) | mejor (better) | el/la mejor (the best) | los/las mejores |
| malo (bad) | peor (worse) | el/la peor (the worst) | los/las peores |
| joven (young) | menor (younger) | el/la menor (the youngest) | los/las menores |

**Menor** refers only to people (*mi hermano menor*); to compare non-persons (e.g. trees) by age,
the book directs using the regular comparative structure instead (*Estos árboles son menos viejos
que aquellos árboles*) (p. 114). (The parallel entry for **grande/mayor** "older" was not visible on
the sampled page but follows the same pattern documented elsewhere in this project's sources.)

### Adverbs that do not take the *-mente* suffix (p. 119, full closed-list capture)

Unlike the productive **-mente** adverb-formation pattern (adjective + -mente, paralleling English
"-ly"), a specific closed set of common adverbs has no **-mente** form and must be learned as
separate words. The book organizes them into four functional groups:

- **Quantity**: mucho (a lot), tanto (so much), poco (a little bit), casi (almost), más (more),
  menos (less), demasiado (too much)
- **Manner**: bien (well), mal (badly), mejor (better), peor (worse)
- **Time/place**: a veces (sometimes), ahora (now), ahora mismo (right now), siempre (always),
  todavía (still/yet/not yet), ya (already), ya no (no longer), temprano (early), tarde (late),
  aquí/allí (here/there), acá/allá (here/there — used with verbs of motion, per the book's own
  note), cerca (nearby), en lo alto (up there), lejos (far off), adelante (in front), al fondo (in
  back/at the bottom), atrás (in back), arriba (up/upstairs), abajo (down/downstairs), adentro
  (inside), afuera (outside)
- **Direction**: a la derecha (to the right), a la izquierda (to the left), derecho/recto (straight
  ahead)

This is a reusable inventory for later slang-mechanics work on adverb-class boundaries (which
adverbial meanings are lexicalized vs. productively derived).
