## Grammar points

### "Cuando" and "después de que" as future-pending subjunctive triggers (continued)

Lección 59 continues Lección 58's temporal-conjunction subjunctive, focusing on "cuando" (when) and
"después de que" (after) specifically in future-referring contexts: "cuando terminen llamarán" (when
they finish, they'll call), "después de que traigan mi carro, te recogeré" (after they bring my car,
I'll pick you up). The lesson's closing secretary dialogue chains several of these triggers together
across a single extended exchange (hasta que, después de que, cuando), reinforcing that all of them
take the subjunctive identically when describing a not-yet-realized future event. (Lección 59,
throughout.)

### Subjunctive of possibility/probability: es posible que, es probable que, puede ser que, quizás

Lección 60 introduces a distinct subjunctive trigger category — epistemic uncertainty rather than
volition or pending time — covering four expressions: "es posible que" (it's possible that), "es
probable que" (it's probable that), "puede ser que" (it may be that), and "quizás" (maybe, which
triggers the subjunctive even *without* an explicit "que": "quizás llueva mañana"). The instructor
explicitly flags a cross-linguistic mismatch: English "It's possible that I'll prepare the report
tomorrow" uses a future-tense "will," but the Spanish equivalent uses the **present subjunctive**
("es posible que prepare el informe mañana"), not a future form — an explicit warning against a
literal tense-mapping error an English-speaking learner might otherwise make. (Lección 60, throughout,
esp. p. 2.)
