# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Prácticas de Lengua Española (Niveles Medio y Superior)

**Source:** Rafael Álvarez Merlo & Emilio Alejandro Núñez Cabezas, *Prácticas de Lengua Española.
Español para Extranjeros. Niveles Medio y Superior* (VG Ediciones, scanned PDF, 193 pp.), a
Spanish-medium pure drill/exercise workbook, 24 units (presente irregular, ser/estar, pronombres
de objeto, comparativas, tiempos de pasado, futuro/condicional, subjuntivo, imperativo, tipos de
oración subordinada, preposiciones, perífrasis, acentuación, pronombres relativos...). No
extractable text layer (`pdftotext` empty on every sampled page) — confirmed vision-required per
the Wave 3 triage survey.

**Vision-reading approach — pivoted strategy:** this book's own "Presentación" (p. 3–4, read in
full) states explicitly, in the authors' own words, the design choice behind the whole volume:
*"hemos pensado que sería más adecuado no incluir cuadros con explicaciones gramaticales"*
("we decided it would be more fitting not to include grammar-explanation boxes") — the book is
**deliberately 100% exercises with an answer key**, meant to be paired with a teacher's or another
book's explanations, not to carry any of its own. Confirmed by direct sampling: pp. 12–13 (Unidad
2, ser/estar) and p. 157 (Unidad 23, qué/cuál/relativos) are pure fill-in-the-blank or
sentence-reordering drills built from generic, repetitive frame sentences with no new vocabulary.
Given there is no explanatory content anywhere in this book to extract, the approach pivoted
entirely to **harvesting idiomatic expressions embedded in the fill-in-the-blank preposition
drills** (Unidad 21, *Preposiciones y verbos con preposición*, pp. 140–142) — the one place in the
book where the drill sentences themselves are built from fixed idiomatic phrases rather than
generic substitution frames, since Spanish por/para/sobre/entre/sin/hacia usage is inherently
idiom-heavy. Read ~6 of 193 pages (~3%) total: the presentation (pp. 3–4), the full first index
page (p. 5), two ser/estar drill pages as a format spot-check (pp. 12–13), and the por/para and
sobre/entre/sin/hacia drill sections of Unidad 21 (pp. 140–142). This is by far the lightest
vision-reading of the five books in this dispatch, proportionate to the book's near-total lack of
extractable reference content.

**Methodology caveat:** because this is a fill-in-the-blank exercise with the blanked preposition
not given on the page (the answer key is in the back, pp. ~175+, not consulted), each idiom entry
below reflects this extractor's own knowledge of the standard, unambiguous preposition for each
well-known fixed phrase (e.g., *por los pelos*, *cada dos por tres*), not a transcription of an
answer key. Entries were only kept where the correct preposition is unambiguous from the idiom's
well-attested standard form.

**Non-redundant-supplement note:** checked established/ for each idiom found. *dar la lata*,
*sobre ruedas*, and *sin ton ni son* already have prior established/ coverage (006, 022, 029, 042,
043, 046, 067, 002, 020) and are included here only incidentally (as part of the preposition-drill
harvest) rather than as new finds. **"Por los pelos" and "no dar el brazo a torcer" returned zero
prior hits anywhere in established/ and are the genuine new finds from this book.**
