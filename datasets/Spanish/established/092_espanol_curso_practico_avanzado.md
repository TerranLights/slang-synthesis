# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *Español: Curso Práctico de Léxico, Gramática y Conversación (Nivel Avanzado)* (sampled, OCR-recovered)

**Source:** M.V. Larionova and O.B. Ibankova (Ларионова М.В., Ибанькова О.Б.), *Español: Curso
Práctico de Léxico, Gramática y Conversación (nivel avanzado)* (Russian title: *Практический курс
испанского языка, продвинутый этап обучения*). Published Moscow, AST/Vostok-Zapad, 2005, for the
Moscow State Institute of International Relations (MGIMO). 254 PDF pages. **This is a
Russian-authored, Russian-medium advanced Spanish textbook for Russian university students** —
all methodological commentary, exercise instructions, and translation-into-Russian prompts are in
Russian, while the core lesson content is authentic, unadapted (only lightly abridged) literary
prose excerpted from contemporary Spanish and Latin American authors (this sampled lesson uses
Daniel Chavarría, a Uruguayan-born, Cuba-based novelist) plus one original-Spanish cultural
essay. The book is organized into 6 thematic lessons (economic/social perspectives of modern
society; education and personal formation; environment/ecology; transport; sport-as-business;
gastronomic traditions/national cuisine) each built from 2 authentic texts + "Ecos" vocabulary
exercises + lexical/conversational/grammar drills, per the book's own Russian-language preface.

**Text layer / vision-reading note:** this PDF is a bitonal (1-bit) JBIG2 scan at 800dpi (confirmed
via `pdfimages -list`), and its embedded OCR text layer is essentially unusable (`pdftotext`
output is scrambled symbol noise on sampled pages — this is almost certainly the original garbled
OCR attempting to recognize Cyrillic Russian text through a Latin-only recognition model, compounding
the usual bad-embedded-OCR problem already seen elsewhere in this corpus). Target pages were
re-rendered to 200dpi grayscale images with `pdftoppm` and re-OCR'd with Tesseract's Spanish (`spa`)
model (same tool/model used for `034` and `091` in this corpus). **OCR quality assessment:** the
Spanish-language narrative portions (the actual literary texts) OCR'd cleanly and legibly; the
Russian-language methodological/exercise-instruction portions, run through the Spanish-only model,
produced garbled Cyrillic-via-Latin-glyph noise (expected and harmless, since only the Spanish text
was the extraction target — the Russian instructions were read by eye for navigation/context, not
transcribed). Transcription Confidence is set to n/a and Vision Reading Confidence to verified for
all rows below, per this corpus's OCR-recovery convention.

**Redundancy check:** this book's authentic-literature-excerpt + Russian-medium-commentary design
is structurally unlike anything else in the Spanish corpus (the closest analogues, `001`/`002`
Colloquial Spanish, use English-medium commentary with constructed dialogues, not real literary
prose). No duplication risk was found with the existing corpus. Deliberately NOT extracted: the
book's verb-polysemy drill exercises on *acabar*, *rato*, *quedar*, *faltar* (translate-the-sentence
exercises illustrating each verb's different senses) — these are drill-exercise padding of the kind
already skipped under this project's standard coverage rule, and the senses themselves (e.g.
*quedar* = "to remain/be left/suit/agree to") are already adequately covered by the general grammar
references already in this corpus.

**Entry count:** 25 vocabulary/idiom/cultural rows, sampled from Lección 1 ("Tradiciones
gastronómicas") only — its two texts (Daniel Chavarría's prison-literature excerpt "El rojo en la
pluma del loro," and an original essay on Spanish tapas culture) — out of this 254-page, 6-lesson
book. Not an exhaustive pass of the whole book.
