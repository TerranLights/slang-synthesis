## What this book is

**Confirmed: title accurately predicts the book's purpose — this is explicitly a vocabulary-building
reader, not a narrative novel**, and it has by far the richest pedagogical apparatus of anything
sampled in this wave. Structure: 10 topic chapters (Cultura, Viaje, Tradición, Celebración, Personas,
Deportes, Música, Historia, Geografía, Gastronomía), each containing 8-13 short (2-page) nonfiction
articles about a specific country/topic in the Spanish-speaking world, **every single article
accompanied by its own full-page bilingual glossary sidebar** (per the front matter, p.XI: "Each
article is accompanied by a bilingual glossary. You can read and learn without stopping to look up
words in a dictionary or phrase book"). English-language "CULTURE NOTE" sidebars supplement several
articles with additional cultural context not present in the Spanish text itself. Each chapter ends
with a "Examina tu comprensión" (Test Your Comprehension) section, answered in a back-of-book Answer
Key (p.202+, not sampled).

**Scope note:** with 100+ articles each carrying 40-80 glossed terms, full extraction would be
thousands of rows and is out of proportion for a Phase 1 pass on a single title in a selectively-scoped
wave. The sample below is deliberately representative — three countries, three chapters, and a
deliberate inclusion of the one article most relevant to this project's slang-synthesis purpose (see
below) — rather than exhaustive. If deeper mining of this title is ever wanted, the "Contents" page
(captured in full above) lists all 100+ article titles/countries/page numbers as a roadmap for
targeted re-sampling.

## Standout find: genuine youth-slang glossary embedded in "El reguetón está 'rankeao'" (Puerto Rico, p.128-129, Música ch.)

This article is explicitly about reggaetón slang itself and contains an **in-text bulleted slang
glossary introduced as "una jerga muy callejera, llena de anglicismos"** (a very street-level slang,
full of anglicisms) — the clearest, most directly on-topic slang material found anywhere in this wave.
All terms below come from that bulleted list plus the surrounding article/sidebar glossary.

`Source Type = grammar_reference` for all rows. `Transcription Confidence = n/a` (vision read).
`Vision Reading Confidence = high` throughout (clean modern typeset print, though sidebar text is
small — noted `medium` for a few entries where glyphs were less certain).
