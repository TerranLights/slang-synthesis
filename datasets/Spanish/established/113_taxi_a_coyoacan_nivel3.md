# Spanish — Established Vocabulary/Reference: Taxi a Coyoacán (Venga a leer, Nivel 3, Serie América Latina)

**Source:** *Taxi a Coyoacán* (Dolores Soler-Espiauba, Difusión, "Venga a leer" collection, Serie
"América Latina," Nivel 3, 50 pp., vision-required PDF — confirmed: `pdftotext` extraction is
essentially empty). Read via vision: the full narrative opening (pp. 5–20), the complete 49-item
endnote glossary (pp. 38–42), and a sample of the post-reading comprehension/exercise apparatus
(pp. 43, 47–49).

**Series/apparatus — distinct format from the other three readers in this wave:** rather than
left-margin gloss boxes, this book uses **in-text italicized terms with superscript footnote
numbers** (e.g. *visa²*, *valija³*, *camión⁴*) that resolve to a **49-item "Notas explicativas"**
endnote glossary at the back of the book (pp. 38–42). Because this is the "Serie América Latina"
entry in the "Venga a leer" collection, and the protagonist is a Spaniard newly arrived in Mexico,
**the entire glossary is structured as an explicit Mexican-Spanish vs. Peninsular-Spanish
contrastive dictionary** — nearly every note is phrased as "in Spain this is called X" or "typical
Mexican expression meaning Y." This is exactly the kind of dialect-contrast material anticipated
for a book set in a named Spanish-speaking country, and is the strongest dialect-specific find of
the whole wave. **All 49 glossary items were extracted below** (the book's own numbering, 1–49, is
preserved as a reference in the Notes column). The post-reading apparatus (comprehension questions,
word-matching exercises built from music/car vocabulary already in the story) was sampled but not
transcribed — it recombines vocabulary already captured here and in the narrative.

**Plot/setting:** David, a Spanish journalist obsessed with (by his own near-confession, "in love
with") the painter Frida Kahlo, flies to **Mexico City (México D.F.)** to visit the Museo Frida
Kahlo in **Coyoacán** and write a feature article. On his first full day he is the victim of an
elaborate **taxi-robbery scam**: a "vochito" (VW Beetle) taxi driver and an accomplice posing as a
random passenger force him at knifepoint to withdraw cash from two ATMs, using a child,
**Telesforo**, hidden in the trunk to collect the stolen money. The next day Telesforo — a
street child with no mother and an abusive, alcoholic stepfather who isn't even his real father —
tracks David down, returns all the stolen money unprompted (he doesn't want to "work" for the gang
anymore), and offers himself as a paid tour guide instead. Through Telesforo, David finally reaches
the Frida Kahlo museum, where its conservator's daughter, **Fernanda**, turns out to bear an
uncanny resemblance to Frida Kahlo herself; a romance develops, and the book ends with David,
Fernanda, and Telesforo together at the airport as a mariachi band plays "Adelita."

**Historical-accuracy note (flagged, not corrected):** endnote (45) describes Teotihuacán as "a
site from the Aztec era" — Teotihuacán was in fact built and had already been abandoned by a
pre-Aztec civilization centuries before the Aztec empire; this is the source's own imprecision,
noted here rather than silently corrected, consistent with this project's practice of flagging
rather than fixing source claims. Endnote (10) also glosses NAFTA with a garbled expansion ("North
American Federation of Trade Associations" rather than the actual North American Free Trade
Agreement) — likely an error original to the book, also flagged rather than corrected.
