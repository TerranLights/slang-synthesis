# Russian — Using Russian Vocabulary (Terence Wade), Unit 14: Education

**Source:** Terence Wade, *Using Russian Vocabulary*, Cambridge University Press, 2009 (this
printing/binding dated 2010). PDF pages 431–453 of "56.Using Russian Vocabulary.pdf" (printed
pages ~423–450), covering the tail of Unit 13's exercises (no new vocabulary — pure drill,
correctly skipped) and the complete Unit 14 ("Education") across Levels 1–3 plus its
"Additional vocabulary" appendix.

**Coverage note.** This is the third of five files splitting one large reference-extraction
dispatch (PDF pages 431–636, the tail of the book). Sibling files: `021b` (Unit 15, Agriculture/
Industry), `021c` (Unit 16, Business and commerce), `021d` (Unit 17, Office/computing/post/
telecommunications), `021e` (Unit 18, Law and finance). The book's actual back matter — a
~60-page "Key to exercises" answer key (PDF pages ~569–636, printed pages ~557–624+, the true
end of the PDF) — was deliberately excluded from vocabulary extraction across all five files: it
is answer-key material (exercise solutions, cross-references, and a handful of etymological
one-liners), not new lexical content or an index, and its rare genuinely-new items (e.g. a
register note on вaхтëршa, or numeral-grade nouns like дво́йкa/тро́йкa/чeтвëркa/пятëркa that
appear only in a Unit 14 exercise sentence) are individually flagged inline below rather than
driving a full re-extraction of the answer key. No handwritten marginalia was found anywhere in
this range — this PDF has a genuine, clean text layer (confirmed via `pdftotext`) and required no
vision-reading.

**PDF-extraction note.** No Cyrillic font-substitution cipher is present in this file — Cyrillic
extracts correctly and consistently, cross-checked against 30+ known word/gloss pairs throughout
this range. The only extraction quirk is cosmetic: `pdftotext -layout` frequently places a
stress-accent mark (´) on its own line below the vowel it belongs to (an artifact of the book's
combining-accent typesetting), which has been silently reattached to the correct vowel when
transcribing below. Vocabulary boxes in this unit are single-column (not the two-column glossary
layout the project's PDF-extraction guide warns about), so no column-scrambling was detected.

**Copyright discipline.** Vocabulary items are given as term+gloss pairs only (the standard
lexicographic extraction format already used across this project's other reference-book files).
Etymological/"Word origins" notes and grammatical notes are paraphrased, not quoted verbatim;
illustrative example sentences from the source are not reproduced, only referenced.

## Grammar points

### Aspectual/governance pairs учи́ть vs учи́ться
The source gives a detailed governance note distinguishing the two verbs (paraphrased above in
each entry's Notes column): учи́ть takes accusative-of-person + dative-of-subject ('to teach
someone something'), or accusative-of-person + infinitive ('to teach someone to do'), or is used
impf.-only to mean 'to be a teacher', or takes accusative + pf. вы́- to mean 'to learn by heart'.
учи́ться instead takes dative ('to learn something'), or infinitive ('to learn to do'), or is used
impf.-only to mean 'to study, be a student'. This four-way + three-way governance split is the
single densest grammar point in Unit 14 and constrains a large share of the unit's example
sentences.

### Consonant-mutation verb classes recurring across the unit's exercises
Unit 14's exercises repeatedly drill first-person-singular consonant mutations that recur
throughout this book's vocabulary (в/вл, д/ж, п/пл, т/ч, с/ш, б/бл, м/мл), attached to specific
verbs introduced in this unit: выступить (п/пл), освободить (д/ж), сократить (т/щ — Unit 16/18
territory but drilled analogously here), стереть (irregular: сотру́/стëр). These are noted
per-verb above rather than restated as a separate grammar block, since the mutation pattern
itself is general Russian morphology already covered in this project's earlier grammar-focused
established/ files (e.g. 001–007), not new content specific to this vocabulary book.

### Nominative plural in stressed -á/-я́
The exercises flag a recurring irregular masculine-plural pattern: вaхтëр → вaхтeрá,
профeссор → профeссорá, but учи́тeль → учителя́ (soft-stem, -я́ not -á) — an orthographic split
worth noting since several nouns in this unit's own vocabulary list belong to this irregular
plural class (профeссор, учи́тeль).
