## Grammar points

### 1. The duration-question diagnostic: сколько времени vs. за сколько времени (pp.6, 8)

НСВ pairs with **сколько времени?** ("how long...?"), diagnosing an action viewed as a
process/duration; SV pairs with **за сколько времени?** ("in how much time...?"), diagnosing the
time *spent to reach* a completed result (*Антон учил новые слова час, но ещё плохо знает их* vs.
*Антон выучил новые слова за час и теперь хорошо знает их*). **Note:** НСВ can also take **за
сколько времени** when the action is understood as habitually repeated (*За сколько времени вы
обычно доезжаете до университета?*) — a narrow but real exception to the НСВ/SV split, worth
flagging since it looks at first like a contradiction of the diagnostic. Also noted, p.5: some
prefixed по- verbs in НСВ (*почитал, устал и лёг спать*) mark a *limited duration* rather than a
completed result — a caution against over-reading по- as automatically signaling result.

### 2. Motion verbs: a three-way question-construction diagnostic (p.49)

This sharpens point 1 specifically for verbs of motion, where the general duration/result binary
splits into three constructions depending on the verb's prefix class:

- **сколько времени?** — with **prefixless** motion verbs (НСВ): duration of the motion itself
  (*Туристы ехали до Клина два часа* — "The tourists were riding to Klin for two hours").
- **за сколько времени?** — with SV motion verbs bearing the prefixes **до-, про-, об-, пере-**:
  time spent to *achieve the goal of the path* (*Туристы доехали до Клина за два часа*).
- **на сколько времени?** — with SV motion verbs bearing the prefixes **при-, за-, в-, у-, вы-,
  по-, от-** (and other verbs whose motion can be conceived as reversible/round-trip): the
  duration for which the resulting state is expected to hold, looking forward from the moment of
  arrival (*Туристы приехали в Клин на два дня* — "The tourists came to Klin for two days [i.e.
  will stay two days]").

**Note (p.49):** **на сколько времени** can take either aspect — unlike the first two
constructions, where aspect is a fixed diagnostic, here the aspectual meaning (whether the
resulting state is annulled or still holds, see point 6 below) can be more communicatively salient
than the temporal-duration meaning itself, so the choice depends on context/communicative intent
(*Он приехал в Москву на два дня и сейчас осматривает город* [SV, result holds] vs. *В прошлом
году он приезжал в Москву на два дня, но ничего не успел посмотреть* [НСВ, result since annulled]).
This is a genuinely specific rule not found in the general reference grammars or in 039.

### 3. Communicative choice between aspects for a single completed fact (pp.27–28)

For a single past action, **either aspect can sometimes be grammatical**, and the choice reflects
the speaker's own communicative framing rather than a fixed rule — a point this book makes more
explicitly than either 039 or the general references:

- Framing the action **by its process** in the past favors НСВ (*Кто строил это здание? В нём
  много недоделок* — the process left visible defects); framing it **by its result** favors SV
  (*Кто построил это здание? Кому удалось создать этот шедевр архитектуры?* — praising the
  achieved result).
- The speaker's **point of view relative to the moment of speech** can also govern the choice:
  *Он называл свою фамилию, но я забыл её* (НСВ — the fact is fully relegated to the past, wholly
  detached from the speech moment) vs. *Он назвал мне свою фамилию, а я записал её* (SV — the past
  action is treated as connected to/relevant at the speech moment).
- A parallel contrast: *Преподаватель уже объяснял этот текст?* (НСВ — asking simply whether an
  explanation happened at all, at any point) vs. *Преподаватель уже объяснил этот текст?* (SV —
  presupposing the explanation was expected/due, and asking whether it has now been realized).

### 4. A semantic class of one-way "state" verbs paired with inchoative-SV counterparts (p.37)

This is presented as its own numbered subsection ("Употребление глаголов НСВ со значением
состояния и глаголов СВ, обозначающих момент возникновения состояния") distinct from ordinary
aspectual pairs. Verbs like **нравиться, любить, чувствовать, молчать** denote an
indefinitely-durative *state* and are (mostly) one-way НСВ verbs with no true SV counterpart of
their own; however, many form a *related* SV verb denoting **the moment the state arises**
(понравиться, полюбить, почувствовать, замолчать), which is itself single-aspect (SV-only). The
two members are linked by a regular semantic relation (state ↔ onset-of-state) rather than by the
usual process/result relation that defines a true НСВ/SV aspectual pair. НСВ here reports a
long-standing state with no connection to the speech moment, wholly located in the past (*Раньше
ему нравились исторические романы*); SV reports the state's *inception*, with that state
continuing to hold at the moment of speech (*Рассказ ему очень понравился* — he likes it now, as a
result of that reading). **Note:** context words like *впервые, первый раз, с первого взгляда, в
тот момент* trigger the SV inceptive member even for an otherwise past-oriented state (*Она
полюбила его с первого взгляда*). This state/inchoative-of-state class is conceptually distinct
from ordinary НСВ/SV pairs and was not captured by 039's aspect-pair-dictionary framing.

### 5. Inchoativity via prefixation vs. начать/стать + НСВ infinitive (p.41)

A closely related point: the "beginning of a state/action" (начинательность) can be expressed
either by a prefixed SV verb (закричал — "cried out [suddenly]") or by **начать/стать +
НСВ-infinitive** (стал кричать). The SV form marks a **momentary, involuntary** onset; the
начать/стать-construction marks an onset that is **willed, repeated, or itself durative** (*Ребёнок
взял любимую игрушку, и он заплакал* [SV, involuntary] vs. *Ребёнок увидел на витрине интересную
игрушку и стал плакать, просить, чтобы родители купили её* [construction, a sustained/willed
crying-and-pleading episode]).

### 6. "Annulled" vs. "retained" result with prefixed motion verbs (p.45)

НСВ motion verbs (уходил, открывал) in past-tense contexts can specifically signal that a result
has since been **annulled/reversed** by the moment of speech (*Врач приходил к больному* — "the
doctor came [and has since left]; he was there, but isn't now"), while the corresponding SV verb
signals the result **still holds** at the moment of speech (*Врач пришёл к больному* — the doctor
is there now). This ties into point 2's directional-prefix classes: because motion verbs prefixed
with **у-, вы-, от-** are the semantic antonyms of verbs prefixed with **при-, за-, в-, под-**, the
НСВ/SV opposition for one prefix set produces the *opposite* real-world implication of the other
(уходил с урока = "was absent, but has now returned" vs. ушёл с урока = "left, and is now
elsewhere"). **Note (p.45):** the "moment" against which result-retention is judged need not be the
speech moment — it can be any other moment named in the sentence, in which case SV is still used
(*3 января 1709 года после победы над Полтавой Пётр торжественно въехал в Москву*).

### 7. Future tense: bare positive/negative nuances (pp.59, 63)

Largely consistent with 039's Grammar point 3, with one addition: the "bare futurity of a fact"
sense of НСВ future is noted (p.59) to semantically converge with the copula-like verb **быть**
itself (*Завтра мы будем писать диктант* ≈ *Завтра у нас будет диктант*). For negation (p.63): НСВ
future negation reads as **absence of intention/desire** (*Я не буду решать эту задачу* — "I don't
intend to, I don't want to"), while SV future negation reads as **inability/impossibractability to
achieve the result** (*Я не решу эту задачу* — "I won't be able to, it's impossible for me") —
matching the negation logic 039 documents for the imperative (Grammar point 4 there), but here
applied to the plain declarative future.

### 8. Imperative: a pragmatic register nuance for bare positive commands (p.68)

Beyond the functional four-way НСВ/SV imperative split 039 already documents, this book adds an
explicit **stylistic/pragmatic** observation: a bare positive SV imperative (as opposed to a
one-off polite request already licensed by context) can carry **an overtone of a sharp, sometimes
outright rude demand** — *Встань, когда говоришь со старшим!* ("Stand up when you speak to an
elder!"), *Сядь на место! Ты мне мешаешь!* ("Sit down! You're in my way!"). This is a register/
pragmatics point not stated as such in 039, worth noting for anyone deriving slang/register
mechanics from imperative-aspect choice.

Separately (p.79), the negated-SV-"warning" imperative construction (same closed verb list as
039's: разбить, обжечь, испачкать, упасть, сломать, уронить, потерять, заболеть, простудиться) is
noted to extend to two further pragmatic readings beyond plain warning: a **reminder** sense (*Не
забудь сегодня купить лекарство, завтра все аптеки будут закрыты*) and an **intensified request**
sense (*Не откажи ему в помощи* — "Don't refuse to help him," i.e. "please do help him").

### 9. Infinitive aspect selection: governing-verb groups (pp.85–87)

This chapter's classification substantially overlaps with 039's Grammar point 6 but groups the
governing verbs by clearer semantic classes and adds several verbs not in 039's list:

- **Begin/continue/end of action → НСВ infinitive**: начать, стать, продолжать, кончить,
  **перестать, прекратить** (the latter two not in 039's list).
- **Speaker's desire to stop the action → НСВ infinitive**: надоесть, устать, расхотеть,
  **отговорить, передумать** (not in 039's list) — надоесть and расхотеть are impersonal,
  dative-subject constructions (*Ему надоело смотреть телевизор*).
- **Ability/skill/habituation → НСВ infinitive**: уметь, научиться, привыкнуть.
- **Successful/failed completion → SV infinitive**: успеть, забыть, **остаться, удаться** (the
  latter two not in 039's list) — остаться and удаться are likewise impersonal/dative (*Ему
  удалось написать интересную статью*; *Ему осталось написать упражнение*).

### 10. Chapter V: the compact НСВ/SV notation for когда-clauses (pp.103–104)

Restates 039's Grammar point 2 in explicit shorthand: **НСВ+НСВ = full simultaneity** of two
durative actions; **НСВ+SV or SV+НСВ = partial simultaneity** (one durative action, one punctual
action occurring within it); **SV+SV = sequence**; and, added here explicitly (p.104), **НСВ+НСВ
also covers repeated/habitual co-occurrence** when a repetition adverbial or "каждый раз" is
present (*Каждый раз, когда преподаватель входил в аудиторию, студенты вставали*).

### 11. Beyond когда: aspect correlations for other temporal conjunctions (pp.65, 129)

Not covered by the book's own grammar chapters, but surfaced by a Chapter VI review text (a factual
account of Alexei Leonov's 1965 spacewalk, p.129) and one earlier footnote (p.65/67), which
together attest a wider family of temporal conjunctions with their own aspect correlations, worth
flagging as a genuine supplementary find:

- **пока не + SV** ("until X happens") — stated explicitly as a footnoted rule (p.67): *После союза
  пока не употребляются глаголы СВ*. Attested in the review text: *...пока не получил приказ от
  Беляева — возвращаться на корабль* ("until he received Belyayev's order").
- **до того как + SV infinitive** ("before doing X"): *До того как совершить этот исторический
  полёт, космонавты долго и упорно тренировались*.
- **после того как + SV** (completed prior action, both clauses SV): *После того как космонавты
  тщательно проверили всё, Леонов вышел... в открытый космос*.
- **пока + НСВ** (durative "while," both clauses durative/overlapping — parallels когда's НСВ+НСВ
  simultaneity rule): *Пока Леонов... парил в безвоздушном пространстве, Беляев передавал на Землю
  сенсационное сообщение*.
- **по мере того как + НСВ** ("as X gradually happened," a gradually-developing parallel process,
  both clauses НСВ): *По мере того как напряжённость ситуации возрастала, Центр управления полётов
  на Земле переживал вместе с космонавтом*.

This suggests the full temporal-conjunction system extending well past когда shares the same
underlying НСВ/SV correlations documented for когда in point 10 and in 039's Grammar point 2 — a
pattern worth confirming against a dedicated syntax reference in a future dispatch if one becomes
available.
