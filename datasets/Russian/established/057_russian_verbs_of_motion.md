# Russian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Verbs of Motion (full book)

**Source:** Leon Stilman, *Russian Verbs of Motion: Going, Carrying, Leading* (Columbia Slavic
Studies, Columbia University Press, 2nd ed. 1951, 11th printing 1967). Full book, PDF pages 1–81
(cover through the final page on плавать–плыть, printed pages [i]–[vi] front matter + printed pages
1–78 of text; the book has no back-matter answer key or appendix — printed page 78 is the last
page of actual content, confirmed by `pdfinfo`'s 81-page count and by the printed-page-number
sequence itself running out at [78]).

**Relationship to `028_russkie_glagoly_dvizheniya_s_pristavkami.md`:** that prior extraction is a
Russian-medium specialist reference (Гордиенко, *Русские глаголы движения с приставками*) scoped
specifically to 16 prefixed forms of the going/carrying/leading verb families. This Stilman book
covers substantially overlapping directional-prefix territory (13 of the same prefixes: в-, вы-,
под-, от-, до-, пере-, про-, за-, об-, с-, при-, у-, раз-) for the same five base-verb families, so
that overlap is **not** re-tabulated row-by-row here — it is instead paraphrased once, concisely,
into the Grammar Points section below (English-medium exposition + the book's own arrow-diagrams,
genuinely different presentation from 028's Russian-medium theme-by-theme tables, but not distinct
underlying content). What this book adds that is **not** in 028 and is captured in full below:

1. **The full base paradigm system** (ходить–итти, ездить–ехать, носить–нести, возить–везти,
   водить–вести) presented with explicit determinate/indeterminate contrastive rules, conjugation
   tables, and the specific "round-trip"/"habitual"/"actual" usage distinctions — 028 assumes this
   system as background and does not re-derive it from first principles the way Stilman does.
2. **The running/flying/swimming verb family** (бегать–бежать, летать–лететь, плавать–плыть) and
   their prefixed forms — entirely outside 028's going/carrying/leading scope, genuinely new
   territory.
3. **A large catalog of abstract/idiomatic/figurative derived meanings** (Part II, "Special Uses of
   Verbs of Motion," pp. 51–78) — verbs like находить/find, происходить/happen, относиться/behave
   toward, приходиться/have to, производить/produce, that have drifted semantically far from
   literal motion. This is the richest and most distinctive content in the book for this project's
   purposes and is captured in full in the Vocabulary table below.

**Coverage note:** every grammar point and distinct vocabulary item from the book is captured per
the coverage rule; the ~85 pages of drill exercises (Sections 6–19's fill-in-the-blank and
translation exercises) are **not** separately tabulated — they recycle vocabulary already captured
from the preceding explanatory sections and introduce no new lexical items.

**Vision-reading note:** this PDF has no text layer (`pdftotext -layout` produced essentially empty

## Grammar points

### Two verb families for "going": ходить-итти vs. ездить-ехать (p. 3)

Russian distinguishes locomotion *without* a vehicle (ходить–итти: walking, but also
self-propelled engines, clocks, progress of time) from locomotion *with* a vehicle
(ездить–ехать: car, boat, horse, etc.). The same split recurs for carrying (носить–нести vs.
возить–везти) and is absent only for leading (водить–вести covers both, though ездить-ехать
substitutes when the leading is explicitly by vehicle).

### Determinate vs. indeterminate imperfective (Sec. 2–4, pp. 3–6)

Seven verb families (going, riding, carrying by hand, carrying by vehicle, leading, flying,
swimming — plus running) have **two imperfective forms**, traditionally labeled determinate
(DET.) and indeterminate (INDET.):

- **DET.** describes motion in a definite direction actually taking place at a given moment ("he
  was going to the movies").
- **INDET.** is used for (1) a general/habitual type of locomotion with no specific direction
  ("people walk, fish swim"), (2) repeated/habitual motion even in a definite direction ("he drives
  to town every day"), and (3), critically, an **INDET. past-tense verb can describe a completed
  round trip** ("I drove to the post office this morning" implies he has already returned) — this
  round-trip reading has no dedicated form in English and is a common source of translation error.

Conjugation: INDET. verbs are all 2nd-conjugation (-ить), with consonant mutations (д→ж, с→ш, з→ж)
and shifting stress in 1st-person singular. DET. verbs are 1st-conjugation (-ти, except ехать),
fixed stress on the ending, no mutations.

### Prefixation and aspect (Sec. 13–14, pp. 23–25)

Directional prefixes do not change the aspect of INDET. imperfectives, but **always perfectivize**
DET. imperfectives. So prefixing gives two verbs identical in meaning but differing in aspect: one
imperfective (from INDET., e.g. уходить) and one perfective (from DET., e.g. уйти). Prefixed verbs
therefore have only **one** imperfective form, and the habitual/actual distinction that
non-prefixed verbs express through INDET./DET. can no longer be rendered morphologically once a
verb is prefixed (уходить covers both "I leave every day at 9" and "I am leaving now").

Formation quirks: verbs of riding form their prefixed imperfective with **-езжать** (not -ездить,
a form that occurs only in prefixed compounds); итти contracts to **-йти** in all prefixed forms
except с при-, which gives прийти (irregular).

### The 13 directional prefixes (Sec. 15–17, 21; pp. 26–38, 71–72)

Each prefix pairs with a specific preposition/case for its destination or source argument. Summarized from the book's own diagrams and review table:

| Prefix | General meaning | Governing preposition + case |
|---|---|---|
| при- | arriving, coming, bringing | в/на + acc., к + dat. (destination); из/с/от + gen. (source) |
| у- | going away, departing, leaving; carrying/taking away | same set as при-, opposed in direction |
| в- (во-, въ-) | entering, coming/bringing into | в + acc. |
| вы- | going, carrying, taking out; ("leaving, setting out" distinct from у-'s "leaving, being absent") | из + gen. (out of); в/на + acc. (out into) |
| под- (подо-, подъ-) | approaching, moving/bringing closer to (without entering) | к + dat. |
| от- (ото-, отъ-) | moving away from (opposed to под-); also "taking to a place" with носить/возить/водить | от + gen. (locomotion sense); any destination preposition (carrying sense) |
| до- | going/carrying/reaching as far as, up to a point | до + gen. |
| пере- | moving/crossing from one point to another, changing location | none (transitive, acc.); через + acc.; various destination prepositions |
| про- | passing, going/taking through or past; covering a distance | none (transitive, acc.); мимо + gen. (past); через + acc. (through) |
| за- | calling briefly, dropping in, stopping by (in passing) | destination prepositions, as with при- |
| об- (обо-, объ-) | moving/carrying around (to avoid something, or visiting several places in succession) | none (transitive, acc.) |
| с- (со-, съ-) | going/coming/stepping down, descending; getting off, stepping aside | с + gen. |
| раз- (разо-) | parting, going in different directions, dispersing (opposed to с-'s "coming together") | (reflexive forms only for the going family) |

Note: в-, под-, от-, об-, с- (all ending in a consonant) insert -о- before -й- (e.g. войти, буду) and
before ш (вошёл); в-/from-consonant prefixes also insert the separative sign ъ before е (e.g.
въехать).

The book distinguishes the near-synonymous **у- vs. по-** pair for "leaving": пойти/поехать
emphasize a trip/errand toward a destination or purpose ("он пошёл в библиотеку возвратить
книгу"), while у-forms emphasize simple absence from a place, with no destination implied ("он
ушёл на полчаса" — he stepped out for half an hour, *not* "он пошёл", which would be ungrammatical
without a stated destination/purpose).

### The prefix с- has three distinct functions (Sec. 21, p. 71)

1. Simple perfectivization of a round trip with INDET. bases (сходить, съездить — "completed
   round trip," see vocabulary table above).
2. Directional "off/down from" with the preposition с + genitive (сойти с трамвая — get off the
   streetcar), opposed to на + acc./loc.
3. "Coming/bringing together" (opposed to раз-, "parting/dispersing") — used almost exclusively
   with -ся reflexive forms for the going/carrying families (сходиться, съезжаться), but as plain
   transitive compounds for carrying (сносить, свозить — "bringing things to one place") and
   leading (сводить — "bringing people together").

### Frequentative (-живать) residues (Sec. 23, p. 74)

The going/carrying verb family has obsolete frequentative forms (хаживать, наживать) that survive
only inside certain prefixed compounds, sometimes as the true imperfective partner where the
regular INDET.-derived imperfective already has a different, established meaning. Examples: выходить
(imprf., "to go out") vs. выхаживать/выходить (a homonymous PRF.-imprf pair meaning "to nurse back
to health"); проводить (imprf., "to spend time," PRF. провести) vs. провожать (the true imprf. of
the *different* PRF. проводить, "to see someone off"); ухаживать (за + instr., "to tend/nurse"; also
"to court" a person, noun ухаживание); расхаживать ("to pace back and forth").

### Running, flying, swimming (Sec. 24, pp. 75–78)

бегать–бежать, летать–лететь, and плавать–плыть follow the same DET./INDET. and prefixation
patterns as the going/carrying/leading verbs, with two special quirks: (1) their **prefixed
imperfective** forms use -бегать, -летать, -плывать respectively (not the bare INDET. stems), and
(2) idiomatically these three families are used **far less broadly** than English "to run/fly/
swim" — engines, vehicles, and water do not take бегать/летать/плавать in Russian (итти and
работать serve for engines; литься/течь for liquids), and "running out of" something is rendered
by выйти, not any бегать-family verb (see "выйти (горючее)" in the vocabulary table).
