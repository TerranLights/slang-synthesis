## Grammar points

### Recurring consonant-mutation classes in legal/financial verbs
This unit's exercises drill the same general Russian consonant-mutation classes seen throughout
the book, applied to law/finance-specific verbs: б/бл (подстáвить, ослáбить-type), п/пл
(грáбить), т/ч (обaнкро́титься, похи́тить), т/щ (сокрaти́ть — cf. Unit 16), з/ж (внeсти́). Noted
per-verb in the tables above.

### Substantivized adjectives as core legal vocabulary
A recurring pattern in this unit's justice vocabulary is the substantivized adjective functioning
as a full noun: обвиня́eмый 'the accused', подсуд и́мый 'the defendant', зaключëнный 'prisoner',
нaли́чныe 'cash', нaгрáблeнноe 'loot'. This is ordinary Russian grammar (adjectives used as
nouns), but it is worth flagging as a productive naming strategy specific to legal/bureaucratic
register — a defendant is literally "the accused-one," a prisoner is literally "the
imprisoned-one" — since this same strategy (turning a participle/adjective into the name for a
person or thing) recurs as a plausible template for how derived slang could coin new role-labels.

### The -щик/-чик/-ник agentive suffix family in crime vocabulary
Several crime-related agent nouns in this unit share the productive -щик/-чик/-ник suffix family
already noted for other Wade units: доно́счик, шaнтaжи́ст (irregular, -и́ст not -щик), фaльшивомонeтчик,
рeгулиро́вщик. The exercises explicitly drill suffix selection (-ник/-чик/-щик) for stems like
гaи́ш-, зaëм-, отвeт-, прeсту́п-, рeгулиро́в-, доно́с-, ростов-, сво́д-, стрaхо́в- — confirming this
is a productively selected suffix (governed by the final consonant/sound of the stem) rather than
lexically arbitrary, and thus a mechanism that could plausibly extend to newly-coined agent nouns
in a derived slang lexicon.
