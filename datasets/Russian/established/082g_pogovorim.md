## Grammar points

### Ironic-disagreement register cluster (Урок 7's core construction)

Framed box, p. 87 — the lesson formalizes a genuinely distinctive Russian colloquial-argument
strategy:
- **`скажешь тоже + adjective/adverb/verb/noun`** — a fixed dismissive-disbelief formula ("yeah,
  right — [X]!") used to reject a claim as exaggerated.
- **Ironic repetition** — echoing the interlocutor's exact word back as a question with
  sarcastic/contradicting intonation (e.g. A: "Прекрасный тренажер" → B: "Прекрасный тренажер?!
  Смотри, уже сломался") — explicitly flagged (p. 88) as relying on *intonation alone* to carry
  the negation, since the words themselves are simply repeated, not negated.
- **`imperative-НСВ + не хочу`** — "you can [verb] all you like" — used to emphasize an
  unrestricted opportunity (p. 87, 89), the reverse-polarity counterpart of the reproach/necessity
  clusters seen in earlier lessons.
- **`чем + comparative, тем + comparative`** — standard proportional-comparison construction,
  drilled via a sport-and-lifestyle correlation exercise, p. 87, 92.

### Register/history note: the Английский клуб (English Club) essay

A substantial historical-expository passage (pp. 95-96) on Moscow's 18th-19th-century English
Club — its membership rules (blackball voting, aristocratic-male-only membership), social
function, and eventual 1918 dissolution — supplies period-specific institutional vocabulary
(баллотировка, забаллотированный, дворянин, роскошный) that is a useful register/era-contrast data
point for `analysis/` (formal historical-institutional prose vs. this book's otherwise-dominant
1990s-2000s colloquial-dialogue register).
