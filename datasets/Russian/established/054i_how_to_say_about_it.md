## Grammar points

### Confirming or correcting the interlocutor's read on a situation (§7, pp. 179–184)

This section addresses a distinct communicative move: judging whether the interlocutor's own mental
model of a situation is accurate, rather than judging the situation itself. Two families are
distinguished:

**Confirming-with-detail-correction: В том-то и дело / То-то и оно / То-то и есть.** These forms,
used bare, simply confirm the interlocutor is right ("В том-то и дело" = you're correct, that's
exactly it). The book's key observation (p. 180, worked minimal pairs) is that the *same* forms,
followed by a что-clause, can instead correct one specific factual assumption embedded in what the
interlocutor said, while still validating their overall diagnosis of the situation as a problem: "Мы
должны ему помочь. — То-то и оно, что не нужна" (you're right that this needs addressing, but you're
wrong that he needs our help — he doesn't). The construction's polarity (confirm vs. correct)
therefore depends entirely on what follows it, not on the phrase itself — a genuine structural
ambiguity the source flags explicitly rather than treating дело/оно/есть as simple agreement markers.

**Confirming-with-added-difficulty: В том-то и беда / В том-то и загвоздка / В том-то и задача
(проблема).** The book's five-row table (p. 181) sorts these by exactly what kind of complication is
being added atop a bare confirmation:
- беда/загвоздка mark the confirmed fact as something *unfortunate* — "вы правы, и плохо, что это
  так."
- задача/проблема mark the confirmed fact as *difficult to act on* — "вы правы, но сделать это не так
  просто" (right, but doing something about it won't be easy).
A further variant, **в том-то и сила**, surfaces only in the literary-quotation set (Е. Шварц, «Тень»,
p. 183) rather than the pedagogical dialogues — worth flagging since it doesn't appear in the book's
own systematic table, suggesting it's a productive extension of the pattern rather than a core member
of the taught set.

**Rejecting the interlocutor's causal framing: Ну при чём тут/здесь [X]?!** This is presented as the
opposite move from confirmation — the speaker asserts that a factor the interlocutor has cited as
relevant (a person, an event, a prior action) actually has *no bearing* on the situation being
discussed: "Ну при чём тут поездка! Будто нет других причин для волнений" (what does the trip have to
do with it — as if there weren't other reasons to be anxious). The book's closing literary
illustration (Bulgakov, «Мастер и Маргарита», p. 184) shows the construction pushed to its most
extreme register — a character flatly refusing an entire explanatory chain ("Аннушка уже купила
подсолнечное масло, и не только купила, но даже и разлила... — Простите, ... при чём здесь
подсолнечное масло, ... и какая Аннушка?") — used here as a register-ceiling example rather than
introducing new lexical content.

This closes **Раздел III. Оценка ситуации** (pp. 153–184) in its entirety. **Раздел IV. Оценка
передаваемой информации в плане её очевидности, достоверности, объективности** begins at printed
p. 185 with **§1. Ссылка на известность информации** — covered starting in `054j`. A first framed
table from that section (p. 186) is worth flagging in advance for whoever picks up `054j`: it already
distinguishes general-knowledge framing (Известно, что.../Мы знаем, что.../Как известно,.../Как мы
знаем, ...) from addressee-specific-knowledge framing (Вы знаете, что.../Вам известно, что.../Вы
помните, что.../Как вы знаете, .../Как вам известно, .../Как вы помните, ...) — and separately notes
that the что-headed variants are restricted to sentence-initial position while the как-headed variants
can appear either initially or medially in the sentence.
