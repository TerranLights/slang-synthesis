## Grammar points

### Idioms bundled directly with specific prefixed motion verbs (a reusable "prefix → idiom" pattern)

Distinctive presentational choice not seen elsewhere in the corpus this systematically: after each
prefix subsection's drill exercises, the book inserts a boxed "Задание N. Запомните данные идиомы и
придумайте с ними ситуации" (Learn these idioms and invent situations with them) list, tying a
handful of fixed idioms directly to the specific prefixed motion verb that head each one — i.e. every
idiom above is drawn from, and filed under, the very prefix section (в-/вы-, при-/у-, про-, об-) whose
literal meaning it figuratively extends. This gives a compact, source-organized mapping from
literal-spatial prefixed-motion-verb meaning to idiomatic extension that could be useful evidence for
the mechanics-analysis phase's work on how slang/idiom formation piggybacks on productive
grammatical morphology (pp. 22, 26, 35, 38).

### Figurative/non-literal uses of bare (unprefixed) motion verbs

p. 10 (§1.1.2) presents a short, dedicated "переносное значение" (figurative meaning) subsection for
unprefixed идти/бежать/лететь — distinct from these same verbs' ordinary literal "to walk/go"
sense: clocks "go" (часы идут точно), time "goes/runs/flies" (время идёт/бежит/летит, an
explicit three-way near-synonym intensity scale for the passage of time), clothing "goes with/suits"
someone (тебе идёт этот костюм), weather "goes" (дождь идёт), and a fixed formal-register discourse
formula "speech/the matter concerns X" (речь идёт о новом фильме). The accompanying illustrative
dialogue explicitly plays on the ambiguity between literal and figurative идти/бежать/лететь ("Время
идёт, а ты всё такая же... Время не идёт, а бежит... Летит!").

### Riddles (загадки) using motion verbs as their central pun

p. 13 gives three traditional Russian riddles built around a motion-verb + subject mismatch (the
punning misdirection is the entire mechanism of the riddle): "По тропинке идёт, / Лес на спинке
несёт" (Ёж — a hedgehog "carries a forest on its back"); "Кто на себе свой дом несёт?" (Улитка — a
snail); "Крылья есть, да не летает; / Ног нет, да не догонишь" (Рыба — a fish "has wings [fins] but
doesn't fly; has no legs but you can't catch it"). Folkloric material, not the book's own invention —
included here as a documented example of ordinary motion-verb vocabulary doing double duty in a
riddle genre, potentially relevant to later slang/wordplay-mechanics analysis.

### Prefix за- : the "start of motion" sense is grammatically restricted

p. 31's "Запомним" box states explicitly that when за- marks the *start* of motion rather than a
spatial "stopping by" sense, this reading is available **only** to non-transitive verbs of the
"ходить" (multidirectional) group — заходить, забегать, залетать, заплавать, заездить — and the
resulting verb is perfective. This is a specific, clean morphosyntactic restriction not spelled out
this explicitly in the corpus's other motion-verb sources sampled so far.

### Prefix про- : spatial vs. temporal-duration double meaning

p. 33-34 draws an explicit two-column contrast for про-: a **spatial** sense ("past/through/by"; НСВ
in the iterative/process reading, e.g. "мы уже проходили по этой улице", "мимо нас проезжали
машины") vs. a **temporal-duration** sense ("to spend [a given amount of time] doing the activity
continuously"; СВ, e.g. "мы весь день проходили по магазинам, но так ничего и не купили", "мы с
другом проездили допоздна"). The book frames these as two genuinely distinct meanings of the same
prefix rather than a single unified sense — a cleaner two-way split than a simple "по-" intensity
gloss would suggest.

### Aspect selection after governing verbs: a compact three-way infinitive-choice rule

p. 68 gives a tidy three-way rule for which aspect an infinitive takes after a governing verb:
(1) after verbs of pure *resultativity* — успеть, суметь, забыть, зайти, удаться (used in the
"кому удалось + infinitive" construction) — the governed infinitive is **always СВ** ("он успел
сказать всё", "как ему удалось понравиться ей?"); (2) after нравиться/любить/уметь, **either aspect**
is possible, with НСВ signaling a general/habitual process (insertable "всегда") and СВ signaling a
single/occasional result ("ему нравится гулять/погулять по парку"); (3) (from p. 69, imperative
side) prohibitions default to НСВ imperative ("не ходи сегодня в кино!"), while a СВ imperative with
не is reserved specifically for warning against an accidental/unintended action ("не разбей!", "не
опоздай!", "не простудись!") — a fairly long illustrative list of ~15 such warning-imperatives is
given (не засни, не забудь, не разбей, не проглоти, не урони, не упади, не простудись, не замёрзни,
не сломай, не разлей, не наступи, не захлебнись, не прижми, не опоздай, не проболтайся, не
проговорись). **This list closely overlaps** the "unintentional action" negated-imperative inventory
already captured in `039_izuchaem_vidy_glagola.md` — flagged as redundant rather than re-tabulated in
full, though the accompanying "смотри — смотри не сделай!" warning-particle framing was not
previously noted.

### Resultative-process НСВ present tense via -ива-/-ва- derivation

p. 51 names a specific derivational pattern for one of the three present-tense readings of НСВ verbs
(the "repeated resultative process" reading, e.g. "он прочитывает две-три книги в месяц", "брат
выкуривает по пачке сигарет в день"): these are typically formed from a СВ base via the suffixes
-ива-/(-ыва-) or -ва- (прочитать → прочитывать, выиграть → выигрывать, расспросить →
расспрашивать, достать → доставать). Worth noting as a compact, explicit derivational-morphology
rule for this specific НСВ subtype, distinct from ordinary НСВ/СВ aspectual pairing.

### Literary/song citations illustrating motion-verb and aspect usage in real registers

The book draws several of its "analyze this usage" exercise texts from named published authors
rather than inventing all examples itself — worth flagging as attested real-register evidence rather
than pedagogical invention, though per copyright discipline only short illustrative fragments are
reproduced here, not full stanzas:

- **Gogol** (p. 43): "Русь, куда несёшься ты? Дай ответ. Не даёт ответа..." — reflexive/figurative
  нестись ("to hurtle/rush") applied metaphorically to the nation, a canonical literary citation the
  book uses to illustrate reflexive-motion-verb usage.
- **Yevtushenko** (p. 24): a short poem built entirely on the literal/figurative contrast between
  ходить (habitual, dismissive: "разнообразные не те") and приходить (a single meaningful visit) —
  "Со мною вот что происходит: / Ко мне мой старый друг не ходит, / А ходят в праздной суете /
  Разнообразные не те..."
- **Okudzhava** (p. 24): a short song stanza repeating "Её утешают, а шарик летит" (a balloon
  "keeps flying away" as three life-stages of a woman are shown crying) — illustrates бежит/летит-type
  present-tense process readings in a real song lyric.
- **Vysotsky** (p. 43): two separate citations — "По миру люди маленькие носятся, / Живут себе в
  рассрочку..." (носиться, multidirectional-habitual reading) and "За нами гонится эскадра по пятам"
  (гнаться/гонится) — both used to illustrate reflexive/figurative multidirectional motion verbs in
  a bard-song register.
- **Dovlatov** (p. 26, p. 64, p. 78): three separate short comic anecdotes/excerpts used as aspect-
  and motion-verb-analysis texts. One (p. 26) is a two-line joke ending in a punchline where a
  character promises "в следующий раз [принесу] марихуану" ("next time [I'll bring] marijuana") —
  a passing, incidental **drug-slang/taboo-adjacent reference** in an otherwise ordinary grammar
  exercise, worth flagging as the kind of register data this project tracks even when accidental.
  The two longer Dovlatov excerpts (p. 64 "Гостеприимный хозяин", p. 78 "На рыбалке") are written in
  his characteristically clipped, fast-aspect-switching narrative style ("сволочи" as a mild
  pejorative appears in the second) but are otherwise ordinary narrative texts used as fill-in-the-
  aspect exercises, not extracted further per copyright discipline.
