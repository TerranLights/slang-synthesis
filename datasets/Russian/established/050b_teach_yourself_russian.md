## Grammar points

### 1. Куда? (where to?)
Used instead of где when asking about direction/motion rather than static position: Куда он идёт?
("where is he going") vs. Где он работает? ("where does he work") (p.29).

### 2. Negation
Verbs are negated with не placed immediately before the verb: Я не говорю по-испански ("I don't
speak Spanish"). не is pronounced fused with the verb it negates (p.29).

### 3. Second-conjugation verbs with a д-stem: д→ж mutation
Verbs like видеть ("to see") conjugate like говорить but mutate the stem-final д to ж in the я-form
only: вижу, видишь, видит, видим, видите, видят. Сидеть ("to sit") behaves the same way. The
personal pronoun subject (except он/она/они) can be dropped in direct speech, e.g. Видите аптеку?
("[Do] you see the chemist's?") (p.30).

### 4. Imperative formation
Take the ты-form of the present tense, remove the last three letters: if what remains ends in a
vowel, add -й(те) (работай/работайте); if it ends in a consonant, add -и(те) (идите). Commands are
conventionally followed by an exclamation mark (p.30).

### 5. Accusative case
Introduced as the case for the direct object of a sentence. For neuter nouns the accusative is
identical to the nominative. For masculine inanimate nouns the accusative also equals the
nominative (animate masculine nouns are deferred to Unit 5). All feminine nouns except those ending
in a soft sign change their ending to form the accusative: -а → -у (Москва → Москву), -я → -ю
(фамилия → фамилию), soft-sign feminine nouns are unchanged (дверь → дверь) (pp.30–31).

### 6. в/на + accusative for motion, vs. + prepositional for position
The prepositional case (Unit 2) describes static position: Иван работает в ресторане ("Ivan is
working in the restaurant"). The same prepositions в/на govern the accusative case instead when
describing motion toward a destination (answering куда?): Иван идёт в ресторан ("Ivan is going
into/to the restaurant") (p.31).

### 7. У вас есть...? ("have you got...?") construction
Literally "by you is there...?" — not a verb but a fixed possessive phrase; the есть can be omitted
without changing the basic meaning (У меня план = У меня есть план, "I have a plan"), though
including есть adds emphasis. Full pronoun paradigm: у тебя (you have, informal), у него (he/it
has), у неё (she has), у нас (we have), у вас (you have), у них (they have) (pp.31–32).
