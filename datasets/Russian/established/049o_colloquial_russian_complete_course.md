## Grammar points

### Lesson 20 (final lesson) — Conjunctions vs. prepositions, future after когда́, gerunds
- **Conjunctions vs. prepositions sharing an English translation**: English words like "after" can
  function as either a conjunction (introducing a full clause) or a preposition (governing a noun
  phrase), but Russian uses **structurally distinct** forms for the two roles even when the English
  gloss is identical — по́сле того́ как (conjunction, introduces a clause: По́сле того́ как мы
  пообе́дали, мы пошли́ домо́й) vs. по́сле + genitive (preposition, governs a noun: По́сле обе́да мы
  пошли́ домо́й). Further conjunction/preposition pairs sharing this "clause vs. bare noun" split
  pattern: до того́ как / прежде чем ("before," conjunctions) vs. presumably до + genitive
  (preposition, established earlier in the course); пе́ред тем как ("just before," conjunction); с
  тех пор как ("since," conjunction, cf. the adverbial phrase с тех пор already seen from Lesson
  15 onward). This systematic conjunction-vs-preposition distinction for concepts English collapses
  into one word is a clean typological contrast point for mechanics-analysis: a conlang could
  likewise force an obligatory clause/noun-phrase distinction on connectives that a naturalistic
  slang register might then productively blur or collapse.
- **Future tense required after когда́ when the main clause is future**: unlike English, which
  typically uses a present tense inside a "when"-clause referring to future time ("when she
  finishes, she will go"), Russian requires the **future tense inside the когда́-clause itself**
  whenever the main clause is future (Когда́ она́ око́нчит университе́т, она́ пое́дет за грани́цу
  — literally "when she will-finish… she will go") — the same requirement extends to по́сле того́
  как. This is the direct temporal-clause analog of the already-noted rule (Lesson 15) that a
  realizable/non-hypothetical conditional е́сли-clause referring to future time also takes a future
  verb rather than a present one — confirming a broader generalization across this language that
  **subordinate clauses referring to future time consistently take future-tense marking**, unlike
  English, which frequently uses present tense in both conditional and temporal subordinate clauses
  referring to the future. Worth flagging as a systematic rule (not two isolated facts) for
  mechanics-analysis.
- **Gerunds — verbal adverbs, distinct from participles**: like some participles, gerunds
  correspond to an English -ing form, but where participles replace adjectival (кото́рый) clauses,
  gerunds replace **adverbial** clauses (time, cause, or a second coordinated verb) — a clean
  category split (participle = verbal adjective, gerund = verbal adverb) mirrored in many other
  Indo-European languages' converb/participle systems.
- **Imperfective gerund formation**: strip the last two letters from the 3rd-person-plural present
  and add -я: чита́ют→чита́я, говоря́т→говоря́. Stress usually matches the 1st-person-singular
  present (смотрю́→смотря́), with a flagged exception (сижу́→ся́дя— actually си́дя, matching neither
  3rd-plural nor 1st-singular stress straightforwardly). The familiar spelling rule again forces -a
  instead of -я after a hushing consonant (слы́шат→слы́ша). Verbs in -авать take -авая
  (дава́ть→дава́я). **Some verbs entirely lack an imperfective gerund** — monosyllabic verbs (пить,
  петь) and several irregular 1st-conjugation verbs (писа́ть, е́хать) — a genuine paradigm gap
  class, comparable to the missing present-passive-participle class from Lesson 19; reflexive
  verbs' imperfective gerund always ends in -ясь (never -яс or -ась).
- **Use of the imperfective gerund**: describes an action performed **at the same time and by the
  same subject** as the main verb, substituting for a когда́-clause with matching tense (Си́дя в
  по́езде, Пи́тер разгова́ривает/бу́дет разгова́ривать/разгова́ривал… — the same single gerund form
  си́дя covers all three tenses of the main clause, since the gerund itself carries **no tense
  marking of its own**, only aspect; its "time" is read off the main verb by strict simultaneity).
  Also substitutes for causal (since/because) clauses (Зна́я пробле́мы Сиби́ри, Пи́тер реши́л
  пое́хать туда́, replacing так как он знал) and for one of two coordinated main verbs joined by
  "and" (Она́ гото́вит обе́д, слу́шая ра́дио, replacing Она́ гото́вит обе́д и слу́шает ра́дио) — a
  three-way functional range (temporal, causal, coordinate) all serviced by one non-finite form,
  disambiguated purely by discourse context, not by any morphological marking distinguishing the
  three uses. The **negative gerund** (не + gerund) renders English "without …ing" (Постро́или
  фа́брики в Сиби́ри, не принима́я во внима́ние экологи́ческие фа́кторы "…without taking ecological
  factors into consideration") — a further use not obviously predictable from the "simultaneous
  same-subject action" core meaning, worth flagging as an additional discourse function layered
  onto the same morphological form.
- **Perfective gerund formation**: strip -л from the masculine perfective past and add -в (or the
  less common -вши): прочита́л→прочита́в, увидел→уви́дев; reflexive verbs take -вшись
  (оде́лся→оде́вшись). A distinct, smaller subclass instead forms a perfective gerund in bare -я,
  built the same way as the imperfective gerund but from the perfective stem — specifically
  prefixed forms of нести́/везти́/вести́/идти́ (принести́→принесу́т→принеся́; прийти́→приду́т→придя́;
  привести́→приведу́т→приведя́) — meaning this one small verb family (the motion-verb roots first
  introduced in Lesson 10, now prefixed per Lesson 14) has a **perfective gerund that looks
  morphologically like an imperfective gerund** (bare -я ending) rather than like the ordinary
  perfective -в/-вши pattern — a genuine formal syncretism across the imperfective/perfective
  gerund system specific to this one lexical family, worth flagging as an irregular residue tied to
  a verb class that has already shown multiple special behaviors throughout the course (Lesson 10's
  indeterminate/determinate split, Lesson 14's prefixed-motion-verb reanalysis).
- **Use of the perfective gerund**: describes an action by the same subject that occurred **prior**
  to the main verb's action, substituting for a по́сле того́ как-clause (Написа́в письмо́, Пи́тер
  пошёл на по́чту), with the same tense-invariance property as the imperfective gerund (the single
  form написа́в covers past, present, and future main clauses alike, since only relative
  before/after ordering matters, not absolute tense). Also substitutes for causal clauses
  (Прочита́в мно́го о Сиби́ри, Пи́тер реши́л пое́хать туда́ — "having read" standing in for "since he
  had read") and for the first of two sequentially-ordered coordinated main verbs (Он оде́лся и
  пошёл на рабо́ту → Оде́вшись, он пошёл на рабо́ту).
- **Mandatory subject-identity constraint on gerunds — the clearest, most explicitly-stated version
  of this constraint in the whole course**: "the subject of a gerund must always be the same as the
  subject of the main clause" — stated as an absolute rule, illustrated by a sentence Russian
  **cannot** express with a gerund at all ("Peter having written the letter, we went to the post
  office" — different subjects for the gerund-action and the main clause), which must instead fall
  back to an ordinary conjunction-based когда́/по́сле того́ как construction. This is a sharper,
  more absolute version of the same-subject requirement already seen conditioning что́бы vs. plain
  infinitive in Lesson 15 (там the requirement governed a lexical choice between two competing
  constructions; here it is a hard grammaticality constraint with no gerund-based alternative at
  all) — an excellent, well-documented case for mechanics-analysis of "same-subject" as a
  recurring, independently-motivated licensing condition across multiple, otherwise unrelated
  non-finite/reduced-clause constructions in this language (что́бы-infinitives, both gerund types),
  suggesting a conlang could productively generalize a single "controlled non-finite clause
  requires subject identity with its matrix clause" principle across its own whole non-finite
  system rather than stipulating it construction-by-construction.
