## Grammar points

### Continued Комментарий-led near-synonym pairs; a chain of related discriminations

This shard's five pairs (ОБЫЧНЫЙ/НЕОБЫЧНЫЙ-ОБЫКНОВЕННЫЙ/НЕОБЫКНОВЕННЫЙ form a linked negated-prefix
quadruple; ОСОБЫЙ-ОСОБЕННЫЙ; СМЕРТНЫЙ-СМЕРТЕЛЬНЫЙ; СКРЫТНЫЙ-СКРЫТЫЙ; РЫБНЫЙ-РЫБИЙ) continue the
`Комментарий`-plus-worked-minimal-pair-example format established in `066h`. Notably, НЕ-prefixed
ОБЫЧНЫЙ/ОБЫКНОВЕННЫЙ receive their own separate `Комментарий` and worked-example treatment (rather
than being presented as simple negations) — the negated pair's discrimination logic parallels but is
not identical to the base pair's (deviation-from-norm vs. intensity-of-manifestation), which is worth
noting as a case where Russian negative-prefix derivation shifts a near-synonym discrimination axis
rather than simply inverting it.

### СКРЫТНЫЙ vs. СКРЫТЫЙ: person-disposition vs. state-of-the-thing

A clean, generalizable discrimination pattern worth flagging for later mechanics work: `-тн-`
(скрытный) marks a person's/animal's *habitual character trait* of concealment, while `-т-`
(скрытый, a past-passive-participle-derived adjective) marks the *concealed state of the thing
itself*, independent of any agent's personality. This mirrors a broader Russian pattern where
participle-derived adjectives (-т-, -н-, -нн- deverbal forms) describe an object's resultant state
while a parallel `-ив-`/`-тн-`-type suffix describes an animate agent's disposition (compare
СКРЫТНЫЙ/СКРЫТЫЙ to earlier ЗАВИСТЛИВЫЙ "envious [person]" vs. a hypothetical *завистный, which the
book does not include — a gap worth noting for future comparative work).
