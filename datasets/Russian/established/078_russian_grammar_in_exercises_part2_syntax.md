## Grammar points

### Раздел 1 overview: three types of subordinate word-combination (p.7)
Glazunova opens by defining a **словосочетание** ("word combination") as a syntactic unit built from
a главное слово ("head word") and one or more зависимые слова ("dependent words"). Three
relationships link head and dependent: **согласование** (agreement — the dependent changes its
form to match the head's gender/number/case, e.g. adjective+noun), **управление** (government — the
dependent takes a fixed case regardless of the head's form, with or without a preposition), and
**примыкание** (adjunction — a smaller, invariable dependent, typically an adverb or infinitive,
attaches purely by meaning with no case-marking mechanism at all, e.g. *бежать быстро*).

### Agreement (Согласование, pp. 7-11)
Agreement-type dependents are inflecting word classes: full-form adjectives and participles,
determinative pronouns, and ordinal numerals — all of which track the head noun's gender, number,
and case. The chapter is essentially a long representative word list (new куртка, новый костюм,
etc.) rather than new rule content beyond this definition; skipped as largely redundant with prior
Wave 1/2 adjective-agreement coverage, since the concept itself (not the drill vocabulary) is what's
new here.

### Government without prepositions: verb + bare-case noun (pp. 12-15)
Government (управление) is defined as a fixed case requirement on the dependent that does **not**
change with the head word's own form. The chapter tabulates Russian's five common
verb-plus-bare-case-noun patterns by the case the verb assigns: genitive (with a closed set of
verbs of desire/attainment/request/waiting/fear whose *government pattern itself distinguishes
abstract vs. concrete objects* — e.g. *хотеть счастья* [abstract, genitive] vs. *хотеть конфету*
[concrete, accusative], footnoted on p.12), dative (verbs of trust, joy, envy, smiling, liking,
sympathy, hindrance, harm, address), accusative (ordinary transitive verbs — an object that is a
"direct target" of the action), instrumental (verbs of interest, absorption, pride, boasting,
distinction, command, illness, suffering, sacrifice, use), and prepositional (which the source notes
always requires a preposition — Russian has no bare-prepositional-case verb government).

A key distinguishing rule (boxed, p.12): a dependent noun attaches *without* a preposition when it
functions as the direct object of the action; it takes a preposition when it represents more of a
"situation" than a concrete object — contrasting *читать книгу* ("read a book," direct object) with
*читать о животных* ("read about animals," situational object).

### Government without prepositions: noun + bare-case noun (pp. 16-21)
Nouns derived from verbs (deverbal nouns) largely inherit their parent verb's government pattern,
but with one systematic shift: nouns derived from verbs that governed bare accusative (and a closed
class of "feeling" nouns) switch their nominal dependent to **genitive** instead — *читать книгу* →
*чтение книги*; *желать счастья* stays genitive → *пожелание счастья*. Nouns can also govern
dative (*слава победителям*, *дорога молодым*), instrumental (*увлечение поэзией*, *руководство
людьми*), and — footnoted as the only case a noun *cannot* take bare, always requiring a preposition
in this position — prepositional. A further rule (p.20-21) covers when a noun+genitive can be
replaced by a synonymous noun+"с"+instrumental (only when the head noun denotes a real container/
substance relation, e.g. *банка сметаны* ↔ *банка со сметаной*; blocked when the head noun denotes
an organizational structure/system, e.g. *изгиб реки*, *набор карандашей*, which cannot take "с").

### Double government (Двойное управление, pp. 22-23)
Some verbs simultaneously govern two dependents in different cases from a single head — e.g.
*дать* + dative (recipient) + accusative (object): *дать студенту книгу*. The boxed rule states word
order is free in this construction; the element carrying new/rhematic information for the sentence
tends to be placed last (*дать студенту книгу*; *дать книгу студенту*; *книгу дать студенту*). A
second double-government pattern covers verb+accusative+instrumental (*считать врача лучшим
специалистом* — "consider the doctor the best specialist," predicative instrumental after a
naming/appointing/evaluating verb).

### Government of adjectives and participles (pp. 24-27)
Adjectives distinguish three dependent-case patterns by semantic type (p.27, boxed): (1) bare
dative, for adjectives expressing a relational attribute toward a person (*верный другу* — "faithful
to a friend"); (2) genitive + для, for adjectives whose applicability is explicitly scoped to a
group (*трудный для ребёнка* — "difficult for a child"); (3) either bare dative or genitive+для for
adjectives expressing a subjective judgment oriented toward a person (*необходимый мне* / *необходимый
для меня*) — except a small exception class (e.g. *симпатичный*) that takes only bare dative, never
для. A separate rule (p.27) explains the *знаком(а) с кем* vs. *кому знакомо* alternation: the
с+instrumental construction requires an animate experiencer subject ("Я знаком с этим человеком");
an inanimate subject forces the dative-without-preposition variant ("Это чувство мне знакомо").

### Government of quantitative numerals (pp. 28-29)
Numerals 2-4 govern genitive singular on their noun; 5 and above govern genitive plural. In
nominative/accusative position, nouns after 2-4 nonetheless appear in a form historically singular;
in all other cases the noun is plural, agreeing with the numeral (*три друга* nom./acc. but *трём
друзьям* dative). Modifying adjectives after 2-4 follow a distinct rule: masculine/neuter adjectives
take genitive plural (*два больших дома*) while feminine adjectives take nominative plural (*две
большие берёзы*) — and this extends to substantivized adjectives (*два прохожих*, *две столовые*).

### Prepositional government (pp. 30-82)
The bulk of Раздел 1 is organized around preposition classes by *meaning* rather than by case, each
with its own boxed disambiguation rule against near-synonymous prepositions: place/direction (pp.
30-38, e.g. из vs. от as distinguished sources of separation — из for extraction from a
container/origin, от for separation from a point or person, occasionally interchangeable with у);
time (pp. 39-47, e.g. за + accusative for a completed time-span the way a magazine "issue" covers a
month, and the "за X до Y" construction for "X time before Y"); cause (pp. 48-52, distinguishing
по + prepositional's evidential "judging by" sense — по лицу, по глазам, по натуре — from causal
"because of" senses, plus the fixed genitive constructions с позволения/с разрешения/с согласия/с
благословения кого, and благодаря/согласно + dative vs. ради + genitive); determinative/attributive
meaning (pp. 53-58, ending in the до+genitive intensifier idiom list); indirect-object meaning (pp.
59-68); purpose (pp. 69-73, distinguishing ради + genitive "in order to achieve X" from в качестве +
genitive / как + nominative-or-accusative "used as X"); condition (pp. 74-77, при + prepositional
for an action set against a backdrop condition vs. по + dative for an action conforming to an
established rule); and quantity (pp. 77-82, covering в/на + accusative for frequency/phase
distinctions and verb-specific directional prepositions after сложить/разделить-type verbs).

### Adjunction (Примыкание, pp. 83-102)
Adjunction dependents carry no case morphology at all and attach purely semantically, answering a
"how/in what manner" question from the head word. Three subtypes are covered: adjunction to a verb
(pp. 83-89, the largest subtype, culminating in the closed idiom list at p.89 — see Vocabulary);
adjunction to a noun (pp. 90-95, covering when an infinitive vs. a deverbal noun is required as a
noun's dependent — a boxed rule on p.95 ties this to whether the sentence expresses a *concrete*
realized action [infinitive: "у нас оставалась надежда спасти их"] vs. a *potential* one [deverbal
noun: "остаётся надежда на спасение"], with infinitive as the fallback whenever the verb has no
corresponding deverbal noun); and adjunction to a pronoun/adjective/adverb (pp. 96-102, covering
constructions like *признак — инфинитив* with the conjunction чтобы, whose presence or absence
signals whether the adjective's quality and the infinitive's action share a single subject — *я
счастлив вас видеть* [same subject, чтобы dropped] vs. *я взял отчёт, чтобы познакомиться с вашей
работой* [different subjects/ongoing relevance, чтобы required], p.102).

### Материал для повторения (p.103, review material)
