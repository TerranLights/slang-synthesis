# Russian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Walking on Russian Vocabulary, Part 7 (Chapter 3, §6: Просторечная лексика "Substandard/Colloquial Vocabulary" — the book's own student/youth-slang chapter)

**Source:** Е.Р. Ласкарева, *Прогулки по русской лексике*, Zlatoust, 2010. This part covers the
sixth and final content sub-section of Chapter 3: "Просторечная лексика" (Substandard/Very
Colloquial Vocabulary), printed pp. 188-194 (PDF pp. 190-196). Same +2 PDF-to-printed-page offset
(re-verified against the visible printed folio on PDF p.190, "188"); no text layer (fully
vision-read); no marginalia found on any page sampled.

**This is the single highest-priority sub-section of the whole book for this project's purposes.**
Unlike every other sub-section of Chapter 3, this one is explicitly framed by the source itself as
student/youth slang and просторечие (substandard speech) rather than standard-register vocabulary —
its own opening text states this vocabulary is "often coarse or simply rude," is excluded from
official RFL certification tests (ТРКИ), and exists purely as an "excursion" for learners curious
about expressive spoken Russian. A second in-text callout (p.190/PDF 192) explicitly names
"студенческий и молодёжный жаргон" (student and youth jargon) as a specific category within this
material. **Coverage here is comprehensive** — nearly every distinct slang item across all 16
exercises is captured below, since this is exactly the register-marked, non-redundant content the
whole project exists to collect (contrast with 060d/060e/060f's compression of basic/neutral drill
vocabulary).

## Grammar points

### Explicit framing as a "sensitive," out-of-scope-for-certification topic (p.188, opening)

The source's own opening explicitly labels this vocabulary "просторечная" (substandard) and
"сниженная" (lowered-register), used by Russians "for greater expressiveness" despite often being
coarse or disapproving in tone; it explicitly states the material is **excluded from official RFL
certification tests** and is offered only for learners specifically curious about expressive spoken
Russian. It further advises learners to use this vocabulary "very carefully and thoughtfully" in
real conversation. This self-aware register framing is itself valuable metadata: the *source*
independently corroborates this project's own Usage_Tier_Taxonomy treatment of "colloquial" and
"vulgar" as distinct, source-flagged tiers rather than an inferred judgment.

### Explicit acknowledgment of student/youth jargon as a named sub-register (p.190/PDF 192)

Mid-chapter callout: "К специфическим просторечным вариантам можно отнести также студенческий и
молодёжный жаргон" (student and youth jargon can also be classed among these specifically
substandard variants) — immediately followed by exam-cheating/failing vocabulary (шпора, срезаться,
засыпаться, передрать, сдуть, зубрить) that is unambiguously student-register slang rather than
general substandard speech. Worth flagging as the book's own two-way split within this
sub-section: general coarse/colloquial speech vs. a narrower student/youth-jargon subset.

### Author's own restraint disclaimer (p.190/PDF 192, after Ex.7)

A second callout explicitly states the list "could be continued" but the authors deliberately cap it
("мы хотим пощадить уши и глаза уважаемых профессоров, которые не выносят сленга" — "we want to
spare the ears and eyes of respected professors who can't stand slang"), closing with "это для вас
экскурс в тему, а не Библия" ("this is an excursion into the topic for you, not the Bible"). This
is useful corroborating evidence that even a source willing to include слэнг at all is consciously
under-representing it relative to the real spoken lexicon — i.e. this sub-section's ~90 items should
be read as a deliberately conservative sample of a much larger living register, not an exhaustive one.
