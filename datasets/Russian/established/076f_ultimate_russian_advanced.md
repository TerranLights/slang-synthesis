## Grammar points

### Lesson 17 (§C): который vs. что/кто as relative pronouns; particles

**который-vs.-что/кто discrimination is a genuinely useful, cleanly-stated rule** not always
isolated this clearly elsewhere: что/кто are used when the antecedent is itself a pronoun (то, всё,
тот/та) and remain indeclinable, while который is used when the antecedent is a noun and declines
for case/gender/number to match its own role in its clause (not the antecedent's). **The particle
inventory (see Vocabulary above) is the lesson's real find** — a complete, functionally-labeled
closed-class list of Russian discourse particles, several of which (ведь, же, вот, -то) recur
constantly in natural spoken Russian but are easy to under-document since they carry no dictionary
meaning of their own; this is exactly the register/pragmatics layer most useful for modeling
naturalistic derived-conlang dialogue.

### Lesson 18 (§C): verbs of teaching/learning (учить/учиться/обучать/преподавать/изучать/заниматься);
word order

**The teaching/learning verb-family discrimination is a genuinely useful consolidation**: учить +
accusative = "to learn" (memorize); учить/обучить/научить (person in accusative, subject in dative)
= "to teach," reserved for school-age/informal contexts, vs. преподавать for university-level
teaching; учиться/научиться (dative or infinitive) = "to study/learn a skill"; учиться на + accusative
= "to train to become (a profession)"; изучать/изучить = "to study in depth" (academic subjects);
заниматься = "to do homework/study" (the activity, not the subject). Six distinct but
etymologically-related verbs each carving out a different frame — a clean worked example of dense
near-synonymy in this semantic field. **Word-order flexibility rules (new information toward
clause-end; adjective/adverb placement; pronoun-must-precede-verb-after-question-words) are
largely redundant** with prior full treatments in this corpus, though the explicit demonstration
that intonation alone can convert a "neutral" declarative into an "expressive" one via word-order
reshuffling (Мы получили хорошие оценки → Оценки мы получили хорошие! → Хорошие оценки мы
получили!) is a clean, concrete illustration of Russian's information-structure flexibility, useful
for the project's own naturalistic-dialogue modeling.
