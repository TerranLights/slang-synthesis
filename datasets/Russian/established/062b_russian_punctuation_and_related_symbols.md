## Grammar points

### Chapter 2 — Secondary sentence elements

**2.1 Extended sentences.** A simple sentence with additional (secondary) elements beyond a bare
one-word subject/predicate is a простое распространённое предложение "simple extended sentence,"
contrasted with the простое нераспространённое предложение "simple non-extended sentence" of Ch. 1
(pp. 10–11).

**2.2 Types of secondary elements.** Five types extend the primary elements:

- **Adverbial modifiers (обстоятельства)**, an adverb or prepositional phrase, of three meaning
  subtypes: time (когда?), place (где?/куда?/откуда?), manner (как?/каким образом?) (pp. 11–12).
- **Object complements (дополнения)**: direct objects (прямóе дополнéние, accusative, answering
  кого?/что?) after transitive verbs (переходный глагол); indirect objects (кóсвенное дополнéние,
  any non-accusative case) after intransitive verbs (непереходный глагол) or alongside a direct
  object with verbs of giving/speaking; an infinitive can also serve as an object complement
  (taking no case ending) after modal verbs (модальные глаголы, e.g. хотеть, желать, любить,
  нравиться, мочь, уметь) (pp. 12–13).
- **Predicate nominals (именные сказуемые)**, already introduced in Ch. 1, following a linking verb
  (глагол-связка: быть, оказаться, оставаться, являться) (p. 13).
- **Attributes (определения)**, modifying any noun and answering какой? or чей?. Coordinated
  attributes (согласованное определение) agree in case/number/gender with their head noun and
  include: adjectives, participles (причастие, a verb used adjectivally), first/second-person
  possessive pronouns (мой/наш/твой/ваш — but NOT third-person его/её/их, which never agree),
  demonstrative pronouns, and ordinal numbers (порядковое числительное). Non-coordinated attributes
  (несогласованное определение) don't agree with their head noun, and include: third-person
  possessive pronouns, prepositional phrases, bare non-nominative noun phrases (e.g. "your friend's
  brother"), adverbs modifying nouns, and infinitives modifying nouns (which can never take endings
  and are therefore non-coordinated by definition) (pp. 13–16).
- **Appositives (приложения)**, nouns modifying other nouns. Coordinated by default (same case as
  the head noun), except: geographic-name appositives (остров, полуостров, озеро, залив, пролив,
  станция) are generally non-coordinated (на острове Сахалин, not the declined на острове Сахалине);
  only город/деревня/река-headed appositives can be coordinated in some registers (в городе
  Новосибирске), while official/military/scientific-geography style keeps even these undeclined (в
  городе Новосибирск). Titles of literary/artistic/periodical works are non-coordinated appositives
  when introduced by a common noun (роман «Анна Каренина» stays undeclined even when роман itself
  takes a case ending), but decline normally once the common noun is dropped (pp. 16–19).

**2.3 Detached secondary elements (обособленные члены предложения).** Any of the four secondary-
element types (adverbial modifier, attribute, appositive, object) can be set off from the rest of
the sentence by a special intonational pause — this is a preview of Chapter 7's comma rules for
detached elements (pp. 19–20).

**2.4 Formally unconnected words.** Four groups of words attach to a sentence without modifying or
being modified by any element: vocatives/direct address (обращения), the words да/нет when they
appear mid-sentence, interjections (междометия, conveying emotion), and parenthetical words
(вводные слова, conveying the speaker's attitude toward the whole utterance — e.g. по-видимому
"apparently" signals the speaker's own uncertainty about a claim; видите ли "you see" is a
listener-attention-getting device). The first three groups are collectively "inserted elements"
(вставные конструкции) (pp. 20–21).

### Chapter 3 — The complex sentence

A слóжное предложéние "complex/compound sentence" (the Russian umbrella term for both) is either a
complex sentence (сложноподчинённое предложение, containing a subordinate/dependent clause,
придаточное предложение, as a grammatical element of a main clause, главное предложение) or a
compound sentence (сложносочинённое предложение, joining syntactically independent sentences — see
Ch. 4). **Key cross-linguistic note the book flags explicitly: Russian always sets off a
subordinate clause with commas, while English usually does not** — this becomes the central rule of
Ch. 7 (p. 22).

- **Explanatory clauses (изъяснительные придаточные части)** extend a verb of perception/emotion/
  speech as its object, introduced by что ("that" — obligatory in Russian even where English can
  drop "that") (p. 23).
- **Attributive/relative clauses (определительные придаточные части)** modify a noun (its
  antecedent, определяемое слово) and must directly follow that antecedent, using a relative
  pronoun (кто, который, какой, чей) or the conjunction что; when the antecedent is expressed by
  a "such"-type pronoun (тот, такой, таков, таковой) the same set of conjunctive words/что/как
  links the clause (pp. 23–24).
- **Dependent adverbial clauses (придаточные предложения)** extend the verb of the main clause,
  in seven meaning subtypes with dedicated conjunctions: time (когда), manner (как), place (где),
  purpose (чтобы), effect (так как), condition (если), concession (хотя). Time/manner/place/
  purpose/condition/concession clauses can be inverted and preposed before the main clause;
  attributive clauses cannot — they must stay bound directly after their antecedent (pp. 24–26).
- **Что vs. как: stressed/unstressed functional split.** что́ and ка́к are stressed when functioning
  as an interrogative pronoun or conjunctive word (что́ "what," ка́к "how"); что and как are
  unstressed when functioning as a plain conjunction (что corresponding to optional English "that";
  как corresponding to "as" or nothing at all). This distinction is a genuine minimal-pair
  phenomenon the book deliberately marks with stress accents throughout, unlike кто "who," which is
  always stressed regardless of function (pp. 26–27).

### Chapter 4 — The compound sentence

A compound sentence (сложносочинённое предложение) joins two or more independently-standing
simple sentences via a coordinating conjunction.

- **Single/simple conjunctions (простые союзы)**, three types: copulative/coordinating
  (соединительные союзы: и, да — да has a colloquial/ironic flavor here too, distinct from its
  contrastive-conjunction sense below); disjunctive (разделительные союзы: или, либо — либо is more
  formal); contrastive (противительные союзы: а implies mild contrast, но stronger contrast, да —
  same word as the copulative да above, but now meaning "but" — implies strong contrast with an
  ironic/colloquial flavor and is restricted to colloquial speech, and unlike и/или, contrastive
  conjunctions are never repeated in pairs) (pp. 29–30).
- **Repeating/compound coordinating conjunctions (сложные сочинительные союзы)** — copulative
  и...и "both...and," да...да (colloquial), ни...ни "neither...nor"; disjunctive или...или
  "either...or," либо...либо (more formal/dry), не то...не то "either...or" (emphasizes the
  speaker's own uncertainty about which), то...то "now...then" (implies alternating action) (pp.
  30–31).
- **Parallel sentence elements (однородные члены предложения)**, lit. "homogeneous sentence
  members" — any two-or-more elements occupying the same structural slot (parallel subjects,
  direct/indirect objects, predicates, adverbials, attributes) can be joined by the conjunctions
  above (pp. 31–32).
- **Generalizing words (обобщающие слова)** summarize a list of parallel elements, most commonly
  всё/все, никто, ничего, везде/всюду, нигде/никуда, всегда, никогда — but other ad hoc word
  combinations can serve the same summarizing role for a given list (p. 32).
- **Parallel sentences (однородные предложения)**: whole independent simple sentences joined into
  one compound sentence, either with a coordinating conjunction (syndetic construction) or without
  one (asyndetic construction) — the book flags that in Russian, unlike English, the presence or
  absence of the conjunction itself does not change the punctuation (a forward-reference to Ch. 8)
  (pp. 32–33).

### Chapter 5 — Overview of sentence types (summary chapter)

A consolidating taxonomy of every structural sentence type introduced so far, plus two new ones:

- simple non-extended (простое нераспространённое предложение)
- simple extended (простое распространённое предложение)
- complex (сложноподчинённое предложение)
- compound (сложносочинённое предложение) — syndetic or asyndetic
- **compound-complex (сложное предложение с сочинением и подчинением)** — new: a compound sentence
  where at least one conjunct itself contains a subordinate clause
- **impersonal (безличное предложение)** — new: has no grammatical subject at all (e.g. Темнеет
  "It's getting dark"), normally translated into English with dummy "it"
- **elliptic (эллиптическое предложение)** — new: omits a subject or predicate recoverable from
  context; distinguished from impersonal precisely because the missing element *could* be restored
  (e.g. a one-word answer to a question, where the missing element's case ending still marks its
  grammatical role — the book notes this makes Russian elliptic sentences less ambiguous than their
  English counterparts, since the accusative ending on новую машину alone reveals it's a direct
  object even standing alone as an answer) (pp. 35–37).
