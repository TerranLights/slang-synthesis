# Russian — Всему своё время (Everything in Its Time): Means and Ways of Expressing Time in Russian

**Source:** С.А. Хавронина, И.В. Казнышкина (S.A. Khavronina, I.V. Kaznyshkina), *Всему своё время:
Средства и способы выражения времени в русском языке* ["Everything in Its Time: Means and Ways of
Expressing Time in the Russian Language"], Русский язык. Курсы, Moscow, 2004, ISBN 5-88337-011-X,
240 pp. A dedicated, single-topic reference/workbook on the Russian temporal-expression system
(time-of-day, dates, duration, sequencing, repetition, approximation) for advanced foreign learners.

## Grammar points

### Telling time: two parallel question/answer paradigms
The book organizes clock time around two question pairs that share the same answer vocabulary but
differ in case: "Который час? / Сколько времени?" (nominative-style "what time is it") vs. "В
котором часу? / Когда?" (locative/temporal "at what time"). Minutes-past-the-hour uses an ordinal
genitive of the *next* hour (три минуты пятого = "3 minutes of the fifth," i.e. 4:03), not the
current hour — a common trap for learners transferring from languages that count relative to the
hour just completed. Half-hour has both a formal (половина седьмого) and a colloquial contracted
form (полседьмого), both usable in either question context. "Без" + genitive-minutes + nominative
upcoming-hour covers the "to the hour" forms (без пятнадцати шесть / без четверти шесть / без трёх
шесть), with минут droppable when a bare number precedes it.

### Register split: colloquial 12-hour vs. official 24-hour clock time
Ordinary spoken Russian caps clock-hour numerals at 12 and disambiguates with утра/дня/вечера/ночи
(itself droppable when context is clear); saying "тринадцать часов" etc. marks official/technical
registers only (announcements, digital displays, transit schedules) — the book explicitly flags this
as a register distinction, not a free variant.

### Dates and calendar units share one productive "which-one" adjective paradigm
Across неделя (week), месяц (month), год (year), and век/столетие (century), the same modifier set —
этот/тот, прошлый, позапрошлый, следующий (or its synonym будущий) — recurs with the same
nominative/prepositional-case alternation for the "which" vs. "when" question forms. Once a learner
internalizes this pattern for one time-unit noun, it transfers directly to the others. Centuries add
one extra layer: BC/AD can be expressed either through the religious до/от Рождества Христова frame
or the secular до/нашей эры (with) frame, presented as coexisting registers rather than one
superseding the other.

### Duration of an action: two competing constructions, register-marked
**1st method (default, spoken register):** a bare accusative noun for the time-unit/period, with no
preposition (Он лежал в больнице неделю — "he was in hospital [for] a week"). This is the default,
unmarked way to state how long something lasted.
**Intensifiers on the accusative-duration noun:** весь/целый (nominative-agreeing "whole/entire"),
долгие (plural-only, "long [years/months/centuries]"), ordinal numerals (второй год = "a second
year running"), and the frozen colloquial idiom битый час ("a solid hour" = very long). весь and
целый are near-synonyms but not interchangeable: целый adds an "unexpectedly/tiresomely long"
connotation absent from weil-toned весь, and весь cannot combine with bare секунду/минуту/час without
an additional modifier (must say целый час, not *весь час, unless a further qualifier like следующий
is added: весь следующий час is fine). Before a numeral+noun duration phrase, целый itself
morphs to the genitive-plural целых (целых двадцать минут). The mirror-image brevity intensifier is
всего (optionally всего только or всего лишь) — genitive-singular-masculine of весь repurposed to mean
"only" (Они жили в России всего пять лет).
**2nd method (bookish/literary register):** genitive time-noun with one of three prepositions — в
течение, в продолжение, на протяжении (all roughly "during/over the course of/throughout") — marked
explicitly by the source as more literary/written and less used in ordinary speech than the bare
accusative.
**Aspect interaction:** duration constructions overwhelmingly co-occur with imperfective verbs (Он
жил здесь два года). Perfective verbs appear only with the delimitative/perdurative prefixes по-
(brief, bounded action: походил десять минут) and про-/от- (extended-duration action: проработал всю
жизнь, отработала десять лет) — a systematic three-way aspectual-prefix split for "for X time" verb
phrases that the book treats as a named grammatical subsystem, not incidental lexical variation.
целый(х) tends to co-occur with the long-duration про-/от- prefixes for emphasis, всего with the
brief по- prefix.

### Start/end boundaries of an action
Start: с + genitive time-unit noun (с сентября, с прошлого года). A parallel с + instrumental
deverbal-noun construction (с приходом весны, с началом года, с открытием выставки) marks a boundary
*event* rather than a bare time-unit, and can carry an implicit causal reading ("with the arrival of
spring [and because of it], it got warmer"). A third с + instrumental pattern — с каждым днём/годом/
часом — is grammatically identical in surface form but marks incremental/gradual change over
repeated units, not a single start-boundary; distinguishing the "boundary event" sense from the
"incremental change" sense is purely contextual/semantic, not marked by different case.
End: до + genitive (до утра, до позднего вечера). Fixed idiomatic до-less pairs like от зари до
зари, от рассвета до заката, от сессии до сессии use от instead of the plain с, restricted to nouns
that are not literal calendar/clock units.
Full-interval "from X to Y": either the same preposition repeated (с утра до вечера, с девяти до
шести) or, when the boundaries are themselves dates/months, по + accusative can replace до + genitive
(с двадцать пятого мая по первое июня) — again flagged as carrying a more literary/bookish tone than
the до-genitive alternative. включительно ("inclusive") is appended after a по-range to explicitly
state the end-boundary itself is covered, not just approached (с восьмого февраля по двадцать первое
февраля включительно).

### Register note recurring throughout this book's sections
Nearly every construction pair in these first four sections is explicitly marked by the authors for
register (bookish/literary vs. colloquial/spoken) rather than presented as free variation — this
running commentary is itself a systematic register map of the Russian time-expression system worth
treating as a standalone finding for the mechanics-analysis phase: the "plainer" construction
(bare accusative for duration, до + genitive for end-boundary, с + genitive for start) is consistently
the spoken/default one, while prepositional alternatives built on в течение/в продолжение/на
протяжении/по + accusative mark literary or official register.
