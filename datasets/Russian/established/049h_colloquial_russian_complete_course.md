## Grammar points

### Lesson 13 — Dative/instrumental/prepositional plural, cardinal numerals 40–1000, partitive genitive, "to put"
- **Dative, instrumental, and prepositional plural are far more regular than the nominative/
  genitive plural already covered** (source's own framing): dative plural is uniformly -ам (hard)/
  -ям (soft, incl. masc. -й/-ь, fem. -я/-ь, neut. -е); instrumental plural is uniformly -ами/-ями;
  prepositional plural is uniformly -ах/-ях. Neuter -мя nouns take -ена́м/-ена́ми/-ена́х (и́мя family).
  A short irregular list is given: друзья́м/друзья́ми/друзья́х, and (for the лю́ди/до́чери/де́ти
  irregular-plural nouns) людьми́, дочерьми́, детьми́ in the instrumental specifically — an
  irregular instrumental-plural ending (-ьми rather than -ями) that doesn't fit the regular pattern
  at all, worth flagging as a genuine irregular residue distinct from the ordinary hard/soft split.
  This asymmetry — three "easy" plural cases vs. two ("hard," genitive and nominative) heavily
  irregular ones — is itself a notable typological fact: irregularity in this paradigm concentrates
  almost entirely in two specific cells, not spread evenly across the six-case system.
- **Adjective dative/instrumental/prepositional plural**: dative -ым (hard)/-им (soft/mixed);
  instrumental -ыми/-ими; prepositional -ых/-их (identical to the already-covered genitive plural
  ending for adjectives) — a genuine syncretism between genitive plural and prepositional plural
  adjective endings, unlike the noun paradigm where these two cases have entirely distinct endings.
- **Cardinal numerals 40–1000**: a full table (со́рок, пятьдеся́т, шестьдеся́т, се́мьдесят,
  во́семьдесят, девяно́сто, сто, две́сти, три́ста, четы́реста, пятьсо́т, шестьсо́т, семьсо́т,
  восемьсо́т, девятьсо́т, ты́сяча) — morphologically these compound numerals are mostly transparent
  concatenations (пять+десят "fifty" etc.) except два/три/четыре-hundred, which show a distinct
  -ста/-ста suffix pattern (две́сти, три́ста, четы́реста) diverging from the regular -сто́ pattern
  used from 500 up (пятьсо́т, шестьсо́т…), i.e. a small numeral-internal irregularity cluster around
  the "hundred" component specifically for 200–400 vs. 500+.
- **Partitive genitive**: a distinct genitive use meaning "some (of)," contrasted directly with the
  ordinary direct-object accusative — Купи́, пожа́луйста, колбасы́ "Buy some sausage" (partitive
  genitive, indefinite quantity implied) vs. Мари́на покупа́ет колбасу́ "Marina is buying the
  sausage" (accusative, the whole/specific object). Certain masculine nouns additionally have a
  **special partitive genitive ending in -у/-ю, distinct from their ordinary genitive singular**:
  сыр→сы́ру (not сы́ра), са́хар→са́хару, чай→ча́ю — this special partitive form can also combine
  with quantity expressions (килогра́мм сы́ру, мно́го наро́ду "a lot of people [there]"), making it
  a third distinct genitive-singular-like form for a small closed class of masculine nouns (ordinary
  genitive vs. this partitive-у/-ю form vs. the Lesson-8 special locative-у prepositional form —
  three separate lexically-restricted "special -у" phenomena attaching to overlapping but not
  identical noun sets, worth flagging for mechanics-analysis as a case where a single surface
  ending marks several distinct grammatical functions depending on which noun and construction it
  appears in).
- **"To put" — a four-way transitive/intransitive × orientation split**: класть/положи́ть (transitive,
  "put/lay" an object into a lying orientation: ви́лки, ножи́, ло́жки) vs. ста́вить/поста́вить
  (transitive, "put/stand" an object upright or in its normal standing position: стака́ны,
  телеви́зор) — both take an accusative direct object plus, since they imply motion into position, a
  preposition + accusative for the destination (положи́л ви́лки на стол). Their intransitive
  counterparts describing the resulting static state are лежа́ть/полежа́ть ("lie, be lying," taking
  a preposition + *prepositional* case, since no motion is involved: ви́лки лежа́ли на столе́) and
  стоя́ть/постоя́ть ("stand, be standing": телеви́зор стои́т в ко́мнате). The system is thus a clean
  four-cell grid crossing {transitive-causative, intransitive-stative} × {lying-orientation,
  standing-orientation} — genuinely reminiscent of similar lie/lay/stand/set distinctions in
  English but fully lexicalized as four distinct verb pairs rather than a single lexeme's
  transitivity alternation. **A specifically flagged exception**: та́релки "plates" idiomatically
  takes ста́вить/сто́ять (the "standing" verb pair) even though a flat plate would seem to lie
  rather than stand — Официа́нтка ста́вит таре́лки "the waitress is putting out/laying out the
  plates" — the source explicitly calls this "rather surprising," confirming it's a genuine lexical
  exception to the otherwise-orientation-based generalization, not a productive rule. A strong
  candidate for mechanics-analysis: a shape/orientation-based verb-choice system that nonetheless
  has arbitrary lexicalized exceptions is a good model for how a conlang's own "physical shape"
  classifier system might likewise accumulate frozen irregularities over time.
