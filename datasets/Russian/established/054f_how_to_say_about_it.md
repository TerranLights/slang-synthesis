## Grammar points

### Agreement and disagreement with a stated opinion: a five-way taxonomy (§8, pp. 141–148)

The book's central framed table (pp. 143–144) sorts responses to an interlocutor's opinion,
evaluation, or supposition into five categories, each with a distinct communicative function beyond
bare "yes/no":

- **(a) Full agreement**: Конечно/Разумеется/Само собой (разумеется) simply confirm the stated view.
  Ещё бы! goes further, denying any alternative assessment is even conceivable. То есть! specifically
  compliments the interlocutor's precision in reading the situation — the source's gloss: "говорящий
  подчёркивает, что собеседник удивительно точно понял и оценил ситуацию."
- **(б) Reserved/hedged disagreement**: Ну да!/Скажешь тоже!/Это ещё как сказать!/Не скажи(те)!/Вот уж
  не сказал бы!/Положим!/Ну, (это) положим!/На чей взгляд! all push back without fully rejecting the
  claim — a graded cluster rather than a single fixed phrase.
- **(в) Disagreement plus surprise at the interlocutor's reasoning**: С чего ты взял?/Почему? adds an
  extra layer — not just "I disagree" but "I'm surprised you'd think that."
- **(г) Doubt about correctness/truth, without full rejection**: Как знать! hedges rather than denies.
- **(д) Categorical, emotionally-marked disagreement**: Что вы!/Ну что вы!/Нет, что вы!/Вот уж
  нет!/Да брось!/Да брось ты! — the source explicitly flags this register split: Да брось(ты)! is
  restricted to informal/casual speech ("употребляется только в ситуации неофициального общения"),
  unlike the more universally usable Что вы!/Нет, что вы!.

A worked passage of literary quotations (Чехов, Булгаков, Шварц, Розов, Кривин, Панова, Стругацкие,
Брагинский/Рязанов) illustrates several of these categories in real dialogue (pp. 147–148), used by
the source as a "identify which category" exercise rather than introducing new constructions.

### Expressing disbelief toward a reported fact (§9, pp. 149–152)

Distinct from §8's opinion-level (dis)agreement, §9 addresses disbelief toward the *fact itself*
being reported. The book distinguishes three sub-categories, again with its own framed comparison
tables:

1. **Doubt about the sheer possibility of the fact** (Ну да? / Не может быть!) — the doubt attaches
   to whether the event could have happened at all.
2. **Challenging the grounds for the claim** (Откуда ты знаешь? / Откуда ты взял? / С чего ты взял?)
   — glossed by the source as equivalent to "что заставляет тебя так думать?" ("what makes you think
   that?"); the book stresses (p. 150) that this construction genuinely expresses disbelief rather
   than neutral curiosity about a source, evidenced by the fact that the questioner routinely follows
   up by explaining *why* they find the claim hard to credit, rather than simply waiting for a
   citation. A near-synonym, **Почём ты знаешь?**, is kept distinct: it doubts the interlocutor's own
   *inferential reasoning* specifically (their logic in reaching the conclusion), not the underlying
   fact or their information source.
3. **Flat rejection of plausibility**: (Да ну,) ерунда! / (Да ну,) не выдумывай! / (Да ну,) глупости!
   — the source glosses да ну as marking the speaker's negative stance toward the received
   information, treating it "как ложной, пустой, ненужной" (as false, empty, unneeded) — the
   strongest rejection register in this section, comparable in force to §8's category (д) but
   targeted at a reported *fact* rather than an *opinion*.
