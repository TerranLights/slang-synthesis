**Source:** Е.Р. Ласкарева, *Прогулки по русской лексике*, Zlatoust, 2010. This part covers the
third sub-section of Chapter 3: "Лексическая сочетаемость" (Lexical Collocation/Compatibility),
printed pp. 142-162 (PDF pp. 144-164). Same +2 PDF-to-printed-page offset (re-verified against the
visible printed folio on PDF p.144, "142"); no text layer (fully vision-read); no marginalia found
on any page sampled.

**Coverage note — this sub-section is explicitly a review/integration chapter, not a source of new
lexical items in itself.** The source states its own purpose at the opening (p.142): lexical
collocation ("what word combines with what word") is presented as the organizing lens for
*everything already taught* — its own bullet list names Synonyms, Foreign Words, Paronyms,
Prefixal Verbs, and Verb Government as the specific topics being revisited through this lens. Per
the coverage rule, this file does **not** re-extract the ~50 exercises' worth of basic
verb+object/adjective+noun collocation drills (задавать вопросы, отвечать на вопросы, готовить
ужин, etc.) — these are elementary, register-neutral, and already implicitly covered by this
project's core-vocabulary extractions across 55+ prior Russian established/ files. Instead this file
extracts: (a) the section's own grammar point about collocation as a teaching principle, (b) the
genuinely new closed-class content that doesn't reduce to "basic word X pairs with basic word Y" —
fixed idiomatic adverbs, fixed idiom-collocations, foreign-word/native-equivalent register pairs,
and a pejorative-insult word list surfaced by a single verb-government drill.

## Grammar points

### Lexical collocation as the section's organizing principle (p.142, opening)

The source's central claim: lexical collocation (which words combine with which — "добиться
успеха" vs. "*добиться* признания?" being borderline) is responsible for "not less than 90% of
errors" foreign learners make at advanced levels, and should be studied as systematically as
grammar rather than treated as incidental vocabulary. It explicitly reframes five previously-taught
topics (Synonyms, Foreign Words, Paronyms, Prefixal Verbs, Verb Government) as all being, at bottom,
collocation problems — the same lexical item's compatible-partner set differing by exact synonym
choice, by borrowing vs. native root, by derivational suffix, or by government pattern. This is a
useful organizing frame for the project's own later mechanics-analysis phase: **register variation
and collocation restriction are treated by this source as two faces of the same underlying
phenomenon**, not independent axes.

### Verb government drilled as case-government + collocation simultaneously (pp.157-161)

A long run of exercises (испытывать + accusative emotion-noun; трястись/оправдываться/раскаиваться
+ prepositional-case government; смилуйся + над + instrumental) reinforces specific verb-preposition-
case triples rather than free government rules — e.g. "смилуйся (надо мной, над ним)" and
"раскаиваться (в этом, в своём преступлении)" are drilled as fixed collocational frames, not
derived from a general rule. Genuinely useful as a checklist of common verb-government pitfalls, but
individually low-novelty beyond the specific pejorative-cluster and figurative-трястись items
captured above.

### Foreign-word/native-equivalent register pairs (p.156)

The source's own micro-lesson: a Latinate/international loanword and its native Russian paraphrase
often occupy different registers even when denotationally near-identical — the loanword skewing
formal/technical/euphemistic (суицид, инфекционный, котироваться) and the native form skewing
everyday/plain (самоубийство, заразный, цениться). This is directly relevant to the project's slang
mechanics: loanword-vs-native-root register splitting is one of the same mechanisms slang coinage
itself exploits, just at the opposite end of the formality scale.
