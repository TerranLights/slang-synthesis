## Grammar points

### "Time after which" — четыре overlapping constructions, register- and syntax-differentiated
через + accusative is the default, usable with any bare time-unit noun (через год, через минуту).
после + genitive requires the time-unit noun to carry a modifier/complement (после двух дней, после
трёх часов дня) — a bare "после часов" is ungrammatical in this sense — but после freely takes
event/institution nouns without that restriction (после завтрака, после школы, после операции).
спустя + accusative is a near-synonym of через, marked as less frequent. по прошествии + genitive is
explicitly flagged bookish and inappropriate in ordinary conversation (contrasted directly against a
casual re-phrasing using через in the source's own worked example pair). Aspect: predicates in these
constructions are typically perfective, switching to imperfective only for regular/repeated actions,
often signaled by обычно/каждый co-occurring in the sentence (Через каждые два часа в палату
заходила медсестра — imperfective for the recurring nurse-visits).

### "Time to result" — за/в + accusative, and why they aren't freely interchangeable with duration verbs
за + accusative measures how long it took to *reach* a result (Мы доехали за полчаса). в + accusative
in this same result-oriented sense additionally connotes unusual speed/immediacy (В одну минуту
мальчик сбегал туда и обратно) — this is a different в-construction from both the clock-time в and
the bare-duration reading elsewhere in the book; distinguishing them is purely a matter of the verb's
telicity and the surrounding discourse, not marked morphologically. Sentence-initial position is
favored for longer за-phrases like за всю историю человечества, за весь вечер. Critically, both за
and в + accusative in this sense require a verb capable of denoting a *completed result* — ordinary
imperfective activity verbs with no natural telic endpoint (работать, заниматься, спать, гулять,
слушать, мечтать, быть) are explicitly excluded from this construction, because they cannot express
"reaching" anything. This is the clearest boundary case in the book between the bare-accusative
*duration* construction (сколько времени? — takes any activity verb) and this за/в *result-time*
construction (за сколько времени? — restricted to telic/perfective-compatible verbs).

### на + accusative: two distinct but form-identical uses
The same surface construction, на + accusative time-unit noun, covers two semantically distinct
functions the book treats as a single grammatical point precisely because they share one form: (1)
"retained result" — the span for which a resulting state holds after an implied prior action (Он
приехал к нам на пять дней = he arrived and will stay five days); and (2) "scheduled/planned moment"
— stating when a planned event is set to occur (Она купила билет на девять часов = the show/train is
set for nine o'clock). Both allow either aspect on the main verb. This на-construction very frequently
co-occurs with prefixed motion verbs, and there the aspect of the *motion verb itself* carries an
additional distinction orthogonal to на's own meaning: perfective prefixed motion-verb past tense
(приехал) marks a single completed arrival whose result (presence here) still holds; imperfective
prefixed motion-verb past tense (приезжал) marks a completed round trip — the subject came, stayed the
stated interval, and has already left by speech time. This aspectual contrast is a recurring,
systematic feature of Russian prefixed motion verbs worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase
as a distinct, general mechanism (not specific to time expressions, but the "на + duration" context is
where this book chooses to introduce it).
