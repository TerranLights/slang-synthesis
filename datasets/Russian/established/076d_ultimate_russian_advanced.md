**Morpheme breakdown (Lesson 12 diminutive/augmentative noun suffixes — representative
examples).**

> **недель-ка** = неделя (week) + -ка (feminine diminutive suffix, affectionate/intensifying) —
> "what a week!" (used ironically for an eventful/exhausting week, not literal smallness).
> **брат-ец** = брат (brother) + -ец (masculine diminutive, affectionate address) — "old chap."
> **дом-ина** = дом (house) + -ина (augmentative) — "a huge house." **ум-ище** = ум (mind) + -ище
> (augmentative) — "a great mind." Several forms have drifted to a distinct lexicalized meaning
> rather than pure size-modification of the base noun: **круг-лка** (crышка, "lid/cap") ← кры́ша
> ("roof"); **дорож-ка** ("path") ← дорога ("road"); **знач-ок** ("badge/pin") ← знак ("sign");
> **клад-бище** ("cemetery") ← класть ("to place"); **чуд-овище** ("monster") ← чудо
> ("miracle/wonder") — the suffix's original size/affect semantics have bleached into new,
> independent lexical items, a lexicalization pattern worth flagging for the conlang's own
> derivational-morphology design (a productive suffix family that occasionally "escapes" its
> productive meaning into fixed vocabulary).

## Grammar points

### Lesson 10 (§C): Active and passive participles (present/past)

**Largely redundant** with prior full participle-system treatments in this corpus (e.g.
Timberlake, Wade). Kept as a useful practical note: the book repeatedly emphasizes that participle
constructions are primarily a formal/written-register phenomenon, and that который-clauses are
the actual spoken-register equivalent — explicitly modeling a register split (participle = formal/
literary; который-clause = conversational) that's a useful register-mapping data point for any
formal/informal register design in the conlang.

### Lesson 11 (§C): Prepositions of spatial relationship (у/в/на/к); subordinating conjunctions

**Largely redundant** location-vs.-direction preposition mechanics. One genuinely useful
consolidation: a clean list of common subordinating conjunctions in one place (потому что,
из-за того что, поскольку, пока, несмотря на то что, впрочем, однако, с тех пор как, после того
как, как только, прежде чем, чтобы), plus the more formal ввиду того что / вследствие того что / в
связи с тем что set explicitly marked as belonging to formal/official register — a clean
formal-vs.-neutral conjunction register split not always gathered in one place elsewhere in this
corpus.

### Lesson 12 (§C): Diminutive/augmentative nouns; comparative and superlative adjectives

**The diminutive/augmentative suffix system (see Vocabulary/morpheme breakdown above) is a
genuinely valuable, not-fully-redundant find** — a productive noun-derivation layer carrying
affect/size/register information, directly relevant to the project's morphological-typology
tracking for this fusional language. Comparative/superlative adjective formation (более/менее +
long form vs. -ее suffix; irregular comparatives; the самый vs. -ейший/-айший superlative split;
formal-register наиболее/наименьший family) is **largely redundant** with prior sources, though
the explicit register note that наиболее/наименьший-type superlatives belong to
"formal writing, literature, and journalism" and are recognition-only vocabulary for learners is a
useful register flag not always made this explicit elsewhere.

### Lesson 13 (§C): каков vs. какой; verbs governing the instrumental case

**Каков/какой discrimination is a genuinely useful, not-always-tabulated distinction:** каков(а/о/ы)
patterns with a copula ("what is X like," predicative), while какой forms part of a noun phrase
usable with any verb ("which X") — plus a secondary exclamative use of the таков-type forms (А
погода-то какова! "What weather we're having!") and the fixed idiom "какова мать, такова и дочь"
("like mother, like daughter"). The instrumental-case-governing-verb list (обладать, интересоваться,
пользоваться, управлять, гордиться, владеть, располагать, заведовать, любоваться, пренебрегать,
увлекаться) is **largely redundant** with prior sources, though grouping them thematically around
"control, use, and attitude" is a clean organizing principle worth a one-line note.
