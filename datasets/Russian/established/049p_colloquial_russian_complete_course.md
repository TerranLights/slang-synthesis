## Grammar points (non-redundant supplement only)

### Тре́тий "third" — full irregular soft-adjective declension
Referenced only in passing in Lesson 9 (numerals) as "an irregular soft-type declension pattern of
its own," but never fully tabulated until this appendix. Full paradigm:

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom. | тре́тий | тре́тья | тре́тье | тре́тьи |
| Acc. | тре́тий/тре́тьего | тре́тью | тре́тье | тре́тьи/тре́тьих |
| Gen. | тре́тьего | тре́тьей | тре́тьего | тре́тьих |
| Dat. | тре́тьему | тре́тьей | тре́тьему | тре́тьим |
| Instr. | тре́тьим | тре́тьей | тре́тьим | тре́тьими |
| Prep. | тре́тьем | тре́тьей | тре́тьем | тре́тьих |

Unlike ordinary soft adjectives (после́дний-type), тре́тий inserts a **ь before every ending**
outside the bare masculine/neuter nominative — a genuinely distinct sub-pattern within the
soft-adjective class, not just an ordinary soft adjective with a different stem, and worth flagging
as its own irregular declension type (the source itself calls it irregular, distinct even from the
after-hushing-consonant soft type already noted in Lesson 9).

### Чей "whose" — full declension (not given in any lesson)
| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom. | чей | чья | чьё | чьи |
| Acc. | чей/чьего́ | чью | чьё | чьи/чьих |
| Gen. | чьего́ | чьей | чьего́ | чьих |
| Dat. | чьему́ | чьей | чьему́ | чьим |
| Instr. | чьим | чьей/чье́ю | чьим | чьими |
| Prep. | чьём | чьей | чьём | чьих |

Notable for retaining a bare-consonant-plus-ь stem (чь-) throughout, rather than patterning like an
ordinary hard or soft adjective stem — a possessive-interrogative with its own idiosyncratic shape,
distinct from the кто/что interrogatives it semantically pairs with.

### Путь "way" — unique mixed-gender declension
Singular: путь, путь, пути́, пути́, путём, пути́. Plural: пути́, пути́, путе́й, путя́м, путя́ми,
путя́х. Путь is **grammatically masculine** despite its -ь ending and largely feminine-looking
soft-stem endings (gen./dat./prep. sg. -и, exactly like a feminine -ь noun such as жизнь) — the
single point where it breaks from the feminine pattern and shows its true masculine gender is the
**instrumental singular путём** (a masculine-type -ём ending, not a feminine instrumental like
-ью). This is a genuinely unique noun in the language (the grammar summary presents it as a class
of one), and a good example for mechanics-analysis of a noun whose gender is grammatically
"hidden" across most of its paradigm and only surfaces in a single cell.

### Сам vs. са́мый — a stress-based disambiguation of near-homographic words
The emphatic intensifier сам ("myself/himself," etc., from Lesson 8) has genitive/dative/
instrumental/prepositional forms **stressed on the ending** (самого́, самому́, сами́м, само́м) — in
explicit contrast to the superlative-marker са́мый ("the very/most," from Lesson 17), which is
**stressed on the stem throughout its entire paradigm** (са́мого, са́мому, etc.). Both decline like
hard adjectives and share substantial surface overlap, so stress placement alone is the
disambiguating cue between the emphatic-reflexive intensifier and the superlative particle — a
clean minimal-pair-by-stress-only case worth flagging for mechanics-analysis as a model of how a
single suprasegmental feature (stress position) can carry an entire grammatical-category distinction
between two etymologically-related but functionally very different words.

### Оди́н's plural form and pluralia tantum agreement
Оди́н has a full plural form (одни́/одни́х/одни́м/etc.) used specifically to agree with **nouns
that exist only in the plural** (pluralia tantum, e.g. кани́кулы, де́ньги) — a small but genuine gap
-filling function for a numeral whose core meaning ("one") would otherwise seem incompatible with
plural agreement entirely, resolved by treating "one [set of]" as grammatically plural for nouns
that have no singular to agree with.

### Семя́ and зна́мя — exceptions to и́мя's declension pattern
The grammar summary flags explicitly that **not every -мя noun follows и́мя's own pattern exactly**:
се́мя "seed" has genitive plural семя́н (not the и́мя-type -мён pattern); зна́мя "banner" has plural
знамёна, genitive plural знамён — a small irregular residue within what otherwise looks like a
fully regular neuter -мя declension class, confirming that even this already-noted special class
(и́мя, вре́мя from earlier lessons) isn't fully uniform internally.

### Compound "hundred" numerals decline in *both* halves simultaneously
Extending the single-numeral declension tables already given lesson-by-lesson, the appendix
tabulates the full oblique-case paradigm of the 200–900 compound numerals, confirming that **both
elements of the compound decline together and independently of each other**, not just the final
element (unlike the "only the last element declines" exception noted for compound-year phrases in
Lesson 15): две́сти→двухсо́т (gen.)→двумста́м (dat.)→двумяста́ми (instr.)→двухста́х (prep.); аналогously
три́ста→трёхсо́т→трёмста́м→тремяста́ми→трёхста́х; четы́реста→четырёхсо́т→четырёмста́м→
четырьмяста́ми→четырёхста́х; пятьсо́т/шестьсо́т/семьсо́т/восемьсо́т/девятьсо́т all pattern like
пятьсо́т→пятисо́т→пятиста́м→пятьюста́ми→пятиста́х. Ты́сяча declines like an ordinary feminine
noun, but uniquely has **two competing instrumental singular forms** (regular ты́сячей alongside an
alternative ты́сячью) — both attested as acceptable, a rare case of genuine free variation
(rather than register- or meaning-conditioned split) documented explicitly in this course.

### Russian surname declension (a distinct semi-adjectival noun class)
Surnames in -ов/-ев (e.g. Гу́ров) decline neither fully like a noun nor fully like an adjective, but
in a **mixed pattern unique to this word class**: masculine Гу́ров/Гу́рова/Гу́рова/Гу́рову/
Гу́ровым/Гу́рове (nominative and instrumental singular pattern like a masculine noun; genitive,
accusative, and dative singular pattern like a hard adjective); feminine Гу́рова/Гу́рову/Гу́ровой/
Гу́ровой/Гу́ровой/Гу́ровой (fully adjectival throughout); plural Гу́ровы/Гу́ровых/Гу́ровых/Гу́ровым/
Гу́ровыми/Гу́ровых (fully adjectival). Surnames in -ин and -ын follow the same mixed pattern.
Surnames that are themselves ordinary adjectives in form (e.g. Чайко́вский) simply decline as full
adjectives with no special mixed behavior. This is a genuinely distinct, lexically-specific
declension class (partly nominal, partly adjectival, and gender-asymmetric — masculine mixed,
feminine/plural fully adjectival) that doesn't reduce to any of the noun or adjective paradigms
already documented elsewhere in this extraction — a valuable data point for mechanics-analysis of
how a language can grammaticalize a special "surname" morphological class distinct from its
ordinary common-noun and adjective systems.
