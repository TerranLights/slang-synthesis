# Russian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Домашнее чтение (Home Reading) Teaching Manual

**Source:** М.М. Глазкова, Е.Б. Патракеева, Н.Г. Серебренникова, *Домашнее чтение: Учебное
пособие* [*Home Reading: A Study Guide*] (Издательство ТГТУ, Tambov, 2009), full 92 printed pages
(136 PDF pages including covers/front matter), `source_reference/languages/Russian/Russian
Learning Pack [up-to-date as of 2012]/Vol 3 of 3/15.Домашнее чтение Учебное пособие.pdf`.

## What this book is

A graded-reader teaching manual for foreign learners of Russian (aimed at intermediate/advanced
students, per the book's own foreword — "среднего и продвинутого уровней обучения"). It packages
twelve excerpted/adapted texts by seven classic Russian authors — Pushkin, Lermontov, Dostoevsky,
Gogol, Chekhov, Bunin, Kuprin — each following a fixed pedagogical schema: author biography +
comprehension questions, a short pre-reading glossary ("Прочитайте и запомните значения слов и
выражений" — "read and remember the meanings of the words and expressions"), the story text, then
post-reading comprehension questions and tasks ("Вопросы и задания").

## Grammar points

This book is a graded literary reader, not a grammar reference — it contains no paradigm tables,
morphology drills, or systematic grammar explanations of its own. Its only pedagogical content
beyond the vocabulary glosses above is per-lesson author biographies and comprehension-question
scaffolding ("Ответьте на вопросы," "Вопросы и задания"), which contain no extractable
grammar/vocabulary data — they are discussion prompts referencing the (unreproduced) story text.
No grammar-points section applies to this source.

### Register/dialect annotation summary (source-marked tags found)

The book's own glossary marks five distinct annotation types, all captured per-entry above:

- **`(устар.)`/`(уст.)`** archaic/dated — the most common tag, mostly period material-culture and
  Table-of-Ranks terms (штабс-капитан, оказия, батюшка, розвальни, башлык, верста, сочельник).
- **`(укр.)`** Ukrainian-dialectal — attached to loanwords in Gogol's "Ночь перед Рождеством"
  (хата, парубок, люлька), consistent with that story's Dikanka/Ukraine setting.
- **`(разг.)`** colloquial — only one entry so tagged (посапывать), though several other entries
  (физиономия, белоручка) read as informal-register without an explicit source tag.
