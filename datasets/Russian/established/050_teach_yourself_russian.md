## Grammar points

### 1. Absence of articles
Russian has no words for "the" or "a" — паспорт can mean "the passport" or "a passport" depending
on context (p.5).

### 2. Absence of the present-tense copula
The verb "to be" is not used in the present tense: Вы турист ("you [are a] tourist" literally "you
tourist"). Where both linked words are nouns, a dash may substitute for the missing verb: Моя
фамилия – Петрова (p.5).

### 3. Statement vs. question — intonation only
Written Russian distinguishes a statement from a question only by the final punctuation mark; word
order does not change. In speech, a statement's pitch falls on the last stressed syllable of the
last word, while a yes/no question's pitch rises there instead; a question opening with a question
word instead raises pitch on that word. A "dip" contour signals an implied contrast (e.g. А это?)
(pp.5–6).

### 4. Noun "gender" groups
Nouns are grouped (traditionally called genders) as masculine (end in a consonant, e.g. стол),
feminine (end in -а, e.g. книга), or neuter (end in -о, e.g. письмо). Secondary endings also occur:
masculine in a diphthong (-ой/-ай/-ей) or soft sign (-ь, e.g. автомобиль); feminine in -я
(неделя), -ия (Англия), or soft sign (тетрадь); neuter in -е (поле) or -ие (здание). Soft-sign
nouns are the only ending shared across genders and must be memorized individually. время and имя
are irregular neuters despite ending in а/я (p.6).

### 5. Personal (subject) pronouns
Full paradigm: я (I), ты (you, sg. informal), он/она/оно (he/she/it), мы (we), вы (you, sg.
formal/pl.), они (they) (p.7).

### 6. Possessive adjectives
мой/моя/моё ("my") and твой (parallel forms, "your" informal) change endings by the gender of the
noun described. ваш/ваша/ваше ("your" formal/pl.) and наш (parallel, "our") work the same way.
его ("his/its"), её ("her/its"), and их ("their") never change form regardless of the noun's
gender (pp.7–8).

### 7. Как? (how?)
Used in common questions: Как вы? (how are you?), Как дела? (how are things?), Как ваш брат?
(how's your brother?) (p.8).

### 8. Nationality nouns
Russian uses paired masculine/feminine nouns (not adjectives) to state nationality: он англичанин
/ она англичанка, он американец / она американка, он испанец / она испанка. The general rule is
that feminine forms end in -а. Russian/Russian-woman is exceptional — он русский / она русская —
because these are true adjectives (in form), not nouns; the point is flagged as anticipating Unit 5
(p.8).

### 9. Forming the present tense (1st and 2nd conjugation)
Two main conjugation patterns: (i) most -ать infinitives (e.g. работать) drop -ть and add -ю,
-ешь, -ет, -ем, -ете, -ют; (ii) most -ить infinitives (e.g. говорить) drop -ить and add -ю, -ишь,
-ит, -им, -ите, -ят. Irregular verbs are given by stem + я/ты/они endings in the back-of-book
vocabulary; e.g. жить has stem жив- with irregular vowel-insertion in the endings (я живу, ты
живёшь, он живёт, мы живём, вы живёте, они живут — the он/мы/вы forms follow the ты pattern, but
я and они differ) (pp.15–16).

### 10. Verbs in -авать
Verbs like преподавать (to teach) drop the middle -ав- throughout the present-tense conjugation:
я преподаю, ты преподаёшь, он преподаёт, мы преподаём, вы преподаёте, они преподают. Same pattern
applies to давать (to give) and продавать (to sell) (p.16).

### 11. Cases — introducing the six-case system
Russian nouns, adjectives, and pronouns change endings ("cases") depending on grammatical function.
The nominative case (used for the subject of a sentence, and for the complement of "to be") is the
form given in dictionaries. There are six cases total, including the nominative (p.17).

### 12. Prepositional case after в and на
The prepositional (locative) case is used after в ("in, at") and на ("in, on, at") to express
location. It is usually formed by adding -е to the nominative singular. A table of remove/add
patterns is given by gender: masculine nouns generally just add -е (or replace a soft sign with
-е, or replace -й with -е); feminine nouns replace -а with -е or -я with -е; neuter nouns in -о
add -е (become -ое), while neuter nouns already in -е are unchanged. Exceptions: feminine soft-sign
nouns, feminine nouns in -я, and neuter nouns in -ие instead take -и (e.g. в тетради, в Англии, в
здании). A separate small group of masculine nouns (аэропорт, Крым, пол, сад, угол, шкаф) takes a
stressed -у ending instead of -е (в аэропорту, в Крыму, etc.); the source notes this list is fully
enumerated in Appendix 1. A small closed class of indeclinable nouns (mostly loanwords, e.g. бюро,
кафе, такси) never change form in any case (pp.16–19).

### 13. Adverbs of manner in по-
Adverbs describing "in [language]" are formed with по- + adjective stem + -и: по-русски (in
Russian), по-французски (in French), по-английски (in English), по-итальянски (in Italian)
(p.19).
