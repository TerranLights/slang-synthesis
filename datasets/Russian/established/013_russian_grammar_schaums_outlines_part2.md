of adjectives, page 168) and does not duplicate that range. This file's own range ends exactly where the
book's own "Answers to Exercises" appendix begins (printed page ~311); the answer-key appendix and the
General Index (printed pages ~311–348) are drill/reference material, not new grammar or vocabulary, and
are excluded per the coverage rule.

## Grammar points

### Adverbs of manner, time, place, and degree (Chapter 5 remainder, pp. 168–174)

The Simple Superlative (pp. 168–169) is formed by adding -ейш- (or -айш- after г/к/х/ст, which soften
to ж/ч/ш/щ) to the long-adjective stem; it expresses "high degree" (интере´снейшая кни´га "a most
interesting book") and is used in both scientific/literary and colloquial registers, contrasted with the
compound superlative (са´мый + adjective).

Adverbs are classified by the question they answer: manner (ка´к?), time (когда´?, ско´лько вре´мени?,
ка´к ча´сто?), place (где´?/куда´?/отку´да?), and measure/degree (ско´лько?, в како´й сте´пени?). A key
usage distinction: о´чень (intensity, "very") vs. мно´го (quantity, "a lot") are not interchangeable when
modifying a verb.

Indefinite adverbs use the particles -то (past-tense/nonrepeated present statements) and -нибудь
(questions, imperatives, future, repeated actions) — the same distribution as indefinite pronouns
(Chapter 4). Negative adverbs split into two series: ни-adverbs (ни-где´, ни-куда´, etc.) used in personal
sentences with a nominative subject and a "double negative" (не + finite verb), versus не´-adverbs
(не´где, не´куда, не´когда, не´зачем) used in impersonal sentences with a dative logical subject, an
infinitive, and бы´ло/бу´дет for past/future.

### Numbers, dates, and time (Chapter 6, pp. 177–208)

**Cardinal numbers** decline as follows: 2/3/4 take genitive singular of a following noun (nom./inan.
acc.), genitive-form animate accusative with genitive-plural noun, or agree in case with a plural noun in
oblique cases; the same "adjective in gen. pl. (m./n.) or nom. pl. (f.)" pattern applies when an adjective
intervenes. Numbers 5–999, when in nom./acc., take the genitive plural of both adjective and noun, and
do NOT undergo the animate-accusative rule. Each numeral 5–20/30 declines like a 3rd-declension
feminine noun (its own paradigm); 50/60/70/80 have both parts declining; 40/90/100 have only one
oblique ending (-a); 200/300/400 and 500–900 each decline in two parts with a distinct пять-... pattern.
ты´сяча, миллио´н, and миллиа´рд/биллио´н behave as ordinary nouns that are themselves quantifiable and
govern the genitive plural of what follows. Thousands/millions in figures use a period or space (Russian)
where English uses a comma.

**Numerical nouns** derived from cardinals (едини´ца…деся´тка) denote grades (F–A), playing cards,
bus/trolley numbers (colloquial), and groups/units (тро´йка "three-piece suit," деся´тка "10-ruble note");
пято´к/деся´ток/со´тня are colloquial group-nouns.

**Collective numbers** (о´ба/о´бе, дво´е…де´сятеро) present a group as a single unit; required with
plural-only nouns and typically with male-person nouns, optional (vs. cardinals) with paired-item nouns,
mandatory-or-optional distinctions do not extend to female-person nouns (cardinal only). о´ба/о´бе
distinguishes gender via -o-/-e- stem vowel throughout its whole paradigm, unlike два´/две´ (nom./acc.
only).

**Ordinal numbers** are adjectives derived from cardinals (except пе´рвый, второ´й); тре´тий has a
unique soft-declension paradigm. Russian uses ordinals where English uses cardinals for room/bus/seat/
channel/page/chapter numbers.

**Fractions**: numerator = cardinal (nom.), denominator = ordinal, nominative feminine form if
numerator is 1, genitive plural if numerator is 2+. Colloquial special nouns че´тверть/тре´ть/полови´на
(pol-) replace 1/4, 1/3, 1/2. полтора´/полторы´ ("one and a half") has a 3-form paradigm (m./n., f.,
oblique). Decimal fractions use a comma for the decimal point and are read with це´лый/це´лых, often
dropped in colloquial simplification.

**Dates**: days of the week are lowercase; a day "on X" uses в + accusative, repeated occurrence uses по +
dative plural. Months are lowercase masculine nouns; "in [month]" uses в + prepositional. Calendar dates
use a neuter ordinal (число´ understood) in nominative for stating the date, genitive with no preposition
for "on [date]," and the ordinal must precede the month (unlike English). Years use a declined ordinal
agreeing with го´д, and "in [year]" uses в + prepositional of both ordinal and году´. Russian date-in-figures
order is day.month.year (opposite of the common US month/day/year).

**Age** is expressed with the person in the dative case and cardinal + case-appropriate form of "year"
(го´д/го´да/ле´т, following the same 1/2-4/5+ agreement pattern as any counted noun).

**Time-telling**: conversational method uses cardinal ("on the hour"), cardinal+ordinal ("in the first half,"
minutes-past-the-implied-next-hour), and "без + genitive" ("in the second half," minutes-until). The
24-hour official system (schedules, broadcasts) uses cardinals only, но´ль for zero. Approximation:
reversing number+noun order, о´коло + genitive, or приме´рно/приблизи´тельно.

### Verb conjugation system (Chapter 7, pp. 209–239)

Russian verbs have six personal ("non-past") endings across two conjugation classes (1st: -е-/-ё- vowel;
2nd: -и- vowel); these same endings express present tense for imperfective verbs and future tense for
perfective verbs. Three facts must be known to conjugate any verb: conjugation class, stress pattern
(stem/end/shifting), and whether the stem-final consonant alternates (1st-conj.: throughout non-past;
2nd-conj.: 1st-person-singular only). Ten verb-stem-suffix classes exist (АЙ, ЕЙ, ОВА/УЙ, НУ, А (3
subtypes), А-ВАЙ, О for 1st conjugation; И, Е, ЖА for 2nd), plus nonsuffixed stems classified by
stem-final consonant (resonants В/Н/ОЙ/ИЙ/М-Н/Р; obstruents З-С/Д-Т/Б/Г-К), each with its own
predictable stress/alternation/truncation behavior per the general "combine stem+ending: truncate first of
two likes, simply add two unlikes" rule. хоте´ть and бежа´ть are irregular verbs of mixed conjugation;
бы´ть, да´ть, е´сть, е´хать, идти´, and расти´ are irregular nonsuffixed-stem verbs requiring memorization.

The past tense is a single form (no aspectual restriction) built by attaching -л/-ла/-ло/-ли to the stem,
with the same combination rules; several stem classes (з, с, б, р, г, к, and (ну)-final) exceptionally retain
the stem consonant and drop -л only in the masculine. The imperfective future is бу´ду + imperfective
infinitive; the perfective future is simply the conjugated perfective verb (using the same non-past
endings).

### Verb aspect: formation and use (pp. 239–253)

Aspectual pairs are formed by (1) prefixation (a prefix perfectivizes without changing meaning/
conjugation — e.g. вы´-, за-, на-, по-, при-/про-/раз-, с-, у-), (2) suffixation (different suffix/conjugation
pattern between the pair, e.g. изуча´ть/изучи´ть), or (3) rare suppletion (бра´ть/взя´ть, говори´ть/
сказа´ть, иска´ть/найти´, кла´сть/положи´ть, лови´ть/пойма´ть). Certain prefixes not only perfectivize
but change meaning (писа´ть → вписа´ть/вы´писать/дописа´ть/etc.); newly prefixed perfectives get a
derived secondary imperfective via -ывай-/-ивай-, -вай-, or -ай-.

Perfective is used for: completion with result, achievement of an intended result (contrasted with the
imperfective's "trying to"), sequential actions, actions completed in a specific timeframe (за + acc.),
actions of limited duration (по-/про- prefixes), and instantaneous/onset actions (ну- suffix for
instantaneous; за-/по-/рас-/у- prefixes for onset). Imperfective is used for: ongoing action, simultaneous
actions (or the backgrounded action when interrupted by a perfective one), repeated/habitual action, and
crucially "annulled" (reversed/round-trip) action — e.g. a past-tense imperfective of "take," "get up," "turn
on" etc. implies the action was later undone (бра´ть/взя´ть, встава´ть/вста´ть, включа´ть/включи´ть,
отдава´ть/отда´ть, открыва´ть/откры´ть, поднима´ться/подня´ться, приноси´ть/принести´). A
negated past-tense imperfective denies intent to perform an action at all, vs. a negated perfective
denying that an intended/expected action was completed. In questions, imperfective past asks merely "did
X happen," while perfective past asks "was an expected/intended X completed."

Aspect in the infinitive follows the same logic; certain verbs require only an imperfective infinitive
(began/finished/stopped-type verbs, habituation verbs, нра´виться) or only a perfective infinitive (забы´ть,
успе´ть, уда´ться). Negated infinitives after exhortation/intention verbs (обеща´ть, проси´ть, реша´ть,
сове´товать, угова´ривать) are imperfective. Absence-of-necessity modals (не на´до/не ну´жно/не
обяза´тельно/не сто´ит) take only imperfective infinitives. нельзя´ + perfective infinitive = "not
possible"; нельзя´ + imperfective infinitive = "not allowed."

### Imperatives (pp. 253–259)

Second-person imperative formation depends on stress: stressed 1st-person-singular ending → stressed
-и´(те) added to the basic stem; stem-stressed verbs either keep the bare stem (stems in -й, and -а-вай
verbs) or add -ь (other stem-stressed verbs, e.g. ре´жь). Irregular imperatives include е´шь(те), да´й(те),
поезжа´й(те), ля´г(те), по´й(те), пе´й(те), and the пи´ть-type verbs' -е́й(те) forms.

Affirmative perfective imperatives request a single concrete completed action; imperfective imperatives
cover repeated/habitual requests, requests to modify an ongoing action (with a manner adverb), prompting
someone to finally begin an already-requested action, and polite social invitations (Входи´те, Сади´тесь).
Negated imperatives are normally imperfective even for one-time actions; a negated perfective imperative
specifically warns against an inadvertent, undesirable action (often with Смотри´/Осторо´жно).

First-person imperative: дава´й(те) + imperfective infinitive (continuous/repeated, also for "let's not …"
via бу´дем), or дава´й(те) + perfective мы´-form (specific one-time action); лу´чше inserts an alternative
suggestion. Third-person imperative: пу´сть/пуска´й + nominative subject + perfective or imperfective
verb.

### Conditional and subjunctive moods (pp. 259–265)

Real conditionals (likely to occur) use the indicative with е´сли…(то´)…, requiring future tense in the
if-clause for a future action (unlike English present tense). Unreal conditionals (hypothetical or
contrary-to-fact) require бы + past-tense verb in BOTH the condition and consequence clauses; the "past
tense" form here is not necessarily past in meaning — context/adverbs (за´втра vs. вчера´) disambiguate.

The subjunctive (бы + past tense, or что´бы + past tense) expresses advice, wishes, desires, requests, and
commands not yet (or not) realized. что´бы + past tense is required when the wishing/requesting verb's
subject differs from the wished-for action's subject (Я´ хочу´, что´бы она´ посмотре´ла…); что´бы +
infinitive is used when the subjects are the same, including purpose clauses (Са´ша включи´л телеви´зор,
что´бы смотре´ть но´вости vs. …что´бы па´па смотре´л но´вости).

### Transitivity, -ся, and voice (pp. 266–271)

Transitive verbs take an accusative direct object (diagnosable via Что´?/Кого´? questions); intransitive
verbs, including all verbs in -ся/-сь, cannot. Verbs in -ся fall into semantic groups: reflexive
(мы´ть(ся)-type, physical actions), reciprocal (встреча´ть(ся)-type, often with дру´г дру´га for verbs
lacking inherent reciprocity), emotion (transitive verb ↔ intransitive-in-ся with the experiencer as
subject and the stimulus in an oblique case — instrumental/dative/genitive/на+acc./о+prep. depending on
the verb), and -ся-only verbs with no transitive counterpart (боя´ться, смея´ться, улыба´ться, наде´яться,
нра´виться).

Voice: active (nominative Agent — transitive verb — accusative Patient) vs. passive (nominative Patient
— intransitive verb-in-ся — optional instrumental Agent) vs. the informal "indefinite personal"
alternative (3rd-person-plural transitive verb, no subject, translatable actively or passively) vs. middle
voice (like passive in form, but categorically excludes any Agent — used for natural/autonomous
phenomena: Со´лнце поднима´ется, Две´рь откры´лась).

### Verbal government and verbs of motion (pp. 271–281)

Certain verbs govern an oblique case directly (dative: звони´ть, помога´ть, сове´товать; genitive:
боя´ться, достига´ть, избега´ть; instrumental: владе´ть, по´льзоваться, руководи´ть) or via a fixed
preposition+case (ве´рить в+acc., благодари´ть за+acc., отлича´ться от+gen., уча´ствовать в+prep.,
гото´виться к+dat., следи´ть за+instr.).

Verbs of motion uniquely have two imperfectives: unidirectional/determinate (continuous motion toward
a goal) vs. multidirectional/indeterminate (random motion, habitual round-trips, general ability, or a
single past round-trip). The five key intransitive pairs are идти´/ходи´ть, е´хать/е´здить, бежа´ть/
бе´гать, лете´ть/лета´ть, плы´ть/пла´вать; the three transitive pairs are нести´/носи´ть, везти´/вози´ть,
вести´/води´ть, which must correlate with an accompanying intransitive motion verb in direction-type
(foot vs. vehicle) and unidirectionality.

Prefixing по- onto a unidirectional verb perfectivizes it (onset of movement, direction/speed change,
future/past one-time departure); по- onto a multidirectional verb perfectivizes it with a "for a while"
meaning. Directional prefixes (при-, у-, в-, вы-, под-, от-, пере-, про-, за-) attached to a
multidirirectional/unidirectional pair create a new imperfective/perfective pair with a specific directional
meaning (приходи´ть/прийти´ "to come," уходи´ть/уйти´ "to leave," etc.), with predictable
о-insertion, hard-sign insertion, or stem changes (е´здить→-езжа´ть, пла´вать→-плыва´ть, бе´гать→
-бега´ть). The prefixed imperfective past tense can denote an "annulled" round-trip action, exactly
paralleling the unprefixed multidirectional's round-trip use.

### Participles and verbal adverbs (pp. 281–290)

Participles combine verb properties (transitivity, aspect, tense — present or past only, voice) with
adjective agreement (gender/number/case), replacing a кото´рый-clause. Active participles: present
(imperfective stem + -ущий/-ющий for 1st conj., -ащий/-ящий for 2nd conj.) replace a nominative
кото´рый + present-tense clause; past (either aspect, stem + -ший or -вший) replace a nominative
кото´рый + past-tense clause. Passive participles: present (limited imperfective transitive verbs, -емый/
-имый) and past (perfective transitive verbs, -тый/-енный/-нный, with the same consonant alternations
as 1st-person-singular non-past) replace an accusative-кото´рый clause; the agent, if present, appears in
the instrumental. Past passive participles have a common short predicative form (drop the adjective
ending, reduce -нн- to -н-) agreeing in gender/number only, used with бы´ть for tense.

Verbal adverbs (gerunds) express an attendant same-subject action, replacing subordinate clauses of time/
cause/condition/manner (когда´, пока´, та´к как, потому´ что, е´сли). Imperfective verbal adverbs
(stem + -а/-я, or -сь for -ся verbs) denote simultaneity with the main verb, in whatever tense the main
verb is in; several irregular verbs (бежа´ть, е´хать, е´сть, мо´чь, спа´ть) lack one. Perfective verbal
adverbs (stem + -в, or -вшись for -ся verbs; a few via -я for з/с/д/т-stem prefixed motion verbs, e.g.
прийти´→придя´) denote an action completed before the main verb's action; the older -ши form is now
obsolete/literary-19th-century only.

### Conjunctions (Chapter 8, pp. 291–310)

Coordinating conjunctions join equal/parallel elements: copulative (и, и…и…, как…так и…, не
то´лько…но и…, да [colloquial]), disjunctive (и´ли, и´ли…и´ли…, ли´бо…ли´бо… [colloquial], не то…не
то… [colloquial, connotes uncertainty], то…то… ["now…now…"]), and adversative (но, одна´ко [more
formal], contrastive/contradictive a). Choosing и vs. a vs. но: и links a shared predicate across
subjects or a logically-following second clause; a links parallel-but-different (not conflicting) statements
(often translatable "whereas/while"); но signals the second clause contradicts/is at odds with the first
(often translatable "however").

Subordinating conjunctions are grouped by relationship type: temporal (когда´ [meaning shifts with
aspect: "after" with perfective predicate, "while/as" with imperfective], пока´, пока´…не, как [sudden
action], как то´лько, в то вре´мя как, до того´ как, пре´жде чем, пе´ред тем как, по´сле того´ как, с
тех пор как, до тех пор пока´…не), causal (потому´ что [must follow main clause], та´к как,
благодаря´ тому´ что [positive], из-за того´ что [negative], поско´льку, and the bookish ввиду´ того´
что/всле´дствие того´ что/в си´лу того´ что), purposive (что´бы [+ inf. same-subject, + past-tense
different-subject/subjunctive], для того´ что´бы, с тем что´бы, за тем что´бы), the subordinators что
(reported statement, always required in Russian unlike optional English "that"; contrast with the stressed
interrogative pronoun что´) and как (untranslated, with perception verbs: Мы´ слы´шали, как она´ ушла´),
resultative (та´к что, equivalent to и + поэ´тому), conditional (е´сли, е´сли…не "unless," раз "since/now
that"), concessive (хотя´/хоть, more-formal несмотря´ на то что), and comparative (как "as," чем
"than," как бу´дто/бу´дто бы/как бу´дто бы/сло´вно "as though/as if").
