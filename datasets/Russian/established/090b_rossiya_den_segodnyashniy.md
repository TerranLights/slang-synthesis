## Grammar points

### The "не + short-form/impersonal" and negotiated-obligation constructions

The text is built around a running comic device of literalized contract clauses derived from ordinary spoken commitments (e.g., turning "I'll always love you" into an enforceable "clause"). No single new grammar rule beyond Chapter 1's material, but worth noting as a stylistic pattern: Russian's short-form-adjective/participle predicates (обделён, поражён, измятая) and impersonal dative constructions (вам тяжело придётся, мне не до) are exploited throughout for their compact, idiomatic force — both recur across chapters in this book and are worth tracking as a register feature of journalistic/literary Russian generally, not exclusive to this text.

### Exercise vocabulary note (non-redundant supplement within this chapter)

Exercise 3 (p. 16) supplies an adjective bank for cloze completion: лёгкий, дурной, романтичный, романтический, героический, особый, бодрый, имущественный, проблемный, противоположный, свежий, домашний, скромный, адвокатский, дружеский — mostly already core vocabulary or already covered by the marginal gloss list above; flagged here rather than tabulated per the coverage rule's drill-exercise exception.
