## Grammar points

### 1. The 11-class RKI (Russian-as-a-Foreign-Language) taxonomy of reflexive verbs, and why it exists
Chagina opens by noting that Russian has roughly 13,798 verbs bearing the postfix -ся (per the *Обратный словарь русского языка*, 1974) out of ~37,319 total verbs — more than a third. Traditional RKI teaching collapsed all of this into three labels (собственно-возвратное, взаимно-возвратное, общевозвратное), which she argues is too coarse: e.g. phase verbs like начинаться/продолжаться/кончаться get lumped as "общевозвратное" with no practical guidance on when a learner should or shouldn't add -ся to a given verb at all (contrast Spanish, where an equivalent notion can be expressed with or without a reflexive marker). Her solution, building on and extending М.Л. Крючкова's semantic classification of emotion verbs specifically, is to define 11 semantic classes, each cross-referenced against its own syntactic case-government pattern, so a learner can predict the correct structure from the verb's meaning class rather than memorizing case government verb-by-verb.

### 2. Собственно-возвратные (self-directed) verbs and the object-incorporation logic
The defining semantic move here: the subject "includes" the object within itself. This explains an important structural asymmetry — some of these verbs retain an optional oblique complement (Она оделась в новое платье / Она оделась), while others systematically lose it in speech, especially "undressing"-direction antonyms (разделась cannot easily take "в зимнее пальто" — the removed item isn't syntactically expressible the same way as the donned one). A key contrastive pair for body-part verbs: when the transitive correlate can only combine with body-part nouns (потупить only взгляд/глаза/взор), the reflexive's subject is the 'owner' of that part (Девушка потупила взгляд → Девушка потупилась); but when the transitive correlate can combine with other object types too (открыть глаза vs. открыть сумку), the reflexive construction's subject becomes the body-part noun itself, with the 'owner' demoted to a genitive modifier (Кот зажмурил глаза → Глаза кота зажмурились, never *Кот зажмурился in that sense). The famous mnemonic couplet "Надеть одежду, одеть Надежду" encodes the odеть/надеть distinction: надеть (+ prefix на-) takes only inanimate objects (clothing), одеть takes only animate objects (a person) — and learners who don't know this produce errors like *Ты уже наделся? or *Я сегодня одела тёплый свитер.

### 3. Взаимно-возвратные (reciprocal) verbs: five formal subgroups and the "коллективность" (collectivity) requirement
Because a reciprocal action logically requires ≥2 participants, the subject must always convey plurality — via a plural noun, a "noun1 и noun2" coordination, "noun с noun" instrumental-comitative construction, a quantified group noun (два друга), or a collective noun (публика, молодёжь). Five formal derivational subgroups are distinguished by how the reciprocal meaning is marked: (1) transitive verb + -ся with matching meaning (встретить→встретиться); (2) verbs that never occur without -ся and have no transitive correlate (здороваться); (3) verbs whose transitive/reflexive forms diverge in meaning (договариваться); (4) verbs where reciprocity comes specifically from a prefix (с-/раз-/пере-) combined with -ся (сходиться/расходиться); (5) verbs marking reciprocity purely lexically, with no -ся at all (спорить, дружить) — this last group is a major error source, since learners over-apply the productive -ся pattern (*мы разговаривались, *мы дружимся с Виктором). A subtle agreement rule: with "X и Y" or "кто с кем" subjects, the verb is plural (Иван и Вера познакомились), but if the nominative element is a singular first-person pronoun (Я с Верой), the verb stays singular (Я с Верой познакомился); the "Мы с Николаем" construction is flagged as a genuine Russian-specific feature with no structural analog in many other languages, expressing not just equality but unity of the two referents. A small residual subclass takes non-person (locational) subjects for spatial coincidence — streets/rivers "meeting" (пересекаться, сливаться) — and is lexically small (only a handful of verbs).

### 4. Полисемия (polysemy) of reflexive verbs, and the fuzzy verb/homonym boundary
A dedicated section (pp. 51–53) demonstrates that most individual reflexive verb *forms* are radically polysemous across the 11-class taxonomy — the same infinitive can belong to 2, 3, or (for собираться) 4 different semantic classes depending purely on context, with no formal marking distinguishing the senses. Chagina explicitly notes that lexicographers themselves disagree on where "the same polysemous verb" ends and "two homonyms" begin, citing Янко-Триницкая's observation that the boundary is "особенно неясны и шатки" (especially unclear and shaky). Worked examples include собираться (self-directed 'intend to' / reciprocal 'gather' / passive 'be assembled by workers' / passive-qualitative 'the sofa folds up easily'), готовиться (self-directed / passive / passive-qualitative), and сниматься (passive-qualitative 'the hood comes off' / passive 'a film is being shot' / reflexive-causative 'the actress is filmed by a director'). This is presented as the single most useful diagnostic exercise in the book for helping a learner recognize which structural rules apply to a given token in context, rather than trying to memorize a fixed class per lexeme.

### 5. Пассив (passive-voice) structures: трёхчленный / двучленный / одночленный, and the aspectual rule governing them
Chagina distinguishes three degrees of participant expression in passive constructions: трёхчленный (both agent and patient named: Школа строится молодыми рабочими), двучленный (agent omitted: Дом строится), and одночленный (neither agent nor patient named, impersonal: Курить запрещается). The single most important rule for foreign learners, repeated at length: **imperfective transitive verbs form the passive with -ся** (Дом строится / строился / будет строиться, expressing process, repetition, or past reference), while **perfective transitive verbs form the passive with a short passive participle, never with -ся** (Дом построен / был построен / будет построен, expressing result). Mixing these — е.g. *Роман написался Шолоховым, *Университет построился Казаковым — is flagged as a frequent, distinctive learner error caused by blending the two patterns. A related constraint: трёхчленные passives with a personal pronoun or proper name as the agent (*Кем принимаются экзамены? Художницей Каролиной Маркс собираются...) sound artificial in real Russian; natural agents in this position are typically professions/titles/social roles (Документы подписываются директором), not individuals — because "the agent follows procedure, not personal will" per the cited analysis (Никитина 2004).

### 6. Возвратно-каузативные (reflexive-causative) verbs and perspective-taking
This class covers "have X done to you by a professional" situations (лечиться у гомеопата, стричься у парикмахера, сниматься у режиссёра). The key insight: the *same event* can be described either from the perspective of the real agent/executor (transitive: Её лечит гомеопат) or from the perspective of the initiator/beneficiary (reflexive: Она лечится у гомеопата) — the reflexive construction is the one used when biographical narrative wants to foreground the initiator rather than the executor, a stylistic convention the source ties explicitly to the biographical-dictionary genre ("Жилярди... учился у отца"). A hard constraint: the subject of a возвратно-каузативный construction must be capable of initiating the situation — hence *Годовалый ребёнок постригся у парикмахера is ill-formed (an infant cannot initiate a haircut), a boundary case distinguishing this class from собственно-возвратные (which requires the subject to actually perform the action itself, not merely commission it).

### 7. Инициирующая vs. преобразующая переходность (initiating vs. transforming transitivity) — quasi-passive
Drawing on В.И. Гаврилова's distinction between "агенс-исполнитель" (agent-executor, who expends continuous energy/skill — Рабочие строят дом) and "агенс-инициатор" (agent-initiator, who merely sets a self-propagating process in motion — Дачники сушат грибы, i.e. the mushrooms then dry themselves without further intervention), Chagina explains why perfective completed-result sentences like Бельё уже постиралось (in a washing machine) are grammatically legitimate "quasi-passive" forms, distinct from true passives, while the same perfective -ся form applied to a manually-executed process (*Бельё уже постиралось вручную) is ungrammatical — only the short passive participle (Бельё уже постирано вручную) works there. This mechanism is presented as increasingly productive in modern Russian precisely because of household technology and computing: суп подогрелся, текст перевёлся/отформатировался/распечатался are all cited as newly natural quasi-passive constructions describing machine-mediated processes.

### 8. Синонимия (synonymy) between structures — the practical payoff of the taxonomy
Each of the 11 classes has a systematic set of alternative/synonymous surface structures the book catalogues in parallel: собственно-возвратные correspond to "verb + себя" paraphrases (лечиться = лечить себя); декаузативные correspond to "модальная модификация" можно/нельзя paraphrases (Посуda бьётся = Посуду можно бить) and to adjectival correlates in -н-/-к- (Стол раздвигается = Стол раздвижной); активно-безобъектные correspond to modal "может" paraphrases (Собака кусается = Собака может укусить) and to -ач-/-уч- or -лив- adjectival correlates (Крапива жжётся = Крапива жгучая); пассивно-качественные correspond to active participle correlates (Посуда бьётся = Посуда бьющаяся) and adjectivized -ем-/-им- participles (Кофе растворяется = Кофе растворимый). The source stresses that fully productive literary participial correlates (e.g. жгущаяся крапива) sound artificial/bookish to native speakers even when grammatically well-formed — a register distinction a learner needs to know to avoid producing stilted Russian even when technically "correct."

### 9. Government-pattern instability as a diagnostic for polysemous emotion verbs
The emotion-verb chapter's central pedagogical claim: rather than memorize case government verb-by-verb, a learner should identify which of the 10 emotion sub-classes a given token belongs to in context, because government is regular *within* each class (increase/удивление takes д.п., боязнь takes р.п., беспокойство takes за+в.п., etc.) but the same verb form can shift class (and therefore government) depending on meaning — the extended worked case is бояться, which takes bare р.п. when naming the source of danger itself (бояться собак) but за+в.п. when naming something at risk of harm (бояться за своё горло), the latter implicitly compressing an entire causal situation into a single prepositional phrase.

### 10. Morphological composition note
Most reflexive verbs catalogued here are treated as simple, non-composed lexical items for this project's purposes — Russian's fusional morphology fuses the reflexive postfix -ся/-сь onto an already-inflected verb form rather than stacking freely segmentable morphemes, so a morpheme-breakdown table (per `00_Word_Concept_and_Morphological_Typology_Guide.md`) was not added row-by-row. Two recurring compositional patterns worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase: (a) prefix + root + -ся decausative/reciprocal derivation, where the prefix supplies directional/aspectual meaning and -ся supplies the valency change (у-величи-ть-ся 'become bigger', с-ходи-ть-ся 'converge'); (b) suffix-stacked adjectival correlates of reflexive verbs (-ач-/-уч- 'жг-уч-ий', -лив- 'драч-лив-ый'), which are themselves a small but productive derivational family worth tracking as a distinct mechanism from the verb inflection itself.
