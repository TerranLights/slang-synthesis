## Grammar points

### 1. Plural nouns — regular formation
Masculine/feminine "hard" nouns (ending in a consonant or -а) take -ы in the nominative plural;
"soft" nouns (ending in ь, -й, or -я) take -и. A key spelling rule applies throughout Russian:
ы/ю/я can never follow г, к, х (gutturals) or ж, ч, ш, щ (sibilants) — и/у/а are substituted
instead. This governs plurals like москвич→москвичи, девушка→девушки (pp.40–41).

### 2. Plural nouns — neuter
Neuter "hard" nouns (-о) take -а in the plural (письмо→письма); "soft" neuter nouns (-е) take -я
(здание→здания). Stress sometimes shifts between singular and plural (письмо́→пи́сьма) and must be
learned per-word (p.41).

### 3. Irregular nominative plurals
A closed set of common nouns have irregular plurals, mostly following recognizable patterns
(stressed -а́ endings: адрес→адреса́, бе́рег→берега́, глаз→глаза́, го́род→города́, дом→дома́,
по́езд→поезда́; suppletive/irregular: ребёнок→де́ти, челове́к→лю́ди, дочь→до́чери, мать→ма́тери,
брат→бра́тья, друг→друзья́, время→времена́, имя→имена́). A fuller list is deferred to Appendix 1
(p.41).

### 4. Accusative plural = nominative plural (inanimate nouns)
As with the singular, the accusative plural is identical to the nominative plural for all
inanimate nouns; the animate-noun exception is deferred to Unit 7 (p.41).

### 5. Adjective agreement and endings
Adjectives must agree with the noun they describe in number, gender, and case. Standard nominative
endings: masculine -ый, feminine -ая, neuter -ое, plural -ые (same across all genders). Where the
adjective stem ends in г/к/х/ж/ч/ш/щ, the masculine singular ending becomes -ий and the plural -ие
(spelling rule from point 1 applied to adjectives) — e.g. ма́ленький, хоро́шие. A small class of
"stressed adjectives" instead take masculine -ой (not -ый): большо́й, молодо́й (pp.41–42).

### 6. Какой (which? / what sort of? / what a...!)
A stressed adjective functioning as an interrogative ("which/what sort of": Како́й фильм ты хо́чешь
смотре́ть?) or exclamatory ("what a...!": Кака́я краси́вая це́рковь!) (p.42).

### 7. Можно / нельзя / надо + infinitive
These three predicate words are all followed by a bare infinitive: можно (permitted/possible),
нельзя (not permitted/not possible), надо (necessary) — e.g. В театре нельзя курить! ("No smoking
in the theatre!") (p.42).

### 8. Irregular verb: хотеть (to want)
Does not follow either regular conjugation pattern; full paradigm must be learned: я хочу, ты
хочешь, он хочет, мы хотим, вы хотите, они хотят — note the conjugation-type switch mid-paradigm
(1st-conj.-looking singular, 2nd-conj.-looking plural) (p.43).

### 9. Любить (to love/like) and the л-insertion rule
A regular 2nd-conjugation verb except for an inserted л in the я-form only: люблю́, лю́бишь,
лю́бит, etc. This л-insertion applies to all 2nd-conjugation verbs whose stem (after removing -ить)
ends in б, в, м, п, or ф — e.g. гото́вить → гото́влю, гото́вишь... (p.43).
