## Grammar points

### Flagging a fact as remarkable, and reacting to it (§1, pp. 153–155)

Раздел III opens with a construction family functionally adjacent to Раздел II §1's news-openers but
narrower in scope: Представь(те) (себе)/Можешь(ете) (себе) представить/Вообрази(те)/(И) Надо
же/Подумать только specifically flag the upcoming content as evoking сильную эмоцию (a strong
emotional reaction — surprise, admiration), not just "new information" in general. The book's own
three-row table (p. 155) sorts the corresponding *reactions*: Что вы говорите?!/Что ты
говоришь?!/Поразительно! signal pure surprise; Не может быть!/Ну да?! signal surprise blended with
doubt; Вот это да!/Надо же! signal admiration specifically. This is a finer three-way split of
reaction-types than the plainer two/three-way clusters seen earlier in the book (Раздел II §2), now
organized specifically around the *degree/flavor* of the addressee's emotional response rather than
belief/disbelief.

### Downplaying a fact as unremarkable — and consoling after a misfortune (§2, pp. 155–159)

A construction family (Ну и что?/Ну и что тут такого?/Ну и что тут особенного?/Подумаешь!/Подумаешь
какое дело!) directly counters §1's "this is remarkable" framing — explicitly glossed by the source
(p. 157) as expressing "несогласие с тем, что событие... необычно, удивительно... подчеркнуть, что в
нём нет ничего особенного" (disagreement that the event is unusual, emphasizing there's nothing
special about it). The book then draws a sharp functional distinction (p. 158) worth flagging
explicitly: a *formally similar-looking* cluster (Ничего/Ничего страшного/Ерунда/Пустяки/Подумаешь
какое дело!) is used not to downplay a claim of unusualness but to **console** someone reporting a
misfortune or failure — reassuring them "нет повода для огорчения" (there's no reason to be upset).
Note Подумаешь какое дело! and (loosely) Ерунда/Пустяки appear in both clusters; the book relies on
context (reaction to "something remarkable happened" vs. reaction to "something went wrong for me")
to disambiguate, not on distinct lexical forms — a genuine potential ambiguity point worth flagging
for later mechanics-analysis work rather than treating the two senses as fully separable by form
alone. A closing literary-quotation drill (p. 159, from Лермонтов's «Бэла» and Е. Шварц's
«Обыкновенное чудо»/Шукшин) illustrates Ну так что?/Ну и что? in real dialogue.

### Asserting an outcome as inevitable, not surprising (§3, pp. 160–166)

§3 splits into two subsections addressing complementary halves of "this outcome couldn't have gone
any other way":

**Subsection 1 (pp. 160–161):** Ещё бы!/Ну ещё бы! and Не удивительно!/Чему ж тут
удивляться?!/Что ж в этом удивительного? assert that a stated outcome was fully expected given known
circumstances — the book's own gloss (p. 161): "уверенность в том, что в данной ситуации так и должно
быть и по-другому быть не может/не могло." A literary-quotation set (Стругацкие, Булгаков, Пушкин, pp.
166) closes the subsection.

**Subsection 2 (pp. 161–166):** a genuinely productive syntactic template family is presented with
unusual systematicity — the book's own two-part paradigm (pp. 162–163, 164) is worth preserving in
full rather than paraphrasing away:

- **Perfective-aspect forms**, contrasting whether the action *did* or *did not* occur:
  - action occurred → **как (же) он мог не + inf** / **как (же) ему было не + inf** ("how could he
    possibly not have [done it]?!") — e.g. Как же ему было не успеть, если роман был у него целый
    месяц?! (how could he not have finished — he had the novel a whole month!).
  - action did not occur → **как (же) он мог + inf** / **как (же) ему было + inf** / **где (ж) ему
    было + inf** ("how could he possibly have [done it]?!" in a context where he didn't) — e.g. Где ж
    мне было выиграть? Я ведь начинающий теннисист (how could I possibly have won — I'm only a
    beginner!).
  The source explicitly notes (p. 164) that this perfective-aspect variant attributes the (non-)
  result to the *person's own qualities or the circumstances they found themselves in* — not to a
  lack of time.
- **Imperfective-aspect forms**, restricted to asserting outright impossibility *specifically because
  there was no time available*: **где (ж) кому было + inf(impf)** / **когда кому было + inf(impf)**
  — e.g. Где мне было этим заниматься?! У меня была гора других дел (when would I possibly have had
  time — I had a mountain of other things to do). The book is explicit (p. 164) that this
  aspectual/semantic split — personal-quality-or-circumstance impossibility (perfective) vs.
  pure time-scarcity impossibility (imperfective) — is the organizing distinction between the two
  templates, not a free stylistic variation.

This is one of the more grammatically elaborate constructions surveyed so far in this book (compare
the simpler fixed-phrase inventories of earlier sections) — genuinely productive across verb choice
and person, not a small closed set of memorized phrases.
