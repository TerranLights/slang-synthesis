## Grammar points

### 1. Reflexive verbs
A reflexive verb (marked by -ся after a consonant, -сь after a vowel on the infinitive) corresponds
to English verbs implicitly reflexive with "oneself" (to get washed, to dress) or having no direct
translation of "self" at all — находиться ("to be situated") literally means "to find itself."
Садиться ("to sit [oneself] down") conjugates: сажусь, садишься, садится, садимся, садитесь,
садятся (2nd-conj., д→ж mutation in я-form) (p.88).

### 2. Short adjectives
Alongside the "long" adjective forms taught since Unit 4, Russian has "short" adjectival forms,
existing only in the nominative and typically used when the adjective is predicative (comes at the
end of a phrase describing the subject): Это место свободно? ("Is this seat free?") vs. свободное
время ("free/spare time," attributive/long form). Model paradigm: масc. нов, fem. нова, neut. ново,
pl. новы. Not every adjective has a short form — colour adjectives (красный) and nationality
adjectives (русский) do not; modern conversational Russian mostly uses the long form, but the short
form is required in certain fixed contexts. Рад ("glad/happy") is flagged as the only common
Russian adjective with *no* long form at all. When a masculine adjective's stem ends in a
consonant cluster, a filler е is inserted in the short masculine form only: вкусный → вкусен,
доволен, свободен, согласен. The short-form neuter also doubles as the corresponding adverb:
быстрый (quick) → быстро (quickly) (pp.88–89).

### 3. The instrumental case
Expresses the instrument/means by which an action is performed (e.g. "spoon" in "I eat ice cream
with a spoon" must be instrumental) and also describes one's job/role (работать + instrumental: "I
work as a waiter"). Also used after certain prepositions, chiefly с ("with/accompanied by," becomes
со before certain consonant clusters), and перед ("in front of"), за ("behind"), между
("between"), над ("over"), под ("under"), рядом с ("next to") (p.89).

### 4. Instrumental singular of nouns
Masculine: -ом (or -ем after a soft sign/й-stem) — официант→официантом, учитель→учителем,
Алексей→Алексеем. Feminine: -ой (or -ей after я/ь-stems) — ложка→ложкой, Тоня→Тоней,
станция→станцией, дверь→дверью (irregular -ью, not -ей). Neuter: -ом (or -ем) —
окно→окном, поле→полем, здание→зданием. As with the earlier gender-based spelling rule, a
masculine or neuter noun ending in ж/ч/ш/щ/ц takes -ем, not unstressed -ом (pp.90–91).

### 5. Instrumental plural
Very straightforward for nouns: -ами (default) or -ями (soft-stem), irrespective of gender —
гриб→грибами, гость→гостями. Irregular-plural nouns build the instrumental plural from the
irregular nominative plural: друг→друзья→друзьями; and the highly irregular люди→людьми,
дети→детьми (p.91).

### 6. Instrumental of adjectives
Singular masc./neut.: -ым (or -им after г/к/х/ж/ч/ш/щ). Singular fem.: -ой (or -ей after the same
consonant set) — identical in form to the feminine noun endings. Plural (all genders): -ыми (or
-ими after that consonant set) (p.91).

### 7. Instrumental of personal pronouns
мной (я), тобой (ты), им (он/оно), ей (она), нами (мы), вами (вы), ими (они) (p.92).

### 8. Кто/что — full declension
Both fully decline across all six cases. Кто: nom. кто, acc./gen. кого, dat. кому, instr. кем,
prep. о ком. Что: nom./acc. что, gen. чего, dat. чему, instr. чем, prep. о чём (p.92).
