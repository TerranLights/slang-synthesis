## Grammar points (terse — standard imperative-formation/aspect material, redundant with prior
established/ sources)

Standard second-person imperative formation rules (based on the они form: -й(те) after -ают/-ают
stems, -и(те) for stressed single-consonant stems, -ь(те) for unstressed single-consonant stems and
double-consonant stems, special formation for -давать/-ставать/-знавать infinitives, a short
exceptions list: бить→бей(те), есть→ешь(те), пить→пей(те), дать→дай(те), лечь→ляг(те), ехать→
езжай(те); видеть/слышать/мочь/хотеть have no imperative). Standard second-person imperative-aspect
rules (positive single-action commands: perfective; positive general/habitual commands and polite
invitations: imperfective; negative commands: imperfective by default, but perfective when warning
against an unwanted accident, e.g. Не упадите! "Don't fall!"). First-person (давай(те) + verb),
third-person (пусть + verb), and indirect imperatives (хотеть/просить/сказать + чтобы + past tense)
covered fully. pp. 63–70. Standard, fully redundant with prior established/ imperative chapters.

**One genuinely non-redundant cultural footnote:** p. 67, a footnote to the example "В Москве́ не
ходи́те в шо́ртах!" ("Don't go around in shorts in Moscow!") states explicitly: "The taboo on
wearing shorts is fading under increasing fashion influence from the West" — a dated (1996)
sociolinguistic/cultural-norm observation about a specific dress-code taboo and its erosion, the
kind of explicit register/norm commentary this dispatch was instructed to prioritize.

### ТЕКСТ 6а — Andrei Belyi (Bely), untitled poem on Russia (1908)
A bitter, embittered address to the motherland ("о ро́дина мать") from the Symbolist poet, calling
on "my poor people" to disperse and shatter, describing "centuries of poverty and subjugation," and
closing with a repeated imperative "Исчезни, Россия, Россия моя!" ("Disappear, Russia, my Russia!").
The book's own pre-reading task deliberately frames this as a poem by "someone who is embittered"
toward the motherland, and its post-reading task (item 4) directly pairs it against ТЕКСТ 6б
(Akhmatova) for contrastive comparison of two opposed narrator-Russia relationships — a deliberate
curricular register/attitude contrast the book itself sets up.

### ТЕКСТ 6б — Akhmatova, untitled poem (1917)
The counterpoint poem: a voice tempts the narrator to abandon Russia ("Оставь Россию навсегда")
and offers to wash away her pain and cover her wounds with a new name/identity, but the narrator
"calmly and indifferently" covers her ears with her hands so this "unworthy speech" will not defile
her "mournful spirit" — i.e., a poem of loyalty/refusal-to-emigrate written in the revolutionary
year 1917, standing in explicit thematic opposition to Bely's poem of despair/rejection of Russia.
The book's own comparison task treats this contrast as the pedagogical point of pairing the two
texts.

### ТЕКСТ 6в — health-advice text on hypertension (contemporary press)
A practical dietary-advice text for people with high blood pressure (limit salt, add salt only to
already-cooked dishes, use lemon/dill/parsley to compensate for reduced salt, reduce animal fats in
favor of vegetable oils, avoid high-cholesterol foods like liver/kidneys/brains/fatty meat/egg
yolks) — formal/instructional register, imperative-dense as expected for its genre, but no
distinctive slang or historical content; included for completeness of coverage.
