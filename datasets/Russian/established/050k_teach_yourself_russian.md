## Grammar points

### 1. Благодарить/поблагодарить (to thank) + за-construction
This verb pair governs за + accusative: благодарю тебя за экскурсию ("I thank you for the
excursion"). Спасибо, too, takes за + accusative (Спасибо за подарок), as does платить/заплатить за
("to pay for") (p.141).

### 2. The past tense — formation and the aspect distinction
Russian has only two past-tense forms (unlike English's several), one built from the imperfective
infinitive and one from the perfective. The imperfective past describes repeated, continuing, or
incomplete actions (Я читал газету каждый день — "I used to/would read the paper every day"; Я
читал газету, когда позвонил телефон — "I was reading the paper when the phone rang" [not
necessarily finished]; Я читал газету два часа вчера — "I read the paper for two hours yesterday,"
where continuation without confirmed completion is implied). The perfective past describes single,
completed actions (Я прочитал газету всё утро вчера — "I read [finished] the paper all yesterday
morning"). Formation is identical for both aspects: remove -ть from the infinitive and add an
ending agreeing in number/gender with the subject — л (masc. sg.), ла (fem. sg.), ло (neut. sg.), ли
(plural, including formal/plural вы regardless of whether one or more people are addressed). Most
irregular verbs (жить, хотеть) form the past this way too. Exceptions requiring their own irregular
stems: вести (вёл, вела, вело, вели), везти (вёз, везла, везло, везли), есть (ел, ела, ело, ели),
идти (шёл, шла, шло, шли — suppletive), мочь (мог, могла, могло, могли), нести (нёс, несла,
несло, несли). Быть's own past tense is needed to give можно/надо/нельзя/пора and existential
"have" phrases past-tense meaning: Вчера мне надо было работать; У меня не было денег (pp.141–143).

### 3. Motion verbs — three possible pasts
Because motion verbs have three infinitives (two imperfective, one perfective), they have three
past tenses: the "habitual/multidirectional" imperfective's past can imply habit or a completed
round trip (он ходил — "he used to go" / "he has been and come back"); the "one-direction"
imperfective's past indicates an action in progress (он шёл — "he was going"); the perfective past
implies a single completed action, often also meaning "set off" (он пошёл — "he has gone, set off")
(p.143).

### 4. Мы увидимся (we'll see each other) — reflexive of видеть/увидеть
Adding reflexive endings to видеть/увидеть produces a reciprocal "to see one another": увидимся
завтра (lit. "we will see one another tomorrow"), a common parting phrase — paralleling
встречаться/встретиться ("to meet one another") from Unit 11 (p.143).

### 5. Prepositional case — completing adjective endings
Masculine/neuter singular adjectives add -ом (default) or -ем (after the unstressed-o spelling
rule); feminine singular adjectives add -ой (default) or -ей (same rule): в новом музее, в хорошей
книге, в старом здании (pp.143–144).
