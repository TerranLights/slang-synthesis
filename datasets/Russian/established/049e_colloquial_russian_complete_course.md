## Grammar points

### Lesson 10 — Verbs of motion (indeterminate/determinate pairs)
- **Core mechanism**: six paired motion verbs each split into an (a)-form (indeterminate/
  habitual/multidirectional) and a (b)-form (determinate/single-occasion/unidirectional):
  ходи́ть/идти́ "walk," е́здить/е́хать "go by vehicle," носи́ть/нести́ "carry," води́ть/вести́
  "lead," вози́ть/везти́ "convey," бе́гать/бежа́ть "run," and (unpaired in this lesson's list but
  presented alongside) лета́ть/лете́ть "fly." This determinate/indeterminate split is a genuinely
  distinct grammatical category layered on top of the imperfective/perfective aspect system
  already covered (these are all still *imperfective* verbs) — flagging as a strong candidate for
  mechanics-analysis: a third dimension of verbal "shape" (habitual-diffuse vs. one-time-
  directional) independent of tense or the perfective/imperfective split.
- **(a) indeterminate verbs** (ходи́ть-type) are used for: repeated round trips (Ка́ждый день
  Пи́тер хо́дит на рабо́ту — daily commute); vague/undefined direction (он хо́дит по Арба́ту —
  wandering up and down); the general ability to perform the motion (Моя́ до́чка уже́ хо́дит "my
  daughter can already walk" — an ability-reading unique to this verb class); and, *only in the
  past tense*, a completed single round trip (Мать уже́ ходи́ла в го́род "Mother has already been
  to town [and returned]" — the past-tense-only exception is explicitly flagged as a discrete rule,
  not a default extension of the "repeated" sense).
- **(b) determinate verbs** (идти́-type) describe: a single occasion in progress (Куда́ ты идёшь?);
  motion toward a specific, named direction/destination (Сего́дня Пи́тер идёт... в кафе́).
- **Conjugation classes**: ходи́ть/е́здить/води́ть/носи́ть/лете́ть are all 2nd-conjugation with
  1st-person-singular consonant mutations (д→ж-pattern absent here but с→ш in носи́ть, з→ж in
  вози́ть/е́здить-type, т→ч in лете́ть); нести́/вести́/везти́ are 1st-conjugation with irregular
  past tense stems (all three share the pattern -ёс/-ла/-ло/-ли or -ёл/-ла/-ло/-ли, i.e. the final
  root consonant drops before a zero-vowel masculine singular past ending); бежа́ть is fully
  irregular (бегу́, бежи́шь, бежи́т, бежи́м, бежи́те, бегу́т — mixed 1st/2nd conjugation endings,
  the same "irregular" pattern independently attested for хоте́ть in earlier lessons).
- **Figurative/impersonal extensions**: in a fixed figurative expression, only one member of a
  motion-verb pair is grammatical, never both — дождь идёт "it's raining," снег идёт "it's
  snowing," вре́мя лети́т "time flies," дела́ иду́т отли́чно "things are going very well," идёт
  фильм "a film is showing," шла торго́вля "trade was happening." These are lexicalized
  collocations, not productive alternations — a useful example of grammatical-category bleaching
  into fixed idiom, relevant to how slang formations might likewise freeze a grammatical
  distinction into an idiom no longer felt as "grammar" by speakers.
- **Passenger vs. vehicle-as-subject**: driving/riding *in* any vehicle always uses the
  е́здить/е́хать pair regardless of vehicle type (Мы е́хали на по́езде "we were going by train," of
  a passenger) — but when the vehicle itself (not a person in it) is the grammatical subject, the
  verb choice depends on vehicle type: public transport (train, bus, tram) takes ходи́ть/идти́ (Вот
  идёт по́езд "here comes the train"), while a car specifically takes е́здить/е́хать (Вот е́дет
  маши́на). This vehicle-type-conditioned subject-verb agreement split (as opposed to the
  passenger-perspective default) is a genuinely fine-grained lexical-semantic rule not reducible to
  the general indeterminate/determinate contrast.
- **Past and future tenses** preserve the same (a)/(b) distinction as the present (Пи́тер уже́ два
  ра́за лета́л в Ло́ндон "has already flown... twice" [repeated] vs. Пи́тер бы́стро шёл по у́лице
  "was walking quickly" [single, directional]); the infinitive likewise selects (a) or (b)
  according to the same semantic logic (Пи́тер лю́бит ходи́ть по Арба́ту [habitual/enjoys walking]
  vs. Сейча́с он хо́чет идти́ в кафе́ [one specific trip]).
