**Morpheme breakdown (fusional/complex-derivation examples, per the extraction spec's morphological
composition guidance and this language's fusional typology).**

> **подоконник** = под- (prefix, "under/beneath") + окон- (root "window," genitive-stem allomorph
> of окно) + -ник (suffix, concretizing/instrument-or-place noun-former) — the source's own worked
> example (p.73) of a prefixal-suffixal (combined) derivation, illustrating that Russian word
> formation is not purely agglutinative-stacking but fuses root allomorphy (окно → окон-) with the
> affixes.

> **безбилетный** = без- (prefix, "without") + билет (root, "ticket") + -н- (relational-adjective
> suffix) — cited by the source (p.74) specifically to illustrate a **prefix-final voicing/devoicing
> alternation rule**: prefixes ending in a voiced consonant devoice to -с before a voiceless root
> consonant (без- → бес- before билет-, giving "бесплатный" for "без-" + "плат-" + "-н-" but the
> orthographic split is "без-" retained as "без" before a vowel-initial root and "бес-" before a
> voiceless-consonant-initial one) — the source's own phonological note: prefixes ending in a
> consonant may show an "о" fill-vowel (отошёл, подобрал), and prefixes ending in "з" spell as "с"
> before voiceless consonants (безбилетный, but бесплатный).

## Grammar points

### Word-formation taxonomy (p.73, footnote — the chapter's single most systematic piece of content)

The source lays out a four-way classification of Russian word-formation methods, presented as
background/reference material ("для желающих — теоретическая информация на максимум... это
ознакомительная, пассивная информация") rather than active drill content:

**I. Morphological methods:**
1. **Affixation** — four sub-types: (1) prefixal (*перечитать*, *сверхзвуковой*); (2) suffixal
   (*городской*, *учитель*); (3) prefixal-suffixal/combined (*бесплатный*, *подоконник*); (4)
   postfixal (*учиться*).
2. **Non-affixal (безаффиксный)** — a root standing alone as a new word without any added affix
   (*стук*, *крик*, *смех* — verb roots becoming nouns by "zero-suffixation").
3. **Compounding (словосложение)** — joining two roots/stems (*диван-кровать* "sofa bed",
   *стихосложение* "versification").
4. **Abbreviation** — acronym/clipped-word formation (*РФ*, *замдекана*, *вуз*).

**II. Morphological-syntactic methods** (a word shifts part-of-speech via syntactic recategorization
rather than adding an affix): 1. Substantivization (*столовая* "dining room," from the adjective
"cafeteria-related"); 2. Adjectivization (*блестящие способности* "brilliant abilities," a participle
used as a pure adjective); 3. Pronominalization (*данное событие* "this/the given event," an
adjective used as a demonstrative); 4. Adverbialization (*идти наудачу* "to go on luck/by chance,"
a noun phrase fossilized into an adverb).

**III. Lexical-semantic method** — a single word splits into unrelated homonyms via meaning
divergence (*мир* = "universe/world" vs. *мир* = "peace," historically distinct meanings that
converged in spelling after the 1918 orthographic reform).

**IV. Lexical-syntactic method** — two words fuse into one via univerbation (*сегодня* "today" from
*сего дня*, "of this day"; *сейчас* "now" from *сей час*, "this hour").

### Affixes, roots, and phonological alternations (pp.74)

Defines **root** (корень) as the meaning-bearing core shared across a word family, subject to
consonant/vowel alternation (белый — белизна — беловатый — белеть — белок, all sharing the alternating
root "бел-/бель-/белок-"). Defines **prefix** (префикс/приставка) as a pre-root element forming new
words/forms, and gives a substantial inventory split by native vs. loan origin and by the part of
speech it typically attaches to:

- Loan-origin prefixes on nouns: *а-, анти-, вице-, де-, дис-, контр-, обер-, ре-, суб-, ультра-,
  экстра-*; on adjectives: *а-, гипер-, интер-, пост-, суб-, супер-, транс-, ультра-, экстра-*; on
  verbs: *де-/дез-, ре-*.
- Native-origin prefixes on nouns: *без-, не-, после-, пра-, сверх-*; on adjectives: *вне-, внутри-,
  меж-/между-, наи-, не-, около-, после-, через-*; on verbs: *в-, вз-, воз-, вы-, до-, за-, из-, на-,
  над-, недо-, низ-, о-, об-, от-, пере-, по-, под-, пре-, пред-, при-, про-, раз-, с-, со-, у-*.

Two phonological rules are flagged explicitly: (1) prefixes ending in a consonant may insert a
filler "о" before certain consonant clusters (*отошёл*, *подобрал*); (2) prefixes ending in "з" are
spelled with "с" when the following root begins with a voiceless consonant (*безбилетный* but
*бесплатный*).

Defines **suffix** (суффикс) as a post-root, pre-ending element forming new words/word-forms
(*отдохнуть*, *студентка*, *домик*, *решение*), and **postfix** (постфикс) as an element that always
attaches to the very end of the whole word, after any ending, rather than to the root (*учиться*,
*какой-то*). Prefixes + suffixes + postfixes are collectively termed **affixes**, and a word's
particular affix combination is its **word-formation model** (словообразовательная модель), with
each part of speech favoring its own characteristic set of models.

### Suffix inventory built up across the suffixation sub-chapter (pp.76-97)

The chapter systematically drills (without providing a single consolidated table, but recoverable
from the exercise headers) the following noun/adjective/verb-forming suffixes and their core
meanings:

- **Diminutive/affectionate noun suffixes**: *-к-* (минутка), *-очк-/-ечк-* (explicitly glossed by
  the source as meaning "маленький" or "любимый" — "small" or "beloved," чашка → чашечка), *-ик-/
  -чик-* (билет → билетик — the source explicitly asks students to judge whether this suffix's
  register is official or colloquial, implying it is colloquial/diminutive), *-ок-/-ёк-*,
  *-очек-/-ёчек-* (further diminutive gradations, сынок → сыночек).
- **Feminine-agent suffix**: *-к-* (студент → студентка).
- **Relational-adjective suffixes**: *-ск-* (Петербург → петербургский, with consonant alternations
  flagged), *-ов-/-ев-* (апельсин → апельсиновый), *-н-* (лимон → лимонный), *-ическ-* (from
  abstract nouns in *-ия*: филология → филологический).
- **Denominal/deadjectival verb-forming suffix**: *-е-* (a color/state adjective becomes an
  intransitive "to become/turn X" verb: белый → белеть, красный → краснеть).
- Later exercises (pp.79-97, not individually tabulated here) extend this same systematic approach
  to further noun-forming suffixes for abstractions (*-ость*, *-ство*, *-изн-*, *-от-*), agent nouns
  (*-як, -ач, -ец*), and denominal verbs (*-и-*, *-ирова-/-изирова-* for loanword-derived verbs like
  *программировать*, *критиковать*, *финансировать*), plus adverb-forming suffixes and prefix-plus-
  suffix adverbial formations (по-английски-type manner adverbs; идти наудачу-type fossilized
  adverbial phrases).

### Prefixation, and combined prefix-suffix derivation (pp.98-115)

Structurally mirrors the suffixation chapter but for verbal prefixes, explicitly flagging (p.98)
that "**one and the same prefix can have different meanings in different words**" — i.e., Russian
verbal prefixation is not a one-meaning-per-prefix system, a genuinely important typological point
for any later slang-mechanics analysis of prefixed verb coinages. The prefix-plus-suffix sub-chapter
(starting p.101) explicitly notes it introduces "principally nothing new" beyond combining the
already-drilled prefix and suffix inventories, but does introduce a substantial new batch of
**geographic/regional compound nouns and adjectives formed with без-/без+prefix, меж-, при-, за-,
etc.** (*Закавказье* "Transcaucasia", *Забайкалье* "Transbaikalia", *Заволжье* "Trans-Volga region",
*Приазовье* "Azov-region", *Подмосковье* "Moscow-region", *Поволжье* "Volga region") — genuinely
useful toponymic word-formation patterns (за- + place name = "the region beyond/across X"; при-/
под- + place name = "the region near/around X") worth flagging for this project's own mechanics
analysis of derivational region-naming, even though it is not itself slang.

### Compound words (сложные слова, pp.108-115)

Covers nouns, adjectives, verbs, adverbs, and numerals formed by joining two roots/stems, with or
without connecting affixes (*мореплаватель* "seafarer" with a suffix added on top of the compound
base; *двухметровый* "two-meter[-long]" similarly). The source's explicit example of pure
root-joining with no affix at all: *хлебозавод* "bread factory," *аэродром* "airfield." Also
introduces compound adjectives expressing a scaled/comparative property (*скоропортящийся*
"perishable, quick-to-spoil," *трудновыполнимый* "hard-to-carry-out," *слаборазвитый*
"underdeveloped," *всепоглощающий* "all-consuming," *труднодоступный* "hard-to-access").

### Substantivization and abbreviation (pp.116-121)

**Substantivization**: an adjective or participle starts functioning as a noun while retaining
adjectival declension (*гостиная* "living room," *ванная* "bathroom," *больной* "patient,"
*говорящий* "speaker," *подчинённый* "subordinate," *русский* "a Russian [person]"; also, notably,
the politically-inflected *левые*/*правые*, "the left/the right [political factions]," Ex.202).
**Abbreviation**: acronym and clipped-word formation, illustrated with institutional acronyms
(*МГУ*, *СПбГУ*, *Госдума*) the source flags as essential early vocabulary given their constant use
in Russian mass media (СМИ).
