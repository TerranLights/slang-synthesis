# Russian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Н. Новикова, О. Щербакова, *Синяя звезда (Blue Star)* — full pedagogical apparatus, all 16 units

**Source:** Н. С. Новикова, О. М. Щербакова, *Синяя звезда: Рассказы и сказки русских и зарубежных
писателей с заданиями и упражнениями* (6th ed., Флинта/Наука, Moscow, 2007), full book, printed
pp. 2–253 (PDF pages 1–258). File: `source_reference/languages/Russian/Russian Learning Pack
[up-to-date as of 2012]/Vol 1 of 3/86.Синяя звезда - Blue Star Stories and tales by Russian and
foreign writers with assignments and exercises.pdf`.

**Coverage note.** This is an adapted-reader-with-exercises (a "book for foreigners learning
Russian"), not a pure grammar reference or pure fiction anthology. Per the extraction spec and this
dispatch's explicit instruction, **the 16 adapted literary narratives themselves (Красная Шапочка,
Три Поросёнка, Золушка, Мальчик с пальчик, Орден, Клевета, Одиннадцать слонов, Синяя звезда, Слон,
Чудесный доктор, Тапёр, Рассказ о говорящей собаке, Козетта, Душечка, Полотенце с петухом, Стальное
горло) were read for context only and are NOT reproduced or extracted as vocabulary/prose content**
— copyright discipline excludes bulk narrative reproduction, and the narrative prose itself is
mostly graded/simplified core vocabulary of the kind already well covered by this project's other
Wave 1 grammar references. Extraction focuses entirely on the **pedagogical apparatus**: the
exercises attached to each of the 16 units, which is where this source's genuinely new content
lives — idiom/phraseological-unit lists, proverb (пословица/поговорка) lists, discourse-particle
explanations (же, уже, -то, -ка, ли), the vvodnye-slova (parenthetical/discourse-marker) taxonomy,
verb-prefix semantic families, register/politeness formulas (requests, refusals, sympathy), and
paired-lexeme distinctions (весь/целый, любой/всякий, смочь/суметь/удаться, болеть-I/болеть-II,
два/двое) that the exercises explicitly teach. Repeated fill-in-the-blank and paraphrase drills that
introduce no new lexical or grammatical content beyond what's captured below were skipped per the
coverage rule.

**PDF-extraction note.** This source has a genuine `pdftotext`-extractable text layer (Adobe
ClearScan OCR, per `pdfinfo`), not a font-substitution cipher — none of the five Cyrillic
corruption variants catalogued elsewhere in this project's Russian corpus were found here. The OCR
quality is uneven: exercise headers, glossary-style word lists, and dialogue are generally clean,
but many narrative-prose lines show heavy character-level OCR noise (e.g. "Красна н LIJапо чка"
for "Красная Шапочка", spurious spacing inside words, occasional stray Latin letters substituted
for lookalike Cyrillic). Since the narrative prose was not being extracted as content anyway, this
did not block the extraction; all exercise-list material quoted/paraphrased below was cross-checked
against clean surrounding context. No handwritten marginalia were found — this is a clean library/
archive-scan copy, and the source has a real text layer throughout, so this is a text-layer
extraction, not a vision-read (Vision Reading Confidence is `n/a` for all rows).

## Grammar points

### Verb-prefix semantic families (morphological composition — fusional Russian, prefix as
derivational morpheme)

Several exercises build out a full derivational family from one verb root plus a set of prefixes,
each prefix carrying a distinct, statable meaning. Per this project's morphological-typology
convention (Russian is fusional but prefixation on verbs is genuinely compositional — a real
morpheme boundary, not a fused inflectional ending), these are given morpheme breakdowns:

> **выписывать/выписать** = вы- (out of, resulting-record sense) + писать (write) — "write out"
> something for issuance (e.g. a prescription/check), or copy out material one wants to keep.
> **записывать/записать** = за- (initiating/for-later-reference sense) + писать — "write down" for
> memory/record.
> **надписывать/надписать** = над- (on top of, on the outer surface) + писать — "inscribe" on top of
> or on the outside of something.
> **описывать/описать** = о- (around, comprehensively) + писать — "describe" in detail.
> **переписывать/переписать** = пере- (again, across/over) + писать — "rewrite/copy out again."
> **подписывать/подписать** = под- (underneath) + писать — "sign" (write one's name underneath a
> document).
> **расписываться/расписаться** = рас- (distributive/completive) + пис + -ыва- (imperfectivizing
> suffix) + -ся (reflexive/self-directed) — "sign" (in an official register/receipt).
> **списывать/списать** = с- (off of, copying-from sense) + писать — "copy" from an original/another
> person's work.
> — Family taught in "Чудесный доктор" ex. 10, p. 141; demonstrates that писать-prefixation is a
> fully productive, semantically transparent derivational system in Russian, directly relevant to
> later slang-mechanics analysis of prefixed-verb neologism potential.

> **выстроить/перестроить/надстроить/встроить/пристроить/достроить** (all from **строить**, "to
> build") = вы-/пере-/над-/в-/при-/до- + строить — six distinct prefixed meanings ("build up/
> completely," "rebuild," "build an extra story on," "build in/integrate," "build an addition onto,"
> "finish building") taught together in "Три Поросёнка" ex. 15, p. 33, alongside a parallel drill on
> the semantically transparent "думать"-family (подумать, передумать, вдуматься, выдумать,
> придумать), ex. 11, p. 32.

> **вчитываться / дочитывать / перечитывать / почитать / зачитывать (разг.) / зачитываться /
> начитаться / отчитывать / отчитываться** (all from **читать**, "to read") = в-/до-/пере-/по-/
> за-/от- + читать (+ imperfectivizing/reflexive suffixes as needed) — "Стальное горло" ex. 13,
> p. 252, explicitly glosses each: вчитываться = read closely to grasp the author's full meaning;
> дочитывать = finish reading; перечитывать = re-read; почитать = read for a short while;
> зачитывать (colloquial) = read a document aloud, *or* colloquially "fail to return a borrowed
> book"; зачитываться = get excessively absorbed in a book; начитаться = read more than one
> should/needed to; отчитывать = reprimand someone; отчитываться = give an account/report of
> completed work — the last two show the same за-/от- prefix producing a semantically unrelated
> pair, illustrating prefix polysemy rather than a single consistent meaning per prefix.

### Prefix-family semantics: за- and раз-/рас-

"Козетта" ex. 5A (p. 202) sets out four core meanings of the verbal prefix **за-**: (1) a minor,
incidental action en route (зайти, занести, заехать, забежать — "drop by/off while going
somewhere"); (2) inception of an action (заговорить, закричать — "start speaking/shouting"); (3)
an action carried to excessive absorption, often to the actor's own detriment (засмотреться,
зачитаться, задуматься, замечтаться — "get lost in looking/reading/thought/daydream"); (4)
excessive degree of an action directed at an object, again to its detriment (запугать, закормить,
захвалить — "frighten/overfeed/overpraise to a harmful degree").

"Полотенце с петухом" ex. 8 (p. 237) sets out a parallel four-part semantics for **раз-/рас-**:
(1) division into parts (разбить, раздробить, разрезать, разорвать, разлить); (2) completeness/
destruction (раздавить, расстрелять); (3) action directed outward in multiple directions/to
multiple locations (разбросать, расставить, развесить, раздать; and the corresponding
intransitive/reflexive set разойтись, разъехаться, разлететься, разбежаться); (4) undoing the
result of a previously-completed action (раздумать, разлюбить, разучиться, расхотеть,
распечатать — "change one's mind," "fall out of love," "unlearn/forget a skill," "no longer want,"
"unseal").

### Aspect/lexeme pairs and near-synonym distinctions taught via contrastive drill

- **весь vs. целый** ("Тапёр" ex. 10, p. 159): весь = "in its entirety, without exception" (весь
  мир, все студенты — the latter usage is plural-only); целый = a) a full/large quantity ("too
  much," целая тарелка, целый час), b) large in scope/significance (целая эпоха), c) undamaged/
  intact (целый стакан). Nouns denoting collective substances (solids/liquids) take only весь
  (весь хлеб, вся вода); nouns of measure (length, volume, weight) take only целый (целый
  километр); nouns for time spans can take either, with a meaning difference: целый день = "a lot
  of time [spent on X]," весь день = "without a break, the whole day through."
- **любой vs. всякий** ("Одиннадцать слонов" ex. 7, p. 89): любой = "whichever, it doesn't matter
  which" (any one you like); всякий = "various, of different kinds."
- **смочь / суметь / (кому) удалось** + infinitive ("Синяя звезда" ex. 11, p. 106): near-synonymous
  ways to express successful accomplishment of an action, drilled as substitutable in context.
- **болеть (I, conjugated like говорить) vs. болеть (II, conjugated like читать)** ("Чудесный
  доктор" ex. 2, p. 137): болеть-I is impersonal/third-person-only and takes a body-part subject
  (у меня болит голова); болеть-II conjugates in all persons and takes an illness-name object
  (я болею гриппом). A genuine two-lexeme homograph pair, not one paradigm.
- **два/двое, три/трое, четыре/четверо ... семь/семеро** collective numeral forms ("Чудесный
  доктор" ex. 1, p. 135–136): the collective forms (двое, трое...) are used only (a) with
  masculine-person nouns, (b) with дети/ребята/люди, (c) with personal pronouns (нас двое), and
  (d) with plural-only nouns (двое суток). They are never used with feminine-person nouns (*двое
  женщин* is ungrammatical — trое студентка must instead be три студентки). The noun following
  takes genitive plural.
- **оказываться/оказаться, показываться/показаться, казаться/показаться** ("Козетта" ex. 8–9,
  p. 204): three near-homophonous verbs with distinct senses — оказаться = "to turn out to be" /
  "to end up (in a place)"; показаться = "to seem" (subjective impression) or "to come into view"
  (physical appearance); казаться = ongoing state of seeming.
- **надевать/надеть (что?) vs. одевать/одеть (кого?)** ("Козетта" ex. 2, p. 200–201): "put on"
  (an item of clothing, direct object = the clothing) vs. "dress" (someone, direct object = the
  person).
- **горе́ть** (six distinct senses drilled together, "Душечка" ex. 9, p. 221–222): (1) to burn/be
  consumed by fire; (2) to run a fever (ребёнок весь горит); (3) to flush/redden with emotion
  (щёки горят от стыда); (4) to sparkle/shine (глаза горят от радости); (5) to be consumed by a
  strong feeling (гореть ненавистью); (6) to fail commercially/lose money (фирма горит); (7)
  (colloquial) for work to be going very well/fast ("работа горит в его руках"). The exercise asks
  students to judge which sense the character Kukin intends by "театр горит" (the sense of "the
  business is failing/losing money," reinforced by his complaint about paying rent and actors with
  no audience).
- **принадлежать (кому?)** ("Козетта" ex. 10, p. 205): possession verb restricted to
  official/legal register (Кому принадлежит эта квартира?), contrasted with the neutral
  "чья это квартира?"
- **выглядеть** ("Душечка" ex. 12, p. 223): "to look/appear [a certain way]," used with an
  instrumental-case or short-adjective complement of appearance/age, distinct from смотреть
  ("to look at").
- **жениться (на ком?) / быть женатым (на ком?) / выходить замуж (за кого?) / быть замужем
  (за кем?)** ("Душечка" ex. 15, p. 224–225): the marriage-verb system is asymmetric by the
  subject's gender — a man "marries onto" a woman (жениться на, женат на), a woman "goes out
  behind" a man (выходить замуж за, замужем за); only the reciprocal пожениться is
  gender-neutral.
- **возникнуть vs. появиться** ("Стальное горло" ex. 8, p. 250): возникнуть = "to spring into
  existence" (sudden, often abstract: an idea, a phenomenon); появиться = "to become visible"
  (gradual, often concrete) or "to come into existence" more generally.
- **жалеть/пожалеть** (three senses, "Стальное горло" ex. 9, p. 250–251): (a) to feel pity/
  compassion for someone; (b) to be unwilling to spend/use up a resource (пожалеть денег); (c) to
  regret/feel sorry about a state of affairs.
- **ни разу vs. не раз** ("Стальное горло" ex. 12, p. 251): ни разу = "not [even] once" (negation);
  не раз = "more than once, many times" (affirmative, despite the surface negative particle) —
  a genuine false-friend pair for learners.

### Causal "от + genitive" construction

"Клевета" ex. 5–6 (p. 81–82) teaches "от + genitive" as a marker of *unwilled, involuntary* cause,
restricted to four verb classes: (1) verbs of dying/perishing (умирать, гибнуть); (2) verbs of
color/appearance/state change (побледнеть, покраснеть, похудеть); (3) verbs of involuntary emotional
expression (смеяться, молчать, кричать, плакать, уставать); (4) verbs of externally-caused physical
effect (падать, задыхаться, прятаться). The governed noun typically names either an internal feeling
(стыд, злость, горе, радость, страх, обида, смех) or an external triggering factor (холод, голод,
жар, шум).

### "Чтобы + past tense" vs. "чтобы + infinitive" (purpose clauses)

"Клевета" ex. 4 (p. 78–79): a purpose clause with чтобы takes the infinitive when the main and
subordinate clauses share the same subject, and the past tense when they have different subjects
(this is presented as a firm rule, contrasting with English "so that" which allows either
construction regardless of subject identity).

### Impersonal "от + genitive" causal chains and impersonalized emotion verbs

"Слон" ex. 6–7 (p. 122): personal хотеть-constructions are systematically converted to impersonal
дативе + хочется constructions (Я хочу → Мне хочется), illustrating a broader Russian pattern of
impersonal experiencer constructions for involuntary states/desires.

### Active vs. passive/participial transformation drills

Multiple units drill (a) converting причастие (participle) phrases to который-clauses and back, and
(b) converting active-voice sentences to passive-voice ones with a instrumental agent (Люди забыли
старинный язык → Старинный язык был забыт людьми — "Синяя звезда" ex. 6–8, p. 105). These are
standard fusional-language voice/participle transformations, not new vocabulary, and are recorded
here only as a grammar point since they recur as the dominant exercise type across nearly every
unit in the book (roughly a third of all exercises in the whole book are of this причастие/
деепричастие-to-который/subordinate-clause conversion type).

### Deepричастие (converb) formation and simultaneity/precedence marking

Nearly every unit includes a "name the source verb and its aspect" deepричастие drill, plus a drill
converting the deepричастие construction into a synonymous coordinate clause with и/когда/потому
что/если/хотя. The consistent grammar point across all of these: a deepричастие formed from an
imperfective verb marks an action simultaneous with the main-clause action, while one formed from
a perfective verb marks an action that precedes it — e.g. "Глядя на портреты, жители расстраивались"
(simultaneous, imperfective глядя) vs. "Увидев открытую шкатулку, кормилица сразу всё поняла"
(precedent, perfective увидев). This rule is stated explicitly in the "Стальное горло" exercise set
(ex. 3–4, p. 247) as the culminating, most systematic statement of it in the book.
