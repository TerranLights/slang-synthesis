## Grammar points

### Opening a piece of news: four registers of "attention-getter" (§1, pp. 95–96)

The book sorts news-openers into four functional groups by what stance toward the addressee's prior
knowledge they encode: (1) Вы знаете — genuinely neutral, the default/most frequent choice; (2)
Слушайте/Послушайте — explicitly presupposes the news is unknown to the addressee; (3) Вы
слышали?/Вам не говорили? — explicitly *allows* that the addressee might already know; (4)
Представьте/Вообразите/Подумать только — frames the news itself as unusual enough to merit surprise.
This four-way split (framed table, p. 96) is presented as the organizing scheme for the whole opener
inventory.

### Reacting to news: interest, doubt, and "already knew" (§2, pp. 97–104)

Three distinct reactive stances are laid out. A first cluster (Да?, Вот как?, Интересно) simply signals
attentive engagement without commitment either way. A second, graded cluster runs from mild disbelief
through near-rejection: the book is explicit that Разве? presupposes the speaker held a *different*
belief about the fact ("я думал(а) по-другому"), while Неужели?! presupposes the speaker considered the
fact close to *impossible* ("я не считал(а), что это возможно") — a distinction worth preserving rather
than treating the two as interchangeable synonyms (p. 102). Не может быть! pushes past doubt into
outright resistance to the claim. A third cluster (Да, говорят / Да, мне говорили) instead signals the
news was *not* new to the speaker at all.

### Escalating "what do you mean?!" pushback on an unwelcome fact (§2 cont'd, pp. 106–110)

A distinct construction family (Как?, Как так X?!, То есть как X?!, То есть как это X?!) challenges a
just-stated fact with increasing indignation — the book's own framed table (p. 107) glosses the plain
"Как? / Как X?" as simple non-expectation, while the "Как так / То есть как" variants mark "крайне
удивлён и даже возмущён" (extreme surprise verging on indignation). The typical *response* to such a
challenge either minimizes the reaction (Очень просто) or flatly declines to elaborate further (А вот
так, X, и всё) — both are explicitly glossed by the source as ways of "dampening" what the responder
perceives as an oversized emotional reaction to an ordinary fact (footnote, p. 106).

### Prompting elaboration vs. asking for the reason (§3, pp. 111–114)

Три related but non-interchangeable continuation-prompts are distinguished: И как?/Ну и как? asks for
*more detail* about an already-known event ("tell me more about it"); И что? asks what *follows* from
what was just said, not merely for more detail; А что?/А зачем тебе? instead turns the question back on
the interlocutor, asking *why* they raised the topic or want the information at all, rather than
continuing to discuss the topic's content itself. The book notes the customary reply to a genuine И
что? typically opens with А то, что... (p. 113).

### Requesting clarification, three granularities (§4, pp. 115–118)

The book distinguishes clarification requests by scope: (1) about a single unfamiliar referent just
mentioned (Какая ещё книга? / Что за спектакль? — "what N are you even talking about"); (2) about the
intended *meaning* of a statement as a whole (В каком смысле? / Что вы имеете в виду?); and (3) about an
entire unclear *situation* (А что случилось? / А в чём дело?), used when the addressee is confused not
just by a word or a sentence but by the whole state of affairs they've walked into.
