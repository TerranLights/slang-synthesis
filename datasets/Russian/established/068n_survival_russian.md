## Grammar points

### поздравля́ть/поздра́вить government — Lesson 15 (p. 225, Table 77) and formality-scale template (p. 223, Table 75)

Government pattern: [person congratulated, accusative] + с + [occasion, instrumental] — С днём
рожде́ния!, С Но́вым го́дом!, С Ро́ждеством!. Three parallel registers are laid out side by side in
Table 75: **casual** (bare Поздравля́ю тебя́ с пра́здником! or an even more casual vocative-only
Оля, с пра́здником тебя́!); **official** (Разреши́те поздра́вить вас с юбиле́ем!, using the
imperative разреши́те "allow [me]" + infinitive — the same разреши́те-softening strategy already
seen in Lesson 2's Дава́йте познако́мимся); and **extending congratulations to a third person**
(Переда́йте мои́ поздравле́ния ва́шим роди́телям! — dative of the third party, since the speaker is
routing wishes *through* the addressee to someone else). This is the clearest single statement in
the book of a genuine three-tier casual/official/relayed register system tied to distinct
case-government patterns rather than just word choice — directly useful reference material for
constructing an analogous register scale in an invented conlang.

### жела́ть/пожела́ть government — Lesson 15 (p. 226, Table 78) and parallel formality scale (p. 224, Table 76)

Government pattern: [person, dative] + [thing wished, genitive] — Жела́ю вам сча́стья и успе́хов!,
От всей души́ жела́ем тебе́ кре́пкого здоро́вья!. The same three-register structure as
поздравля́ть is repeated for жела́ть: casual (bare жела́ю), official (Разреши́те пожела́ть вам...),
and third-party (Переда́йте мои́ пожела́ния... + genitive-of-wish + dative-of-recipient, a doubly
case-marked construction combining both government patterns from поздравля́ть's third-person row).

### Possessive pronouns — prepositional, dative, and accusative cases — Lesson 15 (p. 227, Tables 79-81)

Completes the possessive-pronoun declension system begun with the nominative-only paradigm in
Lesson 2: prepositional (мой→в моём, моя́→в мое́й, моё→в моём, мои́→в мои́х); dative (мой→моему́,
моя́→мое́й, моё→моему́, мои́→мои́м); accusative (мой→моего́ [animate] or мой [inanimate] — the
table shows моего́ throughout, implying it's demonstrating the animate-referent form consistent
with Lesson 7's animacy rule — моя́→мою́, моё→моё, мои́→мои́х [animate] or мои́ [inanimate]).
Third-person его́/её/их are confirmed **invariant across every case** shown here, consistent with
the Lesson 2 observation that these are frozen genitive pronoun forms rather than true declining
possessive adjectives — this table is the first to demonstrate that invariance holds across the
*entire* case system, not just the two cases shown back in Lesson 2.
