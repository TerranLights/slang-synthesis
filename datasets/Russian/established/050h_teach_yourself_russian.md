## Grammar points

### 1. Весь (all, the whole) — determinative pronoun
Declines adjective-like: masc. весь день, fem. вся группа, neut. всё письмо, plural все туристы.
Full case-declension deferred to Appendix 1 (p.102).

### 2. Instrumental case for means of transport and time-of-day/season
Extends Unit 8's instrumental: поездом (by train), автобусом (by bus), самолётом (by plane). Also
used idiomatically in fixed time expressions: утром (in the morning), днём (during the day),
вечером (in the evening), ночью (at night), зимой (in winter), весной (in spring), летом (in
summer), осенью (in autumn) (p.102).

### 3. Future tense of быть (to be)
Unlike the absent present tense, быть has a full future paradigm: я буду, ты будешь, он будет, мы
будем, вы будете, они будут (p.103).

### 4. Telling the time
Requires both cardinal and ordinal numerals (ordinals are true adjectives: первый, второй, третий,
четвёртый... twelfth двенадцатый). On-the-hour time uses the cardinal + час, with the usual
case-governed-by-number rule (nominative sg. after 1, genitive sg. after 2–4, genitive pl. from 5
up): Час, Три часа, Девять часов. Minutes past the hour use the genitive of the *next* ordinal
hour (as if "X minutes of the [n+1]th [hour]"): двадцать минут двенадцатого ("twenty [minutes] of
the twelfth" = twenty past eleven). The half hour uses пол- prefixed to the genitive ordinal:
полшестого / половина шестого ("half of the sixth" = half past five). Minutes to the hour use без
+ genitive: без десяти пять ("[minus] ten, five" = ten to five), без четверти три (a quarter to
three, четверть = quarter). For a.m./p.m., ночи and утра disambiguate the early hours, дня and
вечера the later ones: десять часов утра (10 a.m.), семь часов вечера (7 p.m.). Полдень (midday)
and полночь (midnight) are also given. To answer "at what time," в is used except when the answer
itself involves без (which needs no further preposition): В три часа, В половине шестого, Без
четверти три. Official/timetable contexts use the 24-hour clock (pp.103–104).

### 5. Через + accusative (across, after [time elapsed])
Через + accusative expresses "after [an amount of time] has elapsed": Поезд отходит через сорок
минут ("The train leaves in forty minutes"). Also used for counting stops: Через три остановки
("After three stops") (p.105).

### 6. Two verb pairs for "to go," by habituality
Russian motion verbs distinguish habitual/repeated motion from a single occasion/one direction, and
separately, motion on foot from motion by transport: ходить (habitual, on foot) vs. идти (single
occasion, on foot); ездить (habitual, by transport) vs. ехать (single occasion, by transport) — for
example, Она всегда ходит в город ("she always walks into town," habitual) vs. Сейчас она идёт в
город ("she is walking into town now," single occasion). This habitual/single-occasion distinction
also governs which infinitive is used in a given context, not just the present tense. A further
subtlety: certain vehicles themselves (e.g. trains, trams) are described using the ходить/идти
(on-foot) pair rather than ездить/ехать, e.g. Поезда ходят быстро и часто ("The trains run quickly
and frequently") (pp.105–106).

### 7. Other paired motion verbs
The same habitual-vs-one-direction principle (and each verb has its own present-tense paradigm,
several irregular) extends to: бегать/бежать (to run), водить/вести (to lead), возить/везти (to
transport), летать/лететь (to fly), носить/нести (to carry), плавать/плыть (to swim, sail) (p.106).

### 8. Счастливого пути! (Have a good journey!) — genitive-of-wish
Well-wishing expressions like this use the genitive case because желать ("to wish") always governs
a genitive object, even when желаю itself is omitted and only understood: (Я желаю вам) приятного
аппетита! ("Enjoy your meal!"), (Я желаю вам) всего хорошего! ("All the best!") (p.106).
