## Grammar points

### Slang synonyms given inline alongside neutral drug terminology
Unlike prior chapters where slang appeared in a separately headed box, this chapter's core Часть I
vocabulary list itself parenthetically supplies the slang synonym next to the neutral/clinical term
— марихуана (sl. травка, дурь), гашиш (sl. анаша), героин (sl. гера), морфий (sl. марфа). This is a
denser register-annotation practice than anywhere else in the book so far and directly supports
mechanics analysis of drug-slang formation patterns: гера and марфа both look like
truncation/nicknaming of the neutral term (героин→гера; the морфий→марфа link is less transparent,
possibly influenced by the female name Марфа as a euphemistic personification, worth flagging as a
question for later mechanics work rather than asserting an etymology here).

### кайф as a semantically broadened slang root
кайф/кайфовать/ловить кайф form a small word family built on a single Turkic/Arabic-origin loan
root. The example sentence "он кайфовал от выпитого" (p.92) shows the word applied to alcohol
intoxication even though it's introduced in the drugs subsection — evidence the term functions as
general slang for "a state of euphoria/high" rather than being drug-specific, useful for later
cross-register slang-mechanics work on borrowed intoxication vocabulary.

### сообразить на троих as culturally load-bearing idiom
This idiom (p.91) doesn't just mean "to split a bottle" — the source's own explanation describes an
entire informal social protocol (finding two more people, splitting cost evenly, presumably then
drinking together) compressed into one idiomatic verb phrase. This is a good example of how a
single collocation can encode a whole culturally specific practice, relevant for later synthesis
work on economically-motivated slang formation.
