## Grammar points

### Register tags embedded directly in vocabulary boxes
As in earlier chapters, this textbook marks register inline: классный is explicitly `(coll.)`,
быть в отпаде is explicitly `(sl.)`. This is the third chapter running where the authors annotate
register at the point of introducing a word rather than relegating it to a separate glossary —
a consistent authorial practice worth noting for how this source's register data should be trusted
(high — it's the authors' own explicit judgment, not an inference).

### Explicit attested-era note for a slang term
крутой is unusually well-documented for a textbook vocabulary item: the source states outright that
it "became very fashionable in the early 1990s" (p.72). This licenses an `Attested Era` value of
"early 1990s (source-attested)" rather than the usual coarse whole-book "contemporary (source
published 1996)" fallback — a rare case in this book of item-level (not just book-level) era data.

### Two parallel compounding strategies for elevated/institutional vocabulary
This chapter's Word Study juxtaposes two related but distinct compounding mechanisms: (1) two full
roots joined by a linking vowel (всемирно, высококлассный), and (2) a truncated first root fused to
a second (спецрейс, профсоюз, echoing медпункт/детдом/главврач from Урок 1). Both produce
single-word compounds from multi-word phrases, but only the second involves phonological truncation
— a distinction worth preserving for later slang-mechanics work on Russian clipping/compounding as a
word-formation resource.

### Systematic instrument → performer agentive derivation
The vocabulary list's instrument/performer pairs (гитара→гитарист, барабан→ударник [irregular —
not *барабанщик in this list, though барабанщик is the more common word], саксофон→саксофонист)
illustrate the productive -ист agentive suffix, with one apparent irregular/suppletive substitution
(ударник for the drum-performer rather than a барабан-based form) worth flagging for later
morphology work.
