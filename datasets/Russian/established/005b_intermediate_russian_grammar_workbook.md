**Morpheme breakdown.**

> **краснеть** = красн- (root of красный, "red") + -еть (deadjectival verbalizing suffix, "to
> become more X") — "to grow red/to blush". A fully productive Russian pattern (any color/quality
> adjective + -еть) worth tracking as a candidate mechanism for a derived slang lexicon: it turns a
> descriptive adjective into an inchoative verb without any semantic bleaching.

> **безрукавка**-style compound nouns are not directly attested in this book's units 10–18, but the
> book's own worked example is instructive: **краснокирпичный** = красн- (short neuter form of
> красный, "red") + -о- (compounding linking vowel) + кирпич- (root of кирпич, "brick") + -ный
> (adjectival suffix) — "red-brick(-colored)". Illustrates the general Russian compound-adjective
> rule: only the final adjectival ending declines, the first component freezes in its neuter short
> form.

> **различный** = раз- (rас- before a voiceless consonant; distributive/divergence prefix, cognate
> with English "dis-"/"di-") + личн- (root related to лицо/"face", "person", "aspect") + -ый
> (adjectival suffix) — "different, various". The same раз-/рас- prefix underlies разговор
> ("conversation", literally "speaking in different directions") and рассказ ("short story",
> "telling that moves in different directions"), a productive distributive-sense prefix family
> worth flagging for later Russian slang-mechanics work on prefixal semantics.

## Grammar points

### Lexis: владеть; adverbs of cause (Unit 10)

Владеть governs the instrumental case and covers ownership, linguistic/skill mastery, and control
of territory; владелец/совладелец (with the property owned in the genitive) name the owner. Three
prepositions mark cause: по (+ dat., "for the reason"), за (+ acc., the action that elicited a
response), из (+ gen., the emotion prompting a course of action) (p. 102–104).

### Aspect in biographical narrative; past passive participle (Unit 10)

In biographical texts, present tense covers current states, defining characteristics, and — in a
note-form register — past-held positions (conferring an "official communiqué" authority); past
perfective marks career turning points and continuous-but-bounded actions. Four equivalent ways
exist to express an externally imposed appointment: active-with-subject, active-without-subject,
passive-with-named-agent (instrumental), passive-without-agent — biographical registers favor the
passive, often omitting "to be". The past passive participle (short form) is formed by removing -ть
and adding -н/-на/-но/-ны (from -ать/-ять verbs), -ен/-ена/-ено/-ены (from -ить/-еть verbs), or
-ен-series (from -ти verbs), with consonant mutations for stems ending in б/в/п/м/ф (+л-), д (→жд),
з (→ж), с (→ш), ст (→щ), т (→ч or щ); a small set take -т- instead of -н- (занят) (p. 105–109).

### Alphabetisms (Unit 10)

Organizational-name abbreviations are either spelled letter-by-letter (СССР, generally
indeclinable) or pronounced as words (ОМОН, often declinable) (p. 112).

### Approximation, adverbs of time and place in news reporting (Unit 11)

Written/official registers use the 24-hour clock with cardinal numerals; spoken registers use a
hour/half-hour system (в три минуты шестого = "5:03", в половине шестого = "5:30", без пяти шесть =
"5:55"). Approximation is expressed with примерно, около (+ gen.), or by inverting the normal
preposition-numeral word order (часов в пять = "at about five"). News items conventionally open
with "where and when" before the main clause verb (inverted word order), then chain forward
sentence by sentence (p. 116–121).

### Punctuation of subordination; из-за vs. от (Unit 11)

All subordinate clauses are comma-separated from the main clause; an embedded subordinate clause is
bracketed by commas on both sides. Из-за (+ gen.) marks a negatively impactful cause; от (+ gen.)
marks the source/origin of a state, often of an attitude or health condition (p. 121–123).

### Official-visit narrative register; половина; adverbs of time (Unit 12)

Official announcements (e.g. of a state visit) often make no attempt to build reader rapport,
unlike other narrative registers covered earlier. Половина declines as a feminine noun and governs
genitive singular (uncountable referent) or genitive plural (countable referent); whole-number-plus-
half uses с (+ inst.): два с половиной километра. Verbs in the present tense with future reference
(Нетаньяху прибывает...) open such announcements, with the itinerary detailed in future perfective
verbs (p. 125–131).

### Adjectives: long/short form, hard/soft, compound (Unit 13)

Long-form adjectives are used attributively (and predicatively); short-form adjectives (only four
forms: m./f./n./pl., nominative only) are used predicatively only, and can carry a distinct meaning
from the long form. Adjectives in -ский/-ской and -ний/-шний lack a short form. A linking vowel
(е/о) is often inserted in short masculine forms (красный → красен). Hard adjectives end -ый/-ой;
soft adjectives (~30 total, mostly time/place reference) end in -ий. Compound adjectives combine
two adjectival stems, the first frozen in neuter short form, only the final ending declining
(p. 133–139).

### Deadjectival verbs in -еть; superlatives (Unit 13)

Adding -еть to an adjectival root forms an inchoative verb ("to become more X"): красный →
краснеть. Russian superlatives come in a compound form (самый + adjective, fully declining both
parts) and a simple form (limited set of adjectives, suffix -ейший/-айший with г→ж/к→ч/х→ш
consonant mutation before -айший), often expressing an extreme degree with no explicit comparison.
The prefix наи- intensifies simple superlatives (наичистейший); a fixed set of наи-superlatives
(наибольший, наилучший, наихудший, наивысший, наименьший) exist alongside the наиболее/наименее +
adjective newspaper-register alternative (p. 138–143).

### Short-form neuter adjective/adverb as impersonal predicate; бывать (Unit 13)

Adverbs ending in -о (identical in form to neuter short adjectives) serve as predicates in
impersonal sentences with быть/становиться, with the experiencer (if named) in the dative (Мне
жарко); было/будет marks past/future regardless of person/number. Бывать carries a frequentative/
repetitive sense of "to be" (Мне бывает грустно). The impersonal reflexive construction (verb + -ся,
3rd p. sg. or neuter past, experiencer in dative) expresses an action independent of the
experiencer's will (Мне не спалось) (p. 143–145).

### Linking parts of speech; reflexive/reciprocal pronouns (Unit 14)

И...,и... expresses "both...and..."; не...,а... expresses "not...but..."; одни...,(а) другие...
expresses "some...while others..." (extendable третьи, четвёртые...). Себя (reflexive, no
nominative) always refers back to the verb's agent, invariant across gender/number. Свой (reflexive
possessive) is obligatory with a 3rd-person subject to distinguish "his own X" from "someone else's
X" (его/её/их do not refer back to the subject), near-obligatory with 2nd person singular, and
optional-but-common with 1st person and 2nd person plural; свой also appears in the nominative in
possession/dative constructions referring to the semantic subject (У меня своя квартира) and in set
phrases (Все здесь свои). Сам/сама/само/сами emphasizes the person or thing itself. Друг друга
("each other") declines only in its second element, with prepositions inserted between the two
elements (друг с другом) (p. 148–156).

### 'Any' (любой); 'the same' (Unit 14)

Любой ("any at all") declines fully as an adjective. "The same" has several constructions: тот же
(+ particle же), одно и то же, тот же самый, такой же (+ как for "the same as"), or bare одно for
"the same" (Мы учились в одном институте) (p. 154–156).

### Compound nouns; imperfective gerund (Unit 15)

Hyphenated compound nouns usually decline both elements (старухами-продавщицами), except when the
first element is a recent loanword with non-Russian morphology (пресс-служба, ди-джеями), names a
rank (генерал-губернатора), or is purely descriptive/qualifying the second element (хаус-музыке,
гей-параде) — in these cases the first element stays undeclined. The imperfective gerund substitutes
for an adverbial clause of simultaneity or an independent "and"-clause, always referring back to the
sentence's principal subject and invariant in form regardless of tense/number/gender; formed by
replacing the 3rd p. pl. present ending with -я (or -а after ж/ч/ш/щ); verbs in -вать preceded by
да-/зна-/ста- form the gerund from the infinitive; reflexive verbs take -сь; some common verbs
(ждать, мочь, писать, петь, пить) have no imperfective gerund form (p. 158–165).

### Wearing vs. wears (Unit 15)

Она в чёрной юбке / На ней чёрная юбка both express momentary "she is wearing"; носить or ходить в
(+ prep.) express habitual "she wears" (p. 168).

### Aspect in foregrounding/backgrounding; irregular plurals; perfective gerund (Unit 16)

Perfective verbs move a narrative's events forward in time (foregrounded, dynamic); imperfective
verbs describe repeated/habitual events or background states co-occurring with the foregrounded
action (backgrounded, descriptive). Солдат and человек/раз have a genitive plural identical to
nominative singular; a set of masculine nouns (поезд, город, дом, глаз, вечер, берег, остров,
номер, доктор, профессор) take a stressed -а/-я nominative plural. The comparative degree of many
adverbs equals the short-form comparative of the corresponding adjective, subject to the same
consonant mutations (часто→чаще); много/мало have anomalous comparatives (больше/меньше); "X times
more/less" uses в + numeral + раз. Чем..., тем... renders "the more..., the more...". The perfective
gerund (from perfective verbs, denoting an action completed prior to the main verb) is formed by
replacing masculine past-tense -л with -в (прочитав); reflexive verbs replace -лся with -вшись;
prefixed perfective motion verbs add -я to the 1st p. sg. stem (придя); verbs whose masculine past
doesn't end in -л add -ши (спасши) (p. 169–176).

### Negative pronouns and adverbs (Unit 17)

Russian has two paradigms for "nothing"/"no one" and their adverbial counterparts: нечего/некого
(used with an infinitive, experiencer in dative, было/будет for past/future) versus ничто
(ничего)/никто (used with a negated finite verb); stress falls on the first syllable of the
нечего/некого set and the final syllable of the ничего/никто set. The same не-/ни- split applies to
negative adverbs: негде/нигде, некогда/никогда, некуда/никуда. Where a preposition is present, it
splits the negative pronoun (Не с кем было...) (p. 178–185).

### Word building: раз-/рас- prefix (Unit 17)

The prefix раз- (рас- before voiceless consonants) marks separation/divergence, corresponding
loosely to English "di-/dis-": разный ("different"), разнообразный ("varied"), развлекательный
("entertaining", from "drawing in different directions"), разговор ("conversation"), рассказ
("short story") (p. 184).

### Declension and agreement of numerals (Unit 17)

Distinct declension classes: один (declines like a pronoun, agrees in number/gender); два/две (m./n.
vs. f., special genitive двух); три/четыре (alike except instrumental тремя/четырьмя); 5–20 and 30
(decline like a feminine noun in a soft sign); 40/90/100 (end in -а in all oblique cases); 50/60/70/80
and 200/300/400/500–900 (both components decline); тысяча (feminine noun); миллион/миллиард
(masculine nouns) — the last three always take genitive plural nouns in all cases, unlike "proper"
numerals which agree in case with the noun. Оne/два/три/четыре (and their compounds) distinguish
animate accusative (=genitive) from inanimate accusative (=nominative); от 5 onward, nominative
numerals take genitive plural nouns (p. 185–191).

### Participles: present/past active, and as nouns/adjectives (Unit 18)

The present active participle (imperfective verbs only) replaces a который-clause in the present:
remove 3rd p. pl. -т, add -щий/-щая/-щее/-щие (agreeing with its antecedent in gender/number/case,
usually determined by its position after the noun, with obligatory comma bracketing). The past
active participle (from imperfective or perfective verbs) replaces a который-clause referring to
the past: remove masculine past-tense -л, add -вший (or -ший if the masculine past doesn't end in
-л, e.g. шёл → шедший, вёл → ведший). Both participle types keep -ся unconditionally for reflexive
verbs. Some present active participles have lexicalized as nouns (курящий, непьющая, трудящийся) or
as adjectives with broadened meaning (текущий "current" vs. its literal participial sense "flowing")
(p. 193–199).
