## Grammar points

### Conjunctions: two main types (p.189, carried from Part 14's preview)
Conjunctions are invariable linking words, splitting into **Coordinating** (joining equal
elements) and **Subordinating** (joining a dependent clause to a main clause).

### Coordinating conjunctions: connective, adversative, disjunctive (pp.189-191)
**Connective**: и ("and" — Мне надо купить ручку и блокнот); и...и ("both...and" — Мне надо купить
и ручку, и блокнот); ни...ни ("neither...nor," itself part of the Compound Negative system from
Part 11 — Он не приехал ни вчера, ни сегодня); а used connectively at the very start of a
sentence/turn (А куда вы сейчас идёте?, roughly "so where are you headed now?") — **note this is
the same surface word а as the adversative conjunction below, but the source treats it as a
functionally distinct connective use** when sentence-initial, not a contrastive one.

**Adversative** (а vs. но — flagged explicitly as a persistent point of confusion for foreign
learners): **а** marks the **opposition between two distinct objects/actions/features/states/
facts** placed side by side (Он спит, а я работаю, "he's sleeping while/but I'm working"; У него
есть велосипед, а у меня нет, "he has a bike but I don't" — two separate facts about two
different people, juxtaposed). **но** instead introduces a clause that **contradicts what was
just said, known, or planned**, or adds a qualifying/clarifying twist to it (У меня есть билеты
на хоккей, но я не хочу идти, "I have tickets but I don't want to go" — the "but" clause overturns
an expectation raised by the first clause, not just contrasts two parallel facts; Я приеду, но не
сегодня; Он не приехал сегодня, но обещал приехать завтра). The functional distinction the source
draws is: **а = parallel contrast between two things**, **но = contradiction/reversal of an
expectation** — a genuine semantic split underlying what looks like two interchangeable
translations of English "but."

**Disjunctive**: или ("or" — Мне надо купить билеты на субботу или на воскресенье);
или..., или ("either...or," a more explicit correlative version of the same sentence).

### Subordinating conjunctions (the section this source cuts off partway through) (pp.191-192)
что ("that," introducing reported speech — Он сказал, что приедет завтра); **чтобы**, which
notably governs its verb in the **Past-Tense form regardless of actual time reference** — a
grammatical fact worth flagging since it means "Past tense" here doesn't encode real past time at
all, only the grammatical mood chtoby requires (Он сказал, чтобы я приехал завтра, "he told me to
come tomorrow" — приехал is morphologically Past but refers to a future action); если ("if,"
ordinary conditional — Скажите мне, если он приедет); потому что/потому, что ("because," with
either comma placement accepted); **если бы**, the counterfactual conditional, which — like
чтобы — also requires its verb in the Past-Tense form, here paired with a following бы in the main
clause (Если бы он приехал вовремя, то мы бы успели, "if he had arrived on time we would have made
it" — a genuine counterfactual/subjunctive-equivalent construction, distinct from plain если);
хотя ("although" — Мы пошли в дискотеку, хотя очень устали); как будто ("as if" — Он посмотрел на
меня, как будто мы никогда не встречались); как ("as, like" — Он делает всё, как я); and **чем**
("than"), which the source's own example (Он работает больше, чем его начальник) directly
reprises the чем + Nominative comparative-construction alternative to the Genitive-of-comparison
pattern already covered in Part 10.
