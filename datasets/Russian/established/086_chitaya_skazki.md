## Grammar points

### Fixed narrative frame and formulaic language of the Russian folk tale (pp. 11-12)

The source's own "О некоторых особенностях русской народной сказки" note (paraphrased): a folk
tale's plot sits inside a fixed frame — a stock opening (жили-были...) and a stock closing (either
the "мёд-пиво пил" formula or the shorter "стали они жить-поживать" epilogue), both marking
resolution and the triumph of good over evil. Sentence structure favors inversion (стали они репку
из земли тянуть) and postposed modifiers (дочери мои милые — modifier after the noun). Narrative
tense is distinctive: the future tense is used to mark a habitual/repeated past action (Подойдёт,
посмотрит и опять отойдёт — "He'd approach, look, and step away again"). Constant epithets (серый
волк, добрый молодец, чистое поле, дремучий лес, горючие слёзы) and a set of fixed idiomatic
phrases (долго ли, коротко ли; ни в сказке сказать, ни пером описать; за тридевять земель; откуда
ни возьмись; куда глаза глядят) recur, along with folk proverbs (двум смертям не бывать...; утро
вечера мудренее). Emotional register leans on diminutive suffixes (аленький цветочек, беленький
козлик, сестрица, братец). By content, tales split into magical (волшебные — with a clear
positive/negative character split: Иван as the positive male lead, Елена Прекрасная/Василиса
Премудрая combining beauty+wisdom+virtue as positive female leads, vs. malevolent
magic-wielders like Змей Горыныч, Кащей Бессмертный, Баба-Яга), everyday/genre (бытовые — with a
recurring clever-soldier cycle), and animal tales (о животных — a fixed cast drawn from Russian
forest fauna: лиса "cunning", волк "greedy/foolish", медведь "simple-minded", заяц "cowardly").

### Short-form adjectives (p. 14)

Paraphrased: short forms exist only for qualitative adjectives, formed by dropping the full-form
ending (красивый → красив). They function as the predicate, agreeing with the subject in gender and
number (Иван здоров; Мария здорова; Дети здоровы). In past/future tense, the copula быть is added,
inflecting for gender/number in the past (был здоров, была здорова, были здоровы) and for number
only in the future (будет здоров/здорова; будут здоровы).

### Numerals: cardinal, collective, and adverbs of accompaniment (p. 17)

Paraphrased: cardinal numerals (один, два, три, четыре...) denote quantities of like objects; один
and два alone show gender agreement (один/одна/одно; два/две), and один additionally has a plural
form (одни). Cardinals decline by case and govern the noun differently depending on the numeral:
один agrees fully (gender/number/case); the rest govern all cases except nominative/accusative, and
in the nominative/accusative themselves the counted noun goes genitive singular after
два/три/четыре (два стола) or genitive plural after five and up (пять столов).

Collective numerals (двое, трое...) denote a group of persons/objects as a single unit; they
likewise decline and, outside nominative/accusative, agree with the noun in all cases (о трёх
студентах, троим студентам); in the nominative/accusative the counted noun is genitive plural
(трое студентов, троих студентов). The source lists six required-use contexts for collectives: (1)
with nouns denoting male persons (двое учеников), (2) with дети/ребята/люди (четверо ребят, трое
детей), (3) with personal pronouns in the plural (нас двое), (4) as substantivized numerals (трое в
масках), (5) with plural-only nouns (двое суток), (6) with animal-young names (трое котят).

Adverbs of accompaniment (вдвоём, вчетвером) derive from the collective numerals (двое, четверо),
answer "as what group?" (как?), and — like all adverbs — never inflect.

### Possessive pronouns and свой (p. 22)

Paraphrased: possessives мой, твой, его, её, наш, ваш, их mark possession by one of the three
grammatical persons; свой can mark possession by any of the three. Мой, твой, наш, ваш, свой
decline like adjectives (gender/number/case); его, её, их never inflect. Свой marks possession by
the clause's own agent and (in a simple clause) never itself functions as an attribute of the
subject; in a complex sentence each clause is evaluated separately. Мой/твой/наш/ваш and свой are
often interchangeable — except when the action's agent is 3rd person, where свой becomes obligatory
to mark reflexive possession: Моя сестра взяла свою книгу (never "её книгу" in that reading, since
"её" would then read as someone else's book).

### Verb, adjective, and noun case government — a full rection inventory (pp. 29-31)

The source's own paraphrased framing: below are lists of verbs, adjectives, and nouns governing
each oblique case, with italicized prepositions marking prepositional government where relevant. See
the Vocabulary table above for the full genitive/dative/accusative/instrumental/prepositional
lists — this is the single densest lexical-syntactic inventory in this chunk of the book. Three
further points refine the rule: (1) variable rection exists for some verbs depending on the
definiteness/concreteness of the object (ждать возможности/писем [indefinite/abstract, genitive] vs.
ждать Лену/сестру [definite/animate, accusative]); (2) negated transitive verbs of perception,
thought, or desire (and иметь/получать/доставать-type verbs) take genitive instead of accusative
(не чувствовать запаха, не знать России, не понимать намёков; не достать билета); (3) a partitive
genitive appears when the object is affected only in part (выпить воды, купить хлеба); (4) some verbs
show double government (прощать кому? что?).
