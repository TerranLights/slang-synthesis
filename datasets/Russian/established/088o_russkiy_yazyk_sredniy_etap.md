## What's kept as genuinely non-redundant

1. **Explicit two-register contrast structure.** Unlike a flat formula list, this chapter
   consistently splits each speech act into a formal/neutral list *and* a separately headed
   "Дружески непринуждённый..." (friendly/informal) subsection for greetings specifically (p. 225):

   | Register | Greeting | "How are you?" | Reply |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Formal/neutral | Здравствуйте! / Добрый день! | Как поживаете? / Как у вас дела? | Спасибо, хорошо. / Неважно. |
   | Informal/friendly | Привет! / Какая [приятная] встреча! | Как жизнь? (Как живёшь?) / Как дела? | Отлично. / Нормально. / **Так себе.** / Плохо. |

   **Так себе** ("so-so," a dismissive-neutral self-assessment) is the one lexical item here not
   confirmed already tabulated from `063` or elsewhere in this corpus — a genuinely colloquial,
   mildly deflecting reply-formula worth flagging for the register-scale inventory. The informal
   "Что нового? / Всё по-старому. / Ничего особенного. / Много чего, сразу не расскажешь" exchange
   (p. 228) is likewise a colloquial small-talk formula not confirmed present elsewhere, the last
   line ("there's a lot, I couldn't tell it all at once") being a naturalistic hedge/deflection
   formula worth flagging for discourse-particle/hedging inventory.

2. **Address-to-strangers-by-apparent-age/gender system** (pp. 226–227, "Формы обращения к
   незнакомым") — a clean enumeration of how Russian addresses a stranger whose name is unknown,
   calibrated by apparent age/social role rather than name: **"Молодой человек!"** (to an apparently
   young man), **"Девушка!"** (to an apparently young woman, regardless of actual marital status —
   a well-known sociolinguistic quirk of Russian address), **"(Уважаемые) дамы и господа!"** /
   **"Товарищ(и)!"** for groups, plus the polite-request openers "Простите, вы не скажете...?" /
   "Будьте добры, скажите/дайте...". This age/role-based address-to-strangers system (rather than
   name-based) is a genuine sociolinguistic mechanism worth flagging for the register/politeness-
   mechanics inventory, even though the individual formulas overlap `063`'s content.

3. **"Formы обращения к знакомым"** (p. 227) explicitly lays out the full formality gradient in one
   place: Господин/Госпожа + surname → Товарищ + surname (Soviet-era-flavored, still used
   institutionally) → имя+отчество (name+patronymic, the standard adult-to-adult respectful form) →
   bare given name/hypocoristic (Коля, Нина) for intimates — a clean four-rung formality ladder
   worth cross-referencing against `041`'s five-tier address-form scale and `063`'s own register
   template.
