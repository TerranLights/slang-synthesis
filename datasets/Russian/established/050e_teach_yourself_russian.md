## Grammar points

### 1. В + accusative for "to play (at)" games/sports
A further use of в + accusative: играть в футбол ("to play football"), играть в шахматы ("to play
chess"). If the object is a musical instrument instead, на + prepositional is required instead:
играть на флейте ("to play the flute"), играть на гитаре ("to play the guitar") (p.65).

### 2. Duration expressed with bare present tense
To express an action that has been ongoing up to the moment of speaking ("we have been looking for
three hours"), Russian simply uses the present tense with no preposition or special marker
corresponding to English "for": Мы уже три часа смотрим... (p.65).

### 3. Спасибо за + accusative
"Thank you for..." is expressed with спасибо за + the accusative case: Спасибо за журнал ("thank
you for the magazine"), Спасибо за книгу ("thank you for the book") (p.65).

### 4. The dative case
Principal meaning is "to" or "for" — expresses the indirect object: Оля даёт билеты Виктору ("Olya
gives the tickets to Viktor," where Viktor is put into the dative). English often omits the "to"
overtly ("Olya gives Viktor the tickets") but Russian still requires the dative (pp.65–66).

### 5. Dative singular formation
Table of remove/add endings by gender, largely paralleling the genitive singular's alternation
pattern but with -у/-ю (masc./neut.) or -е/-и (fem.) instead: брат→брату, учитель→учителю,
Алексей→Алексею; собака→собаке, Оля→Оле, Мария→Марии, дверь→двери; окно→окну, море→морю,
здание→зданию (p.67).

### 6. Dative of personal pronouns
мне (я), тебе (ты), ему (он/оно), ей (она), нам (мы), вам (вы), им (они) (p.67).

### 7. Further uses of the dative
- With prepositions к ("towards, to [someone's place]") and по ("along/down," "around/throughout,"
  or "on" for broadcast media): к нам, к Ире, по улице, по городу, по телевизору.
- With можно/нельзя/надо/пора + infinitive, where the person is expressed in the dative: Володе
  пора идти ("it's time for Volodya to go").
- Idiomatically with нравиться ("to like": dative of the person + nominative of the liked thing,
  e.g. Анне нравится этот музей — "Anna likes this museum") and хочется ("to want/feel like":
  dative of the person, e.g. Володе хочется отдыхать — "Volodya wants to rest") (pp.66–67).

### 8. Этот (this)
A demonstrative pronoun with three singular forms (этот masc., эта fem., это neut.) and one plural
(эти), agreeing with the noun it modifies: этот университет, эта гостиница, это здание, эти
театры. Note this neuter это is formally identical to (but grammatically distinct from) the
invariant particle это meaning "it is/this is/these are" (met since Unit 1) (p.68).
