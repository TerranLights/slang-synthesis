## Grammar points

### Cardinal vs. Ordinal, plus three smaller numeral classes (p.167)
Russian numerals split into two main groups — **Cardinals** (один, два...) and **Ordinals**
(первый, второй...) — plus three smaller classes: **Double numerals** (оба/обе), **Indefinite
numerals** (много, сколько), and **Collective numerals** (двое, трое). A full reference list of
Cardinals from 0 to one trillion is given (ноль/нуль through сто, двести...девятьсот, тысяча,
миллион, миллиард/биллион, триллион), and compound cardinals are built by simple concatenation
(175 = сто + семьдесят + пять).

### Cardinals govern the Genitive, and change case themselves when governed (p.168)
Cardinals from 2 up govern the noun that follows them into the **Genitive** (per Part 6's
Genitive-of-quantity rules), but the cardinal itself also declines when the whole numeral phrase
is governed by a preposition, a verb, or a negative construction.

### один: full gender/case paradigm plus three extra meanings (pp.168-169)
один has three gender forms (один/одна/одно) plus a genuine plural form **одни**, used
specifically with plural-only nouns (одни очки, "a pair of glasses" — reprising Part 2's
одни/один). Beyond its numeral sense, один/одна/одно/одни can mean "some unspecified" (Я читал
это в одном журнале, "I read it in some magazine"), "the same" (Мы живём в одном доме, "we live
in the same building"), or "alone" (Я еду туда один, "I'm going there alone") — three genuinely
distinct semantic extensions layered onto the same numeral form. Full six-case declension table
given (Gen. одного/одной/одних, Dat. одному/одной/одним, Instr. одним/одной/одними, Prep.
одном/одной/одних), with Accusative governed by animacy exactly as for adjectives.

### два/две: the only cardinal keeping a Nominative gender split (pp.169-170)
два (Masculine/Neuter) vs. две (Feminine) is the **only** gender distinction that survives among
Russian cardinal numerals — and only in the Nominative; every oblique case (Gen. двух, Dat. двум,
Instr. двумя, Prep. двух) is shared regardless of gender. Три and четыре follow this same
oblique-case pattern (трёх/трём/тремя/трёх; четырёх/четырём/четырьмя/четырёх).

### Numeral declension groups 5-20/30/50-80, and the irregularly-reduced 40/90/100 and 200-900 (pp.170-171)
5 through 20, 30, and 50-80 decline on a soft-sign-noun-like pattern modeled on пять (Gen.=Dat.=
Prep. пяти, Instr. пятью, Acc.=Nom.). **сорок ("40"), девяносто ("90"), and сто ("100")** are
flagged as reduced to just **two** working case forms rather than the full six: Gen.=Dat.=Instr.=
Prep. (сорока/девяноста/ста — also used after более/менее/около) and Acc.=Nom. (unchanged). The
**hundreds 200-900** follow the same two-form reduction (Gen. двухсот/трёхсот/.../девятисот;
Acc.=Nom. двести/триста/etc.). тысяча itself declines as an ordinary noun on the книга-type
pattern from Part 3.

### Compound cardinals: every part declines independently (p.171)
Unlike compound ordinals (see below), **every element of a compound cardinal number changes case
independently** when the whole numeral is governed (Nom. сто + семьдесят + пять → Gen. ста +
семидесяти + пяти) — the source's own worked contrast with the ordinal system is the specific
point being made here.

### Telling time colloquially with cardinals (the second half-hour) (p.171)
The second half of an hour is expressed with cardinal numbers for both the hour and the minutes,
counting down from the next hour via без ("without/minus") + Genitive: без двадцати пяти пять
(4:35), без двадцати пять (4:40), без пятнадцати пять (4:45), без десяти пять (4:50), без пяти
пять (4:55) — the hour itself (пять) stays Nominative while the minute-count takes the Genitive.

### "Halves": полтора/полторы and the пол- prefix (p.172)
половина ("half") reduces to the prefix **пол-** compounded directly with a Genitive noun:
полчаса ("half an hour"), поллитра ("half a liter"), полуфинал ("semifinal," with a linking -у-).
"One and a half" has its own dedicated, gender-agreeing numeral: **полтора** (Masculine/Neuter)
vs. **полторы** (Feminine) — полтора часа, полторы тонны — both governing the Genitive Singular
exactly like the fraction words from Part 6 (Gen. около полутора часов/тонн). "Two and a half,"
"three and a half," etc. use the regular construction **[cardinal] с половиной** + Genitive (два с
половиной метра).

### Numeral + noun combinations: Genitive Plural required in every case but the Nominative (p.172)
When a cardinal numeral combines with a noun, the noun's case in the Nominative phrase follows the
ordinary Genitive-of-quantity rules from Part 6 (два часа — Gen. Sg. after 2/3/4; пять часов —
Gen. Pl. after 5+) — but **in every other case, the noun must be in the Genitive Plural regardless
of which numeral is used** (Nom. два часа → Gen. около двух часов; Nom. пять часов → Gen. около
пяти часов, same Genitive Plural form for both). When a characterizing word (adjective, etc.) is
also present in a numeral phrase, **it too is always Plural** (два новых журнала, "two new
magazines" — новых is Genitive Plural despite журнала itself being Genitive Singular per the
2/3/4 rule; пять новых журналов, both Plural).

### Ordinal numerals: adjectival declension, with третий as the sole exception (pp.173-174)
Ordinals decline exactly like hard-type adjectives (first through highest given in a full
reference table, первый through миллионный) — **except третий ("third")**, which the source
explicitly flags as declining irregularly (on the soft -ний-type pattern from Part 3, not the
regular hard-adjective pattern every other ordinal follows). Ordinals function either as ordinary
characterizing words (первый этаж) or as a predicate complement to быть (Он всегда первый, "He's
always first"). In **compound ordinals, only the final element is itself ordinal and declines**
— everything before it stays in its cardinal form unchanged (двадцать первый этаж, "the 21st
floor" → на двадцать первом этаже, "on the 21st floor," where двадцать stays fixed and первом
alone takes the case ending) — the source's explicit point of contrast with compound cardinals,
where every element changes.

### Uses of the ordinals (pp.174-176)
Ordinals denote the number of: an apartment, hotel room, hospital/ward, building block, floor,
flight, train, carriage, compartment, row, seat, street, congress, page, chapter, TV/radio
channel, trolleybus/bus/tram line, size, school, class. Both cardinals and ordinals can number
pages/chapters interchangeably (страница пять / пятая страница / страница пятая, all valid for
"page five"). **Cardinals substitute for ordinals in official/formal number-reading** — the
source's contrastive example: flight №707 is "семьсот седьмой рейс" (colloquial, using the
ordinal семьсот седьмой) versus "рейс номер семьсот семь" (official, reading the digits as plain
cardinals). **Dates** use the **Neuter** ordinal alone when stating the date itself (Какое
сегодня число? — Десятое января, "the tenth of January") but the **Genitive** of the ordinal
(plus a Genitive noun for the month) when answering "when" (Я вернусь второго мая, "I'll be back
on the second of May"; full-date example: Он родился второго мая тысяча девятьсот семьдесят
пятого года, "He was born on May 2, 1975" — note every element of the compound year-ordinal
takes the Genitive here too). **Telling time (unofficial way): the first half of an hour uses
ordinals** (contrasting with cardinals for the second half, covered in Part 13's earlier
section) — начало шестого ("just after five," lit. "the beginning of the sixth [hour]"), пять
минут пятого (4:05), десять минут пятого (4:10), continuing through двадцать пять минут пятого
(4:25) and половина/полпятого (4:30) up to тридцать пять минут пятого (4:35 — note this
particular reading straddles into cardinal territory per the earlier without-preposition pattern,
flagged by the source as the boundary case between the two half-hour systems). Ordinals also
denote **historical periods** (в девятнадцатом веке, "in the 19th century") and decades, with two
acceptable forms for the latter (в тридцатые годы / в тридцатых годах, "in the thirties").

### Numeral-based nouns: anniversary compounds and evaluation/grade nouns (pp.176-177)
**Neuter** nouns built on a numeral root + **-летие** denote anniversaries/periods: десятилетие
("decade/10th anniversary"), пятидесятилетие ("50th anniversary"), столетие ("centennial"),
двухсотлетие ("bicentennial"), тысячелетие ("millennium"). **Feminine** nouns built on numeral
roots serve as **evaluation/grade nouns**, used in Russian school grading and in card games:
единица (1) through десятка (10), with пятёрка ("5," excellent) singled out as the top standard
school grade (Он получил пятёрку — "he got an excellent mark"). Separate **counting-unit** nouns:
десяток ("a ten," e.g. десяток яиц, "ten eggs"), сотня ("a hundred"), двойня/тройня ("twins" /
"triplets").

### Double numerals: оба (mixed/all-Masculine) vs. обе (all-Feminine) (pp.176-177)
оба/обе both mean "both," referring to exactly two people/animals/objects, but split by gender:
**оба** covers a group of two Masculine nouns *or* one Masculine + one Feminine noun (оба
студента for two male students, and also for one male + one female student — "оба студента"
covers the mixed case too per the source's own worked example); **обе** is used only when *both*
nouns are Feminine (обе девочки, "both girls"; обе машины, "both cars"). The corresponding
personal-pronoun forms are они оба / они обе ("they both").

### Indefinite numerals: invariant form, Genitive-governing (pp.177-178)
много, немного, несколько, сколько, столько, столько...сколько, мало, достаточно all exist in
**only one unchangeable form** — they never decline. Like the quantity words from Part 6, they
take Genitive Singular for uncountables (много снега) and Genitive Plural for countables (много
книг). A flagged **special-case exception**: много народу ("a lot of people") uses an irregular
partitive-Genitive form народу rather than the noun's expected regular Genitive.

### Collective numerals: used only in impersonal sentences, always Genitive Plural (pp.178-179)
двое/трое/четверо (most common) and пятеро/шестеро/семеро (less common) denote a group of people
— either all-male or mixed-sex. Collective numerals **always** take the **Genitive Plural** of
whatever noun/adjectival noun/pronoun follows (Нас трое, "there are three of us"; трое детей,
"three children"). The accompanying verb has its own distinct tense-agreement rule: **Present:
the verb (быть) is simply omitted** (Нас трое); **Past: the Neuter form** (Нас было трое); **Future:
3rd-person singular** (Нас будет трое). The source explicitly states **Collective Numerals are
used only in impersonal sentences** — never as an ordinary grammatical subject with a personally
agreeing verb.

### Part 14 preview: Prepositions (p.179)
Part 13 closes and Part 14 opens on Prepositions, framed as belonging to the "governor" word
class (per Part 3's Agreement-vs.-Governing distinction) — small words that force a specific case
onto their object. The Part is organized alphabetically and explicitly flags that many
prepositions govern different cases with correspondingly different meanings (opening examples:
без + Gen., "without"; в + Acc. for direction vs. в + Prep. for static location/time; вместе с +
Instr., "together with"; вместо + Gen., "instead of"; для + Gen., "for").
