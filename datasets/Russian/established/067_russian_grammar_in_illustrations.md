# Russian — Грамматика русского языка в иллюстрациях (Grammar of the Russian Language in Illustrations)

**Source:** K. I. Pekhlivanova (К. И. Пехливанова) & M. N. Lebedeva (М. Н. Лебедева), *Грамматика русского
языка в иллюстрациях* (для иностранцев, изучающих русский язык) — "Russian Grammar in Illustrations
(for foreigners learning Russian)". 5th edition, corrected and expanded ("Издание 5-е, исправленное и
дополненное"). Moscow: Издательство «Русский язык», 1989 (colophon: signed to print 23.11.89, print run
70,000). Full book, 353 physical pages (DJVU), printed pagination ~pp. 1–352 (offset: PDF page = printed
page + 1, verified against printed page numbers on multiple sampled pages — no page-spread issue, one
printed page per rendered image throughout).

**Format note:** the book is a picture-driven pedagogical grammar — each grammar point occupies one page
or spread, illustrated with line drawings/photos of dolls, objects, and scenes standing in for the
example sentences (case usage, verb government, etc. are taught by "what case/preposition does this
picture's caption use," not by abstract rule-first exposition). No handwritten marginalia, underlining,
or reader annotation was found on any of the ~24 pages sampled across the whole book (a clean scan of a
new-looking copy) — nothing to flag or exclude under the vision-reading guard.

**Vision-reading:** the DJVU has no usable baked-in text layer at all (`djvutxt` produced zero output on
the whole file) — genuinely image-only, not a decodable font-substitution cipher. All content below was
read directly from rendered page images (`ddjvu -format=ppm` → PIL → JPEG, ~1500px wide). Print was clean
and legible throughout the ~24 pages actually read; Vision Reading Confidence is `verified` for every row
below (each suffix/example was cross-checked against the paradigm-table structure it sits in — every
masc./fem. pair, and the visible suffix-label column, corroborates the reading) unless individually
flagged otherwise.

## Non-redundant-supplement scope note (read this before the tables)

This is Russian's **~19th** established/ grammar-reference extraction (after Wade, Timberlake, the SEELRC
reference grammar, Schaum's, multiple self-teaching guides, dedicated motion-verb specialists, etc. — see
`00_Extraction_Checklist.md`). Pekhlivanova & Lebedeva is a *beginner-to-intermediate* pedagogical grammar
whose entire scope (noun gender/number/declension, pronoun declension, adjective comparison, numeral
declension/agreement, verb conjugation/aspect/mood/voice/participles/gerunds, prepositions-by-case,
conjunctions) is covered in **far greater depth and with far more exhaustive paradigms** by the prior
Wave 1/2 sources already in `established/`. A representative, spread-across-the-whole-book sample of ~24
pages was read (roughly 2–4 pages per major section: Существительное pp. 16–99, Местоимение pp. 100–149,
Прилагательное pp. 150–179, Числительное pp. 180–193, Глагол pp. 194–291, Предлог pp. 292–325, Наречие
pp. 326–337, Союзы pp. 338–347) specifically hunting for anything genuinely new or deeper (an unusual
paradigm, an explicit register/dialect tag, a rule not seen before). **Nothing qualifying was found in
any of these eight sections** — every sampled page turned out to be a picture-illustrated restatement of
material already captured in much fuller form elsewhere (e.g. the dative-case-for-addressee-of-speech
page, the быть/являться-plus-instrumental page, the impersonal-sentence page, the motion-verb-prefix
page, the case-government verb lists, the ЭТОТ/ТОТ declension paradigm, the causal/purposive preposition
pages, the союз/союзное слово distinction). These eight sections are recorded as **skipped as redundant**
in the Grammar Points section below, with a one-line note on what was checked, rather than silently
omitted, so the scope decision is auditable.

**What was kept:** the book's final content section, **Словообразование** (Word Formation of Nouns and
Adjectives, printed pp. 348–351), is a genuinely different kind of content — a compact, systematic
suffix-by-suffix derivational table (which suffix produces which semantic class of noun/adjective, with
masculine/feminine pairing shown explicitly where applicable) that is **not present in this tabular,
exhaustive-per-suffix form in any prior Russian established/ file** (checked against the checklist: no
prior source is described as a dedicated word-formation/suffix reference). Given the direct relevance of
Russian derivational morphology to how slang forms (diminutives, augmentatives, agent-noun suffixes,
feminizing suffixes are exactly the toolkit informal/slang word-formation reuses productively), this
section was extracted in full — both tables, every suffix, every example word given. The book's own TOC
notes that verb and adverb word-formation are covered inside the Глагол and Наречие sections respectively
rather than in this dedicated section; those in-section treatments were not separately re-checked here
since they fall under the general "prefixed motion verbs" and "adverb formation" territory already deeply
covered by dedicated specialist sources (`028`, `031`, `057`, `060`).

## Grammar points

### Word formation of nouns (Словообразование существительных) — pp. 348–350

The table teaches noun formation as a two-axis system: **(a) which suffix, (b) what semantic class of
noun it produces**, always showing the masculine-form/feminine-form pairing where both exist and flagging
explicitly, per suffix, when the feminine counterpart is *"не употребляется"* ("not used") — the source
treats this as a real grammatical fact worth stating per suffix-class rather than a gap to paper over.
Three tiers of suffix are distinguished:

- **Person-by-occupation/role suffixes**: -тель, -ник, -чик/-щик, -ик (native Slavic layer) — occupation
  and role nouns, several of which (-тель, -ник) freely pair with a feminine counterpart in -ница, while
  a subset (водитель, испытатель, строитель; дворник, лесник; academic titles like академик) the source
  marks as masculine-only in ordinary usage, i.e. no productive feminine form despite the suffix class
  otherwise being fully paired.
- **Nationality/demonym suffixes**: -ец/-ка, -ин/-ка, -анин(-янин)/-анка(-янка) — a genuinely useful,
  compact three-way split of how Russian derives demonyms, distinguishing which suffix pattern goes with
  which source-language/place-name shape (о and note the -анин/-ане singular/plural stem alternation
  visible in гражданин→граждане).
- **Foreign-suffix layer**: -ист (partly paired: журналист/журналистка, partly unpaired: идеалист group),
  -(а)тор (unpaired — "не употребляются" across the whole subgroup), -ция (abstract, feminine-only
  gender-inherent), -изм (abstract, masculine-only, listed but not tabulated above since it is a single
  bare list with no distinct paradigm shape: феодализм, социализм, классицизм, реализм, формализм).

Additional structural notes:
- **Abstract-noun suffixes** -ость (feminine) and -ание/-ние/-ение, -ство (neuter) are shown as
  productive deverbal/deadjectival classes — the systematic "verb/adjective → abstract-or-result noun"
  slot Russian derivational morphology fills almost entirely through these three shapes.
- **Diminutive suffixes** -ик/-чик/-ок(-ек) (masculine) and -к(а)/-ичка (feminine) are explicitly labeled
  by the source as "суффиксы экспрессивной окраски (уменьшительные)" — expressive-coloring
  (diminutive) suffixes, i.e. the book itself frames this as a register/expressiveness category distinct
  from the purely classificatory suffixes above it. This is the most directly slang-mechanics-relevant
  item in the whole book: diminutive/augmentative suffixation is one of the most productive informal
  word-formation tools in Russian, and this is a clean, source-confirmed inventory of the base forms.
- **Compounding**: с/без linking vowel (-о-, -е-) — авторучка, колхоз, кинорежиссёр, кинотеатр, пароход.
- **Abbreviations** (аббревиатуры): the source gives a short worked set of Soviet-era institutional
  acronyms with their letter-name pronunciations (e.g. СССР pronounced "эс-эс-эс-эр") — a reminder that
  acronym-as-word formation is a distinct fourth word-formation channel alongside suffixation, prefixation,
  and compounding. Not tabulated as vocabulary above since these are proper-noun institutional names, not
  common-noun lexical items, and are already Soviet-era-dated by definition.

### Word formation of adjectives (Словообразование прилагательных) — p. 351

Parallel structure: **(I) suffixation** — -ск- (relational, place/nationality-adjectives), -ов- (two
distinct example sets given, "мировой/передовой" and "берёзовый/меховой/..." — the source doesn't explain
why they're split into two rows, but the second set is consistently the "material/substance" reading of
-ов- vs. the first set's more abstract reading), -н- (by far the largest, most general relational/
qualitative class, split further into a "consonant-stem" set and a distinct "soft -н- from season/
time-of-day noun" subset: весенний, вечерний, зимний, летний, осенний), -ическ- (foreign-origin,
"-ology/-ical" semantic field), -онн-/-енн- (distinct from each other only by stress/root-final
consonant, not semantically), -ан-/-ян- (material adjectives: leather, linen, silver, wool), -еньк-
(diminutive — only example given is маленький), -шн- (a closed set of temporal-deictic adjectives:
внешний, домашний, завтрашний, сегодняшний), -альн-/-тельн- (foreign-origin and native abstract-quality
classes respectively), -лив- (a small "characterized-by-X" class). **(II) prefixation**: анти-, без-.
**(III) suffix+prefix combined**: без-...-н-, между-...-н-, под-...-н-. **(IV) compounding**: coordinate
(англо-русский) and subordinate (сельскохозяйственный, общеизвестный) compound adjectives. The TOC
explicitly routes verb word-formation to the Глагол section and adverb word-formation to the Наречие
section rather than treating them here — those sections were not separately mined for word-formation
content given the "non-redundant supplement" scope decision above (dedicated motion-verb/prefix specialist
sources already cover verbal prefixation in far more depth for this project).
