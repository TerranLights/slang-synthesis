## Grammar points

### 1. В/на + accusative for "to" (destination of purpose)
Extends Unit 3's в/на + accusative rule: when specifying a *place* as destination, в + accusative
is used (билет в театр — "a ticket to the theatre"); when specifying an *event/performance/date*,
на + accusative is used (билет на оперу — "a ticket to the opera"). This на + accusative pattern
also covers "for" in a planned-for sense: Я хочу купить молоко на ужин ("I want to buy milk for
supper"). For цирк (circus) specifically, в + accusative is used because the permanent building is
implied rather than the performance (pp.52–53).

### 2. На + prepositional for a closed set of location nouns
A specific list of nouns takes на rather than в for static location, given as a table: вокзал,
станция, почта, стадион, площадь, улица, концерт, работа, восток/запад/север/юг (compass points).
A fuller list is deferred to Appendix 1 (p.53).

### 3. The genitive case
Introduced as the case whose principal meaning is "of" (Это книга Виктора — lit. "this is the book
of Viktor," i.e. "this is Viktor's book"). Also required: (a) after quantity words (много, сколько,
бутылка); (b) after certain prepositions (без, для, до, из, от, после, у); (c) after numerals 2, 3,
4 (genitive singular; 5+ uses genitive plural, deferred to Unit 7); (d) in negative existential
phrases (У вас нет билета — "I haven't got a ticket") (pp.53–54).

### 4. Genitive singular formation
Masculine and neuter nouns share the same two endings (remove nothing/о/е, add -а or -я depending
on hard/soft stem); feminine nouns end in -ы or -и. Full remove/add table given for all three
genders with hard and soft variants, e.g. университет→университета, автомобиль→автомобиля,
трамвай→трамвая; гостиница→гостиницы, неделя→недели, станция→станции, тетрадь→тетради; письмо→
письма, море→моря, здание→здания (pp.54–55).

### 5. Genitive of personal pronouns
Table given: меня (я), тебя (ты), его (он/оно), её (она), нас (мы), вас (вы), их (они) — these
forms are identical to the accusative forms of the same pronouns. When он/оно/она is governed by a
preposition, an initial н is prefixed: у него, at него, etc. (this is the same phenomenon already
seen in У него есть..., Unit 3) (p.55).

### 6. Animate accusative = genitive singular
Confirms and generalizes Unit 3's note: masculine animate nouns' accusative form is identical to
their genitive singular (Вы уже знаете Бориса? — "Do you already know Boris?"). The genitive form
of personal pronouns likewise doubles as their accusative form (Оля уже знает тебя? — "Does Olya
already know you?") (p.55).

### 7. Numerals 1–20
Full list given: один/два/три/четыре/пять/шесть/семь/восемь/девять/десять,
одиннадцать...двадцать. When counting aloud from 1, раз (lit. "one time") replaces один as the
first count-word. Один behaves as an adjective, agreeing in gender with its noun: один билет
(masc.), одна неделя (fem.), одно письмо (neut.) (pp.55–56).

### 8. Два/две + genitive singular
Два (masc./neut.) and две (fem.) are always followed by the genitive singular: два билета, две
недели, два письма. Numerals 3 and 4 behave the same way (p.56).

### 9. Currency and numerals with рубль/копейка
The genitive singular of рубль after 2/3/4 is рубля (два рубля); numbers above 4 take the genitive
plural (deferred to Unit 7), but рублей (5 roubles = пять рублей) is common enough to flag now.
Копейка parallels this: три копейки (genitive sg.) vs. пять копеек (genitive pl.) (p.56).

### 10. Сколько стоит (how much does it cost)
Стоить is a regular 2nd-conjugation verb like говорить. Use the 3rd-person-plural form (стоят) when
asking about the price of more than one item: Сколько стоят билеты? (p.57).

### 11. Word order flexibility
Russian word order is flexible for emphasis — subject/object/adverbial position can vary without a
grammaticality change, only an emphasis change: Я очень люблю Чехова vs. Я Чехова очень люблю
(both "I really like Chekhov"); Мелочи у вас нет? vs. У вас нет мелочи? (both "Haven't you got any
change?") (p.57).
