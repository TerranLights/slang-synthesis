## Grammar points

### Register-tagged intensifier pair: намного vs. гораздо (18.5, p. 218)

Both mean 'much' as an intensifier before a comparative adjective/adverb (**намно́го/гора́здо
деше́вле** 'much cheaper'). The book explicitly labels намно́го as the formal-usage member and
гора́здо as the colloquial member of the pair — a clean near-synonym register doublet.

### Soviet abbreviation types: initialism vs. acronym vs. "stump compound" (30.3, pp. 368–370)

The book distinguishes three distinct abbreviation mechanisms found in post-1917 Russian: (1)
**letter-name initialisms** read letter-by-letter (МГУ, ЦРУ, КГБ, США); (2) **acronyms** read as a
pronounceable word when the letter sequence permits it (ТАСС, ЮНЕСКО); (3) **"stump compounds"**
(built from truncated word-fragments rather than single letters, e.g. Минлегпро́м, Уралма́ш,
Гостелера́дио, Главсахалинбумпро́м) — the book notes this fragment-based compounding, rather than
single-letter-initial compounding (contrast English "IBM"/"BBC" vs. "Natwest"), is the Soviet-era
Russian preference. This is a genuine, productive word-formation mechanism worth flagging directly
to the eventual slang-mechanics analysis phase — it is a compounding/clipping strategy distinct from
ordinary derivational morphology, and Soviet/bureaucratic slang could plausibly extend or parody it.

### Discourse particles (30.4, pp. 370–371)

A compact but well-glossed inventory of Russian discourse particles with real pragmatic nuance,
beyond bare definitions: **же** (emphasis on the preceding word); **ведь** (speaker expects
agreement/assumes shared knowledge); **бы** (doubt/hypothesis/politeness, e.g. **Я бы купи́л два**
'I would buy two'); **вот** (draws attention to what follows); **ну** (hesitation, 'well'); **-то**
(colloquial, weaker equivalent of же — **В том-то и де́ло** 'That's just it'); **-ка** (softens
imperatives into friendlier invitations, e.g. **Скажи́-ка...** 'Do tell me...'; also marks tentative
first-person-future intentions, e.g. **Заеду-ка к нему** 'I think I might call on him'); **да**
(colloquial, weaker equivalent of а 'and/but', distinct from its use meaning 'yes'); **и** ('even',
emphasizing the following word). Several of these particles (-то, -ка) already appear in this
corpus's `062_russian_punctuation_and_related_symbols.md`; the -ка-softens-imperatives and
tentative-future-intention senses, and the -то-as-weaker-же gloss, are not previously captured in
this much pragmatic detail there.

### "Bookish" as a formally defined register category (Glossary of Grammatical Terms, p. 503)

The book's own back-matter glossary defines **bookish** explicitly: *"used in formal style, not in
everyday conversation."* This corroborates and formalizes the register distinctions this book
attaches at multiple grammatical levels (see 073b's instrumental-plural doublet finding, and this
file's short-form-adjective finding at Lesson 29) — i.e. this source treats "bookish" as a
first-class grammatical category on par with tense/aspect/case, not just an incidental usage note.
