# Russian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *Russian in Exercises* (full book)

**Source:** S. A. Khavronina & A. I. Shirochenskaya, *Russian in Exercises* (Ру́сский язы́к в
упражне́ниях), English-medium edition, Progress Publishers, Moscow. Preface and assignments
translated from the Russian by V. Korotky. 172-scanned-image PDF, no text layer (photographic scan),
covering the full book: front matter/preface/TOC (pp.5-14), Part I "Introductory Lexical and
Grammatical Course" (pp.15-55), Part II "Basic Course" — the case system (Prepositional, Accusative,
Dative, Genitive, Instrumental; pp.57-171), the verb system (Verbs of Motion, Verbs with the
Particle -ся, Aspects of the Verb; pp.173-249), the Complex Sentence incl. direct/indirect speech
(pp.250-270), and Participles/Verbal Adverbs (pp.271 through the book's end, printed page numbers
running to ~342). NOT processed: the parallel Spanish-medium edition *El Ruso en Ejercicios* in the
same source folder (explicitly out of scope per dispatch) and the book's own "Key to the Exercises"
answer key (pure answer-drilling, no new grammar/vocabulary).

**Coverage note:** This is an exercise-heavy pedagogical drill book — hundreds of numbered exercises
reusing a small core vocabulary (the preface states Part I alone is built on "a selected vocabulary
not exceeding 330 words"). Per the extraction spec's coverage rule, **every grammar point** in the
book's table of contents is represented below (Part I's course structure was read near-continuously;
Part II's case-by-case/verb-by-verb chapters were sampled representatively at multiple points per
chapter, since the TOC itself already enumerates every grammar subheading exhaustively and repeated
drill exercises carry no additional grammar or vocabulary beyond what a representative sample already
captures). Every vocabulary item recorded below is a genuine, distinct lexical item encountered on a
vision-read page — not a sample of a larger unlisted set. Two short embedded reading passages ("Вку́сная
ры́ба" / "Tasty Fish," a fairy tale, p.102-103; a poliklinik anecdote, p.111) were paraphrased rather
than reproduced, per copyright discipline.

**Vision-reading guard:** The entire source has no text layer and was vision-read from rendered page
images throughout. No handwritten marginalia (previous-owner pencil notes, underlines, filled-in
exercise answers) was observed on any page sampled — all content read was clearly typeset/printed,
consistent double-column-per-image book-spread scan quality. A genuine oddity worth flagging: the
book's own printed page numbers run noticeably higher in its back third than the table of contents'
page-number column predicts (e.g. the Verbal Adverbs section's own printed page reads "342" while the
TOC lists "291" for the same heading) even though content order otherwise matches the TOC exactly
with no topical gap — treated here as a pagination artifact of this particular scan/printing, not a
missing-content signal, and cross-checked against the TOC's own topic ordering rather than its page
numbers.

## Grammar points

### Part I — Introductory Lexical and Grammatical Course (pp.15-55)

#### The Noun. Singular. Что это?/Кто это?
Что это? asks about an inanimate referent, Кто это? about an animate (person) referent, both answered
with "Это + Nom." Negation: "Нет, это не Х" — the negative particle не is never stressed and merges
in pronunciation with the following word (не́_ру́чка), a footnoted phonetic point (p.16).

#### The Verb. Present Tense
Two conjugation classes are drilled from читать (1st conj.: -аю/-аешь/-ает/-аем/-аете/-ают) and
говорить (2nd conj.: -ю/-ишь/-ит/-им/-ите/-ят). "Я говорю по-русски" is flagged as a fixed idiom to
memorize rather than derive compositionally (p.20).

#### Adverbs answering Как? and Когда?
Manner adverbs (хорошо/плохо, быстро/медленно, громко/тихо, правильно/неправильно,
внимательно/невнимательно) answer Как?; time adverbs (утром/днём/вечером, рано утром/поздно
вечером) answer Когда? — both paired as antonym sets throughout the drills (pp.21-25).

#### The Gender of Nouns; Possessive Pronouns мой/твой/наш/ваш
Nouns sort into masc./fem./neut. by ending; possessive pronouns мой/моя́/моё/мои́,
твой/твоя́/твоё/твои́, наш/на́ша/на́ше/на́ши, ваш/ва́ша/ва́ше/ва́ши agree with the grammatical
gender/number of the possessed noun, not the possessor (p.29-30).

#### The Past Tense
Formed from the infinitive stem with -л (masc.), -ла (fem.), -ло (neut.), -ли (plural), agreeing
with the subject's gender and number rather than carrying person marking (p.31-33).

#### The Future Tense
Compound future: бу́ду/бу́дешь/бу́дет/бу́дем/бу́дете/бу́дут + imperfective infinitive (p.35).

#### The Plural of Nouns
Masc./fem. nouns take -ы or -и (spelling rule: -и after ж, ш, к, г, х, ч, щ); neuter nouns take -а
or -я. Stress can shift between singular and plural (стол → столы́) independently of the ending
pattern. Irregular/suppletive plurals: друг → друзья́, брат → бра́тья, стул → сту́лья,
де́рево → дере́вья, and fully suppletive челове́к → лю́ди, ребёнок → де́ти (pp.37-39).

#### Possessive Pronouns. The Question Чей?
Full possessive-pronoun paradigm across persons (мой/твой for "I/you [sg.]", наш/ваш for "we/you
[pl.]", plus invariable его́/её/их for "he/she/they") each cross-tabulated against Чей?/Чья?/Чьё?/Чьи?
— 3rd-person possessives never agree with the possessed noun's gender/number, unlike 1st/2nd person
ones, a genuine asymmetry in the system (pp.40-41).

#### The Construction У меня, у тебя есть...
Possession is expressed as "У + Genitive-of-possessor + есть + Nominative-of-possessed" rather than
a verb "to have" — есть is dropped in some contexts once the existence of the thing is already
established in discourse (p.43).

#### Demonstrative pronouns and adjectives
Э́тот/э́та/э́то/э́ти contrasted with the question Како́й?/Кака́я/Како́е/Каки́е; adjectives with a
hard-consonant stem take -ый(-ой)/-ая/-ое/-ые (но́вый, молодо́й); adjectives and adverbs are
distinguished by which question they answer (Како́й? vs. Как?) even when built from the same root
(pp.45-52).

#### Verbs хоте́ть, люби́ть, мочь, and до́лжен + infinitive
хоте́ть is irregular across the paradigm (хочу́, хо́чешь, хо́чет, хоти́м, хоти́те, хотя́т — singular
forms shift stem consonant, plural forms don't); люби́ть (люблю́, лю́бишь…) and мочь (могу́,
мо́жешь, мо́жет, мо́жем, мо́жете, мо́гут) both govern a following infinitive; до́лжен/должна́/
должно́/должны́ ("must/have to," also idiomatically "owe") agrees with the subject's gender/number
despite functioning like a modal predicate rather than a true adjective (pp.53-55).

### Part II — Basic Course: The Use of the Cases (pp.57-171)

#### The Prepositional Case
Denotes place (в/на + Prep., в for enclosed/institutional space vs. на for a surface or event —
lexically fixed per noun, e.g. в шкафу́ vs. на столе́, в теа́тре vs. на заво́де); denotes the object
of speech/thought (о ком?/о чём? + Prep., говори́ть/ду́мать/расска́зывать о + Prep.); personal
pronouns take an epenthetic н- after a preposition in 3rd person (о нём, о ней, о них) but not
1st/2nd (о́бо мне, о тебе́, о нас, о вас); reflexive possessive свой refers back to the sentence's
own grammatical subject specifically, a distinction его́/её/их cannot make; time expressions with
months (в + Prep.) and the full year-birth formula ("Я роди́лся в ты́сяча девятьсо́т со́рок пя́том
году́"); "на чём" (means of transport) uses Prepositional of the vehicle (pp.57-77).

#### The Accusative Case
Names the object of an action (Кого?/Что?): masc. inanimate singular = Nom. form, fem. takes -у;
animate direct objects are specifically flagged by Кого? as distinct from Что?; personal pronouns
have their own Acc. forms (меня́, тебя́, его́, её, нас, вас, их); Acc. of direction (куда? + в/на +
Acc.) contrasts against где? + Prep. for static location, tracking the verbs-of-motion pairs
идти́/ходи́ть and е́хать/е́здить; duration is expressed with це́лый/весь + a time-noun in Acc.
(це́лый день, весь год), distinct from ка́ждый + Acc. for habitual recurrence (pp.78-102).

#### The Dative Case
Marks the person toward whom an action is directed (звони́ть/дава́ть/писа́ть/посыла́ть кому́); мать
and дочь have irregular Dative forms ма́тери/до́чери; verbs помога́ть, меша́ть, обеща́ть,
сове́товать, разреша́ть, переводи́ть all govern a Dative complement; a distinct family of impersonal
constructions places the experiencer in the Dative with no grammatical subject at all — "Мне
тру́дно/легко́/прия́тно/интере́сно/ску́чно/на́до/ну́жно + infinitive," and "мо́жно/нельзя́ +
infinitive" for permission/prohibition, "мне ви́дно/слы́шно/поня́тно" for perception predicates
(pp.103-125).

#### The Genitive Case
After negation of existence, нет/не́ было/не бу́дет + Genitive expresses "I don't/didn't/won't have
X," regardless of tense the missing thing itself is always Genitive; numeral-counting constructions
govern Genitive (Ско́лько студе́нтов? — plural Genitive), as do the quantifiers мно́го/ма́ло/
не́сколько/немно́го; the adnominal Genitive expresses possession/relation without a possessive
pronoun ("кни́га бра́та"); the comparative degree can take a bare Genitive object in place of an
explicit "чем"-clause ("Джон вы́ше своего́ бра́та"); dates use a full Genitive chain — ordinal-Gen. +
month-Gen. + year-ordinal-Gen. — distinct from the Nominative used only to *ask* "what date is it
today?"; many prepositions govern Genitive (у, до, от, о́коло, напро́тив, недалеко́ от, с…до for time
or distance ranges); price/cost expressions pair a counted unit with a currency noun in Genitive
(pp.126-154).

#### The Instrumental Case
Personal pronouns have distinct Instrumental forms (мной, тобо́й, им, е́й/е́ю, на́ми, ва́ми, и́ми),
used after the preposition с ("with"); "Что с ва́ми/тобо́й?" is a fixed idiom ("what's wrong with
you?") repurposing the Instrumental pronoun non-literally; plural nouns/adjectives/possessives take
-ами/-ями in the Instrumental. The table of contents additionally lists (not separately re-sampled
here beyond its heading): sociative Instrumental ("мы с бра́том" = "my brother and I"), Instrumental
as nominal predicate ("Он был врачо́м"), Instrumental after verbs занима́ться/интересова́ться,
Instrumental of place/route, and Instrumental marking the agent in passive constructions ("кни́га
напи́сана писа́телем") (pp.155-171).

### Part II — The Verb (pp.173-249)

#### Verbs of Motion — unprefixed pairs
идти́ (unidirectional, "am going now") contrasts with ходи́ть (multidirectional/habitual, or "went
and came back" as a completed round trip); the same split applies to е́хать/е́здить for
vehicle-based motion. The past-tense contrast is a genuine trap: "Мы ходи́ли в парк" implies a
completed round trip, while "Мы пошли́ в парк" (perfective) asserts only that they set off, leaving
arrival/return unstated. пойти́ is the perfective/future companion of imperfective идти́ (p.173-192).

#### Verbs of Motion — prefixed forms
A closed system of directional prefixes attaches to the идти́/е́хать stems, each with a fixed
spatial meaning independent of aspect: **при-** (arrive), **у-** (leave/go away), **в-** (enter),
**вы-** (exit), **пере-** (cross), **под-** (approach), **от-** (move away from), **до-** (reach as
far as), **про-** (pass by/through), **об-** (go around/circumnavigate). Отку́да? ("where from?") is
answered with из/с + Genitive, mirroring куда́?'s в/на + Accusative. Elapsed time uses "че́рез +
Acc." = "in/after [time]" (pp.194-209).

#### Verbs with the Particle -ся
Not a single unified meaning — four distinct functions are drilled: (1) **passive-like**, where an
intransitive counterpart makes the affected object the grammatical subject ("Дверь откры́лась" vs.
transitive "Он откры́л дверь"; "Каранда́ш слома́лся" vs. "Я слома́л каранда́ш"); (2)
**middle-reflexive**, subject acting on itself (умыва́ться, одева́ться, сади́ться, ложи́ться); (3)
**reciprocal**, two-or-more-party mutual action (познако́миться, встре́титься, поссо́риться,
договори́ться, ви́деться); (4) **"proper reflexive,"** lexicalized verbs that exist only in -ся form
(боя́ться, наде́яться, беспоко́иться, интересова́ться, горди́ться, занима́ться, забо́титься,
сомнева́ться, сме́яться, ра́доваться, относи́ться), several of which govern the Instrumental. A
separate, orthogonal three-way "to put" distinction is drilled alongside this: класть/положи́ть (lay
flat) vs. ста́вить/поста́вить (stand upright) vs. ве́шать/пове́сить (hang) — chosen by the resulting
orientation of the object, not by a single general "put" verb (pp.210-222).

#### The Aspects of the Verb
Imperfective merely names an action or names it in progress/repeated; perfective asserts completion
or result. Extensive aspect-pair drilling (чита́ть/прочита́ть, покупа́ть/купи́ть, писа́ть/написа́ть,
получа́ть/получи́ть, встреча́ть/встре́тить, конча́ть/ко́нчить, опа́здывать/опозда́ть,
посыла́ть/посла́ть, учи́ть/вы́учить, помога́ть/помо́чь, стро́ить/постро́ить,
осма́тривать/осмотре́ть, пить/вы́пить, рисова́ть/нарисова́ть, объясня́ть/объясни́ть). The verbs
нача́ть, продолжа́ть, and ко́нчить obligatorily take an **imperfective** infinitive complement no
matter their own aspect. Perfective simple-future ("прочита́ю," a single form) contrasts with
imperfective compound future ("бу́ду чита́ть," auxiliary + infinitive). A recurring three-way trap
for English speakers: ви́деть/уви́деть, знать/узна́ть, and слы́шать/услы́шать each split an ongoing
perception/state verb (imperfective: an ability or established fact) from a punctual "become aware
of" event (perfective: catch sight of/find out/catch the sound of) — English "see/know/hear" doesn't
lexically split this way at all (pp.223-249).

### Part II — The Complex Sentence (pp.250-270)

#### Subordinate clauses; причина/result; conditionals; relative clauses; direct/indirect speech
Interrogative-word subordinators (кто, что, како́й, как, где, куда́, отку́да, почему́, заче́м,
ско́лько) each introduce a clause echoing their matrix question-word function. пото́му что
("because," reason follows) and поэ́тому ("therefore," result follows) are drilled as near-mirror
paraphrases of the same causal relationship with clause order swapped. Conditional clauses with
е́сли take a plain future-tense verb in both clauses, unlike English's conditional/subjunctive shift.
Relative clauses with кото́рый decline for gender/number to match their antecedent but for case
according to their own role inside the relative clause — drilled across all six cases, with and
without a governing preposition. Direct speech converts to indirect speech via a что-clause with the
expected pronoun/tense shift for statements; for a question containing an interrogative word, that
word itself becomes the subordinator and Russian keeps ordinary statement word order inside the
embedded clause (no English-style partial inversion) (pp.250-270).

### Part II — Participles and Verbal Adverbs (pp.271 to the book's end)

#### Participles
Participles are verbal adjectives, declined like adjectives to agree with the noun they modify, and
serve as a compact alternative to a кото́рый-relative clause. **Present active participles**
(imperfective verbs only) take -ущий/-ющий for 1st-conjugation stems (чита́ть → чита́ющий,
жить → живу́щий, идти́ → иду́щий, е́хать → е́дущий) or -ащий/-ящий for 2nd-conjugation stems
(сиде́ть → сидя́щий). **Past active participles** take -вший from the past-tense stem
(написа́ть → написа́вший). **Complete-form passive participles** (from transitive perfective verbs,
e.g. напи́санный, полу́ченный, ку́пленный, откры́тый) decline as full adjectives and can carry a
passive agent in the Instrumental ("письмо́, напи́санное отцо́м"). **Short-form passive
participles** are predicative-only, uninflected for case (only gender/number) — "Кни́га напи́сана" —
directly paralleling the long/short-adjective distinction found elsewhere in Russian. A participle
construction can be built to qualify either a sentence's subject or its object, drilled as an
explicit two-way transform exercise (pp.271-290).

#### Verbal Adverbs
Unlike participles, verbal adverbs (деепричастия) never decline — they're invariable — and describe
an action performed by the same grammatical subject as the main verb, timed relative to it.
**Imperfective verbal adverbs** (from the imperfective present stem, -я/-а: чита́я, слу́шая,
уезжа́я) denote an action simultaneous with the main verb ("Ужина́я, мы разгова́ривали"). **Perfective
verbal adverbs** (from the perfective past stem, -в/-вши: прочита́в, прие́хав, отве́тив) denote an
action prior to the main verb ("Ко́нчив шко́лу, я поступи́л в университе́т"). The transform from a
subordinate clause to a verbal-adverb construction is only valid when both clauses share the same
subject — the source explicitly marks one exercise item as "No replacement is possible" for exactly
this reason, a genuine constraint worth preserving rather than over-generalizing the pattern
(pp.291-297 per the table of contents; the book's own printed page numbers for this section actually
read in the low-to-mid 340s — see the coverage note above on this scan's pagination discrepancy).
