## Grammar points

### Complex sentence overview and сложносочинённые предложения (compound sentences, pp. 264-290)
Раздел 3 organizes compound-sentence connectives (equal-status clauses joined by a coordinating
conjunction) into six meaning-based families, each with a boxed definition and a worked literary
example set (Tolstoy, Lermontov, Krylov, Pushkin, Chekhov, Simonov, Erenburg, Tsvetaeva-adjacent
authors recur as citation sources throughout): **соединительные** (additive: и, да, и...и, ни...ни
— p.264); **противительные** (adversative: а, но, да, однако, зато, же, with несмотря на то что as
the written-register equivalent of concessive хотя — p.269); **разделительные** (disjunctive: или,
либо, ли...ли, то...то, то ли...то ли, иначе/а то/не то, — p.277); **присоединительные**
(afterthought/elaborating: да и, притом, причём, тоже, также — p.282); and **сопоставительные**
(comparative-scalar: не только...но и, не то что...но, не столько...сколько — p.288), the last
distinguishing "ascending" vs. "descending" informational weight between the two clauses. See
Vocabulary for the full register-tagged connective inventory (да, хоть, либо, поскольку, пускай,
etc. all carry the source's own explicit colloquial/archaic/literary tags — a notably rich seam of
register annotation for this раздел).

### Сложноподчинённые предложения (complex sentences with subordination, pp. 291-353)
The раздел's largest section, organized by the semantic role of the subordinate clause, each with
its own boxed connective inventory (fully tabulated in Vocabulary above): **изъяснительная**
(complement clauses — object clauses filling a verb's argument slot, keyed to governing-verb
semantic class: speech/thought/feeling/perception/desire verbs, p.291); **определительная**
(relative/attributive clauses modifying a noun, p.308, plus the который/какой vs. чей distinction
and the почему/отчего/зачем-as-fused-word vs. по-чему/от-чего/за-чем-as-pronoun+preposition
homophone trap); **времени** (temporal clauses, p.317, split by simultaneity vs.
precedence/succession relative to the main clause, and free word order — the subordinate clause may
precede or follow); **места** (locative clauses, p.324, closing with a proverb set — see
Vocabulary); **причины и следствия** (causal/consequential clauses, p.327, with a formal/informal
register split among the causal connectives and a rule that a fronted causal clause is set off when
the cause needs particular emphasis); **цели** (purpose clauses, p.333, with для того
чтобы/с тем чтобы/затем чтобы flagged as written-register formal variants, and лишь бы/только бы
marking heightened desire); **условной** (conditional clauses, p.338, covering the placement rule
for the particle бы — it may attach to the conjunction or the verb in either clause — and раз's
distinctive "condition-as-established-fact" meaning); **уступительной** (concessive clauses, p.342,
covering the хотя/хоть and пусть/пускай formal-colloquial doublets, plus the observation that
main-clause adversatives но/зато/однако/тем не менее can reinforce the concessive contrast); and
**образа действия, меры, степени и сравнения** (manner/degree/comparison clauses, p.345, splitting
degree-based что-clauses needing a correlative intensifier in the main clause from
comparison-based clauses built on an associative/simile relation between clauses).

### Бессоюзное сложное предложение (asyndetic/conjunctionless complex sentences, pp. 354-357)
Covers complex sentences whose clauses are joined with no conjunction at all, purely by intonation
and punctuation, split into two functional groups: those paralleling **compound**-sentence semantics
(simultaneity/sequence — Zabolotsky, Gorky, Paustovsky examples; comparison/contrast — see the two
proverbs in Vocabulary, paired with a Nekrasov example) and those paralleling **complex**-sentence
semantics with an implicit subordinating relation recoverable from context (object: "Жилин видит:
дело плохо"; attributive: "Ей представилась картина: хрупкая ладья несётся по течению"; causal: "Я
несчастлив: каждый день гости"). A boxed rule notes that asyndetic constructions overall skew toward
разговорная речь (spoken register) — the chapter's own closing exercise (477) explicitly assigns
students a first-person narrative task using asyndetic constructions "since they occur most often in
colloquial speech."
