## Grammar points

### по- + adverbial "in [language]" formation — Lesson 5 (p. 56)

A dedicated table pairs each "Какой язык?" adjective phrase (ру́сский язы́к, англи́йский язы́к,
францу́зский язы́к, неме́цкий язы́к, кита́йский язы́к, япо́нский язы́к, коре́йский язы́к, шве́дский
язы́к, италья́нский язы́к) with its corresponding по- adverb (по-ру́сски, по-англи́йски, etc.) used
with говори́ть/писа́ть/чита́ть. This is a productive derivational pattern — по- + the adjective
stem (minus the -ий/-ий agreement ending) + -и — rather than a set of memorized irregular forms,
though the source presents it as a vocabulary table rather than stating the rule explicitly.

### мочь conjugation (irregular, present + past) — Lesson 5 (p. 56, Table 16)

Present: могу́/мо́жешь/мо́жет/мо́жем/мо́жете/мо́гут (a г~ж consonant mutation across the paradigm,
plus a stress shift off the ending after the 1sg form — a genuine irregular Conjugation I verb,
distinct from the regular -ать/-ять classes given in Lesson 3's Verbs overview). Past: мог/могла́/
могло́/могли́ — regular л-suffix formation despite the irregular present stem.

### Read/write/speak/understand verb paradigm — Lesson 5 (p. 55, Tables 14-15)

чита́ть and понима́ть are shown as regular Conjugation I (-ает/-ают endings); говори́ть is regular
Conjugation II (-ит/-ят); писа́ть is Conjugation I but with a consonant mutation (с→ш: пиш-у́,
пи́ш-ешь, ...пи́ш-ут) not previously flagged in the book's Verbs overview (Lesson 3, p. 39), which
described only the "regular" -ать/-ять/-еть/-овать/-евать/-нуть infinitive-ending classes without
mentioning stem consonant mutations — worth flagging as a genuine gap the book's own systematic
grammar tables quietly patch via worked examples rather than an explicit rule statement. Past tense
of all four verbs follows the already-established л/-ла/-ло/-ли pattern uniformly.
