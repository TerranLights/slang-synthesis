## Grammar points

### Three-way МОКРЫЙ-СЫРОЙ-ВЛАЖНЫЙ discrimination: quantity + collocation-domain split

A cleanly graded three-term scale by liquid quantity (мокрый > сырой > влажный), overlaid with a
collocation-domain split that doesn't map onto the quantity scale at all: only СЫРОЙ combines with
time-of-day/season nouns, and only СЫРОЙ carries the extra "cold and dank" / "raw, unprocessed food"
senses. This is a good worked example of how a near-synonym scale in Russian can be organized along
two independent axes simultaneously (a quantity gradient and a collocation-domain restriction) —
directly useful for later mechanics-analysis work modeling multi-axis near-synonym systems.

### ИНОСТРАННЫЙ/ЗАРУБЕЖНЫЙ/ЗАГРАНИЧНЫЙ/ИМПОРТНЫЙ: the richest register-plus-directionality cluster so far

This four-way group combines two independent discrimination axes: (1) a register cline
(ИНОСТРАННЫЙ neutral → ЗАРУБЕЖНЫЙ formal/official → ЗАГРАНИЧНЫЙ colloquial, receding in modern usage)
and (2) a spatial-directionality distinction (ИНОСТРАННЫЙ for something that has arrived in the
speaker's own country vs. ЗАРУБЕЖНЫЙ for something that remains in its home country), with ИМПОРТНЫЙ
carving out its own narrower domain-specific niche (goods/trade specifically). The book's own
explicit exception-flagging (зарубежная делегация/гости/туристы break the directionality rule; only
заграничное, never *зарубежное, combines with плавание) demonstrates that even the book's own
worked contrasts have documented exceptions — a genuinely rich case study for how register and
usage-domain can interact non-systematically in a real near-synonym cluster, worth flagging for the
eventual mechanics-analysis phase.
