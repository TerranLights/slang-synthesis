## Grammar points — Genitive case (pp. 110–137)

The book opens this chapter by noting the genitive is Russian's single most-used case, governed by
over 100 prepositions (~40 simple, ~70 complex — more than all other cases combined), and the only
case capable of chaining (multiple consecutive genitives, e.g. "records *of* the Book *of*
Guinness"). Its unifying abstract definition: **the genitive is a backgrounded item that yields
focus of attention to something else that exists or maneuvers in its proximity.** Four branches:
**A SOURCE** (departure from), **A GOAL** (approach toward — the mirror image of A Source),
**A WHOLE** (a static container of which something is a part), and **A REFERENCE** (a static
proximity point used to locate or compare something else) — the book notes A Whole and A Reference
are simply the two "endpoints" of the A Source/A Goal path frozen in place.

### A Source — three prepositions that mirror the destination system, plus withdrawal verbs

A direct, systematic mirror-image of the destination-preposition system already documented in
065b/065c: **the same three-way split (non-human-container / non-human-surface / human) that
governs which preposition marks a *destination* also governs which preposition marks a *source*,**
just with direction reversed:

| Destination type | "going to" preposition | "coming from" preposition |
|---|---|---|
| non-human, container-like (в + ACC) | в | из + GEN |
| non-human, surface-like (на + ACC) | на | с + GEN |
| human (к + DAT) | к | от + GEN |

1. **из 'from'** — removal from a container (literal: from a trunk, a barn, a region) or,
   metaphorically, a cause/motive (removed "from charity," "from depression"). Forms two frozen
   secondary/complex prepositions: **из-за** "from beyond" (concretely "from abroad" — *из-за
   рубежа*; metaphorically "because of"), and **из-под** "from beneath" (concretely "out from
   under one's feet"; idiomatically e.g. bottles found "from beneath kefir" = kefir bottles).
2. **с 'from'** — removal from a surface (mirrors на's surface-destination sense): from a tree,
   from the ground. With time expressions, marks a starting point (*с пяти лет* "from age five,"
   *с детства* "since childhood," fixed phrase *с тех пор* "from that time on"). Occasionally used
   with human sources when money/dependency is involved (*Сколько с меня?* "How much do I owe
   you?").
3. **от 'from'** — the default preposition for human sources (mirrors к's human-destination
   sense), but also usable for non-human sources, especially with the location-marking preposition
   у (see A Reference 2 below) — removal from a у-marked location uses от. Governs the fixed
   collocation *зависеть от* "depend on." Metaphorically marks a cause, especially of illness or
   death (*умереть от разрыва сердца* "die from a heart attack").
4. **Withdrawal verbs** — a family of verbs of fear/avoidance take a bare genitive object (no
   preposition), all sharing the "moving away from" logic: *бояться* "fear," *избегать/избежать*
   "avoid," *стесняться/постесняться* "be shy [of]," *гнушаться/погнушаться* "abhor, have
   aversion," *остерегаться/остеречься* "beware," *пугаться/испугаться* "be frightened,"
   *дичиться* "shy away from," *стыдиться/постыдиться* "be ashamed," *чуждаться* "shun, stand
   aloof."

### A Goal — the mirror image of A Source

1. **до 'to, up to'** — the primary preposition of approach, in space (reaching a destination),
   time (until/before — *до войны* "before the war," *до свидания* "goodbye," lit. "until we see
   each other again"; paired with с to bracket a span: "с X до Y"), and metaphorical/scalar domains
   (movement into a state: "to the point of international isolation"; extremes on a numeric or
   qualitative scale: "up to two hundred thousand tourists"). The idiom **[dative] + не до +
   [genitive]** means "not in the mood for X" (*Пушкину было не до детей* "Pushkin wasn't in the
   mood for children").
2. **для 'for' and про́тив 'against'** — для marks an approach that confers benefit (writing "for"
   normal people, a sanatorium "for" highly-placed people); ра́ди is a near-synonym meaning "for
   the sake of." Про́тив marks an approach that is adversarial ("against" bribery, "against" a
   virus), occasionally used purely spatially to mean "opposite" with no adversarial sense.
   жаль/жа́лко 'regret, pity' can also trigger genitive (or accusative) in impersonal expressions.
3. **Actual approach — obligatory genitive verbs.** A verb family where the goal is real/touched/
   acquired and genitive use is obligatory: *держаться* "hold to," *добиваться/добиться* "get,
   obtain," *достигать/достигнуть/достичь* "attain, reach," *достойный* "worthy [of]," *желать/
   пожелать* "desire, wish," *заслуживать/заслужить* "deserve, merit," *касаться/коснуться* "touch;
   concern," *слушаться/послушаться* "listen to; obey," *стоить* "be worth, deserve,"
   *удостаивать/удостоить* "receive, be awarded." желать/пожелать underlies a whole family of
   salutation formulas that drop the verb and keep only the genitive wished-for thing: *Всего
   доброго/лучшего!* "All the best!", *Приятного аппетита!* "Bon appétit!", *Спокойной ночи!*
   "Good night!", *Счастливого пути!* "Have a good trip!"
4. **Hypothetical approach — optional genitive/accusative alternation.** A second verb family
   (*ждать* "wait for," *искать* "seek," *ожидать* "expect," *просить/попросить* "request,"
   *требовать/потребовать* "demand, require," *хотеть/захотеть* "want") can take **either** the
   genitive (when the sought item is non-specific/hypothetical — "waiting for *a* bus, any bus
   that comes") **or** the accusative (when the sought item is specific/identified — "waiting for
   *the* bus [I know I need]"). This genitive-vs-accusative alternation as a marker of specificity
   is a genuinely useful, teachable discrimination for the same verb, distinct from the fixed
   case-government pattern of most other verb families in this book.

### A Whole — five sub-senses: 'of', category membership, events, prepositions, quantifiers

1. **'Of', possession, and color** — the "bare case" (no preposition) genitive marking wholes of
   which something is a part (parts of buildings/time-of-day: *утра/дня/вечера/ночи*), possession
   (*ошибку президента* "the president's error"), category membership ("region *of* eternal
   frost"), and — a distinctively Russian pattern — **describing an object's color via genitive**,
   parallel to the English idiom "a horse of a different color": *галстук белого цвета* "a white
   tie," lit. "a tie of white color."
2. **Events, idioms, and chains** — the genitive links an event to its participants the way English
   "of" does (*публикация... книг* "the publication *of* books"); the bare genitive appears in
   fixed idioms *чего* "why," *всего* "in all, altogether, just, only," *своего рода* "in its own
   way, all to itself," *что нового/интересного* "what's new/interesting"; and the genitive is the
   only case that chains (multiple consecutive genitives: "Party *of* Islamic unity *of*
   Afghanistan").
3. **Prepositions and prepositional phrases** — среди 'among', посреди 'in the middle of', внутри
   'inside' all govern genitive:: a whole directly, and Russian has **dozens of complex
   prepositional phrases meaning "of"** built from a preposition + noun + genitive, of which the
   book tabulates ~40 (representative sample, pp. 128–129): *в течение* "in the course of," *во
   время* "during, at the time of," *в результате* "as a result of," *в рамках* "within the
   framework of," *по причине* "by reason of," *с помощью* "with the help of," *с точки зрения*
   "from the point of view of," *под видом* "under the guise of," *за счёт* "at the expense of."
4. **Numerals and quantifiers** — the standard "X of Y" quantification pattern (numeral/quantifier
   + genitive: *сколько* "how many," *несколько* "some," *столько* "so many," *много* "many/
   much," *немного* "not many/much," *мало* "few/little," *больше* "more," *меньше* "fewer/less").
   Notably, the quantifier and quantified item need not be adjacent in a sentence. **Inverting a
   numeral and the quantified genitive item expresses approximation** without needing an
   "approximately" word: *их было штук пятьдесят* "there were about fifty of them" (lit.
   "items-GEN fifty," inverted from the neutral "fifty items").
5. **'Some' (the partitive)** — bare genitive without a numeral to express an unspecified amount
   ("some tea," "some money"); perfective verbs and substance nouns sometimes trigger a distinct
   "second genitive" ending in -у/-ю for this partitive sense. A separate family of quantity verbs
   is tabulated (representative sample, p. 131): *набираться/набраться* "collect, pick up (e.g.
   courage)," *наедаться/наесться* "eat one's fill," *исполняться/исполниться* "become filled,"
   *хватать/хватить* "be enough," *прибавляться/прибавиться* "increase, add," *убавляться/
   убавиться* "decrease, subtract."

### A Reference — dates, у 'by/at', lack, and comparison

1. **Dates and other reference points** — the genitive is the case Russian uses for a specific
   calendar date when something *happens on* it (*третьего декабря* "on the third of December").
   A large family of prepositions locate items in time/space relative to a genitive reference
   point (representative sample of a ~30-item table, p. 133): *без* "without," *близ/вблизи*
   "near," *вдоль* "along," *вокруг* "around," *мимо* "by, past," *накануне* "on the eve of,"
   *напротив* "opposite," *около* "around, approximately," *позади* "behind," *после* "after,"
   *прежде* "before." A distinctive idiom: *меж двух жерновов* "between a rock and a hard place"
   (lit. "between two millstones").
2. **у 'by' — space, possession, causation.** Spatially, у + genitive means "by, near" (*у двери*
   "by the door"). Its most important use is **Russia's standard existential-possession
   construction, у + [possessor-GEN] + (есть) + [possession-NOM]** — literally "by the possessor
   is a possession," the ordinary way to say "the possessor has a possession" (including inalienable
   possession of body parts: *у меня трясутся руки* "my hands are shaking," lit. "by me shake
   hands"). When the genitive item is a person, у can also mean "at [person]'s place" (*у него*
   "at his place") or express causation ("having someone do something for you," e.g. "having
   *someone* do my hair").
3. **Lack (the "genitive of negation")** — negated verbs trigger a genitive on either the subject
   or the object to express non-existence/unavailability (*никаких забастовок не было* "there were
   no strikes"). A dedicated word family expresses lack directly (p. 136): *дефицит* "deficit,"
   *лишать/лишить* "deprive," *лишаться/лишиться* "be deprived," *недоставать/недостать*
   "be lacking," *недостаток* "lack," *нехватка* "shortage."
4. **Comparison** — comparative adjectives/adverbs + genitive-marked standard, of the form
   [item] + [comparative] + [standard-GEN] = "item is more X than standard" (*выше личных
   интересов* "higher [priority] than personal interests").

The Epilogue notes the genitive continues the dative chapter's theme of the special grammatical
treatment given to human beings (к + dative for destinations, у + genitive for locations, от +
genitive for departure points when the reference is a person), and closes by observing that the
genitive uniquely combines an attentiveness to distinctively human qualities (fear, desire,
comparison) with a heavy focus on pure quantification.
