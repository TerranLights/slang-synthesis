## Grammar points

### идти́/е́хать present tense + imperative — Lesson 8 (p. 94, Table 27)

идти́ conjugates ид-у́/ид-ёшь/ид-ёт/ид-ём/ид-ёте/ид-у́т (regular Conjugation I with a stem-final
consonant); е́хать conjugates е́д-у/е́д-ешь/е́д-ет/е́д-ем/е́д-ете/е́д-ут. Notably, е́хать's
imperative is **suppletive**: not derived regularly from its own present stem but replaced
wholesale by е́зжай!/е́зжайте! — flagged directly by the table without further comment, a genuine
suppletion point distinct from every regular imperative-formation example given in Lesson 7.

### выходи́ть present tense — Lesson 8 (p. 94, Table 28)

Regular Conjugation II: выхожу́/вы́ходишь/вы́ходит/вы́ходим/вы́ходите/вы́ходят, with a д→ж
consonant mutation in the 1sg only (a mutation pattern consistent with regular -ить Conjugation II
verbs whose stem ends in д/т/з/с/б/п/в/ф, though the source doesn't generalize the rule explicitly).

### Accusative singular of inanimate nouns — Lesson 8 (p. 95, Table 29)

Explicitly contrasted against animacy (Lesson 7's accusative-of-animate-nouns point): inanimate
masculine and neuter nouns are unchanged in the accusative singular (це́нтр→в це́нтр, посо́льство→
в посо́льство); feminine nouns take -у (у́лица→на у́лицу) or, for -я nouns, -ю (ста́нция→на
ста́нцию); feminine nouns ending in -ь are unchanged (пло́щадь→на пло́щадь, matching -ь nouns'
general behavior of not distinguishing nominative/accusative). A "but" exception block again
singles out -ия nouns behaving differently from plain -а nouns (ста́нция→на ста́нцию,
Росс́ия→в Росс́ию, А́нглия→в А́нглию) — actually the *regular* -ю pattern for -я/-ия stems, so the
"but" framing here highlights that these aren't exceptions to a masculine/neuter-style
no-change rule, just a different declension class entirely.

### Full four-case declension overview: nouns — Lesson 8 (p. 96-97, Table 30)

The book's first side-by-side Nominative/Genitive(до)/Accusative(куда́)/Prepositional(где)
paradigm table, unifying every case rule given across Lessons 6-8 into one reference: masculine
(центр/до це́нтра/в це́нтр/в це́нтре), feminine (у́лица/до у́лицы/на у́лицу/на у́лице; ста́нция/до
ста́нции/в ста́нцию/на ста́нции), neuter (посо́льство/до посо́льства/в посо́льство/в посо́льстве).
A closing "Remember" flags дом ("house/home") as fully irregular/suppletive across all four
locative-type cases: до до́ма (genitive, regular) but домо́й (accusative-of-direction, an adverbial
form unrelated to the regular accusative pattern) and до́ма (prepositional-of-location, likewise an
adverbial-looking form) — i.e. "going home"/"being home" don't use the regular case endings other
nouns in this exact same semantic slot would use, a genuine lexicalized irregularity worth flagging
for mechanics analysis as a place where a single extremely frequent noun resists the productive
case system.

### Full four-case declension: adjectives — Lesson 8 (p. 97-98, Table 31-32)

Masculine adjective genitive/prepositional endings depend on stem type: soft-root or ш/ж-root
adjectives take -его (genitive) and -ем (prepositional) — хоро́ший→до хоро́шего/в хоро́шем — while
the accusative for an inanimate-noun-modifying adjective matches the nominative regardless
(хоро́ший unchanged). Feminine soft/ш-ж-root adjectives take -ей for both genitive and
prepositional, and -ую for accusative (irregular-looking but consistent: хоро́шая→до хоро́шей/в
хоро́шей/в хоро́шую). Neuter adjectives share genitive/prepositional endings with masculine, and
their accusative always matches the nominative (since neuter nouns are inherently inanimate).
A closing worked table (p. 98) applies the whole four-case noun+adjective system to real named
Moscow locations (Большо́й теа́тр, Но́вый Арба́т, Белору́сский вокза́л, Тверска́я у́лица,
америка́нская фи́рма, Кра́сная пло́щадь), directly grounding the abstract paradigm in
culturally-real place names — useful concrete data for later slang-mechanics work on how place
names interact with the case system.
