**Grammar/cultural point — Russian nicknames (diminutives) (pp. 10–11).** Russian nicknames
(diminutives) are pervasive across registers, often diverging sharply from the full given name (e.g.
full name Мария → Маша, Машутка, Машуня, Маня, Манька, Мань, Машенька, Машка, Машушка, Манечка,
Манюня). Common diminutive-forming suffixes: -ка, -енка, -ечка, -очка, -ушка, -юшка; a further
truncated vocative-style form exists for direct address (Саш, Серёж, Тань). Both a male (Alexander)
and female (Alexandra) name can share one diminutive (Sasha). **Warning the author gives:** when in
doubt use the full name; only reciprocate a native speaker's shift to a nickname — using one
prematurely (e.g. Сашка) can read as rude/presumptuous.

**Cultural/grammar point — Дедовщина (hazing) and draft evasion (pp. 21–22).** Since WWII, Russian
conscripts have widely sought to dodge the draft, driven by low morale/pay and an enlisted-run
disciplinary structure (officers largely delegate discipline of new recruits to senior conscripts).
Hazing (дедовщина, lit. "grandfathering") includes physical/sexual abuse, sleep deprivation, and
harsh-weather hazing of new recruits by "deds." Bribery (взятки) to doctors, draft boards, or
universities is a widespread self-preservation strategy; once drafted, further bribes to officers or
sergeants buy favors, rations, or lighter duty.

**Cultural point — Vodka-drinking ritual (pp. 41–42).** Vodka production/consumption in Russia dates
to the 15th century; the Western stereotype of universal heavy drinking is overstated but toasting
ritual is genuinely central. Ritual: guests gather, host pours shots into рюмки/стопки (shot glasses),
never poured backhanded, vodka served chilled and always neat, a moment of reflection observed before
drinking. Standard shot sizes: 50g (small) to 200g ("über-shot"); сто грамм ("100 grams") is the
typical shot, consumed залпом (in one gulp); a late arrival must drink a штрафной (penalty shot). A
toast (see Ch. 4 toast list) always precedes drinking. Post-shot, Russians sniff bread (нюхать хлеб)
or eat закуски (pickles, herring, sausage) to offset the burn — believed to also slow alcohol
absorption, enabling more drinking. Hangover = похмелье; expressed as "Я с бодуна" or "Я с похмелья";
remedies include рассол (pickle brine) or опохмелиться (hair-of-the-dog).

**Cultural point — Russian toasts (pp. 55–57).** Toasting always precedes drinking. Sample toast
categories the source gives verbatim (paraphrased here, not quoted at length): toasts for
International Women's Day (8 марта), toasts for women's beauty/wit, toasts for men (wishing wealth
per one's wife, health per one's mother), toasts for love and money together, New Year toasts
("Goodbye! May we not see each other sober today!"), and short high-frequency toast formulas: за
здоровье (to your health), на брудершафт (to friendship, done arm-in-arm when shifting to ты), за нас
с вами и за чёрт с ними (to us, to hell with everyone else), за жён и любовниц чтобы они никогда не
встретились (to wives and mistresses, may they never meet), на посошок (one for the road).
