## Grammar points

### Chapter 11: The Dash (Тире)

The Russian dash performs a far wider range of functions than the English dash, most with no direct
English equivalent; several are **obligatory**, not merely stylistic, in Russian.

**§11.1 The dash used in place of a linking verb.** Because Russian typically lacks any present-tense
form of быть "to be," the dash stands in for the omitted copula in four structural contexts:

- **§11.1.1 Both subject and predicate are nouns**: Книга — источник знаний "A book is the source of
  knowledge" (p. 121). **A pause** in speech typically marks the omitted copula's place, corresponding
  to the written dash; when the dash is followed by an emphatic это or вот ("this is," untranslated
  filler), the pause is obligatory — especially common with abstract comparisons or an indefinite
  predicate pronoun like нечто "something" or всё "everything": Жизнь — это гармония "Life is
  harmony"; Уважение к минувшему — вот черта, отличающая образованность от дикости (p. 122).
- **§11.1.2 With verbal infinitives**: the dash substitutes for the copula when subject and predicate
  are both infinitives, or when the subject is a nominative noun phrase and the predicate an
  infinitive: Наша цель — добраться до горы засветло "Our goal is to reach the mountain before dark";
  Учить — ум точить (proverb, "To study is to sharpen the mind") (pp. 122–123).
- **§11.1.3 Predicate beginning with один из "one of"**: Москва — один из крупнейших городов мира
  "Moscow is one of the largest cities in the world." **Exception**: if один instead means "alone"
  rather than beginning "one of," no dash is used: Она одна "She is alone" (p. 123).
- **§11.1.4 With number words (числительные)**: dash used if subject or predicate (or both) is
  expressed by a number or a noun phrase containing one: Дважды два — четыре "Two times two is
  four"; Большая Медведица — семь ярких звёзд "The Great Bear is seven bright stars" (p. 123).

**§11.2 Types of sentences where NO dash separates subject and predicate nominal** — eight exception
classes (a–h) to the general rule above:

- (a) **Scientific/technical register**: when the predicate is a bare number phrase in scientific
  writing, no dash is used (Глубина залегания нефти 1 500 м), though neutral style still takes the
  dash (Глубина залегания нефти — 1 500 метров) (p. 124).
- (b) **An overt linking verb is present** (past/future был/будет, or есть, являться, оказаться, etc.)
  — no dash needed since the copula is no longer covert: Его дед был знаменитым врачом; Пословицы
  являются народной мудростью (p. 124).
- (c) **The predicate is a bare adjective with no noun** — dash needed only when BOTH subject and
  predicate contain nouns: Игорь способный "Igor is talented" (no dash) vs. Игорь — способный врач
  "Igor is a talented doctor" (dash, since врач is a noun) (p. 125). **However**, a series of parallel
  predicate adjectives, even bare ones, does take a dash, unlike English which uses no mark: Глаза у
  неё — огромные, чёрные, опушённые густыми ресницами (p. 125).
- (d) **The predicate is negated by не** — no dash regardless of whether subject/predicate are nouns,
  numbers, or infinitives: Бедность не порок "Poverty is not a vice" (contrast the affirmative А по
  его мнению, бедность — порок, which does take the dash) (p. 125).
- (e) **The predicate is a direct comparison** introduced by словно, как, что, как будто, точно, or
  any other word meaning "like/as": Дорога словно змея "The road is just like a snake"; Язык что нож
  "The tongue is like a knife" (pp. 125–126). **However**, the dash MAY be used as an optional
  stylistic device to signal an expressive pause even in comparison sentences — a device particularly
  favored by Maxim Gorky: Он — вор и обманщик! "He is nothing but a thief and a liar!" (emphatic,
  compare neutral Он прекрасный инженер) (p. 126).
- (f) **A parenthetical word, adverb, particle, conjunction, or non-coordinated modifier** stands
  between subject and predicate: Ртуть тоже металл "Mercury is also a metal" (no dash; compare bare
  Ртуть — металл, which does take one) (p. 126).
- (g) **The subject is a personal pronoun**: Они великие люди своей эпохи "They are great people of
  their time" (no dash). **However**, in a compound sentence expressing contrast, the dash IS used
  even with a pronominal subject: Она — врач, а он — физик "She is a doctor, while he is a physicist"
  (p. 127).
- (h) **Additional stylistic omissions**: (i) in simple everyday-topic sentences (Его мама врач "His
  mother is a doctor") — though the dash must be restored if genuine ambiguity would otherwise result
  (Сестра моя — учительница "My sister is a teacher" vs. Сестра — моя учительница "The sister is my
  teacher"); (ii) in emphatic "What a ___!" sentences with predicate preceding subject, the dash is
  ALWAYS omitted even if both elements are nouns/infinitives: Какой зануда этот Вася! "What a bore
  that Vasya is!"; (iii) when the comment (new information) is preposed to the topic (old
  information), likewise no dash: Большое счастье жить на земле! (reversing the order restores the
  dash: Директор завода — Григорий Андреевич) (pp. 127–128).

**§11.3 The dash in elliptic sentences (неполные предложения).** The dash marks the place of ANY
omitted predicate element (not just a copula), provided speech carries a corresponding pause: У
дорожки стояли дома. За ними — огороды "Houses stood by the road; behind them, vegetable gardens"
(full form: тянулись огороды). Because Russian's rich case/agreement morphology disambiguates
syntactic relationships even with words missing, Russian ellipsis can omit content VERBS in contexts
where English (having less overt morphology) cannot use comparable ellipsis and instead uses a
comma: Дети — в койку! "The kids have gotta go to bed!" (full form: Дети должны идти спать) (pp.
129–131).

**§11.4 The dash introducing a generalizing word (обобщающее слово) after a list of items**: Надежду
и пловца — всё море поглотило "Hope and swimmer — the sea swallowed it all." **Mirror-image rule**:
if the generalizing word instead PRECEDES the list, a COLON introduces the list instead of a dash:
Море поглотило всё: надежду и пловца (p. 131). If part of the generalizing phrase precedes the list
and part follows, a colon introduces the list and a dash closes it before the remaining generalizing
material: Все деревья в нашем саду: яблони, груши, сливы, вишни — посажены моим дедом (p. 132). A
list embedded in the MIDDLE of a generalizing phrase can be set off by a pair of dashes (neutral) or
colon-plus-dash (added emphasis) (p. 132).

**§11.5 Dash used in a compound sentence when an adversative conjunction is omitted** — asyndetic
(бессоюзное) contrast between two juxtaposed simple sentences, replacing а/но plus comma: Семь бед —
один ответ (proverb, "Seven misfortunes, one answer"); especially characteristic of proverbs,
aphorisms, and witty/ironic statements (pp. 132–133).

**§11.6 Dash used instead of a comma or comma plus coordinating conjunction in a compound sentence**
— used to emphasize that the second clause conveys unexpected information: Мяч влетел в ворота —
стадион зашумел "The ball flew through the goal and the stadium erupted" (neutral style would use a
comma, or comma plus и). **English does not use the dash this way** in compound sentences — it uses
a plain coordinating conjunction, semicolon, or separate sentences instead (pp. 133–134).

**§11.7 Dash used instead of comma plus subordinating conjunction in a complex sentence** — conveys
special brevity; the first clause is background information/topic (тема), the second (after the
dash) is the main idea/comment (рема). Grouped by conjunction type:

- **§11.7.1 Condition**: dash replaces если "if" — Ничем не рисковать — ничего не иметь "Don't take
  risks and you won't have anything" (full form: Если ничем не рисковать, то ничего не будешь иметь)
  (p. 134).
- **§11.7.2 Time**: dash replaces когда "when" — Настанет утро — двинемся в путь "Come morning we'll
  push on" (full form: Когда настанет утро, двинемся в путь) (p. 135).
- **§11.7.3 Result**: dash replaces поэтому "therefore" — Применили новую технику — повысилась
  производительность труда "A new technology was applied and productivity increased" (full form uses
  comma plus поэтому) (p. 136).
- **§11.7.4 Comparison of dashes and colons for cause/effect**: **the choice between dash and colon
  depends entirely on clause order.** If the CAUSE clause comes first, a DASH is used: Светает — надо
  вставать "It's getting light, so it's time to get up" (cause-then-effect order matches Поскольку
  светает, надо вставать). If the clause order is REVERSED — the RESULT stated first, cause second —
  a COLON is used instead: Надо вставать: светает "It's time to get up because it's getting light"
  (matches Надо вставать, потому что светает). This dash/colon mirror-symmetry based on clause order
  is a systematic pattern running through several of this chapter's rules (§11.4, §11.7.4) (p. 138).

**§11.8 The connective dash (соединительное тире) separating two words denoting space, time, or
quantity** — functional equivalent of the paired preposition от...до "from...to": На линии
Москва—Одесса ходит скоростной поезд "An express train runs the Moscow–Odessa line"; Приходи часов в
семь — восемь "Come around seven to eight" (p. 140). Such connective dashes carry no speech pause and
are typically printed without surrounding spaces between numerals (200—250 человек). **Related
compound-formation uses of the connective dash** (§11.8 Note 3, treated further in Ch. 15):
(a) two personal names juxtaposed to form a phenomenon/disease name — синдром Крейцфельта—Якоба
"Kreuzfeldt-Jakob syndrome"; (b) two personal names joined as a compound appositive naming an event —
матч Карпов—Каспаров "the Karpov vs. Kasparov match"; (c) compound modifiers from common nouns,
postposed and non-coordinated — отношения учитель—ученик "student-teacher relations." English uses a
hyphen, slash, or preposed conjunction/connecting word for all three of these, where Russian uses a
postposed dash-joined compound (pp. 140–141). A **hyphen of uncertainty** can replace the connective
dash when the quantity is spelled out in words and closely spaced (пять-шесть человек "five or six
people," replaceable by или); when the same quantity is given in numerals, a dash (not hyphen) is
used instead: 5—6 человек (p. 141).

**§11.9 The explanatory dash (тире пояснительное)** introducing material that elaborates on a
preceding word's meaning, glossable as "that is" or "more precisely": У новгородцев были свои враги
— варяги "The Novgorodians had their own enemies, the Varangians." English typically uses a comma for
the same function, switching to a dash only when the elaborating material is internally complex or
emotionally charged. When the sentence continues after the explanatory material, Russian typically
uses a PAIR of dashes to enclose it, though a pair of commas is possible for a short interruption (pp.
142–143).

**§11.10 Dashes setting off an inserted element pronounced with special emotive intonation** — a
paired dash marks an abrupt, expressively-marked interruption; the enclosed material may itself end
in an exclamation point or question mark (otherwise reserved for sentence-final position): Как вдруг
— о чудо, о позор! — заговорил оракул вздор (p. 143). The book notes this usage is becoming more
common in English as well.

**§11.11 Heterogeneous combinations of comma and dash** — several contexts require BOTH marks
together, each retaining its own separate syntactic function, with the dash always following the
comma:

- (a) A comma-plus-dash occurs when a subject phrase already ends in a comma-set dependent clause
  (per Ch. 7's rule) AND the dash-for-omitted-copula rule (§11.1) also applies: Знания, которые вы
  освоили,— это великая сила "The knowledge you have gained is a great power" (p. 143).
- (b) Comma-plus-dash is used when a list of items preceding a generalizing word itself ends with a
  dependent clause or participial construction — the comma closes the dependent clause, the dash
  introduces the generalizing word: Перегрузки организма, состояние невесомости, излучение,
  пронизывающее космическое пространство,— таковы воздействия, которым подвергается организм... (p.
  144).
- (c) Comma-plus-dash likewise occurs whenever an explanatory phrase (§11.9) follows a dependent
  clause that is itself comma-set (p. 144).
