"translation" drill — dozens of near-identical exercises pairing an official/bookish word or
construction with its colloquial or neutral equivalent, drawn from genre-specific official texts
(library rules, metro-museum informational brochures, advertisements, medicine-label instructions,

(the section's actual payload) while treating the repeated "find the official variant in this text"
reading-comprehension drills as structurally identical instances of the same mechanism, sampled

## Grammar points

### The official-vs-colloquial "translation" drill method

The chapter's actual pedagogical structure, repeated across dozens of exercises: present a short
genuine official-register text (library-usage rules, a museum's informational brochure, a metro
advertisement, a medicine-label insert, an academic-polemic discourse formula list) and have the
learner (a) find/underline every official-style word or construction, (b) supply its colloquial or
neutral equivalent, and (c) in several exercises, actually rewrite the whole passage in colloquial
register to test whether the transformation is idiomatic, not just word-for-word. The book's own
running commentary (p.53, p.60, p.72) frames this as a **two-register system** every foreign learner
of Russian needs (official/bookish and colloquial), deliberately not subdividing official register
into its traditional three sub-styles (scientific, publicistic/media, official-business) for
pedagogical purposes, even though it acknowledges linguistically that division exists.

### Academic-polemic discourse formulas (pp.66-72)

A dense, largely self-contained sub-section teaches a bank of set phrases for scholarly argument and
counter-argument — hedging (*разумеется*, *безусловно*, *несомненно*, *очевидно*, *вероятно*,
*возможно*, *по всей видимости*), disagreement (*не могу согласиться с вами, что...*, *трудно
согласиться с тем, что...*, *едва ли...*), requesting clarification (*пожалуйста, уточните...*,
*что вы имеете в виду, когда говорите...*), and closing/summarizing a discussion (*итак, мы можем
констатировать следующее...*, *таким образом*, *подведём итоги*). The source explicitly targets
this bank at TRKI-2-and-above test sections ("Лексика. Грамматика," "Письмо," "Говорение") rather
than general conversational competence — i.e., this is deliberately narrow academic-register
material, not everyday colloquial content, and is noted here mainly because it completes the
official-register inventory the chapter builds, not because it is slang-adjacent.

### Chapter close (p.72)

The book explicitly reiterates, in its own words, that it has deliberately treated "official style"
as a single undifferentiated register for RKI (Russian-as-a-foreign-language) teaching purposes,
collapsing scientific/official-business/publicistic distinctions that a native-speaker-facing
stylistics text would keep separate — an explicit simplification decision worth noting for anyone
using this book's register labels as ground truth elsewhere in this project's analysis.
