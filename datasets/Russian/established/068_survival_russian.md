## Grammar points

### The Russian Alphabet (printed pp. 10-11 / PDF 12-13)

A 33-letter Cyrillic alphabet table (Printed form | Handwritten cursive form | Letter name | IPA-ish
sound | closest English-sound analogy). Notable pedagogical simplifications: ъ ("hard sign") and ь
("soft sign") are listed with no sound column of their own — they modify the preceding consonant's
palatalization rather than representing an independent phoneme, consistent with Russian's
fusional/six-case morphology where softness distinctions are morphologically load-bearing (e.g.
soft-stem vs. hard-stem noun declension classes referenced later in the book's own Grammar Tables
appendix, per the TOC). е/ё are treated as a stressed/unstressed near-pair, and я/ю are shown as
iotated vowels ([йа]/[йу]), foreshadowing the source's likely treatment of vowel reduction under
Lesson-chapter stress marking (acute accents appear throughout the reference list below, e.g.
телефо́н, консистent with a pedagogical stress-marking convention rather than genuine orthography).
