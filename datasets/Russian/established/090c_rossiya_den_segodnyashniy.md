## Chapter 3 — «Жить без штампов» ("Living Without [Passport] Stamps")

Printed pp. 20–23 (PDF pp. 23–25). A longer, multi-section article on common-law marriage (гражданский брак) — subsections «Что изменит штамп?», «Глазами мужчины», «Глазами женщины», «Чем гражданский брак хуже?», «А если дети?», «Итого». Source note: adapted from N. Radulova, *Огонёк*, No. 16, 2004. This is the book's first genuinely register-rich chapter — several colloquial idioms and one repurposed military/bureaucratic term (**злостный уклонист**) worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase.

## Grammar points

### чем..., тем... correlative comparative

Illustrated by "чем дольше длится... тем меньше вероятность..." ("the longer X lasts, the less likely Y becomes"). Both clauses front a comparative form (дольше, меньше) governed by чем/тем respectively — a standard, high-frequency Russian correlative construction directly parallel to English "the more..., the more..." Worth noting as a construction the synthesis phase may want a conlang analog for, since it's extremely productive in Russian argumentative/journalistic prose.

### Substantivized adjective as a group-label (continues Chapter 1's pattern)

«Гражданские» here nominalizes and pluralizes an adjective to mean "people in common-law marriages," extending the same substantivization pattern already noted for учёный/домашние in Chapter 1 — but this instance is distinctly colloquial/journalistic rather than a long-lexicalized noun, suggesting the pattern is still synchronically productive for coining new informal group-labels, not just a closed set of inherited nouns.
