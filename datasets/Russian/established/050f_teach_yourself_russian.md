## Grammar points

### 1. Что тебе нужно? (what do you need?)
The person who needs something is expressed in the dative case; literal meaning is "what to you is
necessary?" (p.75).

### 2. Домой vs. дома vs. в доме/в дом
Дом ("house/home") has special irregular forms when it means "home" as a place-concept: дома ("at
home," static location — the regular prepositional would be "в доме," meaning literally "in the
house") and домой ("to home," motion — distinct from the regular accusative-of-motion "в дом,"
literally "into the house") (p.75).

### 3. Numerals — compounds of the tens/hundreds
Full list from 20 to 1000 given (двадцать, тридцать, сорок, пятьдесят, шестьдесят, семьдесят,
восемьдесят, девяносто, сто; сто десять, двести, триста, четыреста, пятьсот, шестьсот, семьсот,
восемьсот, девятьсот, тысяча). Compounds are formed without hyphens. Compounds ending in 2/3/4
still take the genitive singular noun (тридцать два билета — "32 tickets"); compounds ending in 1
take the nominative singular, agreeing as if just "1" (тридцать один билет — "31 tickets") (p.75).

### 4. По + accusative for "price per item"
По followed by the accusative case of a number expresses "X each": Эти книги по 100 рублей ("these
books are 100 roubles each") (p.76).

### 5. Adjectives — genitive singular
Masculine and neuter share endings (as with nouns); feminine has its own. Ordinary adjectives
(старый-type) take -ого (masc./neut.) or -ой (fem.) — pronounced "-ovo," not "-ogo." Adjectives
whose stem ends in ж/ч/ц/ш/щ (which can never precede an unstressed о per the general spelling
rule) instead take -его (masc./neut.) / -ей (fem.) — e.g. хороший → хорошего/хорошей. Мой, твой,
наш, ваш take the same -его/-ей-type endings (p.76).

### 6. Adjectives — genitive plural
Much simpler than nouns: only two possible endings regardless of gender, -ых (default) or -их (if
the adjective's stem ends in a letter that can never precede unstressed ы: г/к/х/ж/ч/ш/щ) — e.g.
старый → старых, маленький → маленьких (p.77).

### 7. Nouns — genitive plural
More complex than adjectives since the ending depends on gender and there are more than two
options. Feminine nouns in -а and neuter nouns in -о simply drop the final vowel; when this creates
a final consonant cluster, a fleeting о or е is often inserted (марка→марок, окно→окон,
девушка→девушек [е inserted after ш], деньги→денег [е replaces a soft sign]) — occasionally ё
appears instead (сестра→сестёр); some clusters need no inserted vowel at all (место→мест).
Masculine nouns add -ов (билет→билетов), -ей (soft-sign/й-stem: автомобиль→автомобилей,
трамвай→трамваев uses -ев after й) — full remove/add table given by gender. Feminine soft-sign
nouns add -ей (тетрадь→тетрадей), -я-stem nouns (неделя, станция) add -ь or -й. Neuter -е nouns
add -ей (поле→полей) or -й (здание→зданий). Irregular nominative-plural nouns also have irregular
genitive plurals: ребёнок/дети→детей, друг/друзья→друзей, человек/люди→людей (pp.77–79).

### 8. Accusative plural
Follows the same animacy split already seen in the singular: inanimate plural nouns/adjectives are
identical in form to the nominative plural; animate plural nouns/adjectives are identical in form
to the genitive plural. E.g. Я очень люблю читать русские газеты (inanimate, = nom. pl.) vs. Я
очень люблю смотреть русских актёров (animate, = gen. pl.) (p.79).
