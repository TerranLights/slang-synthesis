## Grammar points

### боле́ть/заболе́ть, пить/вы́пить, есть/съесть, спать, простуди́ться, чу́вствовать(ся) себя́, принима́ть/приня́ть — Lesson 11 (p. 155-158, Tables 47-53)

Seven more full НСВ/СВ (or single-aspect) conjugation tables. есть is confirmed irregular
(ем/ешь/ест/еди́м/еди́те/едя́т — an archaic athematic-looking conjugation distinct from every
regular Conjugation I/II pattern given so far, closer in shape to Old Church Slavonic's athematic
verb class); its perfective съесть follows the same irregular pattern with a съ- prefix. спать is
likewise irregular in the 1sg (сплю, not the plain **-аю** a naive reading of the infinitive might
predict). A genuinely useful negative-imperative note appears at Table 47: **не боле́й!/не
боле́йте!** ("don't get sick!") uses the *imperfective* infinitive under negation even though the
perfective за-боле́ть exists — the source doesn't explain this explicitly, but it's consistent with
the general Russian tendency (not previously stated in this book) to prefer imperfective aspect
under negated imperatives, since a negated command more often warns against an ongoing state/
process than forbids a single completed event.

### Dative case after motion verbs with к — Lesson 11 (p. 159)

A previously-unstated extension of the dative case: beyond звони́ть/покупа́ть-type verbs (Lessons
6-7) and the possession/age constructions, the dative also appears after verbs of motion when the
destination is a *person* rather than a place, marked with the preposition к (идти́ к врачу́,
е́хать к подру́ге) — answering куда́? or к кому́?, distinct from the plain accusative-of-place
destination pattern (Lesson 8's в/на + accusative) used when the destination is a location rather
than a person. This is a genuine person-vs-place distinction in how Russian marks the goal of
motion, directly comparable to (though independent of) the earlier animacy-driven case splits
already flagged for the accusative.

### Impersonal construction, extended — Lesson 11 (p. 159), explicitly cross-referencing Lesson 10

Restates and drills the Lesson 10 хо́чется/ка́жется + dative + infinitive pattern with new lexical
material (Я хочу́ спать → Мне хо́чется спать "I want to sleep" → "I am sleepy"; Я хочу́ есть → Мне
хо́чется есть "I want to eat" → "I am hungry"), confirming this is a fully general
personal-vs-impersonal alternation applicable to any volitional verb + infinitive frame, not a
closed set of fixed idioms — directly useful for later mechanics analysis of how Russian
systematically encodes agentive-will vs. involuntary-condition through this reflexive/dative
alternation rather than through separate lexical items.

### Decimal-number reading convention for temperature — Lesson 11 (p. 159, "Remember")

Body/weather temperatures with a decimal point are read as [whole number] и [decimal digit(s)]
(e.g. 36,6 → три́дцать шесть и шесть, 37,1 → три́дцать семь и одна́), i.e. literally "thirty-six
and six" rather than any dedicated decimal-point terminology — а lexically minimal, purely
additive way of reading fractional numbers that could plausibly generalize to other decimal
contexts in the language, worth flagging as a small but genuine numeral-system data point.
