## Grammar points

### Three-column religion/denomination derivational paradigm
p.141's "Религии мира" table is a systematically presented derivational paradigm: for each of six
world religions/denominations, the source gives the practitioner noun (often gendered, e.g.
христианин/христианка), the abstract doctrine/system noun (христианство), and the adjective
(христианский) side by side. This is a clean, reusable template for any later
mechanics-analysis pass on Russian's practitioner↔doctrine↔adjective derivational triad — worth
treating as a model pattern rather than isolated vocabulary.

### Irregular locative/nominal forms flagged by the source
Several nouns in this chapter get explicit irregularity call-outs from the source itself: рай/ад
use the archaic locative-in -у (в раю, в аду) rather than the regular prepositional; церковь drops
its fill vowel in oblique forms (церкви, gen. pl. церквей); христиане/мусульмане use the -ане/-яне
collective-plural pattern with irregular genitive plurals (христиан, мусульман) rather than a
regular -ов/-ей ending. These are all worth preserving as concrete evidence of Russian's fusional
irregularity clusters around a semantically coherent domain (religious-affiliation nouns).

### An explicit register/usage-frequency note inside a "core" vocabulary list
The еврей/иудей footnote is a rare case of the source explicitly commenting on which of two
near-synonyms is actually used in ordinary Russian (еврей, ethnicity-primary) versus which is
technically correct but rarely spoken (иудей, religion-practitioner-primary) — the same kind of
explicit register annotation the coverage rule prioritizes, here appearing as a footnote rather than
a labeled slang/jargon box.
