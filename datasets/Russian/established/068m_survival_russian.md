## Grammar points

### занима́ться/увлека́ться/интересова́ться/гото́вить/пригото́вить conjugations — Lesson 14 (p. 206-207, Tables 66-68)

Three more reflexive instrumental-government verbs plus one more НСВ/СВ pair, extending the pattern
already seen for нра́виться (dative) and занима́ться (Lesson 3/9) — интересова́ться and увлека́ться
both conjugate as regular -ова- class reflexives (и́нтересу́юсь, увлека́юсь), following the same
stem-drops-ова-in-present rule already flagged for рисова́ть/танцева́ть in Lesson 13.

### Instrumental case — full noun and adjective paradigm, singular and plural — Lesson 14 (p. 208-209, Tables 69-71)

Consolidates and completes the instrumental case coverage begun in Lesson 12: masculine nouns
-ом/-ем (спорт→спо́ртом, хокке́й→хокке́ем — velar/sibilant-final stems take -ем per spelling rule);
feminine -ой/-ей (му́зыка→му́зыкой, исто́рия→исто́рией); neuter -ом (иску́сство→иску́сством); plural
-ами/-ями uniformly across genders (ша́хматы→шахма́тами, ма́рки→ма́рками). Adjectives: masculine/
neuter singular -ым (but -им after a soft/velar-final stem — ру́сский→ру́сским); feminine singular
-ой (or -ей after a soft/sibilant stem); plural -ыми (or -ими after a stem whose nominative plural
ends in -ие). A closing combined table (Table 71) directly applies this to real
adjective+noun phrases (совреме́нный спорт→совреме́нным спо́ртом, ру́сская исто́рия→ру́сской
исто́рией), completing the instrumental-case system across every gender/number/adjective
combination the book covers.

### Prepositional case — plural nouns and adjectives — Lesson 14 (p. 210, Tables 72-73), the book's last major case-paradigm table

Plural nouns take -ах (teáтры→(в) театра́х, стра́ны→(о) стра́нах) or **-ях** specifically when
the nominative plural itself ends in -и or -я (друзья́→(о) друзья́х, кни́ги→(о) кни́гах — note
кни́ги still takes -ах despite ending in -и, since the -ях/-ах split is conditioned by the stem's
own hardness/softness, not simply the surface -и/-ы spelling; the source's own "Remember" note is
slightly imprecise on this point, worth flagging as a place where the book's own generalization is
looser than the actual conditioning environment). Plural adjectives take -ых (но́вые→но́вых) or
**-их** when the nominative plural ends in -ие (интере́сные→интере́сных — again apparently governed
by stem softness rather than surface -ие itself, since интере́сные in this very table takes -ых
not -их despite superficially looking like it should trigger the -ие rule as stated; a genuine
internal inconsistency between the book's stated rule and its own worked example, worth flagging as
a real gap in this source's own precision rather than a extraction error). This table completes the
book's declension coverage: by Lesson 14, all six cases (nominative, genitive, dative, accusative,
instrumental, prepositional) have been given full singular-and-plural, noun-and-adjective paradigm
tables across the preceding fourteen lessons.
