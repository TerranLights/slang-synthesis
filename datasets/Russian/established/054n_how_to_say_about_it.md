## Grammar points

### Giving advice: five distinct construction types (§3.1, pp. 224–229)

The subsection assembles a wider inventory of advice-giving forms than any prior "suggestion"-type
section in the book, organized loosely by directness/formality:

1. **Bare Советую (+inf)** and its negative counterpart, with an aspect rule flagged explicitly (p.
   224): action advised *against* takes an **imperfective** infinitive (советую не делать), mirroring
   the aspect rule already seen for negated joint-action proposals in §2.4.
2. **Conditional framing**: Я бы сделал / Я бы на твоём месте сделал (p. 226) — casts the advice as
   "what I myself would do," a distancing, less directive register than a bare imperative.
3. **Question-form advice**, structurally identical to the joint-action-proposal templates from §2.4
   but addressed to the interlocutor alone: Не + inf(perfective) ли вам/тебе? / Почему бы вам/тебе не
   + inf(perfective)? / А что, если вам/тебе + inf(perfective)? (p. 226).
4. **Uncertainty-marked advice**: Может быть, вы сделаете/тебе + inf (p. 228) — explicitly glossed as
   "неуверенный совет" (unsure/tentative advice).
5. **-ка-suffixed softened imperative and conditional-wish forms**: Сделай(те)-ка... ("мягкий совет" —
   gentle advice) and Сделали бы вы.../Сделал бы ты... ("совет-пожелание" — advice framed as a wish),
   both p. 228. The productive **-ка** particle attached directly to an imperative form is worth
   flagging morphologically — one of the few morpheme-level colloquial particles the book calls out
   explicitly by name rather than folding silently into a generic "colloquial imperative" gloss.

### Agreeing to follow advice (§3.2, pp. 229–230)

The book notes (p. 230) that agreement-to-advice largely reuses the same acceptance forms already
catalogued for joint-action proposals in §2.5, adding two further distinctions: **конечно** marks full
agreement that the action is genuinely necessary, while **да, пожалуй** signals the addressee still
harbors some doubt about whether the action is really called for, even while going along with it — a
finer-grained wedge between "yes" and "yes, but" than most of the book's other binary agree/disagree
tables draw.

### Refusing to follow advice: a three-tier gradient (§3.3, pp. 230–232)

A three-tier refusal taxonomy is laid out in the section's closing framed table (p. 231), the most
explicitly graded refusal system encountered in the book so far:

1. **Soft refusal, expressing doubt rather than flat rejection**: Какой смысл?/(А) зачем?/(А) стоит
   ли?/К чему? — "не говорится категорическое 'нет', а выражается сомнение в целесообразности
   действия."
2. **Categorical refusal**: Ни за что (на свете)!/И не подумаю.
3. **Sharp, dismissive refusal, register-restricted to informal speech only**: Очень надо./Охота
   была./Ещё чего!/Вот ещё! — the source explicitly restricts this tier: "такой ответ возможен только
   в неофициальной ситуации," paralleling the same kind of explicit register-flag already seen for Да
   брось(ты)! in Раздел II §8.
