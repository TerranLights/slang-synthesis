## Grammar points

### Lesson 14 (§C): the use of что; verbal adverbs (gerunds)

**Что as a subordinator/substantivizing particle is largely standard**, though the exclamatory/
emphatic particle use (Ты что, с ума сошёл? — "Have you gone nuts, or what?") is a useful
colloquial-register note not always flagged alongside что's more grammatical uses. **Gerund
(деепричастие) formation is largely redundant** with prior full treatments in this corpus, though
the book's explicit framing — gerunds are common in written/formal register and should mainly be
learned for recognition rather than active production — is a clear register data-point, paralleling
the same register split already noted for participles in Lesson 10 (file 076d).

### Lesson 15 (§C): indefinite numerals (сколько/столько/несколько/много/мало); interjections

**Indefinite-numeral case-government rules are largely redundant** with prior sources. The
мало-based idiom family (see Vocabulary above) is a genuinely useful non-redundant find — a single
quantifier word spinning off five distinct fixed idioms, a recurring pattern in this book (parallel
to хватать, терпение, каша, and старина/старый idiom families found elsewhere in these lessons).
**The interjection/onomatopoeia system (§2) is the lesson's real find** — a clean, complete,
two-way-classified closed inventory (feeling-expressing vs. command-inducing) plus a productive
onomatopoeia→verb derivational pathway, not previously tabulated this completely elsewhere in the
corpus and directly relevant to the project's sound-symbolism tracking for the derived conlang.

### Lesson 16 (§C): verbal prefixes (в-/вз-/вы-/до-/за-/из-/на-/о-/от-/пере-/под-/при-/про-/раз-/с-/у-); the infinitive

**The verbal-prefix inventory is a genuinely valuable, not-fully-redundant consolidation** — a
complete list of Russian's ~15 core verbal prefixes with their directional/aspectual meanings,
demonstrated systematically against a single base verb (бросать/бросить, "to throw") to show how
each prefix changes its meaning (вбросить "to throw in," выбросить "to throw out," добросить "to
throw as far as," забросить "to toss back," набросить "to toss over," отбросить "to cast aside,"
перебросить "to throw across," подбросить "to toss under," разбросать "to squander/scatter,"
сбросить "to throw off"). This one-verb-many-prefixes demonstration is a clean, complete worked
example not seen gathered this systematically elsewhere in the corpus, and is directly useful for
the project's own morphological-mechanism modeling (a single root + a closed set of directional/
aspectual prefixes as a productive word-formation layer). **Infinitive-usage rules (imperfective vs.
perfective after various verb classes, imperative use of the bare imperfective infinitive for
official prohibitions like "Не курить" — "No smoking") are largely redundant** with prior sources.

### Lesson 16, Строго по делу: Social Etiquette DO'S/DON'TS

Not a grammar point, but worth flagging as a compact, actionable cultural-pragmatics checklist:
remove shoes indoors (тапочки provided); check coats at cultural venues; never shake hands over a
threshold; never whistle indoors; never pour wine back-handed (considered insulting); never make
the "thumb between first two fingers" gesture (considered obscene); avoid showing shoe soles/
propping feet up; avoid rough language in company; never light a cigarette from a candle (considered
bad luck). A clean, compact folk-superstition/etiquette inventory, useful reference for any
in-universe social-taboo modeling exercise.
