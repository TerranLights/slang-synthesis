## Grammar points

### Uni-directional vs. multi-directional motion verbs — Lesson 12 (p. 176), the book's first explicit statement

A dedicated "For reference" explanation, long overdue given how heavily идти́/е́хать have already
been used since Lesson 8: Russian motion verbs come in **paired sets** distinguishing (a) whether
direction is specified and (b) whether the motion is single-direction or habitual/repeated/
multi-direction. **Uni-directional verbs** (идти́, е́хать) describe motion actually happening in
one direction at the moment of speaking, or a direct question about motion-in-progress (Куда́ вы
идёте?). **Multi-directional verbs** (ходи́ть, е́здить) describe motion in different directions
at once (Лю́ди хо́дят по у́лице — "People are walking [around/various directions] on the street"),
habitual/repeated motion (Ма́ма ка́ждый день хо́дит на рабо́ту), or — notably — a **single
completed round trip** (Вчера́ я ходи́ла в теа́тр "Yesterday I went to the theater [there and
back]"), the last of which is a genuinely counterintuitive usage worth flagging: multi-directional
ходи́ть/е́здить, despite their name, are the correct choice for a single past errand precisely
*because* the round-trip itself involves two directions (there, then back), not because the event
was habitual.

### по-/при- directional-prefix system on motion verbs — Lesson 12 (p. 178, "Remember")

Confirms explicitly what earlier lessons only demonstrated through individual worked forms:
attaching the prefix **по-** to a uni-directional motion verb (идти́→пойти́, е́хать→пое́хать)
creates its perfective counterpart, marking "the beginning of a movement or the beginning of a new
stage of the movement" (a genuinely aspectual, not purely directional, use of по-). Attaching
**при-** (идти́→прийти́, е́хать→прие́хать) instead marks "the result of the movement," i.e.
arrival. Crucially, **prefixed multi-directional verbs (приходи́ть, приезжа́ть) stay
imperfective** rather than becoming perfective — the prefix's aspectual effect is conditioned by
which motion-verb stem (uni- vs. multi-directional) it attaches to, giving a four-cell system
(bare uni-directional = "in progress now"; по- + uni-directional = perfective "set off"; bare
multi-directional = "habitual/multi-direction/round-trip"; при- + multi-directional = imperfective
"arriving habitually/repeatedly"), with при- + uni-directional (прийти́/прие́хать) supplying the
perfective "arrived [this once]" member. This prefix system is one of the most productive
derivational mechanisms in the whole book and a strong candidate for direct relevance to
slang-mechanics work on prefix-driven meaning shifts in an invented conlang.

### ложи́ться/лечь — Lesson 12 (p. 170, Table 54)

Another suppletive НСВ/СВ pair (ложи́-/ляг-, unrelated roots), directly comparable to брать/взять
(Lesson 9) and уйти́/вы́йти (Lesson 7) — confirming suppletion recurs across this book's aspect
pairs often enough to be a real, recognized irregularity class rather than a handful of isolated
exceptions.

### Instrumental case — Lesson 12 (p. 173-174, Table 57), full noun paradigm

Masculine: -ом (hard-stem: студе́нт→студе́нтом) or -ем (-ь/-й-final stems: преподава́тель→
преподава́телем, Алексе́й→Алексе́ем); plural -ами/-ями by the same hard/soft split, with a
suppletive-looking друг→друзья́ми (irregular plural stem друзь-, not друг-ами). Feminine: -ой
(hard -а stems: подру́га→подру́гой) or -ей (я-stems or ш/ж/ч/щ-root stems: Та́ня→Та́ней); -ью for
-ь-final nouns (тетра́дь→тетра́дью — a distinctively longer ending than any other declension class
gives its instrumental); plural uniformly -ами (-ями for soft/tetrad-type). Neuter: -ом (hard:
письмо́→письмо́м) or -ем (soft: мо́ре not directly shown but inferable); plural -ами/-ями. Two
distinct semantic uses of instrumental с + noun are explicitly footnoted: accompaniment/
"with whom" (иду́ в кино́ с дру́гом) versus a descriptive "characterized by" sense answering како́й?
(бутербро́д с сы́ром — "a sandwich [characterized by/containing] cheese") — a genuine polysemy in
the instrumental preposition с worth flagging as two distinct grammatical functions sharing one
surface form.
