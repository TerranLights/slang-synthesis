## Grammar points

### Antonym-pair organizing structure (pp.137-144)

Confirms the structural shift noted at the end of `066f`: this book's remaining adjective content is
organized as spatial/gradable antonym pairs sharing one numbered sense-structure (a single "1)"
heading covers both a) the БЛИЗКИЙ pole and б) the ДАЛЁКИЙ pole of the same sense), rather than the
derivational-suffix families used pp.39-132. Three such pairs appear in this shard: БЛИЗКИЙ/ДАЛЁКИЙ
(distance and time), the doubly-nested БЛИЖНИЙ/ДАЛЬНИЙ (a second, more narrowly relative distance
pair the book treats as a distinct lexical problem from БЛИЗКИЙ/ДАЛЁКИЙ despite the shared root),
ВЫСОКИЙ/НИЗКИЙ (vertical extent, abstract magnitude, pitch, and moral register), and ВЕРХНИЙ/НИЖНИЙ
(relative vertical position and inner/outer clothing layering).

### Explicit lexical-gap notes ("Обратите внимание") as register/usage data

This shard's "Обратите внимание" notes are unusually rich in explicit *non-usage* claims (a headword
is flagged as NOT used in a particular sense its antonym partner does cover) — e.g. БЛИЗКИЙ is not
used in ДАЛЁКИЙ's "happened long ago" or "takes a long time" readings; БЛИЗКИЙ/ДАЛЬНИЙ (not
ДАЛЁКИЙ) is the pair used specifically for degree-of-kinship. These gap-flags are themselves a form
of explicit usage-constraint annotation and are exactly the kind of fine-grained collocational-
restriction data the coverage rule's register/annotation clause is meant to preserve, even though
they document an *absence* of use rather than a register label per se.

### Register notes in this shard

ВЫСОКИЙ's sense 5 (elevated importance: высокий гость, высокий пост) is explicitly tagged `(книжн.)`
— bookish/elevated register — a further confirmed instance of a single headword's senses splitting
across register lines (joining ГОРДЫЙ, ЧЕЛОВЕЧИЙ, and КРОВНЫЙ from earlier shards). Within НИЗКИЙ's
sense 6, the specific diminutive form душонка ("a petty/base little soul") is register-marked
(`разг.`-flavored diminutive) inside an otherwise neutral headword — a finer-grained instance of the
same pattern, at the level of a single derived collocate rather than a whole numbered sense.
