## Grammar points

### Attracting special attention to a reported fact (§5, pp. 120–123)

The book presents А (ты) знаешь, ...? / А (вы) знаете, ...? as the default construction for framing
an upcoming statement as something the speaker considers unusual or noteworthy enough to merit the
addressee's special attention — distinguished from §1's plain "attention-getter" openers by the
initial «А», which the source treats as adding a marked, "guess-what" quality. The information itself
is typically phrased as a question containing a wh-word (А ты знаешь, кто жил в этом доме?) but can
also appear as a что-clause (А ты знаешь, что в этом доме жил Пушкин?). A parallel construction, И
знаешь, ...?, is reserved specifically for *continuing* an already-opened piece of news with a second,
associatively linked fact — the source frames this second form as adding "попутная информация"
(incidental information that occurred to the speaker along the way) rather than opening a fresh topic
(pp. 121–122). A worked historical-Moscow passage (pp. 124–126) is used as a drilling text for this
construction, cross-referencing real historical facts (founding of Moscow 1147, Ivan Kalita, Dmitry
Donskoy, Ivan III) purely as exercise material, not as linguistically distinct content.

### Poputnoe (incidental) remarks and topic-switching: кстати / между прочим / впрочем (§6.1, pp. 126–131)

A three-way contrast is drawn between superficially similar digression-markers. Кстати marks a
*directly, argumentatively relevant* aside — the source's own gloss: the second statement can serve
as an argument supporting the first (e.g. "hoping you'll call Natasha, by the way [кстати] ask if she
needs this book" — the кстати-clause explains/motivates the phone call). Между прочим instead marks a
*loosely associated, low-importance* aside — content-wise a genuine tangent the speaker's mind
wandered to, unconnected to any argumentative purpose. Впрочем is functionally different from both:
it introduces a remark that *retracts or cancels* the speaker's own immediately preceding statement
(e.g. proposing to bring an umbrella, then впрочем-marking a retraction: "actually, it won't rain").
The source's own summary line (p. 128): второе высказывание "как бы зачёркивает, сводит на нет"
(effectively crosses out, nullifies) the first.

### Sudden recall mid-conversation: чуть было не забыл vs. ах да (§6.2, pp. 132–134)

Three near-synonymous "oh, I just remembered" formulas are distinguished by *what kind* of thing is
recalled. Да, чуть было не забыл(-а) (сказать) marks recall of something the speaker *intended,
planned, or was obligated* to say/do — an action or communicative intent, not a bare fact. Ах да, ...
marks recall of something merely *known* to the speaker (a fact, not an intention) that becomes newly
relevant to the conversation. Да, совсем забыл(-а), ... is presented as neutral between the two —
usable in either situation, without disambiguating which kind of recall is involved (p. 133) — the
book treats this as a genuine, unresolved ambiguity in the construction rather than an oversight in
its own analysis.

### Resuming an interrupted train of thought (§6.3, p. 135–136)

A cluster of resumption formulas (Так вот, .../О чём я говорил? Ах да!/Так на чём я остановился?
Да!/Так о чём я? ... Ах да! (Так вот) .../Да, так о чём я? ... Ах, о ...) is presented as the toolkit
for returning to a narrative thread broken off by an interruption — a question from the interlocutor,
an unrelated event that seized the speaker's attention, or a request that pulled the speaker away
mid-explanation. The book's own framed box (p. 136) presents these as interchangeable variants of the
same functional move rather than assigning each a distinct nuance, unlike the finer-grained
distinctions drawn for the other construction families in this section.

### Drawing a conclusion into the dialogue: three kinds of inference-marking (§7, pp. 137–140)

The book distinguishes three functionally distinct ways a "conclusion, drawn from what was said or
observed" gets folded into a reply, laid out in a three-row framed comparison table (p. 138):

1. **Значит, ... / Стало быть, ...** — "истолкование ситуации" (interpreting the situation): the
   speaker works out and states an inference from the facts just discussed (e.g. train leaves at 8,
   travel takes half an hour → значит, we should leave home around 7).
2. **То есть ... / Надо понимать, (что) ... / Ты хочешь сказать, что ... / Что ты хочешь сказать? ... /
   Иными словами, ...** — "истолкование слов собеседника" (interpreting the interlocutor's own
   words): the speaker restates what the other person just said in the speaker's own, allegedly
   clearer terms, without adding new inferential content — a paraphrase-check, not a new deduction.
3. **Короче (говоря), ... / Одним словом, ...** — "обобщение сказанного" (summarizing what was said):
   compresses a longer, more discursive statement from the interlocutor into a short, blunt
   restatement, explicitly framed by the source as the speaker "trying to express more briefly and
   clearly what the interlocutor said rather verbosely" (p. 138).

The three categories are functionally non-interchangeable even though several members share a
colloquial register — Значит answers "what follows," То есть answers "did I understand you right,"
and Короче/Одним словом answers "can this be said more briefly."
