## Grammar points

### Systematic consonant-mutation classes in short-form comparatives (pp. 56–58)

The source tabulates short-form comparatives (the invariable comparative used predicatively/
adverbially, e.g. бли́же 'closer') by **consonant-mutation class**, which is a more systematic
presentation than a simple alphabetical list: в→вл (кра́сивый → краси́вее is regular, but this
class covers cases like дешёвый→деше́вле), г→ж (дорого́й→доро́же, стро́гий→стро́же), д→ж
(молодо́й→моло́же, твёрдый→твёрже), з→ж (бли́зкий→бли́же, ни́зкий→ни́же, у́зкий→у́же), к→ч
(гро́мкий→гро́мче, кре́пкий→кре́пче, лёгкий→ле́гче, мя́гкий→мя́гче), с→ш (высо́кий→вы́ше,
то́лстый→то́лще groups partly here), ст→щ (просто́й→про́ще, чи́стый→чи́ще, густо́й→гу́ще), т→ч
(бога́тый→бога́че, кру́тый→кру́че, туго́й→ту́же). All these end in a single unstressed -е once
mutated (the к is sometimes simply dropped rather than mutated, e.g. бли́зкий→бли́же shows з→ж
with к omitted). This complements — with a cleaner mutation-class organization — the comparative
material already captured from the Comprehensive Grammar and Schaum's Outline.

### Surname declension as a hybrid noun/adjective paradigm (p. 25)

Russian surnames ending in -ев/-ёв/-ов/-ин/-ын decline as a **hybrid**: nominative/accusative
resemble noun endings, but other cases (especially instrumental singular and the whole feminine
paradigm) take adjectival endings. Surnames in -ский/-ой (e.g. Толсто́й, Ма́йский) decline fully as
adjectives. Surnames in -о (Бондаре́нко, Хитрово́) don't decline at all. Male foreign surnames
ending in a consonant decline as nouns but their female counterparts don't (а́дрес Фри́дриха
Ши́ллера vs. а́дрес А́нны Ши́ллер). Foreign surnames in -е/-и/-о/-у/-ы and stressed -а́/-я́ (Гёте,
Тати́, Моро́, Шо́у, Дюма́) never decline. This is a genuinely distinct, cleanly stated rule not
found tabulated this concisely in the Comprehensive Grammar's own noun-declension chapter.

### Calendar date/year constructions (p. 95)

Three distinct question/answer formulas, each governing a different case on the ordinal: "What is
the date?" (Како́е число́?) takes a **nominative** neuter ordinal with the month in genitive;
"On what date?" (Како́го числа́?) takes a **genitive** ordinal, with the month and year also in
genitive; "In which year?" (В како́м году́?) uses **в + prepositional**, where only the final
numeral component of a compound year is an ordinal (the rest stay cardinal) — e.g. "in 2020" =
в две ты́сячи двадца́том году́ (две ты́сячи stays cardinal, двадца́том is the ordinal-in-
prepositional). This three-way case-government contrast for essentially the same semantic
question ("when") is a compact, usable summary not seen assembled this concisely elsewhere in the
corpus so far.

### Verb list's per-verb case-government notation (pp. 158–171)

Distinct from its redundant conjugation-paradigm content (superseded by the Big Silver Book), the
Verb list's format tags **each verb's case government directly inline** (e.g. `завидовать/по-
(+ dat.) 'to envy'`, `защити́ть (от + gen.) 'to defend (from)'`, `преврати́ть (в + acc.) 'to
transform (into)'`) for roughly 600 common verbs in a single continuous alphabetical run. This is a
denser single-location government reference than assembling the same information from prepositional
government scattered across the Comprehensive Grammar's Preposition chapter (§401–453) — worth
flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase as a possible quick-lookup source for verb+case
collocation patterns, even though the underlying government facts themselves are not new.
