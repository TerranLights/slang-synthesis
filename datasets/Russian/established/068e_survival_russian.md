## Grammar points

### Age via dative case — Lesson 6 (p. 62-63, Table 17-18)

Age is expressed with the person in the dative case + a bare numeral + лет/год/года (Мне 20 лет,
Ему́ 30 лет), not a possessive/nominative "I am" construction — a genuinely distinct grammatical
strategy from English. Personal-pronoun dative paradigm: я→мне, ты→тебе́, он/она́→ему́/ей, мы→нам,
вы→вам, они́→им. Singular noun dative formation shown via a "Кто? / Кому?" contrastive table:
masculine hard-stem nouns add -у (Анто́н→Анто́ну, брат→бра́ту), masculine nouns ending in -й or -ь
take -ю instead (Игорь→И́горю, Алексе́й→Алексе́ю per the source's own "Remember" rule); feminine
-а nouns take -е (И́ра→И́ре, сестра́→сестре́), while feminine nouns ending in -ия or -ь take -и
(Мари́я→Мари́и, дочь→до́чери — до́чери also shows a stem-vowel insertion not explicitly flagged by
the source, a genuine morphophonological wrinkle beyond the plain ending-swap rule given).

### год/года/лет numeral-agreement with age — Lesson 6 (p. 64)

A "Remember" box establishes the same collective-numeral agreement pattern that structures Russian
number+noun phrases generally: 1 (оди́н) год, 2-4 (два/три/четы́ре) го́да, 5+ (пять, шесть...) лет
— i.e., a three-way singular/paucal/plural split keyed to the numeral's own final digit, mirroring
standard Russian noun-after-numeral declension (nominative singular after 1, genitive singular
after 2-4, genitive plural after 5+) applied specifically to the suppletive triad год/го́да/лет.

### Genitive of possession with у — Lesson 6 (p. 64-65, Tables 19-20)

Possession is expressed as "у + [possessor, genitive] + есть + [possessed, nominative]" rather than
a "to have" verb. Personal-pronoun genitive: я→у меня́, ты→у тебя́, он→у него́, она́→у неё, мы→у
нас, вы→у вас, они́→у них. Singular noun genitive formation: masculine hard-stem nouns add -а
(Анто́н→у Анто́на, муж→у му́жа), masculine -й/-ь nouns take -я (И́горь→у И́горя); feminine -а nouns
take -ы (И́ра→у И́ры, сестра́→у сестры́) *unless* preceded by к/г/х/ш/ж/щ/ч, in which case -и is
used instead (ба́бушка→у ба́бушки, since -шк- ends in к). Worked example pair given explicitly
(У Анто́на есть жена́ / У И́ры есть муж) contrasting masculine and feminine possessor declension
side by side.

### Genitive of negation — Lesson 6 (p. 65, Table 21)

Negating "have" flips both the verb (есть → drops out) and the possessed noun's case: "У меня́
есть брат" → "У меня́ нет бра́та" (possessed noun moves from nominative to genitive under не́т).
This mirrors genitive-of-negation as a broader case-government pattern rather than an isolated
"have not" idiom — the source gives a parallel worked table for both masculine and feminine
possessed nouns (брат→бра́та, муж→му́жа, сын→сы́на, оте́ц→отца́ [with a fleeting-vowel drop
о→∅ not explicitly flagged]; сестра́→сестры́, жена́→жены́, семья́→семьи́, дочь→до́чери, мать→
ма́тери, ба́бушка→ба́бушки) plus the two irregular plurals де́ти→дете́й and роди́тели→
роди́телей given in a closing "Remember" note.
