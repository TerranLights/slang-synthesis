## Grammar points

### Offering to do something for the addressee (§2.1, pp. 212–213)

The book identifies a distinct construction: a bare perfective infinitive phrased as a question
(Открыть окно?/Поставить книги в шкаф?/Помочь тебе приготовить ужин?) used specifically when the
speaker believes the action is desirable/expedient for the addressee and is offering to carry it out
themselves. The source is explicit (p. 213) that this construction requires a **perfective**
infinitive — a one-off completed act being offered, not an ongoing/repeated one.

### Accepting or declining an offer (§2.2, pp. 213–215)

A straightforward two-cluster response system: acceptance (Да, пожалуйста./Да, будь добр/будьте
добры./Да, было бы хорошо/неплохо./Да, если можно./Да, если (вам/тебе) не трудно.) vs. decline (Нет,
не надо./Спасибо, не надо./Спасибо, не беспокойтесь.), laid out in the framed table on p. 215.

### Offering to act in someone else's place (§2.3, pp. 215–216)

A distinct offer-type: Давай(те) я/Позволь(те) я, мне/Разреши(те) я, мне (+ future or bare) —
specifically for volunteering to perform an action that was originally someone *else's*
responsibility or intention (e.g. someone else was going to move the table; the speaker offers to do
it in their stead). The book's own framed table (p. 216) shows each of the three verb roots (давать,
позволить, разрешить) accepting both a bare-verb and future-tense-completed variant.

### Proposing joint action, and its syntactic variety (§2.4, pp. 216–219)

This subsection is unusually rich in syntactic detail. The book identifies **four distinct
constructions** for inviting the addressee to do something *together* with the speaker (p. 217's own
summary):

1. A bare perfective verb in 1st-person-plural future (Отдохнём немного.).
2. That same verb-form combined with давай(те)/может быть/а что, если мы (Давай(те) немного
   отдохнём.).
3. A negated infinitive question (Не отдохнуть ли нам?).
4. A bare infinitive combined with may be/hedge markers (Может быть, нам немного отдохнуть?/Неплохо
   бы/хорошо бы нам немного отдохнуть.).

Two further refinements are drawn out explicitly: **а что, если нам + inf** specifically marks the
proposal as an idea that "just occurred" to the speaker in the moment (p. 218), and joint-action
proposals are frequently prefaced by Послушай(те), знаешь/знаете in place of a vocative address (p.
217).

A genuinely notable **aspectual-lexical rule** closes the subsection (p. 219, worth flagging for
Russian's fusional/aspect-rich morphology per this language's typology note): in a **давай + inf**
construction proposing a round trip, the plain unidirectional motion verbs идти/ехать are
systematically replaced by their multidirectional/round-trip counterparts сходить/съездить (Давай
сходим вечером куда-нибудь. / Давай съездим в город.) — **not** by ordinary prefixation rules, which
instead govern already-prefixed directional motion verbs unchanged (Давай подойдём к ним./Давайте
пройдём вперёд./Давайте заедём на почту. — these follow the general prefixed-motion-verb pattern,
contrasted explicitly by the source against the special идти/ехать → сходить/съездить substitution).
This is a genuine morphological substitution rule specific to the bare unprefixed determinate motion
pair, not a broader aspectual generalization, and is worth flagging precisely for that reason.

### Responding to a joint-action proposal (§2.5, pp. 220–223)

Response patterns mirror those of §2.2/§2.3 but are structurally distinct in one respect the source
flags explicitly (p. 222): **acceptance of a joint-action proposal is formed by repeating the
proposed action itself** (И правда, давай сделаем; Хорошо, давай подождём) rather than a
content-free "yes," while **decline is regularly accompanied by the Зачем? challenge-to-necessity
question** (И правда, зачем нам это делать?) — reusing the same construction already introduced in
§1.3/§1.5 for a third distinct pragmatic slot. A further three-way gradient (p. 222, framed table)
distinguishes hesitant acceptance (Ну что ж.../Ну что ж, можно.) from indifferent
deferral-to-the-addressee (Как хочешь/хотите. — the same lexeme as §1.5's permission-response use)
from outright disagreement (Зачем?/Нет, что вы!/Ну что вы!/Вот ещё!/Нет, не стоит/не надо.).

subsection 1 (Совет) — covered starting in `054n`.
