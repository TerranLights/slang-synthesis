# Russian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Walking on Russian Vocabulary, Part 6 (Chapter 3, §4-5: Идиомы "Idioms" and Пословицы и поговорки "Proverbs and Sayings")

**Source:** Е.Р. Ласкарева, *Прогулки по русской лексике*, Zlatoust, 2010. This part covers the
fourth and fifth sub-sections of Chapter 3: "Идиомы" (Idioms), printed pp. 163-171 (PDF pp.
165-173), and "Пословицы и поговорки" (Proverbs and Sayings), printed pp. 173-187 (PDF pp.
175-189). Same +2 PDF-to-printed-page offset (re-verified against the visible printed folio on
PDF p.165, "163"); no text layer (fully vision-read); no marginalia found on any page sampled.

**Coverage note.** Unlike the drill-heavy Synonyms/Paronyms/Collocation sub-sections, both of these
sub-sections consist almost entirely of **closed-form fixed expressions with no book-provided
translation or gloss** (the exercises ask the student to work out/discuss the meaning; the book's
own answer key is not vision-read in this file — see 060h for the answer-key appendix). Every idiom
and proverb listed is a genuinely distinct lexical item (a fixed multi-word unit, not a
free-combination phrase), so per the coverage rule this file extracts a large, representative
selection spanning every thematic exercise cluster the source uses (organized around body-part
idioms — рука/hand, голова/head, нога/leg, сердце-душа/heart-soul — plus general idioms, plus a
broad sample of the ~150 proverbs/sayings across the pословицы sub-section), while not attempting
literal 100% coverage of every single item across ~25 pages, since many additional items within the
same thematic cluster (e.g. a 6th или 7th рука-idiom in a list of 15) would add repetitive rather
than new structural information once the cluster's pattern is established. Glosses below are this
subagent's own translation/paraphrase (the book itself does not supply them), based on standard
Russian idiom usage.

## Grammar points

### Idioms vs. free collocations (p.163, opening definition)

The source's own definition: phraseologisms (фразеологизмы) are complex fixed units whose word
order and grammatical forms cannot be freely varied (with rare exceptions it names itself: от всей
души / от всего сердца), unlike ordinary collocations. It further narrows "Идиомы" (this
sub-section's own scope) as excluding proverbs/sayings and register-specific set-phrases from other
fields, which get their own dedicated sub-section (Пословицы и поговорки) immediately after.

### Proverb vs. saying (пословица vs. поговорка), p.173 opening definition

The source draws a precise distinction directly relevant to any future proverb/saying-tagging work
this project does: a **пословица** (proverb) carries an explicit didactic/moral point and is
syntactically self-contained ("Волков бояться — в лес не ходить"), while a **поговорка** (saying)
is syntactically incomplete and requires context to finish its meaning ("После дождичка в
четверг..." — "[not until] a Thursday after rain [i.e. never]"). The source also flags that
proverbs/sayings are pitched above CEFR-equivalent level 2 for RFL certification exams (ТРКИ-2) but
argues they're valuable enough to introduce earlier, since they are unusually central to Russian
public/political rhetorical style even today (its own example: contemporary politicians deliberately
reaching for proverbs to sound more "folksy"/relatable).

### Body-part idiom clusters as a productive pattern

The idioms sub-section is organized by recurring body-part lexemes (рука "hand", голова "head",
нога "leg", сердце/душа "heart/soul") each generating 10-20 idioms. This clustering-by-body-part
principle is itself worth flagging as a cross-linguistically common metaphor-generation mechanism
for the project's later mechanics-analysis phase — Russian productively extends рука/голова/сердце
into dozens of idioms the same way many languages build slang around body-part metonymy.
