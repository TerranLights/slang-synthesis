**Morpheme breakdown:** оренбу́ргский плато́к = Оренбу́рг (place name, a city in the Southern
Urals) + -ск- (relational-adjective suffix) + -ий (masculine nominative adjective ending) — a
transparent place-name-to-adjective derivation pattern repeated identically for
павловопоса́дский (Павловопоса́д + -ск- + -ий) and жо́стовский (Жо́стово + -ск- + -ий), showing this
is a fully productive toponym-adjective formation rather than lexicalized one-offs.

## Grammar points

### покупа́ть/купи́ть, сове́товать/посове́товать, брать/взять, нра́виться/понра́виться — Lesson 9 (p. 114-117, Tables 33-36)

Four more full НСВ/СВ present/past/future/imperative paradigms, continuing the aspect-pair pattern
established in Lesson 7. брать/взять is a genuine **suppletive** verb pair (unrelated roots
бра-/возьм-, unlike the shared-root pairs seen so far), directly comparable to the уйти́/вы́йти
pairing already flagged, but stronger since the two roots share no phonological material at all.
нра́виться/понра́виться is presented with a dedicated dative-experiencer table (Кому? нра́вится/
нра́вятся Что?) — "мне нра́вится костю́м" is literally "the suit is pleasing to me," the same
dative-subject strategy already flagged for Вам нра́вится Москва́? in the front-matter vocabulary
list, now given its full conjugation and explicitly noted as a verb whose future tense is
essentially unused in practice — a genuine usage-frequency note (not merely grammatical
possibility) worth flagging for register/mechanics purposes.

### Demonstrative pronouns э́тот/тот — Lesson 9 (p. 117, Table 37)

Full nominative/accusative paradigm across all four gender/number classes: masculine э́тот/тот,
feminine э́та/та, neuter э́то/то, plural э́ти/те — each agreeing with its noun the same way
adjectives do (paired directly against a како́й?/кака́я?/како́е?/каки́е? question-word template
identical in structure to the adjective-agreement pattern from Lesson 4).

### Numeral-triggered case selection (2-4 vs. 5+) generalized — Lesson 9 (p. 117-118)

Extends the год/лет and остано́вка/ста́нций paucal/plural split (Lessons 6, 8) into an explicit,
generalized rule: **after 2, 3, 4 the noun takes genitive singular; after 5, 6... (and quantity
words ско́лько, мно́го, ма́ло, немно́го) the noun takes genitive plural.** Worked through rubль,
грамм, ба́нка, ты́сяча, буты́лка, па́чка, шту́ка, each also flagging a further exception: when
specifying exactly *one* item as a direct object (rather than asking "how many"), the noun takes
plain **accusative singular** instead (да́йте одну́ ба́нку, not the nominative one̍ ба́нка a naive
paradigm might predict) — a genuinely three-way case-selection rule (accusative for exactly-one-as-
object, genitive singular for 2-4, genitive plural for 5+/quantity words) that the book builds up
piece by piece across three separate lessons before finally stating it in one place here.

### Genitive plural noun formation — Lesson 9 (p. 118-119, Table 38)

The book's most systematic morphology point yet, given as an explicit rule set rather than only a
worked table: masculine nouns take -ов (in most cases), -ей after ш/ж/ч/щ-root nouns, or -ев after
a nominative-singular -й ending (герой→геро́ев); feminine -а/-я nouns lose their vowel ending
entirely with **no overt genitive-plural suffix** in the regular case (кни́га→книг), gaining -ек
if the stem ends in ш/ж/ч, -ей if the nominative ends in -ь, -ий if the nominative ends in -ия, or
-ок if the stem ends in a consonant cluster other than ш/ж/ч; neuter -о nouns are likewise
suffixless in the genitive plural (окно́→о́кон) but insert a **fill vowel** (-о- after a hard
consonant, -е- after a soft one) to break up an otherwise-illegal consonant cluster left behind
(пи́сьмо→пи́сем — inserted -е-), with -ей for nominative -е nouns and -ий for nominative -ие nouns.
This vowel-insertion mechanism is a genuine morphophonological repair strategy (not just an ending
choice) directly relevant to fusional-morphology mechanics analysis: Russian's zero-suffix genitive
plural for many feminine/neuter nouns can create phonotactically awkward consonant clusters, which
the language resolves by inserting an epenthetic vowel rather than tolerating the cluster or adding
an audible case suffix.
