# Russian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Кogda ne pomogayut slovari... Часть II (full book, 9 lexical-semantic-group chapters)

**Source:** Э.В. Аркадьева, Г.В. Горбаневская, Н.Д. Кирсанова, И.Б. Марчук, *Когда не помогают
словари... Практикум по лексике современного русского языка для иностранцев и российских
студентов-филологов. Часть II* (Москва, изд-ва "Флинта"/"Наука", 2006, ISBN 5-89349-938-7 /
5-02-034636-5), pp. 3–252 (all 127 PDF pages; front matter + 9 chapters + answer keys + picture-based
exercises + table of contents). Part I (established/029_kogda_ne_pomogayut_slovari_part1.md) covers a
disjoint set of 10 verb groups (БРАТЬ was NOT in Part I — Part I's 10 groups were посещать/навещать,
строить, смотреть, пользоваться/использовать, учить/изучать, стремиться/стараться, думать,
помнить, тоже/также, and one more); this Part II volume's 9 groups (БРАТЬ, ДАВАТЬ/ДАТЬ, РАБОТАТЬ,
ЖИТЬ, ПЛАТИТЬ, МЕНЯТЬ, СТУПАТЬ, ГОВОРИТЬ, СКАЗАТЬ) are entirely non-overlapping with Part I's
chapter list, so this is a full, non-redundant extraction, not a "supplement" dispatch.

**PDF-extraction note (vision-only source, no text layer at all).** `pdftotext`/`pdftotext -layout`
both returned essentially empty output (127 bytes total across the whole 127-page file); `pdffonts`
showed only a single non-embedded `Times-Roman` placeholder font, confirming this PDF has no usable
text layer whatsoever — every page had to be vision-read from rendered images
(`pdftoppm -png -r 150`). Two source-specific gotchas confirmed here, both already flagged as
standing risks in `00_Reference_Extraction_Spec.md`:
1. **Two printed book pages per PDF image (`page rot: 90`, A4 landscape spreads).** `pdfinfo` reports
   127 PDF pages for a book whose own printed pagination runs to 252 — each PDF page is actually a
   left+right two-page spread of the physical book, confirmed by direct inspection of the first few
   rendered images (front cover on its own page, then title-page spreads, then two consecutively
   numbered printed pages per image throughout the body). All page citations below use the book's
   own printed page numbers (visible at the bottom of each half of the spread), not PDF page indices.
2. **No Cyrillic font-substitution cipher was found** — unlike several other Russian-corpus PDFs
   flagged in the spec's gotcha list, this source's problem is purely "no text layer," not a
   corrupted one; once rendered to images, the print itself was clean, high-contrast, and fully
   legible on every page sampled. No handwritten marginalia was found on any page — this is a clean,
   unmarked scan.

**Coverage note.** Like Part I, this is a lexical-collocation practicum, not a grammar-paradigm
textbook. Its entire content is 9 chapters, each built around one verb (БРАТЬ, ДАВАТЬ/ДАТЬ,
РАБОТАТЬ, ЖИТЬ, ПЛАТИТЬ, МЕНЯТЬ, СТУПАТЬ, ГОВОРИТЬ, СКАЗАТЬ) plus its full family of prefixed
derivatives — in every chapter here (unlike Part I, where only 5/10 chapters had a large prefix
family), the prefix family IS the chapter's main content. Every prefixed verb introduced is captured
below with its core sense(s), typical case/preposition government (abbreviated from the source's
own government charts — collocate lists are sampled, not reproduced in full, per copyright
discipline), and any named idiom/fixed collocation the source boxes as "Запомните
словосочетание(-я)"/"Устойчивые словосочетания"/"Запомните пословицу/фразеологизм." Skipped, per the
coverage rule: the hundreds of individual "Упражнение N" drill items (fill-in-the-blank, sentence
transformation, dialogue completion) that only re-use vocabulary already captured from the same
chapter — confirmed by direct inspection that these are pedagogical padding, not new lexical items.
Also skipped: the book's final two sections, "Ключи к упражнениям" (pp. 224–229, answer key to the
drills — by definition redundant with material already captured) and "Задания к картинкам" (pp.
230–252, ten picture-based storytelling exercises built around cartoons by Danish cartoonist H.
Bidstrup, testing the whole course's vocabulary — no new lexical items, purely a picture-prompt
exercise format). Several dozen brief illustrative literary quotations (Pushkin, Tolstoy, Chekhov,
Gogol, Bulgakov, Gorky, Lermontov, Tsvetaeva-era and Soviet-era prose writers, etc.) are referenced by
author only below, not reproduced, per copyright discipline.

This dataset follows Part I's departure from "one row per bare headword": each base verb is
presented with its numbered senses summarized, then every prefixed derivative gets its own row
(base gloss + government pattern + any idiom), because the pedagogical and slang-mechanics payoff of
this whole source is the *prefix system*, not individual glosses.

## Grammar points

### This source's basic organizing unit and how it differs from Part I

Like Part I, each chapter here is built around a lexico-semantic group and distinguishes near-synonyms
by **case/preposition government**, **aspectual behavior**, and **register**. The key structural
difference from Part I: here, in **all 9 of 9 chapters** (vs. 5/10 in Part I) the chapter's main
content is the base verb's full **prefixed-derivative family**, not a cluster of independently-rooted
near-synonyms. Every chapter opens with the bare base verb's numbered senses, then proceeds
prefix-by-prefix through its derivatives, closing (in 6 of the 9 chapters — ДАВАТЬ, ЖИТЬ, СТУПАТЬ,
ГОВОРИТЬ, СКАЗАТЬ, and partially БРАТЬ) with a further layer of **reflexive (-СЯ) derivatives of the
prefixed forms** — a second productive layer this source treats as systematically as the first.

### Confirmed cross-chapter productive prefix + reflexive (-СЯ) patterns

This is the single most reusable finding for later Russian slang-mechanics work, because it appears
**explicitly named by the source itself**, independently, in two different chapters of this same book
(ЖИТЬ and ГОВОРИТЬ) — corroborating the identical pattern already flagged in Part I's `думать`/
`смотреть`/`учить` chapters:

- **ЗА- + -СЯ = "become excessively/absorbingly involved in an action, to a fault or one's own
  detriment."** Named explicitly in this volume for засидеться, засмотреться (cross-referenced from
  the source's own list) and instantiated by заговориться (p.203-204) and заработаться (p.97). This is
  now confirmed in *three* independent chapters across the two volumes (СМОТРЕТЬ/УЧИТЬ/ДУМАТЬ in Part
  I; ГОВОРИТЬ and РАБОТАТЬ in Part II) — a highly productive, cross-verb-family template.
- **НА- + -СЯ = "do an action to the point of full satisfaction/satiety, with a positive/neutral
  valence"** (the semantic mirror-image of ЗА-...-СЯ). Named explicitly for наесться, накупиться,
  надышаться and instantiated by наговориться (p.204) and набраться (духу/сил/ума, p.26-27). Not
  previously named this explicitly in Part I, though ЗАСМАТРИВАТЬСЯ/НАСМОТРЕТЬСЯ there hinted at the
  same opposition — this is now the clearest single statement of the НА-...-СЯ pattern found in either
  volume.
- **РАЗ- + -СЯ = "an intensive result that builds up gradually, once a threshold is crossed"** (distinct
  from the "undo/reverse" РАЗ- family named in Part I for разучиться/расхотеть/разлюбить). Named
  explicitly for расшуметься and разболеться ("a headache worsening"), instantiated here by
  разговориться (p.203). **This confirms РАЗ- is not semantically uniform even within this single
  volume** — the plain-РАЗ (no -СЯ) family in Part I ("undo a prior result": разучиться, etc.) and this
  РАЗ-+-СЯ family ("gradual intensive buildup": разговориться, расшуметься) are two distinct,
  non-overlapping templates sharing only the prefix's phonological shape. A slang-coinage analysis must
  track the -СЯ postfix as part of the template, not just the prefix.

### БРАТЬ's dual derivative layer: manipulation-sense prefixes vs. spatial-motion-sense prefixes with -СЯ

БРАТЬ is unique among this book's 9 base verbs in explicitly splitting its prefix family into two
structurally distinct groups, each covered in its own subsection (p.16-47 vs. p.48-53): (1) the
"ordinary" transitive prefixed verbs (выбирать, отбирать, подбирать, набирать, собирать, разбирать,
перебирать, убирать, забирать) which keep БРАТЬ's core "seize/acquire/select" meaning and take a
direct object; and (2) a **reflexive spatial-motion subgroup** (добираться, выбираться, забираться,
взбираться, пробираться, перебираться, подбираться) that the source explicitly analogizes to ordinary
verbs of motion (входить, приезжать, убегать) but marks as differing from them by adding an inherent
"with difficulty / through exertion" component that the corresponding plain motion-prefix lacks
(сравните: выйти "to exit" vs. выбраться "to get out with difficulty"; доехать "to arrive by vehicle"
vs. добраться "to reach, after expending effort"). This is a clean, source-stated example of a single
verb root spawning two independently-productive prefix-derivation subsystems — directly relevant to
later slang-formation analysis, since a novel coinage on -БИРАТЬСЯ/-БРАТЬСЯ would signal "difficult
motion" regardless of which spatial prefix attaches.

### Register/register-pair contrasts named across this volume's 9 chapters

- **РАБОТАТЬ vs. ТРУДИТЬСЯ**: РАБОТАТЬ names participation in a work process tied to a specific
  job/task; ТРУДИТЬСЯ is more abstract, bookish/literary, and foregrounds diligence/perseverance
  independent of the specific task — the derived adjectives (none given directly, but note РАБОЧИЙ vs.
  РАБОТНИК as a related, explicitly contrasted noun pair) track the same split. p.84-86.
- **ВЫРАБАТЫВАТЬ vs. РАЗРАБАТЫВАТЬ**: ВЫРАБАТЫВАТЬ = "create a first/basic version," РАЗРАБАТЫВАТЬ =
  "study something in full detail to bring it to complete finalization" — a productive-vs-perfective
  depth distinction rather than a register distinction. p.88-89.
- **ПРОРАБОТАТЬ vs. ОТРАБОТАТЬ**: both mean "spend a period working," but ОТРАБОТАТЬ marks the work
  period as now *complete/exhausted* while ПРОРАБОТАТЬ marks only its *duration*, with no implication
  the period has ended. p.93-95.
- **ВЫПЛАЧИВАТЬ/ВЫПЛАТИТЬ vs. ОПЛАЧИВАТЬ/ОПЛАТИТЬ**: mirror-image argument structure — with
  ВЫПЛАЧИВАТЬ the subject *receives* money for their own labor/study; with ОПЛАЧИВАТЬ the subject *pays*
  for labor/services rendered to them. p.122-123.
- **СМЕНЯТЬ/СМЕНИТЬ vs. ЗАМЕНЯТЬ/ЗАМЕНИТЬ**: with animate objects, СМЕНЯТЬ marks regular/sequential
  succession ("one after another, in order," e.g. shift workers), ЗАМЕНЯТЬ marks ad hoc temporary
  substitution for someone absent; with inanimate objects, СМЕНЯТЬ (кого/что) marks one thing's
  natural end giving way to the next, while ЗАМЕНЯТЬ (что + instrumental чем) marks deliberately using
  one thing in place of another. p.135-137.
- **ДОГОВАРИВАТЬСЯ/ДОГОВОРИТЬСЯ vs. УГОВАРИВАТЬСЯ/УГОВОРИТЬСЯ vs. СГОВАРИВАТЬСЯ/СГОВОРИТЬСЯ**: all
  three share "come to an agreement about a joint action," but ДОГОВАРИВАТЬСЯ is stylistically neutral,
  УГОВАРИВАТЬСЯ is colloquial-register, and СГОВАРИВАТЬСЯ has a narrower, more construction-bound
  usage restricted mainly to fixed phrases (как будто/словно сговорились). p.201-202.
- **ПРОГОВОРИТЬ vs. ВЫГОВОРИТЬ** (the "utter" sense): both can mean "pronounce/utter," and are
  sometimes mutually substitutable, but ВЫГОВОРИТЬ specifically implies overcoming an articulatory or
  emotional obstacle to get the words out, while ПРОГОВОРИТЬ implies plain, unremarkable utterance (or
  repeated practice-drilling, as in phonetics classes) — this blocks free substitution in most contexts
  despite the surface overlap. p.187-188.
- **ПЕРЕЖИВАТЬ/ПЕРЕЖИТЬ vs. ИСПЫТЫВАТЬ/ИСПЫТАТЬ**: both can describe undergoing a strong feeling, but
  ПЕРЕЖИВАТЬ/ПЕРЕЖИТЬ marks passing through a powerful *psychological* experience, while
  ИСПЫТЫВАТЬ/ИСПЫТАТЬ is used for physical sensations (боль, жажду, голод) or a fixed list of named
  emotional-state nouns (желание, потребность, отвращение, нежность, жалость, привязанность, отчаяние,
  гордость, чувство одиночества/тоски) in the sense "to come to know/comprehend." p.106-107.

### Aspectually-defective and suppletive pairs named in this volume

- **СКАЗАТЬ** is explicitly identified as the suppletive perfective partner of ГОВОРИТЬ (sense 2 only)
  — and, crucially, its own prefixed derivatives form an *entirely separate word-family* from
  ГОВОРИТЬ's prefixed derivatives (рассказать/пересказать/высказать etc. vs.
  разговаривать/выговаривать/договаривать etc.), a clean instance of suppletion blocking shared
  prefix-derivation between an aspectual pair's two roots. p.212.
- **ПОГОВОРИТЬ** and **ПЕРЕГОВОРИТЬ** are both explicitly flagged as *not* true aspectual partners of
  ГОВОРИТЬ despite functioning as perfectives of it in their specific senses ("talk for a bounded
  while" / "discuss to conclusion," respectively) — the source distinguishes "functions as a CB in this
  sense" from "is the CB" throughout this chapter. p.188-189, 193.
- Several prefixed forms are explicitly marked **CВ-only, with no corresponding НСВ**: ПОРАБОТАТЬ,
  ЗАРАБОТАТЬСЯ, СРАБОТАТЬСЯ (РАБОТАТЬ chapter); ПОЖИТЬ (ЖИТЬ chapter); ОТПЛАТИТЬ, ПОПЛАТИТЬСЯ (ПЛАТИТЬ
  chapter); ПЕРЕМЕНИТЬ, ПРОМЕНЯТЬ (МЕНЯТЬ chapter); ПЕРЕГОВОРИТЬ, ПОГОВОРИТЬ, РАЗГОВОРИТЬСЯ,
  ЗАГОВОРИТЬСЯ, НАГОВОРИТЬСЯ, НЕДОСКАЗАТЬ (ГОВОРИТЬ/СКАЗАТЬ chapters) — these delimitative/perfectivizing
  prefixes attach to name a single bounded event without the source supplying (or the language
  productively forming) an imperfective counterpart.
