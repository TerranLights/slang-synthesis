## Grammar points

### Выражение времени (Expression of time) — pp. 57–59

A compact, well-organized four-way question/preposition/case template distinguishing:
- **На сколько времени? (На какое время?)** — на + accusative (intended duration: "взял книгу на
  неделю").
- **Сколько времени? (Как долго?)** — imperfective verb (process) + bare accusative (actual
  duration: "переводил рассказ неделю").
- **За сколько времени? (За какое время?)** — perfective verb (result) + за + accusative (time taken
  to complete: "перевёл рассказ за неделю").
- **Когда?** — several sub-patterns: через + acc. (after an interval), за...до... + gen./gen. (before
  an event), через...после... + acc./gen. (after an event), по + dat. plural for habitual recurrence
  ("по субботам"), во время/перед + gen./instr. for "during/before," с...до... + gen./gen. for a
  span, and adverbs of time-of-day (утром/днём/вечером/ночью) vs. genitive-marked clock-time
  ("в семь часов утра").

This is a clean, well-organized pedagogical summary of time-expression case government; the
underlying case/preposition facts are already well covered elsewhere in this corpus, but the
compact question-word-to-construction mapping (На сколько? vs. Сколько? vs. За сколько? vs. Когда?)
is a genuinely useful organizing template not confirmed present in exactly this four-way form
elsewhere — kept briefly here as reference, not with the full drill content.

### Reading-text content (paraphrased)

- **"Таинственная церковь"** (по А. Астафьеву, pp. 56–59) — Christmas ghost-story: the narrator
  recalls being found unconscious near an old wooden cross in a snowstorm as a boy, having believed
  he'd taken shelter in a church that (per local legend) sinks into the ground and resurfaces only
  once a year, on Christmas Eve. Supplies the religious/Christmas vocabulary cluster above.
- **"Русское Рождество"** (p. 67) and **"Старый Новый год"** (p. 68) — cultural-explainer texts.
  Genuine cultural fact worth flagging: Russians following the Orthodox calendar celebrate New Year
  twice — the standard Dec 31/Jan 1 (Gregorian) and "Старый Новый год" (Old New Year) on the night
  of Jan 13/14, corresponding to New Year's Eve under the old Julian calendar still used
  liturgically. The Pushkin anecdote (pp. 68) is a family legend about the poet's infancy witnessed
  at a New Year's gathering "на рубеже веков" (Pushkin was born in 1799, at the turn of the 18th/19th
  centuries) — source of the two threshold-metaphor idioms tabulated above.
