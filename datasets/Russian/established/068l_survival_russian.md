## Grammar points

### встреча́ться/встре́титься, ждать, проводи́ть/провести́, ри́совать/танцева́ть-class (-ова/-ева suffix verbs), петь — Lesson 13 (p. 192-195, Tables 60-65)

Six more conjugation tables, notably introducing the productive **-ова-/-ева- verb class**
(рисова́ть, танцева́ть) as a named suffix pattern for the first time — present-tense forms drop the
-ова-/-ева- entirely and conjugate off a bare consonant stem (рису́ю, танцу́ю), a genuinely
non-obvious stem-formation rule (the infinitive's visible suffix disappears in the present tense)
worth flagging for morphology-mechanics analysis as a case where surface infinitive shape actively
misleads a learner about the conjugated stem.

### Instrumental case of personal pronouns — Lesson 13 (p. 194, Table 63)

Full paradigm: я→(со) мной, ты→(с) тобо́й, он→(с) ним, она́→(с) ней, мы→(с) на́ми, вы→(с) ва́ми,
они́→(с) ни́ми — third-person forms show an н- prefixed onto the base pronoun after a preposition
(ним/ней/ними vs. bare его́/её/их elsewhere), a general Russian rule (not stated explicitly here,
but visible directly in the paradigm) that 3rd-person personal pronouns insert н- after any
preposition, distinguishing prepositional-governed forms from the bare possessive-looking genitive
forms used elsewhere in the language.

### 1st-person-plural imperative ("let's...") — Lesson 13 (p. 193-194)

A previously-unstated construction: "let's [do X]" is formed with дава́й/дава́йте + a verb in the
1st person plural, but the *aspect* of that verb differs by whether the action is a single planned
event or an ongoing/generic one. **Perfective** 1pl future forms are used for a single concrete
proposed action (Дава́й(те) пойдём в теа́тр! "Let's go to the theater!", Дава́й(те) встре́тимся в 18
часо́в! "Let's meet at 18:00!"), while the **imperfective infinitive** is used for a more general
activity without emphasis on a single completed instance (Дава́й(те) смотре́ть фильм! "Let's watch
a movie!", Дава́й(те) танцева́ть! "Let's dance!"). This is a further concrete application of the
aspect system established since Lesson 7, now extended into a distinct "let's..." construction not
covered by the earlier singular/plural imperative-formation rule from Lesson 7 (which built
2nd-person commands, not 1st-person-plural proposals).

### в + accusative (games) vs. на + prepositional (instruments) — Lesson 13 (p. 192)

играть takes two structurally distinct complement patterns depending on the semantic type of its
object: **в + accusative** for games/sports (игра́ть в ша́хматы, в футбо́л) versus **на +
prepositional** for musical instruments (игра́ть на гита́ре, на пиани́но) — the same в-for-games/
на-for-instruments split recurs in many Slavic languages and is worth flagging as a genuinely
systematic verb-government distinction based on the *kind* of object (competitive activity vs.
physical instrument played), not free variation.
