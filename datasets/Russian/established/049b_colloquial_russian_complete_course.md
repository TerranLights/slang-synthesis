## Grammar points

### Lesson 4 — Genitive case, present-tense irregular verbs, short adjectives, expressions of time
- **Genitive singular endings**: masc. consonant-stem +а, neuter -о→-а; masc. -ь/-й and neuter -е
  → -я (Кремль→Кремля́, чай→ча́я, общежи́тие→общежи́тия); some masculines drop a fill vowel
  (пирожо́к→пирожка́, день→дня, оте́ц→отца́); fem. -а→-ы, fem. -я/-ь→-и (with spelling-rule
  effects, e.g. ча́шка→ча́шки); мать/дочь are irregular (ма́тери, до́чери).
- **Uses of genitive**: "of" (стака́н молока́); possessive "-'s" (день студе́нта); after a large
  set of prepositions (без, до, из, напро́тив, о́коло, от, по́сле, у, из/с as opposite of в/на,
  далеко́ от); after the numerals два/две, три, четы́ре (два бутербро́да) — два/masc.+neut. vs.
  две/fem.; after мно́го/ма́ло; as the object of a *negated* verb (она́ не ест мя́са) in place of
  accusative; after нет ("there is no..."); and in the у + genitive construction for "to have" (у
  Мари́ны ле́кция — "Marina has a lecture"; negated: у Мари́ны нет ле́кции). This "have" pattern —
  possession expressed via a genitive-of-possessor + nominative-of-possessed, no dedicated "to
  have" verb — is a structurally significant fact for later slang/mechanics analysis.
- **Present tense, more 1st-conj. classes**: verbs in -авать (встава́ть, продава́ть, преподава́ть)
  contract to -аю/-аёшь/... in the present, keeping -ва- only in the infinitive.
- **2nd conjugation regular** (стоя́ть) vs. **irregular есть** (irregular throughout singular,
  regular 2nd-conj. endings in the plural: еди́м/еди́те/едя́т).
- **Prepositional case, further endings**: fem. -ь nouns → -и (в о́череди); neuter -ие → -ии (в
  общежи́тии); fem. -ия → -ии (на ле́кции).
- **Short adjectives**: predicative-only form (де́вушка голодна́ "the girl is hungry," verb "to be"
  implicitly located between subject and adjective); formed by dropping -ый/-ий (masc.), or adding
  -а (fem.), -о (neut.), -ы (pl.); NOT all adjectives have a short form — excluded are -ский
  adjectives and all soft adjectives. Fill vowel (usually -е-) inserted before a final -н in the
  masculine short form (голо́дный → го́лоден). вы triggers plural short-adjective agreement even
  when addressing one person (Вы гото́вы? "Are you ready?").
- **Masculine nouns/agreement with -a/-я ending**: nouns and diminutives referring to a male but
  ending in -а/-я (e.g. boys' diminutive names) take *masculine* agreement despite the
  feminine-looking ending (Ми́ша го́лоден, not голодна́) — an important morphology-vs-semantics
  mismatch worth flagging for later typology work.
- **Time expressions without a preposition**: у́тром, днём, ве́чером, но́чью; сего́дня combines
  freely (сего́дня у́тром "this morning"); че́рез + accusative = "in/after (a period of time)."
- один/одна́/одно́ agree with the gender of the counted noun.

### Lesson 5 — Names, personal pronouns (acc./gen.), до́лжен, весь, irregular verbs
- **Russian three-part names**: и́мя (first name) + о́тчество (patronymic, from father's first
  name + -ович/-евич for a son, -овна/-евна for a daughter) + фами́лия (surname, with a distinct
  feminine form in -а, e.g. Петро́в/Петро́ва). Diminutives (Ди́ма ← Дми́трий, Та́ня ← Татья́на)
  are pervasive and not merely childish — used among adults in informal registers; genuinely useful
  register data for onomastics/slang-adjacent analysis.
- **Personal pronoun accusative = genitive** (меня́, тебя́, его́, её, нас, вас, их are identical in
  both cases); after a preposition his/her/its/their (его́/её/их) become него́/неё/них (н- prosthesis)
  but *not* when used as an invariant possessive (у её семьи́ "her family has..." — no н-).
- **Accusative of animate masculine nouns = genitive** (она́ лю́бит сы́на), distinct from the
  inanimate-masculine rule (accusative = nominative) already given in Lesson 3 — animacy is thus a
  real grammatical category in Russian, not just a semantic one.
- **Neuter nouns in -мя** (ten such nouns total, е.g. и́мя, вре́мя): irregular declension inserting
  -ен- before case endings other than nom./acc. singular (и́мени, вре́мени).
- **2nd-conjugation labial-stem epenthesis**: verbs with a stem ending in б, в, п, ф, м insert -л-
  before the 1st person singular present-tense ending only (люби́ть→люблю́ but лю́бишь; гото́вить→
  гото́влю but гото́вишь).
- **Irregular verbs мочь** (able) and **хоте́ть** (want): мочь alternates г/ж stem consonant across
  the paradigm (могу́...мо́жешь...мо́гут); хоте́ть is 1st-conj. in the singular but 2nd-conj. in the
  plural (a genuinely mixed-conjugation verb, worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase as a
  possible model for how irregular/suppletive paradigms could analogously develop in an invented
  slang register).
- **до́лжен** ("must"): four-form short-adjective-style agreement (до́лжен/должна́/должно́/должны́)
  + infinitive.
- **весь** ("all, whole"): весь (masc.)/вся (fem.)/всё (neut.)/все (plural) — всё also means
  "everything" and все also means "everyone," a semantic extension from the plural form worth
  tracking typologically.
- Instrument vs. game/sport verb-argument pattern: игра́ть на + prepositional for musical
  instruments (игра́ть на скри́пке) vs. игра́ть в + accusative for games/sports (игра́ть в
  футбо́л/ша́хматы) — a lexically split argument-structure pattern.
