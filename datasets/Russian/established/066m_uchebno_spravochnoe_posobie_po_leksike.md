## Grammar points

### СТАРЫЙ's ten-sense entry: the largest single headword in the book so far

At ten distinct numbered senses, СТАРЫЙ is by a wide margin the most polysemous headword
encountered in this extraction — exceeding even ОБЩЕСТВО's four senses (`066`) and ПРАКТИЧЕСКИЙ's
three. This reflects СТАРЫЙ's status as one of the most basic, high-frequency adjectives in Russian,
and its polysemy fans out along at least four separable axes (biological age, historical duration,
physical wear, temporal obsolescence/former status, epistemic familiarity, relational duration,
occupational tenure) — a genuinely rich case study for later slang-mechanics work on how a single
maximally frequent adjective accretes senses that more specialized near-synonyms (СТАРИННЫЙ,
ДРЕВНИЙ, ВЕТХИЙ) each peel off a narrow slice of.

### ТВЁРДЫЙ/ЖЁСТКИЙ/ЖЕСТОКИЙ: a physical-hardness-to-moral-cruelty cline, without a shared root

Unlike most prior triples, this group shares no common derivational root (твёрдый, жёсткий, жестокий
are historically related but not synchronically transparent derivations of one another) and receives
no opening `Комментарий` block — the discrimination must be inferred from the drill exercises'
worked contrasts, which the book supplies unusually richly here (e.g. "твёрдый характер" [admired,
principled firmness] vs. "жёсткий характер" [unlikeable severity] vs. "жестокий характер" [actual
cruelty] — three points on one physical-to-moral hardness scale). This is one of the clearest examples
in the book of a near-synonym cluster whose discrimination logic must be reconstructed from usage
examples rather than stated outright — worth flagging methodologically for later mechanics work.

### New grammatical category: pronoun/determiner discrimination begins (p.208)

КАЖДЫЙ-ЛЮБОЙ-ВСЯКИЙ marks this book's first extension of its near-synonym-discrimination method to
a part of speech other than noun/adjective — these three are pronominal determiners (traditionally
classed as "определительные местоимения" in Russian grammar) that decline and agree like adjectives
but function referentially rather than descriptively. This entry's full discrimination (distinguishing
КАЖДЫЙ's distributive-coverage sense from ЛЮБОЙ's agent-indifference sense and — not yet reached in
this page range — ВСЯКИЙ's own nuance) continues into the next shard.
