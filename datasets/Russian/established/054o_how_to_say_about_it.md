## Grammar points

### Choosing тоже vs. и: information structure, not free variation (§1.1, pp. 233–240)

This subsection develops one of the most information-structurally sophisticated grammar points in the
book, building directly on the general observation (already implicit elsewhere, but made explicit
here, p. 234) that a Russian sentence's *actual* new information (its rheme) sits at the sentence's
end under neutral intonation, while what the sentence is *about* (its theme) sits at the start. The
book's own worked example (pp. 234–235) with a numbered set of "Antón also arrived" variants makes
the underlying logic concrete: whether и or тоже is chosen, and where it's placed, depends entirely
on **what exactly the second sentence is asserting as new** relative to the first:

- If the second sentence's new content is that *another person/subject* did the same thing as the
  first (the subject itself is new) → **и** precedes that subject: "Я приехал во вторник. — И Антон
  приехал во вторник."
- If the *subject* is already known/given and instead some **other component** (time, place,
  manner, etc.) matches the first sentence exactly → **тоже** marks that shared component, and its
  position moves depending on which component is being aligned: "Антон тоже приехал во вторник" (the
  whole action matches) vs. "Антон приехал тоже во вторник" (specifically the *day* matches).

The core generalization (p. 237, boxed as "Обратите внимание!"): **"новое" is judged by content, not
by matching wording** — the second sentence can describe the aligned fact using entirely different
words from the first and still count as non-new information requiring тоже, provided the
*content* matches (e.g. "Я с удовольствием прочитала этот рассказ. — Антону тоже этот рассказ
понравился." — different verbs, same underlying "liked it" content, still тоже). A long graded
exercise set (pp. 236–240) drills recognizing which grammatical constituent is actually new across
sentence pairs describing weather, exam results, travel plans, etc. — used to train the underlying
information-structure judgment rather than introduce further new lexical content.

### Тоже's positional flexibility as a scope-marking device (§1.2, pp. 241–245)

Building on subsection 1, this part shows тоже's position within the second sentence is not free
variation but actively **marks which component of the sentence is being asserted as aligned** with
the first sentence — reordering тоже changes what, precisely, is being claimed as similar. The book's
central worked example (p. 242, an "Antón solved three problems in an hour" exchange) demonstrates
four distinct scope readings purely by moving тоже's position: тоже решал задачи (the *action* itself
matches) / тоже три (the *quantity* matches) / тоже за час (the *duration* matches) — summarized in
the boxed rule (p. 242): тоже can align the action itself, its time, its duration, or its location,
depending purely on where in the sentence it's placed relative to the aligned constituent. Извлечь
this distinction is drilled at length (pp. 243–245) against several open-ended factual-description
exercises (comparing two chemical gases, two Moscow squares), asking learners to generate their own
тоже/и-marked comparison sentences rather than fill in blanks — a notably more productive exercise
format than most of the book's earlier drills.
