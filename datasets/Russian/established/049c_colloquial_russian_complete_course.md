## Grammar points

### Lesson 6 — Reflexive verbs, dative case
- **Reflexive verbs**: formed with -ся (after a consonant) or -сь (after a vowel), marking the
  subject acting on itself (одева́ться "to dress oneself" vs. transitive одева́ть "to dress
  [someone/something]"). Reflexives can also express a passive-like sense (называ́ются кроссо́вки
  "they are called trainers," lit. "call themselves"; цветы́ продаю́тся "flowers are sold"). Some
  -ся verbs (стара́ться "to try") carry **no** literal reflexive or passive meaning — an
  arbitrary lexicalized -ся, worth flagging for later analysis of how a slang register might
  reanalyze a marked verb-form as unmarked-but-obligatory.
- **Dative case singular**: masc. consonant-stem +у, -о→-у; masc. -ь/-й and neuter -е →-ю (Кремль
  →Кремлю́); fem. nouns take the same endings as their prepositional singular (же́нщина→же́нщине,
  профе́ссия→профе́ссии, по́мощь→по́мощи); irregular мать→ма́тери, дочь→до́чери; neuter -мя nouns
  →-ени (и́мя→и́мени).
- **Dative personal pronouns**: мне, тебе́, ему́, ей, нам, вам, им.
- **Uses of dative**: indirect object ("to give/tell/show to X"); required object of certain verbs
  that take a direct object in English but dative in Russian (помога́ть, сове́товать); after the
  prepositions к ("towards"/"to someone's house"/fixed time expressions) and по ("along," "by,"
  "according to" — profession sense: по профе́ссии); with impersonal predicatives (тру́дно,
  на́до) + dative "for X"; and with several **dative-subject reflexive constructions** where the
  English subject becomes a Russian dative and the verb stays 3rd-person-neuter: хоте́ться (Пи́теру
  не хо́чется встава́ть — "Peter doesn't feel like getting up," contrasted explicitly with the
  volitional Пи́тер не хо́чет встава́ть "Peter doesn't want to get up" — a genuine
  volition-vs.-desire semantic split encoded grammatically); нра́виться, which additionally *agrees
  in number/gender with the liked thing*, not with the "liker" (мне нра́вятся ва́ши пирожки́ — the
  pirozhki, not "I," control verb agreement — Russian's structural equivalent of "please" as in
  archaic English "it pleases me"); and the short adjective ну́жен/нужна́/ну́жно/нужны́ + dative
  ("X is necessary to Y" = "Y needs X").
- Prepositional case of personal pronouns given in full (мне, тебе́, нём, ней, нас, вас, них); о
  becomes об before a vowel; в/к/с become во/ко/со before words starting with a consonant cluster.

### Lesson 7 — Imperfective past tense, instrumental case, чей
- **Imperfective past tense**: drop -ть from the infinitive, add -л (masc.)/-ла (fem.)/-ло
  (neut.)/-ли (all plural) — agrees in number/gender, not person. Reflexive past adds -ся/-лась/
  -лось/-лись. As Russian's only past-tense form for imperfective verbs, игра́л can translate
  "played," "was playing," or "used to play" — a genuine tense/aspect gap relative to English worth
  flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase (how would an invented register disambiguate this, if
  at all?).
- **Irregular past tenses**: идти́→шёл/шла/шло/шли (suppletive stem); есть→ел/е́ла/е́ло/е́ли;
  мочь→мог/могла́/могло́/могли́; быть→был/была́/бы́ло/бы́ли (note stress shift to the ending in
  the feminine only — a recurring stress-mobility pattern worth tracking).
- **Past-tense agreement quirks carried over from present-tense impersonal/dative constructions**:
  до́лжен был... (до́лжен agrees with its subject, был with до́лжен, forming a compound); нужна́
  была́ по́мощь (agrees with по́мощь, the "needed" thing, not the "needer"); тру́дно/мо́жно/на́до +
  бы́ло (neuter, since these are impersonal); э́то была́ моя́ мечта́ (быть agrees with the
  nominative predicate мечта́, not with the neuter "it"); у меня́ была́ сестра́ (agrees with the
  possessed item, since there's no dedicated "to have" verb — same have-construction from Lesson 4
  extended into the past); не́ было (past tense of нет) + genitive.
- **Нра́виться/хоте́ться in the past**: agree with the liked/wanted noun where present as subject,
  but default to neuter singular before an infinitive (мне нра́вилось рисова́ть "I liked to draw";
  Са́ше хоте́лось быть футболи́стом "Sasha wanted to be a footballer").
- **Instrumental case singular**: masc. consonant-stem +ом (-ём if stressed on ending, or forced by
  the о/е spelling rule: врач→врачо́м); masc. -ь/-й and neuter -е →-ем/-ём; neuter -мя →-енем
  (и́мя→и́менем); fem. -а →-ой (-ей if spelling-rule-affected or stressed -ёй); fem. -я →-ей/-ёй;
  fem. -ь nouns →-ью (гимна́стика... — note: this file paraphrases the source's -ью feminine-soft
  instrumental rather than reproducing its full table verbatim); мать/дочь insert -ер- throughout
  the oblique cases except nom./acc. Instrumental personal pronouns: мной(мно́ю), тобо́й(тобо́ю),
  им, ей(е́ю), на́ми, ва́ми, и́ми.
- **Uses of instrumental**: "with/by" the instrument of an action (рисова́ть карандашо́м); after
  verbs занима́ться, интересова́ться, увлека́ться, станови́ться ("to become") — all governing
  instrumental for their complement; as the complement of быть when in infinitive/past/future
  (хоте́л быть футболи́стом; Тама́ра была́ чемпио́нкой — note the feminine occupational-noun form
  чемпио́нка used here); after the prepositions пе́ред ("in front of/just before"), под ("under" —
  but он идёт под мост "he's going under the bridge" takes *accusative* since it's motion, a
  location/motion split parallel to в/на from Lesson 3), с ("together with" — мы с Тама́рой
  занима́емся спо́ртом "Tamara and I go in for sport," a construction pattern — "1st person + с +
  instrumental" meaning "X and I" — worth flagging as a structurally distinct way to express
  compound subjects), над ("over"), за ("behind," also possible with accusative).
- **Season/time-of-day expressions use the bare instrumental**, no preposition: весно́й, ле́том,
  о́сенью, зимо́й, у́тром, днём, ве́чером, но́чью.
- **Чей/чья/чьё/чьи** ("whose") agrees in gender/number/case with the possessed noun, not the
  possessor; declines fully (чьим рису́нком etc.) — full paradigm deferred to the book's own
  Grammar Summary at the back.
- **у меня́ as possessive**: extends the "have" construction (у + genitive) to also stand in for
  "my" in some contexts (у меня́ в ко́мнате "in my room").
- Time expressions: с + genitive = "since" (с де́тства); в + prepositional = "in" for some abstract
  time nouns (в де́тстве).
