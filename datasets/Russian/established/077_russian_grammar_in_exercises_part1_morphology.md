комментариях. Морфология* [Russian Grammar in Exercises and Comments: Morphology], 4th ed.,
St. Petersburg: Zlatoust, 2007 (1st ed. 2000) — 424pp

**Source file:** `source_reference/languages/Russian/Russian Learning Pack [up-to-date as of
2012]/Vol 1 of 3/47.Russian Grammar in Exercises and Comments. Part 1. Morphology.pdf`

**Extraction method:** Vision-read. `pdftotext`/`pdfimages` confirmed this copy has **zero text
layer of any kind** — every one of its 424 pages is a single full-page CCITT/JPEG raster image
(`pdfinfo`/`pdffonts` show no embedded fonts at all). This is distinct from the "Cyrillic-as-
raster-images-inside-a-real-text-layer" gotcha already logged for a *different* copy/edition of
this same title in `00_Reference_Extraction_Spec.md` — this particular scan has no text layer
whatsoever, English or Cyrillic, and required full vision-reading throughout. Pages were rendered
via `pdftoppm` at 150dpi and tiled 2×2 (4 physical pages per image) for reading efficiency; no
handwritten marginalia was found anywhere sampled (a clean, unmarked copy). PDF-page-to-printed-
page offset verified empirically as `PDF page = printed page + 1` (confirmed against the printed
page numbers visible on rendered pages, not assumed from arithmetic).

**Book structure** (from its own table of contents, pp.3-6): Раздел 1 Имя существительное
(Noun, pp.7-103), Раздел 2 Имя прилагательное (Adjective, pp.104-153), Раздел 3 Местоимение
(Pronoun, pp.155-195), Раздел 4 Имя числительное (Numeral, pp.196-216), Раздел 5 Наречие (Adverb,
pp.217-239), Раздел 6 Частицы (Particles, pp.240-254), Раздел 7 Глагол (Verb, pp.255-368), then
an answer key and a "Грамматические таблицы" (Grammar Tables) appendix (pp.401-424). This is Part
1 (Morphology) of a two-part reference; Part 2 (Syntax) is a separate source not covered here.

**Non-redundant-supplement scope decision:** this language now has ~27 established/ sources
covering Russian grammar comprehensively, including the six-case declension system, verb aspect,
and motion-verb prefixation in exhaustive depth (esp. `002/003`, `006/007`, `016/017/018`, `028`,
`057`, `059`). This is an intermediate/advanced **exercise book for learners already fluent in
Russian** — it has no English glosses at all, and its core content (case paradigms, verb
conjugation drills, motion-verb prefix drills) is genuinely near-total overlap with what's already
established. Its real, non-redundant value is concentrated in: (a) its "commentary" rule-boxes,
which often state a sharper, more precise usage distinction than prior sources; and (b) a
substantial number of closed-set **idioms, proverbs, and similes built on specific grammatical
categories** (short-form adjectives, demonstrative/negative pronouns, ordinal numerals, reduplicated
adverbs) that are directly relevant to register/slang-mechanics analysis and were not captured from
prior sources in this form. Coverage below is deliberately **uneven by design**: Sections 2
(Adjective), 3 (Pronoun), 5 (Adverb), and 6 (Particles) — the near-synonym/idiom/register-richest
chapters — got a full page-by-page vision-read (see `077b`–`077e`). Sections 1 (Noun), 4
(Numeral), and 7 (Verb) — the most heavily case/aspect/motion-verb territory, already
exhaustively covered elsewhere — got a **representative sampling pass** (multiple spot-checks
across each section's page range) confirming near-total redundancy, with only genuinely new
items kept (see `077f`). The back-matter "Grammar Tables" appendix (pp.401-424, a systematic
preposition-meaning table) was spot-checked and found to be a compact restatement of case/
preposition territory already established; not transcribed in full per copyright discipline.

**Known gap, honestly flagged:** Sections 1, 4, and 7 were not read page-by-page in full — only
sampled. Given their confirmed heavy redundancy with 27 prior sources, this is judged an
acceptable, deliberate scope decision rather than an oversight, but it means some genuinely novel
item in an unsampled page range of those three sections could exist and was not caught. No
handwritten marginalia was found in any sampled page.

### Grammar points

**Pluralia tantum, closed list.** The source's own list of nouns that exist only in the plural:

(clock/watch), духи (perfume), деньги (money), обои (wallpaper), дрожжи (yeast), щи (cabbage
soup), хлопоты (chores/trouble), ворота (gate). Same closed set already implicit across prior
sources but not previously gathered into one compact list.

**Register-marked footnote items (pp.46-49).** Two proverb-embedded words explicitly footnoted by
the source itself as **просторечие** (folk/substandard register): "не плошай" (don't be careless,
from the proverb "на Бога надейся, а сам не плошай" — trust in God but don't slack yourself) and
"проруха" (a blunder/mishap, from "и на старуху бывает проруха" — even an old woman can slip up).
Also the fixed idiom "смотреть в корень" (get to the root of the matter).

## Section 4 (Numeral) — light supplement pass, pp.196-216 (PDF pp.197-217)

Sampled pp.196-199 (ordinal numerals) and pp.212-216 (collective numerals + review unit).
Confirmed standard territory (ordinal/collective/fractional numeral morphology) already covered
elsewhere. Genuinely useful finds are a cluster of numeral-based idioms and two colloquial-register
usage notes, kept below; full drill content skipped as redundant.

### Grammar points

**Colloquial clock-time convention.** In everyday spoken Russian, times are conventionally given
with ordinal numerals rather than cardinal 24-hour style: "сейчас два часа дня" (it's 2pm now,
lit. "two o'clock of the day") rather than "14 часов," and minutes past/to the half-hour are
given as ordinals up to 30 ("без двадцати два" = "twenty to two," not "1:40"). A register
convention rather than a strict grammar rule, but a genuine spoken/written divide worth flagging.

**Abbreviation conventions.** в./вв. for век/века (century/centuries), г./гг. for год/годы
(year/years), (до) н.э. for (до) нашей эры (BCE/CE) — standard written-register abbreviations.
