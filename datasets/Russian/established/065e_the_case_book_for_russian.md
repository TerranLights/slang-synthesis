## Grammar points — Locative case (pp. 138–152)

The locative network has a single branch, **A PLACE** — a setting in space, time, or a
metaphorical domain — reflecting that the locative is the only case in Russian always paired with
a preposition (never a "bare case" use). Five prepositions are covered: в 'in', на 'on, at',
при 'by, at', о 'about', and по 'after' (a historical leftover, distinct from the more common
dative-governing по covered in 065c).

### в 'in' — enclosed, three-dimensional space (and its metaphorical extensions)

Marks location inside a bounded container — literal (apartments, an elevator, cellophane
wrapping) or metaphorical (marriage as a bounded state one is "in": *в браке* "in marriage";
inflation figures "in" a calculation). Clothing is treated as a container for the body (*в
коротком пальто* "in a short coat"). "At a distance of" is expressed with a distance measurement
+ в + locative (*в 800 километрах* "at 800 kilometers").

### на 'on, at' — two-dimensional surfaces (and the "на-word" exception list)

Marks location on a surface (literally: chairs, a face) or, since a flat expanse readily doubles
as an unbounded area, a broad category of locations that Russian conventionally treats as surfaces
even where English would say "in": **the book flags this "на-word" list as one of the least
predictable parts of Russian case usage**, since there's no perfect semantic rule — students are
advised to simply memorize the list. Representative categories from the book's inventory (pp.
144–145, itself organized into sub-lists):

- **Islands, peninsulas, mountain ranges, clearings**: на Аляске "in Alaska," на Кавказе "in the
  Caucasus," на поляне "in a clearing"
- **Points of embarkation for travel**: на аэродроме "at an airfield," на вокзале "at a [train]
  station," на станции "at a [bus] station"
- **Events**: на балу "at a ball," на выставке "at an exhibition," на конференции "at a
  conference"
- **Compass points and other generalized locations**: на востоке "in the east," на юге "in the
  south," на природе "in the open air, outdoors"
- **Certain buildings, worksites, and academic units**: на фабрике "at a factory," на факультете
  "at a department (of a university)," на рынке "at the market"
- **Upper floors of buildings**: на балконе "on the balcony," на этаже "on the [given] floor"

A small set of words tolerate **either** в or на with little difference in meaning (*в
доме/на доме* "in a house," *в кухне/на кухне* "in a kitchen," *в квартире/на квартире* "in an
apartment"). на is also standard for weekday-anchored recurring time expressions (*на этой
неделе* "this week") and for domains of abstraction rarely marked as a location at all
("relations *on* two countries," "when a shift *on* priorities happens").

### The second locative (locative-2, -у/-ю)

**About 150 masculine nouns have an alternate, always-stressed "second locative" ending in -у/-ю,**
used only with the prepositions в and на, and only in a spatial (never temporal or metaphorical)
sense: *в мозгу* "in the brain," *на мосту* "on the bridge," *в бреду* "in delirium," *на ходу*
"on the move/while moving." This is a genuinely specific morphological fact — a second, semantically
restricted paradigm alongside the "regular" locative ending — flagged by the book as worth a
dedicated list precisely because the choice between the ordinary and second-locative endings for
the same noun is not fully predictable from meaning alone (the noun must simply be known to belong
to this small class).

### при 'by, at' — permanent/inherent connection

A less frequent locative-governing preposition marking a *permanent* attachment to something,
contrasted implicitly with temporary attachments elsewhere in the case system (e.g. instrumental's
с + instrumental, 065's An Adjunct). Concretely: something physically built onto/permanently
connected to a larger structure (a stove built into a furnace); metaphorically, a condition
attached to something (a loan given "on the condition that" — *при условии*), and the frozen idiom
*при себе* "on one's person," *при чём* "what's the relevance" / *ни при чём* "irrelevant."

### о 'about' — topics of thought, speech, and writing

The primary preposition for topics one thinks, speaks, or writes about (*слышал по радио о
счастье* "heard on the radio about happiness"; "wrote a novel about the working class"). The book
notes this is one of the more semantically transparent locative uses, mapping closely onto
English "about."

### по 'after' — a historical/bureaucratic leftover

A now-narrow, formal-register use of the locative-governing по (distinct from the far more
productive dative-governing по covered in 065c) meaning "after, upon" — restricted to a small set
of bureaucratic-flavored phrases: *по окончании работы* "upon completion of work," *по приезде*
"upon arrival," *по возвращении* "upon return." Also used for emotional longing "for" a
person/place/time (*тосковать по* + locative or, alternatively, dative — the book notes both cases
are attested for this verb).

The Epilogue draws the chapter's overarching generalization: Russian conceptually treats most
locations either as containers (в) or as surfaces (на), and because many real-world locations are
not unambiguously either, Russian relies on convention (the на-word list) rather than a clean
semantic rule to resolve the ambiguity — the same container/surface metaphor recurs across the
whole case system (echoing the destination/source symmetry documented in the accusative, dative,
and genitive chapters).

## Appendix and back matter (printed pp. 153–316) — scope decision

The book's remaining ~163 pages consist of: an **Appendix** (pp. 153–168, spelling rules and
declension-paradigm summary tables for nouns/adjectives/pronouns/numerals/prepositions), three
**Levels of graded Exercises** (pp. 169–286, drill exercises organized by case and by the
book's own three proficiency levels), and separate **English** and **Russian indexes**.

- **Appendix**: spot-checked (spelling rules on hard/soft consonant pairing for case endings, plus
  full noun/adjective/pronoun/numeral declension tables by case). This is the one section of the
  book that *does* re-teach paradigms — but it is presented purely as a reference summary (the
  same six paradigm tables, one appended after each case chapter throughout the book, collected
  here into one place), not as new pedagogical content. **Confirmed fully redundant** with this
  dataset's existing declension-paradigm sources (Wade, Timberlake, Schaum's Outline, etc.) and not
  re-extracted.
- **Exercises (Levels I–III, pp. 169–286)**: per the coverage rule, these are repeated drill
  exercises reusing vocabulary already introduced in the case chapters above (nominative/
  instrumental/accusative/dative/genitive/locative exercises, mixed-case exercises, multi-case-
  preposition exercises) — they introduce no new grammar points or vocabulary beyond what the
  chapters themselves already cover, and are correctly skipped per the "comprehensive but not
  exhaustive" coverage rule. No answer key/exercise content was extracted.
- **English and Russian indexes**: pure back-of-book navigational aids (word/topic → page number),
  not extractable content in their own right.
