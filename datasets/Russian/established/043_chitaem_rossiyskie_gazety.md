# Russian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Куркина/Пляскова, *Читаем российские газеты* (full book)

**Source:** А.С. Куркина, Е.А. Пляскова, *Читаем российские газеты: Учебно-методическое пособие
для вузов* (Воронежский государственный университет, 2007), full 67-page book — "Presentation of
the newspaper" intro + Раздел 1 (Визиты. Переговоры), Раздел 2 (Экономика — 5 newspaper texts),
Раздел 3 (Проблемы XXI века — 3 newspaper texts).

**Coverage note:** Clean Cyrillic text layer, no font-substitution cipher and no vision-reading
needed (`pdftotext -layout` produced correctly-rendered Cyrillic throughout; verified against dozens
of recognizable Russian words on inspection, e.g. correct rendering of «государство», «переговоры»,
«суверенитет» etc. across widely separated pages) — none of the five Cyrillic corruption variants
found elsewhere in this project's Russian corpus (see `00_Reference_Extraction_Spec.md`'s PDF-gotchas
section) are present in this file. No handwritten marginalia encountered (this was a clean digital
text-layer PDF, not a scan).

This is a **journalistic-register reading/writing practicum**, structured very differently from a
standard vocabulary/grammar reference: rather than lesson-by-lesson core vocabulary, each of its 8
newspaper texts (real articles from АиФ, «Известия», «Парламентская газета», «Литературная газета»)
is bracketed by (a) pre-text vocabulary/term-matching exercises and (b) a large bank of fixed
**journalistic-register communicative constructions** ("Кто? отправился с визитом куда?", "Автор
приходит к выводу, что…") used to summarize, compare viewpoints, and write annotations/abstracts
(аннотация, реферат-описание, реферат-обзор) about news content. Per this dispatch's register
priority, the construction banks are treated as the primary content here (captured as **Grammar
points**, since they are register-bound syntactic/discourse patterns, not individual lexical items)
and pair directly with `established/014_newspaper_russian_vocabulary.md`'s administrative/commercial
idiom vocabulary from Wave 1 — this book supplies the *discourse-level* journalistic register (how
a Russian-language news article states, hedges, compares, and concludes) that 014's word-list format
does not capture. Vocabulary extraction below captures every distinct term-matching set, abbreviation
set, and idiom/expression bank the book itself designates as vocabulary (its own explicit pedagogical
vocabulary content) — not every incidental word appearing in the eight reprinted newspaper articles'
running prose, which would balloon into near-verbatim reproduction and violate copyright discipline.
Repeated fill-in-the-blank drills reusing the same vocabulary are skipped per the coverage rule.

## Grammar points

### Numeral declension (сорок/девяносто/сто; -десят/-сот compounds; тысяча/миллион/миллиард)

Paraphrased from the book's "Запомните!" box, p.11-12. Сорок, девяносто, сто have only two forms:
nominative/accusative -о (сорок, девяносто, сто) vs. every other case -а (сорока, девяноста, ста);
the accompanying noun takes the case that governs the whole numeral phrase. Compound numerals built
on -десят (пятьдесят, шестьдесят, семьдесят, восемьдесят) and -сот (пятьсот...девятьсот) decline
*both* parts, each declining like a 3rd-declension noun. Двести/триста/четыреста decline both parts
too. Тысяча/миллион/миллиард decline like ordinary nouns and always force the accompanying noun into
the genitive. A fully compound numeral (e.g. триста двадцать четыре) declines every one of its
component words. Full six-case paradigm tables given for сорок/девяносто/сто, for
пятьдесят/пятьсот/двести, and for триста двадцать четыре.

### Reading fractional and decimal numerals

Paraphrased, p.26-27. A simple fraction reads as cardinal-numerator (nom.) + ordinal-denominator
(gen. pl.): 3/5 = «три пятых». Numerator 1 uses the feminine cardinal «одна» + feminine ordinal
denominator (1/5 = «одна пятая»); numerator 2 uses feminine cardinal «две» + genitive-plural ordinal
denominator (2/3 = «две третьих»). The noun modified by any fractional numeral is always genitive.
Decimals read as [cardinal whole number] + целая/целых + [cardinal fractional part], e.g. 3.8 = «три
целых восемь десятых»; a whole/fractional part ending in 1 uses the feminine singular form («одна
целая»), ending in 2 uses genitive «целых» with a feminine cardinal. In colloquial speech, ноль and
целая/и are often dropped (0.8 = «восемь десятых»; 1.3% = «одна и три десятых процента»), and
четверть/треть/половина/полтора(-ы) substitute for 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 1.5 respectively.

### Distinguishing и-differentiating stem-related paronyms (economic/political register)

The book repeatedly drills near-homophonous or same-root derivational sets whose members are easily
confused in formal writing but carry distinct register-specific meanings — e.g.
экономный/экономичный/экономический (thrifty [of a person] / fuel-efficient [of a device] / of-the-
economy [adj.]) and нищий/нищенский (a literal destitute person/adj. vs. a figurative-colloquial
"measly" intensifier). This paronym-discrimination pattern recurs across the book's vocabulary
exercises and is presented as a standing register-literacy skill for journalistic Russian, not a
one-off drill.

### Active↔passive construction transformation (journalistic register)

Paraphrased from the "Вспомните и повторите!" box, p.16, and drilled repeatedly across all five
economics texts. Active: nominative subject + imperfective verb + accusative object ("В статье автор
рассказывает о проблемах…"); its impersonal passive counterpart uses a reflexive (-ся) verb with the
former object promoted to subject and the former subject demoted to instrumental or dropped entirely
("В статье рассказывается о проблемах…"). For perfective verbs, the passive instead uses a short-form
passive participle ("В статье автор затронул проблемы" → "В статье затронуты проблемы"). This
active/passive alternation is presented as the single most pervasive register marker distinguishing
neutral narration from impersonal, "objective"-sounding journalistic/administrative prose — precisely
the register split most relevant to a synthesis phase modeling formal vs. informal registers.

### Participle formation (present/past active and passive)

Full paradigm table, p.36. **Active participles**: present active takes -ущ/-ющ (1st conjugation,
e.g. писать→пишущий) or -ащ/-ящ (2nd conjugation, e.g. слышать→слышащий); past active takes -вш (from
a vowel-final stem, e.g. изучать→изучивший) or -ш (from a consonant-final stem, e.g. нести→нёсший).
**Passive participles**: present passive (from imperfective transitive verbs) takes -ем (1st
conjugation, e.g. решать→решаемый) or -им (2nd conjugation, e.g. любить→любимый), with a small closed
class of verbs (нести, везти, влечь) taking -ом (несомый); past passive (from perfective transitive
verbs) takes -нн/-т (from a vowel-final stem, e.g. прочитать→прочитанный) or -енн/-ённ (from a
consonant-final or -и stem, e.g. принести→принесённый).

### Converting subordinate который-clauses to participial phrases

Drilled at length, p.36-37: any relative clause introduced by который can be converted to a
participial phrase agreeing in gender/number/case with its antecedent — this is presented as the
default stylistic move for compressing spoken-register complex sentences into written journalistic
register, and the book's own worked examples (10 sentences) show the transformation applied across
subject, object, and multiply-embedded relative clauses.

### Journalistic-register communicative construction banks (register flagship content)

The book's central pedagogical content is a series of large, function-organized banks of fixed
semi-lexicalized constructions used in Russian news writing/summarizing — essentially collocational
templates rather than single vocabulary items. Paraphrased by function (page ranges cited); direct
quotes are the book's own illustrative skeleton templates, not reproduced running prose:

- **Reporting a state visit's start/progress/end** (p.5-6): "Кто? намерен посетить/осуществить
  поездку/отправиться с визитом куда?"; "Кто? отбыл/прибыл с (официальным/неофициальным) визитом
  куда?"; "Кто? завершил визит куда?" — a fixed cline from intention → departure → arrival →
  conclusion, each with its own dedicated verb.
- **Reporting negotiations' start/resumption/cancellation** (p.6): "Кто? провёл/возобновил переговоры
  с кем?"; "(были) возобновлены/отменены переговоры кого с кем?".
- **Reporting negotiation topics** (p.6-7): "Главной темой переговоров стал(-а,-о,-и) …"; "На
  переговорах стороны намерены обсудить вопросы …".
- **Reporting stated opinions/positions** (p.7): a bank of ~15 reporting verbs each with fixed
  government pattern — "заявил, что…", "высказал мнение о чём?", "поддержал/отклонил предложение
  кого?", "выразил что? (заинтересованность/уверенность/сомнение/озабоченность/надежду/
  удовлетворение)" — this reporting-verb-plus-abstract-noun-object pattern ("выразить + noun" instead
  of a semantically equivalent single verb) is itself named as a stylistic marker of official register
  in exercise 6a (glossary-to-verb replacement drill, p.20-21: выразить заинтересованность/
  уверенность/озабоченность/сомнение/удовлетворение/надежду ↔ their single-verb near-equivalents).
- **Summarizing/annotating a news article** (p.16-19): fixed templates for naming (Статья называется
  …), structuring (Статья делится на … частей), stating theme (Тема статьи – …; Автор даёт обзор
  чего?), highlighting main vs. secondary information (В статье подробно рассмотрены следующие
  вопросы; автор также касается вопроса чего?), citing illustrative material (Автор приводит
  следующие примеры/факты/данные), and stating conclusions (В заключение автор делает следующий
  вывод, что …) — this full apparatus underlies both the аннотация and реферат-описание genres.
- **Comparing/contrasting multiple viewpoints** (p.22-23, expanded p.49-51 for реферат-обзор across
  two full articles): agreement ("Как кто?, так и кто? считает, что…"; "Кто? и кто? сходятся во
  мнении"), disagreement ("В отличие от кого? кто? считает…"; "Кто? и кто? расходятся во мнении"), and
  the meta-text author's own stance ("Я (полностью) согласен/не согласен с точкой зрения кого?").
- **Expressing causation** (p.28-30 exercises reference a dedicated causation-construction set used
  throughout): "Экономический рост связан с чем?"; "Причина … заключается/объясняется тем, что…".
- **Expressing comparison/contrast of quantities** (p.30): "Если в России что? составляет сколько?, то
  в других странах – сколько?"; "По сравнению с другими странами что? в России ниже/выше на сколько?".
- **Expressing emotional/evaluative stance in a conclusion** (p.30-31): a fixed "выражает + abstract
  noun" pattern — сомнение/надежду/озабоченность/опасение/радость — by poduct of chto-clause,
  paralleling the exercise-6a pattern above.
- **Explaining causal factors of success** (p.42-43, Text 4's exercises): an extensive bank of near-
  synonymous causation templates specifically for "X was the reason/factor behind success," e.g. "Залог
  успеха (авторы видят) в том, что…"; "Что? сыграло немаловажную роль в чём?"; "Что? дало сильный
  толчок чему?" — 10+ near-paraphrases of the same underlying causal-attribution meaning, presented as
  interchangeable register-appropriate variants rather than semantically distinct constructions.
- **Expressive discourse markers for "surprise reception" vs. "certainty" in spoken-register framing**
  (p.45): a paired table distinguishing utterances that prepare a listener for surprising information
  ("Представляете…", "Можешь себе представить…", "Подумать только…", "Надо же…", "Вообрази…") from the
  hearer's stock certainty-affirming reply ("Ещё бы, ведь…", "Это и не удивительно, ведь…", "Чему ж тут
  удивляться, ведь…") — a fixed call-and-response discourse pattern the book identifies as
  characteristic of colloquial Russian storytelling even when the content being narrated is formal/
  economic in nature.

### "Non-redundant supplement" scope note

This book's grammar content (numeral declension, participle formation, active/passive transformation)
substantially overlaps material already captured more systematically in prior Wave 1 established/
files (e.g. `006/007_reference_grammar_of_russian_cambridge_part1/2.md` for participles, and several
other sources for numeral declension) — those paradigm tables are recorded here only in brief since
the fuller systematic treatment exists elsewhere; nothing here should be read as a more authoritative
paradigm than those sources. What is genuinely new and non-redundant, and the reason this file is
worth its own extraction, is the **construction-bank material** above: no prior Russian extraction in
this project's `established/` directory has captured fixed journalistic-register communicative
templates at this density, and `014_newspaper_russian_vocabulary.md`'s word-list format doesn't
capture discourse-level constructions at all. This is exactly the register-pairing this dispatch
flagged as the reason to prioritize this source.
