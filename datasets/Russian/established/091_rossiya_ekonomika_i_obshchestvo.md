# Russian — Rodimkina & Landsman, *Россия: экономика и общество* (Russia: Economics and Society) — Texts and Exercises, Zlatoust, St. Petersburg, 2007, 160 pp.

**Source type:** thematic reading/vocabulary course for B1–B2 learners — 16 adapted texts drawn from
contemporary (2002–2006) Russian periodicals (economics/business/society journalism), each with its
own Russian–English margin glossary, comprehension/lexical exercises, and a "Ключи" (Answer Keys)
section at the back.

**Coverage note.** No text layer (`pdftotext` returned nothing; `pdfinfo` shows `Creator: ScanKromsator`
— a scanned book). Vision-read via rendered page images (150 dpi). All 16 chapters' running texts and
their full Russian–English margin glossaries were read and extracted — every distinct glossed
vocabulary item across the whole book. The exercise pages that follow each text (true/false tables,
synonym/antonym drills, gap-fill, word-restoration puzzles, translation drills) were read but **not**
separately extracted per the coverage rule: they recycle the same vocabulary already captured from the
text+glossary pages and introduce no new lexical items, only drill repetition. The **Ключи** (Keys,
printed pp. 142–158) answer-key section was sampled and confirmed to contain only exercise answers —
no new vocabulary — and was skipped entirely, consistent with prior "answer key = pure drill, skippable"
findings elsewhere in this corpus (`048/048b_living_russian_grammar.md`).

**Page coverage:** full 160 pp. (PDF pages 1–160; printed pagination 1–158, offset +2 from PDF page
confirmed empirically against printed page numbers rather than assumed — see spec's page-range-offset
gotcha). All 16 chapters (printed pp. 6–141) covered; front matter (title/copyright/TOC/introduction,
printed pp. 1–5) and back matter (Keys, printed pp. 142–158) reviewed but not extracted as vocabulary
content.

**Vision Reading Confidence, book-wide default:** the great majority of entries below are `verified` —
each glossed term appears **twice** in the source itself (once bold in the running text, once in the
margin glossary), which is a built-in cross-check the format provides for nearly every entry. Entries
marked `plausible_unverified` are ones with only a single occurrence or a slightly ambiguous scan region
(none required a `low_confidence` flag — print quality throughout is clean and consistent, no
marginalia, no font-substitution cipher of any kind found in this source).

**Attested Era note:** unlike a single-author textbook, each of this book's 16 texts is adapted from a
specific dated newspaper/magazine article (cited at the end of each chapter). Where a specific citation
date is available, `Attested Era` below records it (e.g. "contemporary (article: *Коммерсантъ*,
16.02.05)") rather than only the whole-book 2007 publication year — a finer-grained legitimate case per
`00_Historical_and_Geographic_Context_Guide.md`, since the era claim is about the specific text the term
was drawn from, not an inference from lesson placement.

**Register/slang relevance (coverage-rule priority).** This source is a strong register/journalistic/
economic-register find as instructed. Beyond ordinary financial/business vocabulary, several chapters
surface genuine slang or colloquial-register items the source itself explicitly flags as such:

- Ch. 6 (*Плюсы и минусы дешёвого «Биг Мака»*) glosses **«зелёный»** as *"slang name for the American
  dollar"* (greenback) and **«деревянный»** as *"slang name for the Russian ruble"* (wooden) — both
  explicit source-marked currency slang.
- Ch. 9 (*Богаче английской королевы*) uses **нувориш** (nouveau-riche), **денежный мешок** ("money
  bag" — plutocrat), and the idiom **богатеть как на дрожжах** ("to get rich quick", lit. "to grow rich
  like yeast") in a wealth-reportage register.
- Ch. 10 (*Куда вложить деньги*) has the colloquialism **заначка** ("stash", money hidden from a
  spouse/family — a recognizable everyday-Russian household-economy word, not formal financial
  vocabulary).
- Ch. 11 (*Продаются чужие идеи*) has the business-slang idiom **нюх на идеи** ("a nose/flair for
  ideas") and **раскрутка** ("promotion/hype-building", colloquial business usage).
- Ch. 12 (*Бренды покорили глубинку*) has student/youth colloquialisms **сдать хвосты** ("to clear
  outstanding [exam] backlog", lit. "hand in tails") and **крутиться** ("to hustle, be constantly busy",
  colloquial), plus **подмять под себя** ("to squeeze out/dominate", idiom).
- Ch. 13 (*Реклама.net*) footnotes an actual swear-word-avoidance typographic convention: the ad slogan
  **«Цены просто ох..!»** is glossed with an explicit footnote explaining the dots stand in for a
  swear-word ending, and that this gap-letter convention is standard practice — a genuine, source-marked
  taboo-avoidance orthographic device.

## Grammar points

This source is a thematic reading/vocabulary course, not a grammar reference — it has no dedicated
grammar exposition chapters of its own. The few recurring structural patterns worth noting for the
mechanics-analysis phase:

1. **Bureaucratic/economic-register abbreviation density.** Nearly every chapter introduces at least
   one institutional abbreviation glossed with its full expansion (МВФ, ЖКХ, ВВП, ВЦИОМ, РАО ЕЭС, Мосгор-
   исполком, промкомбинат, лесхоз, охотхозяйство, Минздрав, теракт, сельхозпродукция). This is a
   productive Russian word-formation register (stump-compounds and initialisms) already flagged as a
   flagship finding in `073_..._073c_new_penguin_russian_course.md`'s Soviet abbreviation typology — this
   source adds contemporary (2000s) post-Soviet economic/administrative examples to that inventory
   (ЖКХ, ВЦИОМ, сельхозпродукция) alongside carried-over Soviet-period ones (Мосгорисполком).
2. **Deverbal-noun-heavy economic register.** A striking share of this book's vocabulary is abstract
   deverbal nouns in -ение/-ание (взимание, начисление, налогообложение, обсуждение, размещение,
   присоединение, воспроизводство, распределение) — a productive register marker for formal
   economic/journalistic Russian, contrasting with the concrete, colloquial register of the flagged
   slang items above (заначка, крутиться, раскрутка, глубинка). The register gap between these two
   vocabulary layers within the *same* 16-chapter corpus is itself a useful mechanics data point: formal
   economic journalism and its colloquial commentary voice code-switch within single articles (e.g.
   ch. 6's serious currency-parity discussion immediately followed by the slang aside about «зелёный»/
   «деревянный»).
3. **Source-marked taboo-avoidance orthography** (ch. 13, «Цены просто ох..!») — a real-world instance
   of the gap-letter/ellipsis convention for softening a swear word in print advertising, useful evidence
   for the taboo-avoidance-naming mechanics already tracked from SCB's Phase 1 findings.
