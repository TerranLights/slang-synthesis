## Grammar points

### Lesson 14 — Prefixed verbs of motion, "to sit"/"to lie down", numeral-noun inversion
- **Prefixed motion verbs — systematic formation rule**: a prefix attached to any of the six
  indeterminate/determinate motion-verb pairs from Lesson 10 yields a new imperfective/perfective
  pair, where the **prefixed imperfective is built from the old indeterminate (a) verb**, and the
  **prefixed perfective is built from the old determinate (b) verb** — i.e. the
  indeterminate/determinate contrast from Lesson 10 is entirely repurposed as the
  imperfective/perfective contrast once a directional prefix is added, and the original
  habitual-vs-single-occasion meaning disappears (a prefixed verb no longer carries that
  distinction; it now carries ordinary aspectual + directional meaning instead). This is a genuinely
  important reanalysis: the same morphological opposition (a-form vs. b-form) does double duty
  across the unprefixed and prefixed systems for entirely different semantic purposes, one
  worth flagging strongly for mechanics-analysis as a model of a grammatical category being
  "recycled" for a new function once a derivational layer (prefixation) is added.
  Each prefix carries a fairly stable directional/aspectual meaning, each construction requiring a
  specific governed preposition+case: в-/вы- (in/out, в+acc / из+gen), до- (as far as, до+gen),
  за- (to call at [в/на+acc] vs. call on a person [к+dat] vs. call for [за+instr] — three distinct
  senses of one prefix depending on the following preposition), об- (go round, +acc no preposition),
  от- (step away from, от+gen), пере- (cross, через+acc or bare acc.), при- (arrive, в/на+acc),
  под- (approach, к+dat), про- (go past, ми́мо+gen), с- (go/come down, с+gen), у- (leave [a place,
  из/с+gen] vs. leave [a person, от+gen] — the same prefix у- again splitting into two governed
  constructions by what's being left).
- **Stem-allomorphy under prefixation**: ходи́ть keeps its stem and conjugation unchanged when
  prefixed (вхожу́, входи́л, etc.); идти́ changes its bare root to -йти́ once prefixed (пойти́,
  войти́, etc.), with a linking -o- inserted when the prefix itself ends in a consonant (в+йти →
  вой­ти́). Perfective future of most -йти́ compounds follows войти́'s pattern (вошёл/вошла́/
  вошло́/вошли́ in the past; войду́/войдёшь in the future) — **except прийти́, whose future
  (приду́, придёшь) is an explicitly flagged irregular exception** to that otherwise-regular
  pattern, and вы́йти, whose **stress is always fixed on the prefix itself** (вы́йду, вы́шел) rather
  than floating as it does in most of this family — two separate, independent irregularities
  layered onto an already-derived paradigm. Similarly, е́здить is wholesale replaced by -езжа́ть
  (1st conjugation) once prefixed, while éхать keeps its own conjugation but requires an inserted
  hard sign ъ after any prefix ending in a consonant before -езжать/-ехать (объезжа́ть, etc.) —
  a straightforward orthographic-phonological rule (hard sign marking a prefix-stem boundary before
  a following iotated-adjacent sound) rather than a meaningful morpheme, but essential for correct
  spelling of this entire verb family. Носи́ть/нести́, вози́ть/везти́, води́ть/вести́, лета́ть/
  лете́ть, and бе́гать/бежа́ть all prefix "regularly" (conjugated exactly like their unprefixed
  forms), yielding parallel derivational families (вноси́ть/внести́ "bring in," ввози́ть/ввезти́
  "import," вводи́ть/ввести́ "lead in," влета́ть/влете́ть "fly in," вбега́ть/вбежа́ть "run in," etc.)
  — six independent lexical families all sharing the same directional-prefix inventory, a highly
  productive and systematic derivational layer worth flagging as a strong candidate model for a
  conlang's own directional-prefix system built on a small closed set of "manner of motion" roots.
  Some prefixed forms drift semantically further from their literal motion sense (находи́ть/найти́
  "to find," происходи́ть/произойти́ "to happen," приходи́ться/прийти́сь "to have to" — an
  impersonal-dative construction, мне прихо́дится/придётся "I have to/will have to") — a clear case
  of a productive directional-derivation system also serving as the historical source of abstract,
  non-spatial idioms, paralleling the везёт/повезло́ "to be lucky" idiom (also motion-root-derived,
  dative-subject impersonal) noted in the vocabulary.
- **Ложи́ться/лечь "to lie down" and сади́ться/сесть "to sit down"**: both are motion-into-position
  verbs (as opposed to the already-covered лежа́ть/сиде́ть, which describe the resulting static
  state) and consequently both govern a preposition + **accusative** (motion), contrasting directly
  with лежа́ть/сиде́ть's preposition + **prepositional** (location): Он ложи́тся на крова́ть "he
  lies down onto the bed" [accusative, motion] vs. Он лежи́т на крова́ти "he is lying on the bed"
  [prepositional, state] — the identical acc./prep. motion-vs-location contrast already seen with
  класть/ста́вить vs. лежа́ть/стоя́ть in Lesson 13 is shown to generalize to a third pair
  (ложиться-лечь/сидеть, садиться-сесть/сидеть), confirming this acc.=motion vs. prep.=location
  split as a systematic, productive principle across Russian's whole "position" verb class rather
  than a peculiarity of any one pair. Both лечь and сесть are irregular perfectives: лечь (perfective
  future ля́гу/ля́жешь…ля́гут; past лёг/легла́/легло́/легли́; imperative ляг/ля́гте) and сесть
  (perfective future ся́ду/ся́дешь…ся́дут; past сел/се́ла/се́ло/се́ли; imperative сядь/ся́дьте) —
  neither conjugation is predictable from a regular pattern and both must simply be memorized.
- **Inversion of numeral and noun for approximation**: placing the numeral *after* its noun
  (rather than the ordinary numeral-before-noun order) marks the quantity as approximate rather
  than exact — часа́ три "about three hours" (contrast три часа́ "[exactly] three hours"); any
  preposition governing the phrase stays attached to the numeral, not the noun, even under
  inversion (часо́в в во́семь "at about eight o'clock," not *в часо́в во́семь). This is a clean case
  of word order alone (rather than any additional particle or morpheme) carrying a specific,
  systematic semantic contrast (exact vs. approximate quantity) — a strong, simple candidate
  mechanism for mechanics-analysis: an invented slang variety could mark
  approximation/hedging purely through constituent-order inversion rather than inventing a new
  lexical hedge-word.
