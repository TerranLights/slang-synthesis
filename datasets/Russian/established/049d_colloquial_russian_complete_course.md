## Grammar points

### Lesson 8 — этот/тот/весь/один in oblique cases, свой, prepositional -у
- **Full adjective agreement recap**: adjectives (including this/that/all/one) agree in gender,
  case, and number; masculine accusative = nominative for inanimate nouns but = genitive for
  animate nouns (я зна́ю отли́чный буфе́т vs. я зна́ю отли́чного студе́нта) — the animacy split
  from Lesson 5 extends to adjectives modifying animate nouns, not just the nouns themselves.
- **Masc./neut. genitive -ого/-его pronounced "-ovo"/"-yevo"** despite the г spelling — a fixed
  pronunciation exception, distinct from ordinary devoicing rules.
- **Possessives мой/ваш/наш/твой decline fully** (agree in gender/case/number with the possessed
  noun, not just nominative); его́/её/их remain invariant in all cases.
- **Свой** — the reflexive possessive, the single most structurally important point in this
  lesson: used instead of мой/твой/наш/ваш/его́/её/их whenever the possessive refers back to the
  **subject of its own clause**. Са́ша лю́бит свою́ жену́ = "Sasha loves his own wife"; Са́ша лю́бит
  его́ жену́ = "Sasha loves someone else's (his) wife" — the choice of свой vs. его́ is the *only*
  grammatical marker disambiguating these two readings, since Russian has no separate word for
  "his own" vs. "his." Critically, свой tracks the subject of the clause it appears in, not an
  earlier clause's subject in a complex sentence (Са́ша зна́ет, кто лю́бит его́ жену́ — "his" must be
  eго́ here because "who" is the subject of its own embedded clause, not Sasha). Свой virtually
  never appears in the nominative (since it never IS the subject) — the sole exception is the
  у + genitive "have" construction (у ка́ждого студе́нта своя́ ко́мната). This mechanism —
  disambiguating "his own" from "someone else's" via an obligatory subject-bound reflexive
  possessive — is a strong, well-attested candidate for adaptation into invented-slang possessive
  marking; flagging prominently for the mechanics-analysis phase.
- **Prepositional -у ending**: certain masculine nouns take a stressed -у́ (not the regular -е)
  in the prepositional case, but only after в/на denoting location: в лесу́, в саду́, на берегу́,
  в углу́, в шкафу́, в Крыму́, в про́шлом году́. After any other preposition (е.g. о) the regular
  -е returns (исто́рия о ле́се). A closed, lexically-specified exception class — useful reference
  point for how an invented language might mark a small "special-locative" noun class.
- **на + accusative for intentional duration**: "for" a period of time is на + accusative *only*
  when the subject sets out with the specific intention of spending that time (е́ду в Сиби́рь на
  год); if not framed as an intention, the bare accusative (no preposition) is used instead (была́ в
  Сиби́ри год) — a subtle intentionality-conditioned case-marking split.

### Lesson 9 — Imperfective future tense, numerals, dates, друг дру́га
- **Future tense of быть**: бу́ду/бу́дешь/бу́дет/бу́дем/бу́дете/бу́дут — the only synthetic future
  in Russian. All other verbs form their (imperfective) future periphrastically: бу́ду + infinitive
  (бу́ду игра́ть). Because this is the *imperfective* future specifically, it covers both English
  "will play" and "will be playing" — the same one-form-covers-many-English-forms gap already seen
  in the imperfective past (Lesson 7); a genuinely systematic aspect-driven ambiguity worth
  cross-referencing in mechanics analysis.
- **Impersonal/agreement patterns carry into the future** exactly as they did into the past:
  и́нтересно/на́до + бу́дет (default 3rd-sg.-neuter); до́лжен/нужна́/etc. + the appropriate form of
  быть, agreeing the same way its short-adjective/agreeing-noun counterpart did in the present and
  past (ка́ждому ну́жен бу́дет костю́м; нам нужны́ бу́дут при́зы — бу́дут agrees with при́зы, the
  grammatical subject, exactly paralleling нужны́ бу́дут's own agreement); не бу́дет + genitive is
  the future of нет/не́ было.
- **Numerals**: cardinals 1–30 and ordinals 1st–30th given in full. Ordinals are adjectives with
  full gender/case/number agreement; in compound ordinals (два́дцать пе́рвый "twenty-first") only
  the *final* element is an adjective — earlier elements stay as invariant cardinal forms. All
  ordinals are hard adjectives except тре́тий, an irregular soft-type declension pattern of its own
  (distinct even from the after-hissing-consonant soft type).
- **Dates**: "What's the date?" = Како́е сего́дня число́? (число́ neuter, agreeing with како́е);
  "the Nth of [month]" puts the ordinal in the genitive with the month also genitive (два́дцать
  пе́рвое декабря́ "the 21st of December" as an answer, но два́дцать пе́рвого декабря́ "on the 21st
  of December" — nominative-as-date-label vs. genitive-as-"on-this-date" is itself the marker of
  the "on" meaning, with no preposition).
- **Time expressions with в/на + accusative**: в ту ночь, в нового́днюю ночь, в по́лночь, на Но́вый
  год, на Рождество́.
- **Spelling rule (я/ю)**: я and ю never follow г, к, х, ж, ч, ш, щ, ц — replaced by а and у
  respectively; this conditions certain 2nd-conjugation verb endings (лежа́ть: лежу́/лежа́т instead
  of лежу́/*лежя́т; слы́шать: слы́шу/слы́шат).
- **Друг дру́га** ("one another"): fixed reciprocal expression — the first друг never changes, the
  second declines like the noun друг according to whatever case/preposition the governing verb
  requires (accusative after поздравля́ть, dative after писа́ть, o + prepositional after ду́мать —
  a preposition, when required, is inserted **between** the two elements: друг о дру́ге).
