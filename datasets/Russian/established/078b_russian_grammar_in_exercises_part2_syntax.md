## Grammar points

### Раздел 2 overview (p.126)
Glazunova defines простое предложение as a grammatically-formed word combination that (1)
reflects the reality/unreality (possibility, desirability, necessity) of the events described, (2)
conveys the speaker's attitude toward them, and (3) has semantic and intonational completeness.
Three organizing aspects are distinguished: **formal** (main members подлежащее/сказуемое vs.
secondary members определение/обстоятельство/дополнение), **semantic** (субъект/предикат/объект),
and **communicative** (actual articulation — тема/рема, elaborated at p.235, see below).

### Ways of expressing the subject and the semantic subject (pp. 127-145)
Subject (подлежащее) can be realized as: a noun/pronoun in the nominative (the default case); any
part of speech used substantively (*Сытый голодного не разумеет* — "the well-fed doesn't understand
the hungry," a substantivized adjective pair); a quantity-word combination (*Пришло несколько
человек*); a noun with an approximative preposition (около/до + genitive, с + accusative — *с
десяток прохожих*, where subject-verb number agreement can vary, боxed p.127); or a fixed idiomatic
phrase. A modifier+noun combination counts as a single subject unit only when modifier and head
form one indivisible concept (*главная задача* is separable, but *контрольная работа* — "test/exam"
— or the idiom *филькина грамота* are not). The broader semantic "субъект" (experiencer/doer,
distinct from grammatical подлежащее) can likewise surface via nominative, via dative in
impersonal-experiential constructions (*Мне холодно*), via genitive (*У меня грипп*), or with
optionality between dative/genitive-with-у depending on whether a feeling or an institutional
possession relation is meant (p.145 boxed rule: bodily/spatial-location states default to
prepositional/у+genitive; states-of-being like illness default to genitive-only "У меня грипп";
emotional states allow either).

### Ways of expressing the predicate: verbal and nominal сказуемое (pp. 146-176)
Verbal predicates cover simple (single conjugated verb) and compound-verbal forms; the latter often
built on a "modal/phasal verb + infinitive" skeleton, where a further rule (p.157, boxed) requires
the conjunction чтобы whenever the governing verb phrase is used in its *literal* sense (*Он дал
письмо, чтобы передать его другу*) but takes a bare infinitive when the phrase is used
idiomatically/figuratively (*Он дал обещание помочь*) — with a single lexicalized exception, *взял
слово* ("took the floor to speak"), which itself requires чтобы despite being idiomatic. Nominal
predicates with a copula (быть) in the past tense default to instrumental-case complement (*Он был
героем*); nominative is admitted only as a colloquial variant. If the predicate's applicability is
explicitly bounded in time/space, only instrumental is grammatical (*Он был героем во время войны*).
With semi-lexical copulas (стать, казаться, выглядеть) and posture/motion-as-state verbs (идти, жить,
работать), nouns appear only in instrumental, while adjectives/participles may appear in either
instrumental or nominative (p.165, boxed). This chapter's exercise 240 supplies the fixed idiom
inventory usable as a compound nominal predicate (see Vocabulary: идти ва-банк, вертеться как белка
в колесе, etc.).

### Infinitive in subject/predicate position; quantity-subject agreement (pp. 166-176)
Covers infinitive-as-subject and infinitive-as-predicate constructions, including the significant
значит/означает distinction (see Vocabulary) and predicate agreement when the subject is a
"quantity" noun phrase (много, немало, сколько, кто-нибудь/кто-то + genitive) — such subjects take
a singular (often neuter) predicate regardless of the notional plurality (*Много людей погибло*).

### Connections between homogeneous members (pp. 177-193)
The largest subsection in Раздел 2, organized entirely by connective semantics with a dense
inventory of coordinating conjunctions and their register variants (see Vocabulary: да/хоть/иль as
explicitly source-tagged colloquial/archaic-poetic doublets of и/хотя/или). Categories covered:
соединительные (additive: и...и, ни...ни, и+вместе с тем, а+главное/иногда/может быть — the
last three explicitly parenthesized in the source as further stylistic variants of "и с оттенком
уточнения"); противительные (adversative: но, не...а, а не, не...но, но не); уступительные
(concessive: хотя и); разделительные (disjunctive: или...или, либо...либо, ли...ли, если не...то);
alternation (то...то); "non-differentiation" (то ли...то ли / не то...не то); and causal-consecutive
(и+поэтому, а(и)+потому/оттого/следовательно/значит). Also covers pronoun-omission as a stylistic
device (exercise 315, drawn from Dovlatov/Gubarev/Dombrovsky excerpts, showing how removing
first-person pronouns shifts a passage's register toward a more literary/terse style) and rules for
comma placement with generalizing words before/after a homogeneous series (*В лесу мы собирали
ягоды: малину и чернику* — colon after the generalizer when it precedes the series; dash when the
appositive/generalizer follows, p.216).

### Isolated (obособленные) sentence members (pp. 194-234)
Covers isolated определения (adjectival/participial modifiers set off by commas, pp.194-203 —
including the rule that a postposed participial phrase attached to a proper name or personal
pronoun is set off when it adds non-identifying supplementary information, but not when it needs to
stay adjacent to distinguish it from the predicate, p.203 boxed example: *Иван Иванович, в пальто
и шляпе, встретил нас в прихожей*); isolated обстоятельства (adverbial/gerundive phrases, pp.
204-209, including the closed list of фразеологизм-status gerund phrases exempt from comma-setting,
p.210 — see Vocabulary: засучив рукава, сломя голову, затаив дыхание, положа руку на сердце);
isolated приложения (appositive nouns, pp. 210-216, distinguishing single-word appositions
hyphenated directly to a common noun [*сосед-студент*] from ones set off by commas when postposed
after the noun's own modifiers [*мой сосед по общежитию, студент, слушал музыку*], plus the rule
that a sentence-final appositive is marked with a dash rather than a comma: *В аудиторию вошёл Иван
Иванович — наш преподаватель*); and isolated уточнения (clarifying/narrowing modifiers, pp.
217-219, defined as modifiers that restate and sharpen a preceding adjective's meaning as a near-
synonym, never joined by и: *Дом был небольшой, в два этажа*).

### One-part sentences (Односоставные предложения, pp. 220-234)
A clean six-way taxonomy, each type with the source's own defining example (see Vocabulary row for
the full six-way list): **определённо-личные** (definite-personal — action of speaker/addressee
implied by verb form alone, no separate subject needed: *Замечу кстати...*); **неопределённо-
личные** (indefinite-personal — actor deliberately unspecified: *В деканате вам выдадут
студенческий билет*); **обобщённо-личные** (generalized-personal — the action is predicated of
anyone in general, typically proverbial: *Из песни слова не выкинешь*); **безличные** (impersonal —
no subject is possible at all, covering weather/environment and physical/mental states: see the
state-predicative and modal-predicative vocabulary rows above, plus безличные verbs like
"светает/смеркается" and personal verbs used impersonally like "в трубе воет," p.225); infinitival
(main member is a bare infinitive independent of any other word: *Лицом к лицу лица не увидать* —
Yesenin) with a further sub-pattern using бы for a wish/desirability reading (*Ему бы промолчать*);
and номинативные (main member is a bare nominative noun asserting the existence/presence of a
thing: *Москва. Дом Шуйского. Ночь. Сад. Фонтан* — Pushkin), which can further include
substantivized parts of speech, genitive-quantity constructions expressing abundance (*Крику! Шуму!*)
or quantity-plus-noun combos (*Десять часов*).

### Word order / actual articulation (Порядок слов — Актуальное членение предложения, pp. 235-250)
Unlike English, Russian word order is formally free but functionally constrained by **actual
articulation**: every sentence divides into тема (theme — information already known to the
listener) and рема (rheme — new information, normally answering an implicit question). The boxed
example: *Брат купил книгу* answers "What did the brother do?" (рема = купил книгу); *Книгу купил
брат* answers "Who bought the book?" (рема = брат); *Брат книгу купил* answers "Did the brother buy
the book or borrow it?" (рема = купил). Sentences containing both parts are
"коммуникативно-расчленённые"; sentences that are pure rheme (introducing wholly new information,
typically text/paragraph-opening) are "коммуникативно-нерасчленённые" (*Приезжает брат; Наступила
осень; Началась гроза*). Written Russian observes stricter word-order rules than speech, since
written registers cannot lean on intonation to mark the rheme. The chapter's drill material (pp.
236-244) draws on expressive/marked word order from a wide span of authors (Pushkin, Grin,
Dumbadze, Ilf & Petrov, Nabokov, Astafiev, Zalygin, Iskander, Bulgakov) illustrating how fronting or
inverting elements shifts emphasis.
