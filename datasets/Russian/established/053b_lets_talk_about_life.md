## Grammar points

### The vuz acronym as spoken register norm
p.36 explicitly notes that вуз (высшее учебное заведение) is what people "обычно говорят" in place
of the full multi-word official term — the same written-form-vs-spoken-form register pattern
already seen with комсомол/ВЛКСМ in Урок 1 (file 053). Worth tracking as a recurring pattern:
Russian bureaucratic/institutional multi-word terms regularly compress to a short acronym or
clipped form in actual speech.

### An explicit coll. tag inside a core vocabulary list
p.36's vocabulary list itself tags занимать/занять деньги as "(coll. ...)" relative to
брать/взять деньги — i.e. the source marks colloquial register directly inline in its vocabulary
presentation, not only in a separate slang section. Worth noting for the mechanics-analysis phase:
this textbook's authors distinguish register at the individual lexical-item level even within
"neutral" vocabulary lists.

### Student jargon as a semantically coherent micro-register
The "Из студенческого жаргона" box (p.43) is presented as a distinct, bounded register domain
(student slang around exam failure/backlog), and its five items form a tight semantic cluster
built on extended metaphor: хвост ("tail" = unfinished obligation trailing behind you) generates
сдавать хвосты; засыпаться/засыпать (lit. "to get buried/fall asleep," here "to fail") pairs
reflexive (student fails) vs. transitive (professor fails the student) forms of the same verb —
a single root supporting both agent and patient perspectives on the same event. This kind of
verb-voice-based register vocabulary (rather than pure noun-slang) is a useful mechanics data point.
