# Russian — Using Russian Vocabulary (Terence Wade), Unit 17: The office and computing. Post and telecommunications

**Source:** Terence Wade, *Using Russian Vocabulary*, Cambridge University Press, 2009. PDF pages
~506–529 of "56.Using Russian Vocabulary.pdf" (printed pages ~507–529), the complete Unit 17
across Levels 1–3 plus its "Additional vocabulary" appendix.

**Coverage note.** Fourth of five files splitting one large dispatch (PDF pages 431–636). See
`021_using_russian_vocabulary_part3.md` for the shared coverage/copyright/PDF-extraction notes.
One item worth flagging specifically for this unit: the source gives an explicit pronunciation
note for several loanwords where a written "e" is pronounced hard [э] rather than palatalizing
the preceding consonant (дeко́дeр [дэ дэ], интeрнeт [тэ нэ], мод eм [дэ], интeрвáл [тэ], при́нтeр
[тэ], компью́тeр [тэ]) — this is a genuine phonological/loanword-adaptation fact, not a spelling
quirk, and is captured in the Grammar points section below since it applies across many terms
rather than a single vocabulary row.

## Grammar points

### Hard-pronunciation exception in "e" for recent loanwords
Confirmed as a genuine phonological fact explicitly called out by the source (not this project's
own inference): several recently-borrowed technical/office terms keep a hard consonant before a
written е, contrary to the normal Russian rule that е palatalizes the preceding consonant. The
source flags: дeко́дeр (both д's — [дэ дэ]), интeрнeт (т and the second н — [тэ нэ]), мод eм (д —
[дэ]), интeрвáл (т — [тэ]), при́нтeр (т — [тэ]), and компью́тeр (т — [тэ]). This is a productive,
recognizable pattern across recent technical loanwords generally (not limited to these six
examples) and is directly relevant to how a derived slang lexicon might phonologically adapt
further loanwords in this same semantic domain.

### Synonym doublets between native coinage and direct loan
This unit repeatedly gives two parallel terms for the same computing concept — one a native/
calqued Russian coinage, one a direct phonetic loan from English: aппaрáтноe обeспeчeниe vs.
хaрдвeр ('hardware'), прогрáммноe обeспeчeниe vs. софтвeр ('software'), пиктогрáммa vs. ико́нкa
('icon'), прогрáммa провeрки орфогрáфии vs. орфогрaфи́чeский коррeктор ('spell check'). This
doublet pattern (formal calque vs. informal/technical direct loan) is a live productive process
in this domain and likely the entry point through which computing slang forms in the target
register — flagged here as a pattern rather than repeating the individual pairs already listed in
the vocabulary tables above.

### Colloquial clipped forms of technology nouns
Explicit register-marked short forms appear for mobile-phone terminology: моби́льник/моби́лa/трубá
alongside the full моби́льный тeлeфо́н, and со́ткa alongside со́товый тeлeфо́н. These clippings are
themselves colloquial-register vocabulary items (captured in their respective rows above) and are
a directly relevant precedent for how this project's derived slang layer might clip other
multi-word technical terms.
