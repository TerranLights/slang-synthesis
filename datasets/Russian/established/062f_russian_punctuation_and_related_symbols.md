## Grammar points

### Chapter 8: The Comma of Separation (Запятая разделительная)

Unlike the paired comma of enclosure (Ch. 7), the comma of separation is single, not paired, and
frequently co-occurs with coordinating conjunctions (и, или, но).

**§8.1 Parallel items not joined by a coordinating conjunction.** A comma separates parallel sentence
elements (adverbs, predicates, subjects, or objects) when no coordinating conjunction joins them:
Справа, слева, впереди забелели берёзовые стволы "To the right, left, and straight ahead appeared
white birch trunks" (p. 93).

**§8.2 Punctuation in strings of adjectival modifiers** — the most complex subsection in the chapter,
with eight distinguishable cases (a–h):

- (a) Two adjectives of **different semantic type** — a qualitative adjective (external/gradable
  attribute, e.g. высокий "tall") plus a relational adjective (intrinsic quality — material, origin,
  e.g. кирпичный "brick") — are NOT parallel and cannot be joined by и or divided by a comma:
  высокие кирпичные дома "tall brick houses" (no comma, no и possible) (p. 94). Two adjectives of the
  SAME type (two qualitative, or two relational) in a series ARE divided: новые, красивые, высокие
  дома "new, beautiful, tall houses."
- (b) Modifiers denoting **different varieties of the same category** (e.g. different colors, sizes)
  ARE parallel and comma-divided (or joined by и): Красные, розовые, жёлтые цветы "Red, pink, and
  yellow flowers" (p. 95).
- (c) A **demonstrative pronoun, possessive pronoun, or possessive adjective** is never comma- or
  conjunction-divided from a following relational/qualitative adjective: тот же самый красный шарф
  "that same red scarf," Олегова кожаная куртка "Oleg's leather coat." When a possessive co-occurs
  with both a qualitative and relational adjective, normal ordering is possessive → qualitative →
  relational: наш холодный северный климат "our cold northern climate" (pp. 95–96). However, a
  SERIES of possessives (e.g. listing different people's things) IS comma-divided: Его, ваш и мой
  дома находятся рядом "His, your, and my houses are next to each other" (p. 96).
- (d) Modifiers from different semantic categories can still be treated as parallel if together they
  characterize the noun **from a single unified point of view** (e.g. all contributing to "bad
  weather" or "dangerous fangs"): Небо такое дождливое, серое, грустное "The sky is so rainy, gray,
  and sad" (p. 97).
- (e) Adjectives **postposed after their noun for emphasis** are comma-divided regardless of their
  semantic type, representing a written stand-in for a special intonational pause: Они выстроили дом
  кирпичный, большой "They built a brick house, a big one" (vs. neutral-order Они выстроили большой
  кирпичный дом with no comma) (p. 97).
- **Exception to (e)**: official taxonomic names of plants/animals with postposed adjective strings
  take NO comma even though postposed: капуста цветная ранняя "early cauliflower," пшеница озимая
  морозостойкая "hardy winter wheat" (p. 97).
- (f) Modifiers in **predicative position** (after a linking verb/dash, describing the subject) are
  always comma-divided regardless of semantic parallelism: Машина наша, новая "The car is ours and
  it's new" — contrast the preposed, non-parallel, no-comma наша новая машина "our new car" (pp.
  97–98).
- (g) A comma between two adjectives **can change meaning**: Дай другой красный карандаш ("другой"
  relational + "красный" qualitative → "Give me another red pencil," no comma) vs. Дай другой,
  красный карандаш (both now treated as qualitative/contrastive → "Give me another pencil, a red one
  this time," comma required, more emphatic pause) (p. 98).
- (h) A final type of parallel modifier: one adjective **clarifies/intensifies** a preceding one,
  optionally reinforceable with а именно "namely" or то есть "that is": Шёл унылый, моросящий дождик
  "Dreary, drizzling rain was falling" (p. 98).

**§8.3 Comma with repeated words.** Lexical repetition (еду, еду; нет, нет!) is comma-set, used
stylistically to emphasize duration, quantity, or emotion — more common in Russian than English. A
dash can replace the comma if the repetition is followed by an expressive pause: Уходи — и
побыстрее уходи (p. 99). Note: Russian also builds compound words from repeated elements (еле-еле
"barely," чуть-чуть "a tiny bit") where one element loses primary stress — treated separately in the
book's Chapter 15.

**§8.4 Punctuation with parallel elements joined by a simple coordinating conjunction:**

- (a) A single и, да (= и), или/иль, либо (= или) joining exactly two parallel items takes NO comma:
  Дни стояли тёплые и ласковые "The days were warm and gentle" (p. 99).
- (b) Unlike English, a comma is **never** used before the conjunction joining the LAST two items of
  a longer series (no "Oxford comma" equivalent): красные, синие и жёлтые шары, never *красные,
  синие, и жёлтые (p. 100).
- **Exception — abbreviations**: и др., и т.д., и т.п. are never comma-set even though English "etc."
  requires a comma (p. 100).
- (c) If items in a series are themselves organized in **conjoined pairs**, a comma separates each
  pair from the next: Слева и справа, спереди и сзади белели стволы берёз (pp. 100–101).
- (d) A comma IS required before или when it means то есть/иными словами ("that is," introducing an
  appositive rather than a genuine alternative) — the или-phrase is enclosed by a paired comma:
  Только тушканчик, или земляной заяц, ещё бодрствует "Only the gerbil, or ground rabbit, was still
  active." If или introduces a genuinely different alternative rather than a restatement, no comma:
  В таких лесах преобладает сосна или пихта (pp. 101–102).

**§8.5 Punctuation in parallel sentences combined into a compound sentence:**

- (a) When the two halves of a compound sentence share a **common member (общий член)** — most often
  a single adverbial modifier serving both clauses — no comma precedes и: По улицам мчались
  автомобили и неслись трамваи "Cars rushed and tramcars sped along the streets" (common member: по
  улицам). If each clause has its own separate modifier, the comma is required: По улицам мчались
  автомобили, и по тротуарам быстро шли пешеходы (p. 104). This matches English usage with "and."

**§8.6 The comma plus simple adversative conjunction.** Adversative conjunctions (а, но "but," etc.)
are ALWAYS comma-preceded in Russian, regardless of a common member or any other factor — unlike
English "but," where the comma is only a stylistic option: Путники долго блуждали в темноте, но всё
же им удалось добраться до реки вовремя (p. 106).

**§8.7 Commas that introduce repetitions of a conjunction** — the most intricate subsection:

- (a) With the **repeating conjunction и...и** ("both...and," also да...да colloquially):
  - (i) A **single pair** of simple (unmodified) parallel words needs NO comma: И плещется и мечется
    буйная осенняя тайга. If either item is modified/extended in any way, the comma becomes
    obligatory regardless of other factors: Срубленные деревья придавили собой и траву, и мелкий
    кустарник (comma required because кустарник is modified by мелкий) (p. 106). Even unmodified
    simple words CAN take a comma as an optional stylistic device to emphasize separateness of the
    items (p. 107).
  - **Fixed idioms with и...и are never comma-divided** — see Vocabulary above (и день и ночь,
    и стар и млад, etc.) — because the paired elements form one semantic whole, not a genuine list
    (p. 107).
  - (ii) With **three or more** parallel items joined by repeating и (or да), a comma is always
    required with each repetition, even for unmodified simple words, and even if the conjunction only
    repeats twice within a longer list: Я люблю эти тёмные ночи, эти звёзды, и клёны, и пруд
    (pp. 107–108).
  - **Non-repeating и serving separate functions in the same sentence takes no comma** — distinguish
    a genuinely repeating conjunction from two unrelated uses of и: Мы мучились от жары и жажды летом
    и замерзали зимой (first и joins жары/жажды, second и joins the two verbs — no comma with either)
    (p. 108).
- (b) With **paired/repeating disjunctive conjunctions** — либо...либо, не то...не то, ни...ни,
  то...то — a comma is **always** obligatory regardless of item count (unlike и...и's more permissive
  rule): Он не любил ни отменять своих намерений, ни откладывать их... (p. 109). English tends to use
  the repeating conjunction INSTEAD OF a comma, not together with one — a real point of divergence.
- **Fixed idioms with ни...ни are never comma-divided** — see Vocabulary above (ни туда ни сюда, ни
  то ни сё, etc.) (p. 109).

### Chapter 9: The Semicolon (Точка с запятой)

The semicolon is markedly less common in Russian than in English, and per the book's own remark, is
rare enough that a literary citation is given for every illustrative example.

- **§9.1 Extended items in a series.** When items in a series are themselves internally complex or
  contain their own internal punctuation (распространённые однородные члены), a semicolon rather
  than comma separates them, avoiding ambiguity between item-internal and item-separating commas — a
  Turgenev passage illustrates this with four internally-punctuated parallel clauses joined by
  semicolons (p. 110).
- **§9.2 Semicolon + coordinating conjunction in clauses with internal punctuation.** When a compound
  sentence with а/но/и is itself embedded in a larger compound sentence whose clauses already contain
  their own commas, a semicolon (not comma) separates the larger sentence's own parts, for clarity
  (p. 114).
- **§9.3 Semicolon setting off extended dependent clauses in a series.** A series of dependent clauses
  that each already contain internal punctuation is set off from each other (and from the main
  clause) by semicolons rather than the usual comma — Chekhov's Был тот предночный час, когда
  стираются очертания, линии, краски, расстояния; когда ещё дневной свет путается... (p. 114).

### Chapter 10: The Colon (Двоеточие)

Only a few contexts require the colon in Russian, generally paralleling English usage.

- **§10.1 Colon introducing a series.** А generalizing word (следующие, следующее "the following")
  commonly precedes the colon (Нас было трое: моя сестра, её подруга и я), but the colon can
  introduce a series even without one, especially in business style (pp. 117–118).
- **§10.2 Colon explaining the reason for an action** — can substitute for потому что "because":
  Сержусь на самого себя: сам кругом виноват "I'm mad at myself: it's all my fault." **In Russian,
  the word after the colon is never capitalized**, unlike English, which may optionally capitalize it
  if the following phrase can stand as a complete sentence (p. 118).
- **§10.3 Colon introducing quoted material after reporting words** — a colon is used regardless of
  whether an introductory word like следующее is present: Он сказал: «С этим я согласиться не могу».
  English requires a colon here only when an introductory word ("the following") is present;
  otherwise a comma is used. If the quoted material is a complete sentence, it begins with a capital
  in both languages. **Note: in Russian the period is placed AFTER the closing quotation mark**,
  whereas English places it before (pp. 118–119; full treatment deferred to Ch. 14).
- **§10.4 Colon introducing words describing a vivid sensory impression** — no quotation marks, no
  capital letter after the colon, used more freely in Russian than English (which requires some
  introductory word like "this" for the same construction): И тут они увидели: изчезла белая фигура
  "And there they saw it: the white figure had vanished." A dash can substitute for this same
  function to convey a sudden/unexpected impression, especially in children's literature/folk-tale
  style: Видит — стоит избушка на курьих ножках (p. 119). Further dash usage is treated in the next
  chapter (Ch. 11).
