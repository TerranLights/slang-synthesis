## Grammar points

**н-epenthesis after prepositions.** 3rd-person personal pronouns (он, она, оно, они) take an
inserted н- after most prepositions (у него, с ней, для них) — but personal pronouns keep их/её/его/
им *without* н- after the prepositions благодаря, навстречу, вопреки, согласно (Благодаря им мы
закончили работу вовремя). Possessive его/её/их never take н- and never decline.

**этот/тот/это, functional split.** это as a grammatical subject does not change for gender/number/
case and always opens the sentence when used this way; as an object it can stand alone (наливать что
в это). тот is used specifically to refer back to something already mentioned or now absent from the
speech situation, and forms fixed subordinators (ввиду того что, по мере того как, с тем чтобы).
этот/тот/это combine with самый/именно/вот for emphasis (то самое место). A subtle register note:
это + же as demonstrative pronoun-plus-particle is written as two words, while наречие тоже
("also") is one word — a common orthographic confusion the source explicitly flags.

**Возвратное местоимение себя (reflexive себя).** Has no nominative form, does not inflect for
person/gender/number (я буду у себя, ты будешь у себя, он будет у себя — identical across
persons), and always redirects the action back onto the grammatical subject that performs it.

**свой vs. personal possessives, and vs. собственный.** свой marks possession relative to the
grammatical subject of the clause and is close to obligatory whenever the possessor is also the
subject (Я хочу услышать о твоей работе but Я хочу рассказать о своей работе). свой does **not**
combine with собственный ("own") when собственный means "belongs to a specific individual, not
just anyone" — a subtle blocking rule the source states explicitly (own-emphasis is instead built
as свой собственный only in the "even more explicitly one's very own" reading, e.g. свои
собственные родители). In a passive construction (no expressed agent), свой is not used; when the
agent is 1st/2nd person (я, ты, мы, вы), свой can generally replace the corresponding possessive.
свой in subject position with no following noun can carry a generalized "family/one's own people"
sense (свой человек, почти родственник).

**каждый vs. любой vs. всякий.** каждый = "one of a series, taken in turn" (снимай с полки каждую
книгу — implies going through them one by one); любой = "whichever you like, freely chosen" (можешь
взять любую книгу); всякий = synonym of разный/разнообразный when it means "all sorts/kinds" (взял
всякие словари — various kinds), but overlaps with каждый/любой in other contexts. каждый cannot be
the subject of a perfective-past-tense verb, cannot combine with a plural noun (except pluralia
tantum or a "noun+numeral" group), and cannot combine with a noun that itself resists numeral
combination (e.g. небо, время). любой does not combine with разу (any occurrence of "time"), does
not appear where no real freedom of choice exists, and does not normally appear in the accusative
when marking time. всякий does not combine with word "раз."

**весь vs. целый vs. полный (pronoun-level, cf. adjective-level contrast in `077b`).** весь =
"from beginning to end / not one part missing," and can carry an emotional intensifier sense
(огромный/чрезмерный) distinct from a neutral quantifier; целый marks that nothing has been removed
from an original whole (эмоционально нейтральнее); полный = "as full/complete as something can be."
весь does not combine with секунда/минута/час/вечность; целый does not combine with понедельник,
вторник, январь, время (i.e., not with calendar-unit nouns naming a specific day/month, nor with the
abstract noun время itself).

**отрицательные местоимения (negative pronouns), не/ни split.** никто/ничто/никакой/ничей take the
particle **не** (Никто не знает); некого/нечего (a genuinely distinct paradigm, no nominative form)
combine with нет/нельзя/невозможно and take **ни** in the corresponding positive-existential frame.
нечего + infinitive in colloquial registers means "there's no need to" (Нечего об этом
беспокоиться!). In predicate position, negative pronouns can appear without не (Это ничья собака).
Prepositions insert between the negative particle and the pronoun stem (ни к кому, не с кем, ни на
чём) — never after.

**неопределённые местоимения (indefinite pronouns) нécто/нéкто/нéкий/нéкоторый.** нéкто appears only
in the nominative; нечто appears in nominative and accusative only. несколько answers "how many?"
(implicitly, up to about five) while некоторый means "a distinct few, out of some larger set" — a
genuinely fine distinction the source draws out over several exercises, with subject-verb agreement
diverging depending on which is chosen (несколько+noun triggers a collective/neuter-singular
predicate reading in some constructions; некоторый+noun agrees normally).

## Idioms and proverbs (a major closed-set find, by pronoun)

**себя-idioms:** себе на уме (sly, keeps his own counsel), быть не по себе (feel uneasy/off), взять
себя в руки (pull oneself together), само собой (разумеется) (goes without saying), выйти из себя
(lose one's temper), быть самим собой (be oneself), брать на себя смелость (take the liberty of),
сам по себе (in itself, of its own accord).

**свой-idioms:** называть вещи своими именами (call a spade a spade), не в своей тарелке (feel out
of place — the classic false-cognate idiom, тарелка "plate" from a mistranslation of French
"assiette" meaning both "plate" and "state/mood"), на свой страх и риск (at one's own risk), жить
своим умом (live independently, by one's own wits), мастер своего дела (master of one's craft),
умереть своей смертью (die of natural causes), своя рубашка ближе к телу (charity begins at home),
своя ноша не тянет (one's own burden doesn't weigh you down), совет да любовь (traditional
newlywed-blessing phrase, "advice and love").

**тот/это-idioms:** ни то ни сё (so-so, neither one thing nor the other), во что бы то ни стало
(whatever it takes), ни с того ни с сего (out of the blue), если на то пошло (if it comes to that),
встать не с той ноги (get up on the wrong side of bed).

**отрицательные местоимения-idioms:** делать нечего (nothing to be done about it), от нечего делать
(out of boredom), ни к чему (pointless), ничего подобного! (nothing of the sort! — a strong denial),
как ни в чём не бывало (as if nothing had happened), ни за что (not for anything), нечего греха
таить (no use hiding the truth).

**каждый/любой/всякий proverbs (footnoted with diminutive/archaic glosses in the source):**
каждый человек кузнец своего счастья ("every man is the smith of his own fortune"); на каждый роток
не накинешь платок ("you can't cover every mouth" — you can't stop people talking; роток is a
diminutive of рот); всяк сверчок знай свой шесток ("each cricket, know your own hearth-nook" — know
your place; archaic всяк for всякий, шесток a diminutive of шест); каждый кулик своё болото хвалит
("every sandpiper praises its own bog" — everyone favors their own; кулик = sandpiper, a regional/
rural-register bird name).

**Other:** быть не все дома (not all there mentally — colloquial), покрутить пальцем у виска (tap
one's temple — gesture implying someone is crazy), дом полная чаша (a house of plenty), хлопот полон
рот (swamped with chores/things to do), в общем и целом (on the whole), любыми путями (by any
means). Archaic deferential particle **-с** (footnoted in the source as an abbreviation of сударь/
сударыня): appended to any word in pre-revolutionary speech to signal deference or subservience,
sometimes ironic — corroborates the same register marker independently found in `035` (the Chekhov
reader).
