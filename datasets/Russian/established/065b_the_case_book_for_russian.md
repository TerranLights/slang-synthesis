## Grammar points — Accusative case (pp. 52–80)

The accusative network has three branches: **A DESTINATION** (the case's core, largest sense — an
endpoint of directed motion/activity, spatial or metaphorical), **A DIMENSION** (an extended,
bounded measure — distance, duration, size, comparison — that the action moves *across* or *along*
rather than arriving at), and **AN ENDPOINT** (a special, temporally-focused sub-case of
destination reduced to a point in time rather than a place). The book's own visual metaphor: A
Destination is the wide end of a telescope, An Endpoint the narrow end, and A Dimension is the
tube in between.

### A Destination — ten sub-senses, one preposition-per-meaning table

Ten numbered sub-senses are given, each licensed by a different preposition or (for the direct
object and subjectless-verb cases) no preposition at all:

1. **в 'in, into'** — the basic destination-of-motion preposition (entering an enclosed/bounded
   space): *пошла в другое место* "went to another place." Extends metaphorically to entering a
   state (*влюбиться в* "fall in love with," *превратиться в* "turn into") and to being promoted/
   renamed into a role or category (*редактор переименовал издательство в "Невидимую книгу"* "the
   editor renamed the publishing house 'The Invisible Book'"). A specific frozen exception: idioms
   like *в гости* "[go] visiting" and *в кошки-мышки* "[play] cat-and-mouse" preserve an archaic
   accusative form that does **not** follow the normal animate-accusative-equals-genitive rule
   (i.e. they look like old accusatives frozen before that syncretism took hold) — flagged
   explicitly by the book as a historical throwback.
2. **на 'to, onto'** — treats the destination as a surface rather than an enclosed space (parallel
   to на's locative sense "on/at" a surface). Used for climbing/crawling onto something, and
   metaphorically for policy/attention "directed at/toward" a goal (*направленная на*
   "directed at/aimed at").
3. **за 'beyond, behind'** — the destination lies behind/beyond a reference item, common in
   marriage idioms (*выйти замуж за* "marry [a man]," lit. "go out behind a husband") and in
   *взяться за руки* "join hands," *посадить за руль* "put behind the wheel."
4. **о/об 'against'** — contact/collision made against a surface, often forceful:
   *ударить головой о что-то* "hit one's head against something."
5. **до 'up to'** — activity regressing from full space to a point where it stops; metaphorically
   extends to "as far as," e.g. writing being characterized by frequency of activity "up to X
   [meetings/pages] per day."
6. **под 'under'** — destination "under" something, both literal (*попасть под колёса* "fall/get
   run over under the wheels") and metaphorical (*взять под наблюдение* "put under observation,"
   *взять под гарантии* "place under guarantee/collateral").
7. **destinations in time** — a preview of the Endpoint branch: в and на both used for time-point
   destinations (*в пятницу* "on Friday," *на прошлой неделе* "last week"); *через* used for "in
   X time" (an event happening after an interval elapses).
8. **the direct object** — the accusative's most basic, prepositionless use: marks the patient of
   a transitive verb, framed by the book as the same "destination" logic — the action moves from
   agent to patient like a force moving to an endpoint. Works with the vast majority of verbs
   except those idiosyncratically governing instrumental/dative/genitive instead.
9. **objects without subjects** — a "raw force" impersonal accusative exactly parallel to the
   instrumental's An Agent With No Subject: verbs like *тошнить* "feel nauseated," *рвать*
   "throw up," *трясти* "shake" appear with an accusative experiencer and no grammatical subject
   at all (*пассажиров трясёт* "the passengers are shaken/jolted" — no subject, "shaking" happens
   to them as an ambient force). The book explicitly draws the parallel to the instrumental's raw-
   force pattern (065's A Means 13) as the same "force acting without an agent" logic mirrored
   across two different cases.
10. **destinations of purpose** — за 'for, in favor of/fighting for' (*бороться за* "fight for"),
    на 'for [a stated purpose]' (budget allocations "for" education, healthcare), and a common
    "answer to a question" construction (в ответ на 'in answer to').

### A Dimension — four sub-senses: distance/duration, size/capacity, comparison, activity domains

1. **Distance and duration** — the accusative marks a bounded stretch traversed, whether spatial
   (*жизнь не поле перейти* "life isn't [like] walking across a field" — a proverb) or temporal
   (в + accusative for "spend [an amount of] time": *провести время* "spend time"; на + accusative
   for a planned/intended duration: *взять отпуск на месяц* "take a month's vacation"). Time-unit
   frequency phrases use в: *раз в неделю* "once a week," *три раза в месяц* "three times a
   month." Duration constructions distinguish accusative ("for X time," a bounded/completed
   stretch) from genitive/other cases used for ongoing or open-ended time spans.
2. **Size and capacity** — measuring the extent of an object against a standard scale: weight
   (*весом в 850 килограммов* "weighing 850 kilograms"), age (*трёхлетний* uses a different
   construction, but comparative age-difference phrasing takes accusative: *старше на десять лет*
   pattern's counterpart), monetary value, floor count (*в два этажа* "two-story[a two-floor
   building]"). в is also used to state a rate/exchange value (*курс... в пять тысяч рублей* "the
   rate... at five thousand rubles").
3. **Comparison** — a marginal domain use, most often expressed via prepositionless accusative in
   an approximation idiom: *с + accusative* means "about, approximately" via a rhyming diminutive
   pattern (мальчик с пальчик "a boy about the size of your finger," lit. "Tom Thumb") — extended
   productively to any size comparison: *размером с курицу* "about the size of a chicken." Also
   на + похож construction for "looks like" comparisons (looking like a gorilla, a hairstyle
   "styled after").
4. **Activities in various domains** — accusative names the activity/game/topic that fills an
   extended domain of time or attention: играть в + accusative for naming a sport/game played
   (*играть в шахматы, регби, футбол* "play chess/rugby/soccer" — this construction is explicitly
   noted as a fixed, non-generalizable list per sport/game, not a live semantic rule), a
   thinking/speaking/writing topic (о + prepositional is the default "about" case in Russian, but
   this book notes a **verить/поверить в + accusative** "believe in [a concept/entity]" vs
   **верить + dative** "believe [a person/their words]" contrast — a genuine near-synonym case-
   government discrimination worth flagging, since the two constructions have distinct meanings
   depending on whether the object of belief is a person (dative) or an abstract claim/entity
   (в + accusative)). Also сквозь 'through' for perception passing through an obstacle (through
   tears, through a veil of sleep) and через 'via, by means of' for information relayed through an
   intermediary channel or person.

### An Endpoint — time reduced to a point

Parallel structure to A Destination but restricted to the time domain: в and на both mark a
point-in-time endpoint (в + [day-of-week accusative], на + [прошлой/следующей + неделе — locative
actually, but "на этой неделе"-type expressions pattern differently]). The chapter tabulates a
set of **time-expression postpositions/prepositions built on АССUSATIVE: AN ENDPOINT**:

| Construction | Meaning | Example gloss |
|---|---|---|
| а + [time] вперёд | X time from now (forward) | *через неделю вперёд* "a week from now" |
| через + [time] | in/after X time | *через неделю* "in a week" |
| [time] назад | X time ago (postposition, follows the noun) | *месяц назад* "a month ago" |
| [time] тому назад | X time ago (variant, same meaning) | *неделю тому назад* "a week ago" |
| [time] спустя | X time later (postposition) | *четыре года спустя* "four years later" |
| через каждые + [time] | every X time (periodic recurrence) | *через каждую неделю* "every week" |

The Epilogue draws an explicit typological point: the Russian *accusative* is conceptually about
directed force/motion reaching a target (spatial, temporal, or activity-based), setting up a
contrast with the *dative* case (covered next), which the book previews as "continuing this theme
of forces directed at targets, albeit in a less immediate way."
