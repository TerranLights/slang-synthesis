## Grammar points

### Aspect choice as a discourse decision, not just a verb-form rule (Unit 20, p. 64)

A genuinely useful pedagogical framing not always made this explicit elsewhere: the book presents
imperfective-vs-perfective choice in the past tense as a **paired decision table** — process/duration
vs. result; repeated/habitual (always imperfective, with the explicit trigger-word list часто, редко,
всегда, иногда, каждый день, несколько раз) vs. single completed action; "did the event happen at
all" (perfective) vs. "was it as planned, checking it actually occurred" (imperfective) vs.
"simultaneous" (imperfective for both) vs. "sequential" (perfective for both). Useful as a
compact decision-tree cross-reference even though each individual rule is independently attested in
Wave 1's grammars.

### The imperative-formation stem/stress rule (Unit 22, p. 68)

A tidy 4-row table: stem ending in a vowel → +й regardless of stress (игра-ют → играй); stem ending
in one consonant with present-tense stress on the last syllable → +и (пиш-ут → пиши); stem ending in
one consonant with stress elsewhere → +ь (став-ят → ставь); stem ending in two consonants → +и
regardless of stress (молч-ат → молчи). Plural is singular + -те. Reflexive -ся verbs add -ся after a
consonant ending, -сь after a vowel ending. Exceptions: пить→пей, лить→лей, бить→бей, шить→шей,
давать→давай, вставать→вставай, узнавать→узнавай, есть→ешь(те).

### Main prefixes of direction — consolidated government table (Unit 25, p. 76)

**Likely overlaps with 028_russkie_glagoly_dvizheniya_s_pristavkami.md** (the dedicated prefixed-
motion-verb specialist source) — not independently re-verified against that file's exact content
here, flagged for cross-check rather than treated as fully redundant. This book's version: a single
table pairing each directional prefix with its meaning, governed preposition+case, and a worked
example — в-/во- (entry, в/на+acc.), вы- (exit, из/с+gen.), при- (arrival, в/на+acc.), у- (leaving,
из/с+gen.), до- (reaching, до+gen.), под- (approaching, к+dat.), от- (movement away, от+gen.), пере-
(crossing/moving between, через+acc.), про- (traveling a distance: bare acc.; going past: мимо+gen.;
going over: по+dat.; going through: через+acc.), за- (going behind: за+acc.; stopping off: в/на+acc.
or к+dat.), об-/обо- (going round: bare acc., or вокруг+gen.). Explicit opposite-pairs called out:
в- ≠ вы-, под- ≠ от-, при- ≠ у-.

### Adjectival pronouns весь/каждый/всякий/любой — usage discrimination (Unit 34, pp. 108-110)

A compact four-way semantic discrimination not always laid out this explicitly: **весь** (all, the
whole of — declines with distinct plural всё/все for neuter/other genders collapsing to one plural
form); **каждый** (each/every, normally singular-only, exceptions for no-singular nouns like каждые
сутки and with cardinal numbers like каждые два дня); **любой** ("any, you choose" — free choice);
**всякий** ("all sorts of"). In many contexts the four are interchangeable (это знает каждый = это
любой знает = это всякий знает = все это знают), but the semantic core differs. Fixed expression
flagged: на всякий случай (just in case).

### The должен/нужен dative-vs-nominative government contrast (Units 43, 49-50, pp. 128-150)

A genuinely useful contrastive point spread across three units but worth stating together: **должен**
(obliged to) takes its subject in the **nominative**, with должен/должна/должно/должны agreeing with
that nominative subject in gender/number, and past/future formed by adding быть in the corresponding
tense (Лена должна была понять; Лена должна будет встретить). **нужен** (needed) inverts the case
roles entirely — the person who needs is in the **dative**, and нужен/нужна/нужно/нужны agrees with
the **item needed**, which sits in the nominative as the grammatical subject (Ивану нужен словарь —
literally "to-Ivan is-needed a-dictionary"); past/future уses быть agreeing with the *item*, not the
person (Анне была нужна виза). The two constructions are easy to conflate for a learner and this
source's side-by-side unit sequence makes the contrast unusually explicit. The есть-possession
system (у + genitive + есть/nothing + nominative; negation нет/не было/не будет + genitive) and the
можно/нужно(надо)/нельзя modal triad (можно = permitted/possible; нельзя + imperfective infinitive =
forbidden vs. нельзя + perfective infinitive = impossible; нужно/надо = obligation; the "for a
person" dative construction мне нужно/можно/нельзя) round out the same functional-word cluster.

### Reported speech conjunction selection (Unit 57, p. 166)

Compact rule table: statements reported with **что**; questions with an interrogative word (где,
куда, когда, сколько) reported using that same word as the conjunction; questions with **no**
interrogative word reported with the particle **ли** placed directly after the word being questioned
(distinct from — and not to be confused with — the conditional «если»); imperative requests reported
with **чтобы** + past-tense verb (even when the reported request concerns the present or future).
Tense inside reported speech stays the same as in the original direct speech/question (no
"sequence of tenses" backshift, unlike English).
