## Grammar points

### Softening a request beyond the bare imperative (§1.1, pp. 199–201)

The book's opening move: cataloguing non-imperative ways to phrase a request, laid out in a framed
box (p. 200) as a graded politeness scale — Не сделаешь?/Не сделаете? (a negated-future question,
mildly polite) through Ты/Вы не мог(ли) бы + inf? and Тебе/Вам не трудно (будет) + inf? (both glossed
explicitly, p. 200, as "очень вежливую просьбу," very polite requests). A separate, distinct
construction — У тебя/вас нет + genitive? ("you don't have a spare X, do you?") — is presented (p.
201) specifically for requesting the addressee *give* the speaker something, functionally different
from requesting an *action*.

### Accepting a request (§1.2, pp. 201–203)

The compliance side is organized into two registers: a baseline agreement cluster
(Пожалуйста/Сделаю/Хорошо/С удовольствием/Сию минуту/Минуточку/Отчего же, сделаю — p. 202, with Сию
минуту/Минуточку specifically flagged as marking speed of compliance), and an emphatic-willingness
cluster (Что за вопрос?!/О чём разговор?!/О чём речь?!/О чём ты говоришь?! — p. 203) used to stress
that granting the request required no real deliberation at all.

### Declining a request (§1.3, pp. 203–205)

Refusal forms (К сожалению, .../Жаль, но .../Извини(те), но .../При всём желании не смогу/С
удовольствием сделал бы, но .../Рад бы (сделать), но ...) are structurally distinct from a flat "no":
the book notes explicitly (p. 204) that these openers require either не могу/не смогу or a stated
reason to complete them — a bare refusal marker alone is not a complete, well-formed response in this
system. A separate refusal type, Зачем?/А зачем? (p. 205), targets the *necessity* of the request
rather than the speaker's capacity to fulfill it — "why would that be needed?" rather than "I can't."

### Asking for and granting permission (§1.4–§1.5, pp. 206–209)

A six-row paradigm table (p. 207) systematically lays out permission-request syntax across
increasing informality: можно (кому) + inf? → можно кто + future? → bare кто + future? on one branch,
and разрешите (кому) + inf? → вы не разрешите (кому) + inf? → вы/ты не против, если кто + future? on
a parallel, differently-rooted branch (built on разрешить/против rather than можно). Responses are
then sorted into three tiers (p. 209): unconditional grant (Пожалуйста/Конечно/Разумеется/Что за
вопрос?!/О чём разговор?!), deferred/hands-off grant (Как хочешь — permission given but the
decision explicitly handed back to the addressee), and refusal (А зачем? — the same lexeme already
seen in §1.3, here reused in a distinct grammatical slot to specifically decline granting permission
rather than declining to perform an action). This is another case (like `054i`'s note on В том-то и
дело) of a single Russian form carrying genuinely different pragmatic functions depending on which
slot in a larger dialogue-move system it occupies.

### Suggesting an action believed necessary or permissible (§1.6, pp. 210–212)

The final subsection introduces a distinct move from straightforward request: a "wish-request"
(просьба-пожелание) used when the speaker believes some action is called for in the current
situation, without framing it as a personal favor. Two templates are given (p. 210): Нельзя ли + inf?
and the parallel Не + inf + ли + dative? (e.g. Не отдохнуть ли нам сейчас немножко?). A closing
comparison table (p. 211) is worth noting for its systematic four-way grid distinguishing this
construction's semantic space from neighboring ones: Можно + inf? asks about *permission*; Нельзя ли
+ inf? phrases a *request*; a bare Надо + inf? question asks whether something *needs* doing; and Не
+ inf + ли? phrases a *suggestion/recommendation*. The book frames all four as syntactically similar
but pragmatically distinct moves, useful for disambiguating superficially similar Russian question
forms in later mechanics-analysis work.
