## Grammar points

### §15.1 The Apostrophe (Апостроф)

Rarely used in Russian; also called запятая наоборот, "backwards comma."

- **§15.1.1** French surnames beginning with **d'** are rendered with an apostrophe, with the letter
  immediately following it capitalized while д itself is lowercase: Жанна д'Арк, "Joan of Arc" (p.
  168).
- **§15.1.2** Irish surnames beginning with **O'** are likewise rendered with an apostrophe, with BOTH
  the О and the following letter capitalized: О'Брайен, "O'Brian" (p. 168).
- **Historical note**: in older typewritten Russian lacking a hard-sign (ъ) key, the apostrophe (or,
  failing that, the кавычки-лапочки double-quote symbol) could substitute for the hard sign:
  объявление = об'явление (об"явление), "advertisement." No meaning distinction exists between the
  variants; modern keyboards universally have the hard sign, so this substitution is now rare (p.
  168).

### §15.2 The Hyphen (Дефис or Чёрточка)

Not a mark of punctuation at all (unlike the dash, тире, which it resembles in shortened form), but
an extensively-used spelling device. Russian has three strategies for spelling a multi-word/
multi-root unit: separately with a space (раздельно), fully fused as one word (слитно, sometimes
truncating a root to form a blend), or hyphenated (сложное слово с дефисом). Twenty lettered
subsections (a–t) enumerate the closed categories requiring a hyphen:

- **(a) Noun + appositive compounds**, in six semantic subcategories — proper+common noun, antonym
  pairs, synonym pairs, profession/title/rank, item+specific-type, item+emotionally-nuanced
  description (full lists in Vocabulary above). Neither constituent is abbreviated and there's no
  connecting vowel; both nouns take full stress and both receive case endings in a sentence: Нас
  встретили хлебом-солью "They met us with traditional hospitality" (pp. 169–170).
- **(b) Compound units of measure**: километр-час "kilometer per hour." If the first noun isn't
  normally a unit of measure, a connecting vowel (usually -о-) is still added along with the hyphen:
  человеко-день "man-day" (p. 170).
- **(c) Foreign technical/scientific prefixes** (пресс-, блок-, стоп-, дизель-, вакуум-, etc.) are
  ALWAYS hyphenated to the following noun, even for non-technical senses: пресс-клуб "press club" (p.
  171).
- **(d) Foreign profession/rank/status prefixes** (вице-, экс-, камер-, лейб-, обер-, унтер-, штаб-,
  штабс-) are hyphenated: вице-президент, штаб-квартира. The first component typically carries
  secondary stress, sometimes marked with a grave accent in dictionaries (p. 171).
- **(e) The half-prefix пол-/пол-** takes a hyphen when attached to a noun beginning with a capital
  letter, any vowel, or the letter л; it fuses WITHOUT a hyphen before any other lowercase consonant:
  пол-Европы / пол-яблока / пол-литра (hyphenated) vs. полвторого / полметра (fused) (p. 172).
  **Morphological complications**: (1) a noun with пол/пол- takes the genitive singular (половина
  яблока = пол-яблока); (2) genitive/locative/dative/instrumental forms optionally insert -у- (пол
  ме́тра vs. пóлумéтра), literary style favoring insertion, colloquial speech typically omitting it;
  (3) a modifying adjective takes a PLURAL form in the nominative/accusative (каждые пóлмили "every
  half a mile"); (4) in the remaining cases, a modifying adjective takes a SINGULAR form agreeing with
  the noun's gender (в этом пóл-яблоке, literary пóлу-яблоке); (5) the verb of a пол/пол- subject is
  normally 3rd-person neuter singular (Пóлгода пройдёт "Half a year will pass"), UNLESS an adjective
  modifies the noun, in which case the verb becomes plural, agreeing with the adjective (Остальные
  пóлмили были пройдены с трудóм) (pp. 172–173).
- **(f) Political-group/movement compounds** are hyphenated and NOT capitalized despite sometimes
  containing proper-noun-derived roots: марксизм-ленинизм, национал-социалист (p. 173).
- **(g) Certain geographic compound terms**: (i) geographic names containing a preposition
  (Ростов-на-Дону "Rostov-on-Don"); (ii) geographic names formed from two nouns, a noun+adjective, or
  two adjectives, including foreign place names (Канзас-Сити, Буэнос-Айрес); (iii) the foreign
  place-name prefixes лос-, лас-, сан-, сен-, сейнт-, санкт- are hyphenated (Лос-Анджелес,
  Санкт-Петербург) — **these prefixes are themselves indeclinable**; only the second component of
  such a name takes case endings (Мой брат живёт в Лос-Анджелесе, а не в Сан-Франциско) (pp.
  173–174). (iv) Adjectives formed from hyphenated place names retain the hyphen but are NOT
  capitalized (Нью-Йорк → нью-йоркский), following the general Russian rule against capitalizing
  proper-noun-derived adjectives; (v) the small West-European function words ла, де, ле, лос, лас
  found in place names ARE capitalized and hyphen-attached to the following part (Ла-Манш "the
  English Channel"); (vi) English/American street/square/park/avenue names are hyphenated when
  transliterated (Гайд-парк "Hyde Park"); grammatical endings attach only to the LAST component (от
  Гайд-парка) (p. 174).
- **(h) Compound surnames** are hyphenated, both native (Немирóвич-Дáнченко) and foreign
  (Склодóвская-Кюри); both parts normally decline (except names ending in -о/-и/-у/-ю, which never
  decline) (p. 175). **Chinese personal names are historically hyphenated on their last two
  syllables** in Russian, mirroring older English convention (Мао Дзэ-дун), **but current orthographic
  practice fuses these two syllables into one unhyphenated word instead** (Мао Цзэдун) (p. 175).
- **(i) Compass-direction compounds** combining more than one cardinal point: юго-восток
  "southeast," северо-запад "northwest." Compound PROPER adjectives from such directional terms are
  hyphenated and capitalized (Южно-Африканская Республика "Union of South Africa"), but the
  corresponding COMMON adjectives are fused into one lowercase word (южноафриканские алмазы "South
  African diamonds") (p. 175).
- **(j) Foreign, indeclinable modifiers preceding their noun** require a hyphen: брутто-вес "gross
  weight," мини-юбка "mini skirt." If the same indeclinable modifier instead FOLLOWS the noun, no
  hyphen is needed: язык суахили "Swahili" (p. 176).
- **(k) Compound adjectives**, six subcategories: (i) two color terms, connector vowel usually -о-,
  only the final adjective takes grammatical endings, hyphen obligatory in Russian (optional in
  English) — жёлто-красный "yellow-red"; (ii) two same-semantic-group adjectives with connector -о-,
  often corresponding to an English noun phrase — русско-английский "Russian-English"; (iii) two
  different-semantic-group adjectives each equally essential to the whole meaning — общественно-
  исторический "socio-historical"; (iv) unusual/self-contradictory semantic pairings, often
  corresponding to a whole English phrase — кисло-сладкий "sweet and sour"; (v) compounds with first
  component воённо- — воённо-морской "naval" (contrast fused, non-hyphenated substantivized forms
  like военнослужащий "member of the armed forces," военнопленный "prisoner of war"); (vi) adjectives
  formed from a famous person's first+last name, spelled with lower case letters — оскар-уайльдовский
  "of Oscar Wilde" (pp. 176–177).
- **(l) Words repeated for special emphasis, with primary stress on the first element**, forming a
  true hyphenated compound (distinct from Ch. 8's comma-separated repetition, which lacks this
  stress pattern): (i) mirror/full repetitions — большой-большой; (ii) partial repetitions, generally
  MORE intense than full repetitions — давным-давно, точь-в-точь; (iii) two synonyms repeated to
  convey one fused concept — жили-были "once upon a time" (pp. 177–178).
- **(m) Number-word combinations expressing approximation**: два-три дня "two or three days." If
  written with digits rather than spelled-out numbers, a DASH replaces the hyphen: 2—3 дня (p. 178).
- **(n) Compound indefinite pronouns of the type "где-где, а"** ("where else but"): Где-где, а в
  нашем крае дремучие леса "Where else but in our district can you find such ancient forests?" If the
  pronoun is governed by a preposition, a COMMA replaces the hyphen (Уж у кого, у кого, а у нас всё в
  порядке); such sentences are punctuated as statements in Russian even though their English
  translation often reads as a question (p. 178).
- **(o) Compounds with a conjunction/particle/finite verb form** — some plant names are formed this
  way: мать-и-мачеха "coltsfoot," не-тронь-меня "touch-me-not." **Note**: compound nouns of imperative
  + noun structure are usually NOT hyphenated, instead fully fused: скопидóм "skinflint" (literally
  "hoard+house"), горицвéт "ragged robin" (literally "burn+color") — сорвиголовá "daredevil"
  (literally "tear off+head"); exception: перекати-пóле "baby's breath" (literally "roll across+
  field"), which IS hyphenated (p. 178).
- **(n, continued as "adverbs abbreviating longer phrases")**: three groups — (i) language-of-
  communication adverbs (по-русски, cf. на русском языке); (ii) по + dative adjective with noun
  omitted, itself split into opinion-adverbs (по-моему), animal/manner adverbs ending in -ьи
  (по-кошачьи), and temporal/perceptual adverbs (по-новому); (iii) "in the Nth place" ordinal adverbs
  (во-первых, в-третьих), compound ordinals hyphenate both constituents (в-тридцать-восьмых) (p. 179).
  Exceptions fused without a hyphen: попусту, понемногу.
- **(o) A hyphen in a few prepositions of movement**: из-за, из-под, по-за, по-над (p. 179).
- **(p) Hyphenated particles**, four types: (i) the indefinite-pronoun/adverb formatives -то,
  -нибудь, -либо, and кое- (prefixed); (ii) the COLLOQUIAL emphatic particle -то attached to ANY word
  in a sentence for stress, contrast, or emphasized negation (Не так-то просто..., "It's just not all
  that easy..."); (iii) the COLLOQUIAL particle -ка attached to imperatives, softening a command into
  gentle prodding, or (attached to ну) intensifying it (Убери-ка посуду; Ну-ка отойди!); (iv) the
  unstressed particle -таки, meaning "nevertheless/anyway," most common as всё-таки (all registers)
  but also colloquially attachable to other words (pp. 180–181).
- **(q) A hyphen connects a digit-written number to a word ending written in letters**: 10-процентный
  = десятипроцентный. A fully spelled-out number+noun/adjective needs no hyphen. Ordinal numbers
  abbreviate the same way: 1-й = первый (p. 181).
- **(r) A hyphen in words of the type X-лучи, "X-rays,"** where the first part is a letter (Cyrillic
  or foreign-alphabet) pronounced as its NAME rather than its phonetic value, second part a native
  Russian word — retained even when the letter-name is spelled out in full: α-частица =
  альфа-частица (p. 181).
- **(s) A hyphen in abbreviations spelling out foreign letter-names as syllables**, usually
  organization acronyms: Би-Би-Си "BBC," Эм-Ти-Ви "MTV" — each element capitalized (p. 181).
- **(t) A hyphen indicating an elongated/drawn-out syllable**, an expressive device shared by
  Russian and English: Ну-у! Великолепно! "We-ell! That's marvellous!" (p. 181).
