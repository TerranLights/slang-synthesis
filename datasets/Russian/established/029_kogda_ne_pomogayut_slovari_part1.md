# Russian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Кogda ne pomogayut slovari... Часть I (full book, 10 lexical-semantic-group chapters)

**Source:** Э.В. Аркадьева, Г.В. Горбаневская, Н.Д. Кирсанова, И.Б. Марчук, *Когда не помогают
словари... Практикум по лексике современного русского языка для иностранцев и российских
студентов-филологов. Часть I* (Москва, изд-во "ДИК", 2001, ISBN 5-8213-0079-7), pp. 3–197 (all 206
PDF pages; front matter + 10 chapters + table of contents/colophon). Part II is a separate later
dispatch (Wave 2); the .doc duplicate of this same Part I in the source folder was not processed.

**PDF-extraction note:** `pdftotext -layout` produced a clean, fully readable Cyrillic text layer —
no font-substitution cipher was found in this file (checked per the project's standing Russian-
corpus gotcha; this source decoded cleanly on the first pass, unlike some other Russian-corpus
PDFs). A handful of OCR/scan artifacts (stray characters, occasional word-scrambling inside
example-sentence blocks, e.g. "nOBbSBATb" for "ПОБЫВАТЬ") appear in the running exercise text but
never in the boxed collocation patterns or headword definitions that this extraction actually
draws vocabulary from — these are noted inline only where they could affect a specific reading.

**Coverage note.** This is a lexical-collocation practicum ("practicum on the lexicon of
contemporary Russian"), not a grammar-paradigm textbook: its entire content is 10 chapters, each
built around a small cluster of tight near-synonym verbs/adverbs/conjunctions (a lexico-semantic
group, "ЛСГ") that Russian learners systematically confuse — плюс, for 5 of the 10 groups, the
group's full family of prefixed derivatives (e.g. смотреть → осматривать, рассматривать,
просматривать, пересматривать, всматриваться, засматриваться, ... — a dozen+ prefixed verbs per
base). This prefix-derivation material is exactly the kind of content most relevant to
slang-mechanics work (Russian prefixation is one of the most productive slang-formation devices),
so it is extracted in full rather than sampled. Every base verb/adverb/conjunction group's core
distinguishing collocation pattern, every prefixed derivative introduced, and every named idiom/
fixed-expression is captured below. Skipped: the hundreds of individual drill sentences and
fill-in-the-blank exercises (Упражнение N) that only re-use vocabulary already captured from the
same chapter — per the coverage rule, these are pedagogical padding, not new lexical items. Several
long illustrative literary quotations (from Pushkin, Tolstoy, Chekhov, Gogol, Bulgakov, Kaverin,
etc.) are referenced by author only, not reproduced, per copyright discipline. No handwritten
marginalia was found (this source has a genuine text layer — no vision-reading was needed).

This dataset intentionally departs from a "one row per bare headword" table: each lexico-semantic
group is presented as a **cluster** (base verb + its near-synonyms + its prefixed derivatives),
because the entire pedagogical point of this source — and its main future value for slang-mechanics
analysis — is the *distinguishing collocation/case-government pattern* between near-synonyms, not
the individual gloss. Each cluster gets one summary Notes cell describing the distinguishing
mechanism; prefixed derivatives get their own rows.

## Grammar points

### The near-synonym cluster as this source's basic organizing unit

Unlike a paradigm grammar, this practicum's basic pedagogical unit is a **lexico-semantic group**
(ЛСГ) — 2 to 7 near-synonymous verbs/adverbs/conjunctions that Russian-as-a-foreign-language
learners systematically confuse because a bilingual dictionary collapses them to one gloss. Each of
the book's 10 chapters teaches one such cluster by contrasting **case/preposition government**,
**aspectual behavior** (does the verb have a normal aspect pair, is it aspectually defective, does a
prefix change which aspect-pairing applies), and **register** as the three axes that actually
distinguish the near-synonyms — not the dictionary gloss, which is often identical across the whole
cluster. pp.3-4 (Предисловие), and repeated as the explicit method at the head of every chapter.

### Productive prefix as the source's real grammatical payload

For 5 of the 10 groups (СТРОИТЬ, СМОТРЕТЬ, УЧИТЬ, ДУМАТЬ, ПОМНИТЬ) the chapter's main content is not
the bare verb but its **full family of prefixed derivatives**, each contributing a distinct,
namable meaning-shift from the same base root — e.g. СМОТРЕТЬ's family spans at least 15 prefixed
forms (осматривать, рассматривать, разглядывать, просматривать, пересматривать, досматривать,
высматривать, подсматривать, предусматривать, всматриваться, засматриваться, насмотреться,
присматриваться, осматриваться, оглядываться — plus a further 5 formed only from the ГЛЯДЕТЬ variant:
выглядывать, выглядеть, заглядывать, проглядывать, взглянуть). The source explicitly cross-references
a recurring **"ЗА- + reflexive -СЯ = excessive/absorbing action, often to the subject's own
detriment"** pattern across otherwise-unrelated verb families — заучиться, засмотреться,
заслушаться, зачитаться, задуматься, засидеться, заиграться, загуляться all instantiate the same
prefix-plus-reflexive template (p.120, cross-referenced again at p.163). It likewise names a
**"РАЗ- = undo/reverse the result of a prior action"** family (разучиться, расхотеть, разлюбить,
разонравиться, распаковать, разоружить, p.121) and an **"ОТ- = cancel/undo a prior action"** family
(отучить(ся), отговорить, отсоветовать, отменить, p.122-123). These three named productive
prefix-patterns are the single most reusable finding in this source for later Russian
slang-mechanics analysis, since novel/slang coinages built on an existing verb root plus one of
these prefixes would be immediately interpretable to a native speaker via the same template.

### ПОЛЬЗОВАТЬСЯ vs. ИСПОЛЬЗОВАТЬ vs. ПРИМЕНЯТЬ vs. УПОТРЕБЛЯТЬ (the "use" cluster)

The source draws a three-way distinction across this whole cluster: (1) minimal-context-sufficient
vs. context-requiring (ПОЛЬЗОВАТЬСЯ's routine-function sense needs no elaboration; ИСПОЛЬЗОВАТЬ
always needs a manner/place/purpose adjunct); (2) active-only vs. active/passive-capable
(ПОЛЬЗОВАТЬСЯ has no passive-construction counterpart at all; ИСПОЛЬЗОВАТЬ and ПРИМЕНЯТЬ both form a
reflexive used specifically in passive constructions — ИСПОЛЬЗОВАТЬСЯ, ПРИМЕНЯТЬСЯ); (3) scale/register
(ПРИМЕНЯТЬ favored with "widely, universally, on a large scale," ИСПОЛЬЗОВАТЬ favored when a
specific, concrete purpose is named). УПОТРЕБЛЯТЬ is kept structurally separate: its primary modern
senses are metalinguistic ("use a word/expression") and a formal-register "consume food," though the
source notes its older/broader usage (употребить власть/деньги/время) as now archaic-flavored.
pp.82-96.

### УЧИТЬ vs. ИЗУЧАТЬ vs. ЗАНИМАТЬСЯ (the "study" cluster)

Central distinguishing test given by the source: objects requiring rote memorization (стихотворение,
роль, правило, таблица умножения) can only take УЧИТЬ, never ИЗУЧАТЬ, because ИЗУЧАТЬ presupposes
deep/systematic disciplinary knowledge rather than memorized content. Where the same object-noun
(e.g. физика, литература) can follow either verb, the choice signals a register/depth difference:
УЧИТЬ = "have as a school subject / do homework in," ИЗУЧАТЬ = "have as a university-level
discipline / do research in." ЗАНИМАТЬСЯ intersects both: its "где?" complement names the *room*
where study happens (contrast УЧИТЬСЯ где?, which names the *institution* one is enrolled in), and
in the "have as a scholarly preoccupation" sense it overlaps with ИЗУЧАТЬ but without ИЗУЧАТЬ's
implication of producing new/original findings. pp.105-118.

### СТРЕМИТЬСЯ vs. СТАРАТЬСЯ vs. ПЫТАТЬСЯ vs. ПРОБОВАТЬ (the "try/strive" cluster)

СТРЕМИТЬСЯ is aspectually defective (no СВ partner at all) because it denotes an inherently durative
process — motion toward a goal — rather than a bounded event; this aspectual gap is offered as
direct evidence for the semantic claim, cross-checked against the derived adjective стремительный
("fast-moving," of literal/figurative motion) versus старательный ("diligent," of a person's
work-manner) — the adjectives inherit the base verbs' "dynamic vs. effort-focused" contrast.
ПЫТАТЬСЯ is distinguished from СТАРАТЬСЯ by containing an "unsuccessful-or-difficult" component
directly in its lexical meaning (hence its strong co-occurrence with contrastive "но"), while
ПРОБОВАТЬ overlaps with ПЫТАТЬСЯ only when the action described is interrupted/inconclusive, and
lacks ПЫТАТЬСЯ's "exertion of effort" component. A further prefix note: ПЕРЕ- is shown to be
semantically non-uniform across this cluster — ПЕРЕСТАРАТЬСЯ's ПЕРЕ- means "excessive degree" while
ПЕРЕПРОБОВАТЬ's ПЕРЕ- means "exhaustive coverage of a whole set," a useful caution against assuming
one prefix always contributes one fixed meaning. pp.132-140.

### ПОМНИТЬ / ЗАПОМНИТЬ / ВСПОМНИТЬ three-way memory-verb contrast

The source gives an explicit unified model: ЗАПОМНИТЬ names the moment information enters memory
(encoding), ПОМНИТЬ names the durative state of retention, and ВСПОМНИТЬ names the moment of
retrieval — summarized in the source's own words as "we can only remember [помнить] what we
successfully encoded [запомнили]." A second orthogonal distinction applies to all three verbs (and
to НАПОМНИТЬ): direct-object (кого/что) government marks attention on the concrete content/structure
of the memory itself, while о-plus-Prepositional government (о ком/о чем) marks attention merely on
the *fact* that something exists/needs handling (an obligation, an appointment) rather than its
content. НАПОМНИТЬ further splits by subject-animacy: an animate subject reminds another person
verbally; an inanimate subject (a smell, a melody, a photograph) evokes a memory by resemblance or
association — and only the inanimate-subject sense requires a following Dative object. ПРИПОМНИТЬ is
carved out as a distinct, effortful/gradual retrieval verb (implying the information was never
consciously attended to when first acquired), with a secondary colloquial "hold a grudge" sense
governing Dative ("Я тебе это припомню"). pp.173-191.

### ТОЖЕ vs. ТАКЖЕ (connective particles)

ТОЖЕ marks strict identity/parallelism between two clauses' predicates and obligatorily carries
phrasal stress; ТАКЖЕ marks mere addition of supplementary information to what's already stated and
never carries phrasal stress or opens a sentence. Register split: ТОЖЕ is neutral, common in fiction
and colloquial speech, rarer in scientific prose; ТАКЖЕ is favored in scientific/publicistic
register, and in colloquial speech is restricted to the "besides, in addition" sense. "А ТАКЖЕ" in
an enumeration functions as a near-synonym of the correlative "не только..., но и...". pp.146-147.
