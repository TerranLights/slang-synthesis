## Grammar points

### Глаголы с суффиксами -ну-//-и-: the second inchoative/causative chapter, closing Раздел III «Глаголы»

This four-pair chapter (ГЛОХНУТЬ/ГЛУШИТЬ, СЛЕПНУТЬ/СЛЕПИТЬ, КРЕПНУТЬ/КРЕПИТЬ, СОХНУТЬ/СУШИТЬ) is
structurally the same become-X/make-X derivational pattern as the preceding -е-/-и- chapter (066s),
but built on a different suffix pair and, notably, the source's phrasing of the -и- verb's meaning
here ("the object of the action performs actions that cause it to acquire the quality") is subtly
inconsistent with the -е-/-и- chapter's phrasing (there, the -и- verb's *subject* causes the object
to acquire the quality) — likely a loose paraphrase rather than a genuine grammatical distinction,
but worth flagging for later mechanics-analysis work checking whether Russian causative morphology
is uniformly subject-causes-object across both suffix families or has real internal variation.
GLUSHIT'/ЗАГЛУШИТЬ is the richest single entry in this chapter, carrying three senses spanning a
literal-competition sense (plants blocking each other's light), a colloquial mechanical sense (a
motor stalling), and a figurative-suppression sense (preventing a desire/dream/innovation from
developing) — a compact illustration of how a single causative verb's senses can fan out from a
concrete physical base into fully abstract territory. This chapter's completion (p.292) closes out
Раздел III «Глаголы» entirely; the book's next top-level division is «НАРЕЧИЯ» (Adverbs).

### НАРЕЧИЯ (Adverbs): a new top-level chapter, organized as paired/grouped near-synonym discriminations exactly like the verb chapters

The adverb chapter follows the identical presentational format as the verb chapters — a named pair
or group, an optional `Комментарий` block giving the contrastive rule, then per-item numbered senses
with collocation lists — which means the same extraction methodology applies unchanged. Several of
this range's pairs show a recurring **information-structure (topic/focus) distinction** rather than
a purely denotational one: ДАЛЕКО vs. ВДАЛЕКЕ/ВДАЛИ is explicitly framed by the source as a
word-order/communicative-goal distinction (sentence-final "where is X" vs. sentence-initial "X is
at a distance, as a fact") rather than a difference in what "far" means — a genuinely different kind
of near-synonymy from anything seen in the verb chapters so far, and a strong candidate for later
mechanics-analysis work on how Russian's relatively free word order interacts with lexical choice.
The five-way ОПЯТЬ-СНОВА-ВНОВЬ-ЗАНОВО-ЕЩЁ РАЗ group cleanly reprises the register-cline template
(colloquial ОПЯТЬ / neutral СНОВА / bookish ВНОВЬ) already seen multiple times in the verb chapters,
while adding a genuinely distinct fourth axis (ЗАНОВО's "fresh-start/different-method/first-time"
senses, and ЕЩЁ РАЗ's action-not-state restriction) not reducible to register alone — the richest
single adverb group in this range. ИЗДАЛЕКА-ИЗДАЛИ (opened p.304) is left mid-comment for the next
shard; the source's cross-referencing convention (`см. снова 1`, `см. издалека 1`) — one entry's
definition sense pointing at a partner's identical numbered sense rather than restating it — is a
new economy-of-presentation device not seen in the verb chapters and worth noting for anyone later
cross-referencing specific senses back to page numbers.
