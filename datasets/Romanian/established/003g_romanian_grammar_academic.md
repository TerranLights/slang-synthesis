## Grammar points

### Interrogative/indefinite quantifiers cât, atât, oricât, câtva, tot: a shared paradigm shape (§3.5.1)

Five quantity pronoun/adjective families (cât "how much/many," atât "that much/many," oricât "any
amount," câtva "some amount," tot "all") share a common structural template: identical pronoun and
adjective forms in the nominative-accusative, a masculine/feminine singular split and an invariant
plural across genders, pre-nominal placement with no article on the modified noun, and — distinctively
— genitive-dative forms that exist *only in the plural* (câtora/câtor, atâtora/atâtor, oricâtora/
oricâtor, tuturor for tot) with no singular genitive-dative at all. As pronominal adjectives, these
quantifiers also commonly form a non-standard-but-attested genitive/dative using the prepositions `a`/
`la` plus the plain nominative-accusative form, flagged by the source as "perceived... as not
completely correct" yet frequently heard — a live, source-acknowledged prescriptive/descriptive tension
worth tracking.

### Indefinite-quantity adjectives mult/puţin/destul/suficient (§3.5.1.6)

A closed set of four-form adjectives (mult "much/many," puţin "little/few," destul and suficient both
"enough," the latter explicitly more formal/Latinate) mark large, small, or sufficient quantity;
singular forms pair with non-countable nouns, plural with countable nouns, in either pre- or
post-nominal order with the modified noun never taking an article.

### Cardinal numerals 0–19: irregular low numbers, a spoken-register short form (§3.5.2.1–2)

Only "one" (4 forms: unu/un/una/o) and "two" (2 forms: doi/două) show gender agreement; 0 and 3–10 are
fully invariable. 11–19 are built compositionally from the units plus `-sprezece` (from spre "on" +
zece "ten"), with 12 alone showing a masculine/feminine split (doisprezece/douăsprezece). The spoken
register colloquially contracts `-sprezece` to `[-şpe]` (unşpe, doişpe...) while the written form never
reflects this — an explicit, source-marked spoken/written register split distinct from a dialectal one.

### Tens, hundreds, thousands: compositional formation and the obligatory linking `de` (§3.5.2.3–7)

Tens (20–90) compose as unit+zeci (douăzeci = două+zeci); 50 and 80 have irregular pronunciations
diverging from spelling. Any numeral of 20 or more (and any compound numeral whose final component is
20–99) requires the linking preposition `de` before its noun (douăzeci de studenţi); numerals 1–19
never do. `sută` "hundred" and `mie` "thousand" behave as fully regular nouns (feminine) that decline,
pluralize, and take articles; `milion`/`miliard` behave as regular neuter nouns likewise. Crucially,
Romanian cannot express multiples of 100 as compound "twenty-hundred"-style numbers the way some
languages might — 2,000 must be "two thousand" (două mii), never a hundreds-multiple construction.
Compound numerals over 100 string their components with no connector at all (o sută treisprezece
"113"), and the linking `de` only resurfaces if the *final* component in the string is itself 20–99.

### Genitive-dative of cardinal numerals: inflected only for "one," prepositional otherwise (§3.5.2.8)

Only the numeral "one" (unul/una as pronoun, un/o as adjective) has true inflected genitive-dative forms
(unuia/uneia; unui/unei), following the possessive-article rule (al/a/ai/ale) exactly like other
pronouns when used independently/remotely. sută/mie/milion/miliard decline as the regular nouns they
grammatically are. Every other cardinal numeral (2 and up) expresses genitive/dative purely
periphrastically, via the prepositions `a` (genitive) and `la` (dative) placed before the numeral+noun
group — a structurally distinct mechanism from the inflected system used everywhere else in Romanian's
nominal morphology.

### Five additional numeral types, each a distinct derivational template (§3.5.3)

- **Distributive** (câte + cardinal): "N at a time/each" — câte doi "two by two"; its own genitive/
  dative again uses `a`/`la`.
- **Collective** (amândoi/amândouă for exactly two, with a more formal ambii/ambele synonym pair
  explicitly flagged administrative/journalistic register; toţi/toate+cardinal for three or more,
  optionally itself supported by the cel/cea adjectival article; archaic-flavored tustrei/tuspatru-type
  forms for "all three/four" explicitly flagged non-standard).
- **Adverbial** ("N times"): de+cardinal+ori, with the suppletive irregular o dată for "once" rather
  than a predictable *o oară.
- **Multiplicative** ("N times as much"): în-/îm- prefix + -it suffix on a handful of cardinals
  (îndoit, întreit, împătrit...), explicitly noted as losing ground to newer Latinate synonyms (dublu,
  triplu, cvadruplu...) that the source says are now *more frequent* in contemporary usage — a live
  lexical-replacement-in-progress finding.
- **Fractional** ("1/N"): -ime suffix on cardinals (doime, treime, pătrime...), functioning
  grammatically as feminine nouns and pluralizing when the numerator exceeds one; also repurposed as
  formal music terminology for note-value divisions. Percentages/per-mille use a fixed cardinal + la +
  sută/mie frame, and decimals use a comma (virgulă), not a period, as the decimal marker — una
  virgulă cinci "1.5" — a notational convention with direct relevance to any numeral-heavy corpus
  parsing downstream.

### Numerical approximation: a formal/colloquial register pair for "about N" (§3.5.4)

Romanian has (at minimum) two parallel sets of approximation markers for "approximately N," and the
source explicitly assigns them to different registers: `aproximativ`/`circa` for documents and mass
media (formal), vs. `în jur de`/`cam`/`vreo` for colloquial speech — directly useful for the
slang/colloquial-vocabulary phase. Separately, "a bit over/under a round number" has its own dedicated
vocabulary (peste, mai mult de, mai bine de for excess; aproape, sub, mai puţin de for shortfall), and a
colloquial double-cardinal construction (trei-patru zile "three or four days") expresses a vague range
without any connecting word.

### Ordinal numerals: adjectival agreement, a two-system split for "first," genitive-dative via the article (§3.5.5)

Ordinals agree in gender/number/case with their noun like adjectives, answering the question al
câtelea?/a câta? "which one in the series?" (itself built on the cât-interrogative already documented
in §3.5.1.1). Every ordinal past "first" is formed by prefixing the possessive article al (masc.)/a
(fem.) onto a derived cardinal-numeral stem with suffix -lea (masc.) or -a (fem.) — with a predictable
set of final-vowel/consonant-insertion adjustments spelled out by the source (final -ă/-u drop before
-a; a linking -u- is inserted between a final consonant and -lea). "First" itself is split between two
non-fully-interchangeable forms: primul/prima (the default, usually pre-nominal) and întâiul/întâia
(an older/more formal alternative) — the source gives a concrete minimal-pair case where substituting
one for the other changes meaning (clasa întâi "first grade" vs. prima clasă "the first classroom"). A
closed pair of Latinate ordinal synonyms, secund/secundă and terţ/terţă, survive only in a few fixed
collocations (locul secund, terţă persoană) rather than as general-purpose ordinals. Ordinals can
optionally be reinforced by the adjectival article cel/cea (cel de-al doilea = al doilea, stylistically
equivalent in the nominative-accusative) — but this reinforced form is the one that actually inflects
for genitive-dative (the article cel/cea bears the case marking, exactly as already seen for the plain
adjectival article in §3.2.2). A specific closed set of nouns (clasă, an, grupă, etaj, rând, lecţie,
exerciţiu) allows the ordinal to instead follow the noun without the adjectival article and without
case inflection at all — and, except for clasă, these same nouns can substitute a cardinal numeral for
the ordinal entirely in casual speech (actul trei for actul al treilea) — the source flags this
cardinal-for-ordinal substitution in book/play/film part-naming (capitol, volum, act, scenă) as
specifically colloquial-register, contrasting with the prescriptive ordinal-numeral norm.
