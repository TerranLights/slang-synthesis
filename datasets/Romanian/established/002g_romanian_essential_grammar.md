# Romanian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Chapters 15-16 (Opinions/attitudes, Judgement/evaluation) + Glossary

**Source:** Ramona Gönczöl-Davies, *Romanian: An Essential Grammar* (Routledge, 2008), pp. 181-200.

**Coverage note:** covers Chapter 15 (Expressing opinions and attitudes), Chapter 16 (Judgement and
evaluation), and the book's own Glossary of grammatical terms (p. 199-200) in full. Clean text
layer throughout. **This is the single richest stretch of explicitly source-flagged slang/
colloquial material found anywhere in this book** — the source itself tags multiple items "(coll.)"
or "(very coll.)" or, in one case, "(slang)" outright, and this file flags every one of them
individually in the Vocabulary table, per the coverage rule's priority on explicit register
annotation. The Glossary of grammatical terms is included as a compact cross-reference (paraphrased,
not reproduced verbatim) since it is the book's own metalanguage key, useful for resolving any
terminology ambiguity across all `002*` files in this set.

## Grammar points

### 15.1-15.5 Expressing opinions and attitudes (pp. 181-186)

This chapter is organized by discourse function (agreement/disagreement, remembering, possibility/
probability, incomprehension/clarification, certainty/uncertainty), each with a question-set and an
answer-set. The disagreement and incomprehension sub-sections carry the chapter's clearest
register gradients: disagreement responses range from the mildest `Nu.` through idiomatic
emphatic-refusal doublets (`Nici vorbă.`/`Nici gând.`, lit. "not a word"/"not a thought") up to the
religious exclamation `Doamne ferește!` ("God forbid!"); incomprehension responses include two
items the source explicitly flags "(coll.)" — the one-word interrogatives `Cum?`/`Ce?` and the
idiom `Nu mă prind.` ("I don't get it," lit. "I don't catch myself") — each contrasted directly
against a register-neutral synonym given in the same list (`Ce ai spus?`, `Nu pricep.`
respectively). This paired neutral/colloquial presentation (the book consistently lists a plain
form immediately next to its colloquial counterpart) is a recurring structural device across this
whole chapter and the next, making the register contrast legible without extra commentary.

### 16.1-16.3 Pleasure/liking, displeasure/dislike, interest (pp. 187-189)

**This is the book's single highest-value register-marked passage.** Expressing strong positive
reaction escalates from neutral intensifiers (`Ce superb!`, `Ce minunat!`, `Ce incredibil!`) to two
items explicitly marked **"(very coll.)"**: `Ce mișto!` ("how cool!") and `Ce ca lumea!` ("how
wicked/great!" — lit. "how like-the-world," an idiom with no transparent compositional meaning).
The displeasure section mirrors this exactly in structure: neutral intensifiers (`Ce groaznic!`,
`Ce oribil!`, `Ce îngrozitor!`) escalate to one item marked **"(very coll.)"**: `Ce nasol!` ("how
bad!"). Together, `Ce mișto!`/`Ce nasol!` form a clean positive/negative slang pair in the exact
same syntactic frame (`Ce` + predicate + `!`), confirming the bare interjections `mișto!`/`nasol!`
already flagged in Chapter 11 (see `002d_romanian_essential_grammar.md`) are fully productive
inside this larger exclamatory construction, not limited to standalone use. The interest section
(16.3) distinguishes `Ai chef de.../Ai chef să...` (explicitly "(coll.)," governing a noun-object
via `de` or a clausal complement via `să` — a genuine syntactic split within one idiom) from the
more food-specific `Ai poftă de.../Ai poftă să...` (not flagged colloquial, and the book draws the
distinction by typical collocation rather than formality). The reply section contains this book's
single most explicit slang flag anywhere: `Mi-e paralel.` is the **only phrase in the entire source
tagged "(slang)"** rather than "(coll.)"/"(very coll.)" — given alongside two register-neutral
synonyms for the identical meaning ("it's all the same to me": `Mi-e tot una.`, `Mi-e indiferent.`),
producing a clean three-rung register ladder (neutral / neutral / slang) for one semantic slot,
exactly the kind of minimal-pair register data the later mechanics-analysis/synthesis phase will
want most.

### 16.4-16.10 Surprise, hope, satisfaction, approval, preference, gratitude, happiness/unhappiness (pp. 190-196)

Religious-register exclamations recur heavily in the surprise and hope sections, with an apparent
intensification-by-stacking pattern: `Doamne!` ("Oh God!") → `Doamne Dumnezeule!`/`Doamne Sfinte!`
("Good/Dear God!," adding a second divine-reference vocative) — structurally similar to the
diminutive-stacking pattern already found in the family-address vocabulary of Ch. 13
(`002e_romanian_essential_grammar.md`), suggesting Romanian broadly licenses this kind of
same-slot word-stacking for emotional intensification across more than one grammatical category.
Approval responses include `Ţine-o tot așa!` explicitly marked "(coll.)," directly paired with its
register-neutral twin `Continuă tot așa!` in the same list — the same paired-presentation device
noted above. The happiness section is unusually rich in vivid embodied idioms, several with
cross-linguistic idiom-comparison value: `Sunt în al nouălea cer de fericire.` ("I am over the
moon," lit. "I am in the ninth heaven of happiness" — compare English's *seventh* heaven, a
different numeral for the same superlative-happiness image); `Plutesc de fericire.` ("I am
ecstatic," lit. "I float from happiness"); `Nu-mi mai încap în piele de fericire.` ("I am
overflowing with happiness," lit. "I no longer fit in my own skin from happiness"). The matching
unhappiness/anger section includes a colour-metaphor idiom, `Sunt negru/neagră de supărare.` ("I am
seething with anger," lit. "I am black from anger").

### 16.11-16.14 Apologizing, disappointment/worry/fear, regret/indifference, accusing (pp. 196-198)

Apology vocabulary shows a clear intensity/register gradient: `Pardon!` (casual, international
loanword) < `Îmi cer scuze!` (neutral) < `Îmi cer mii de scuze!` (intensified via numeral hyperbole,
"a thousand excuses") < `Vă implor să mă iertaţi!` ("I beg you to forgive me!," most dramatic). The
worry/fear section distinguishes near-synonymous `teamă`/`frică` (both "fear") without an explicit
register note, and includes body/possession-metaphor idioms for "having something on one's mind"
(`Ai ceva pe suflet?`, lit. "on your soul") paralleling the "on your head" idiom for busyness
(`Ai atâtea pe cap!`). The accusing section (16.14) is comparatively flat in register — mostly
direct, blunt accusation formulae with no colloquial/formal split drawn by the source.

### Glossary of grammatical terms (pp. 199-200)

The book's own back-matter glossary, included here as a compact cross-reference since every term
it defines recurs across all seven `002*` files in this set. Paraphrased definitions: **Case** —
the Romanian system has five (Nominative/subject, Accusative/direct object, Genitive/possession,
Dative/indirect object, Vocative/direct address) — cross-ref 2.4. **Mood** — a verb category
expressing fact/command/wish/condition; the glossary's own split of **personal moods**
(indicative, subjunctive, conditional, presumptive, imperative — i.e. the finite, person-marking
moods) vs. **impersonal moods** (infinitive, gerund, participle, supine — the non-finite,
person-invariant moods) is the book's official terminology for the five-vs-four mood split already
described in 7.1.1. **Reflexive** is defined narrowly as "verbs and pronouns where the doer and the
recipient are the same" — the book's own gloss confirms the diagnostic already stated in 5.9 for
distinguishing a true reflexive from an object pronoun that merely shares a clitic form.
**Comparative**/**Superlative** are defined as the second/third degrees of comparison respectively
(the "first" / unstated degree being the positive/base form) — cross-ref Ch. 4 and 8.2.
