## Grammar points

### §69–§73 — Gender system
Romanian has three genders: masculine, feminine, and "mixed" (the book's preferred term; footnoted
as also called neuter) — mixed-gender nouns are masculine in the singular and feminine in the plural.
Gender-by-ending defaults: masculine = nouns ending in -u or a consonant (băiat, ochiu, om, socru;
cap "head/chief" is masc., but cap meaning "head, peak, top" is mixed). Feminine = most nouns in -e
(carte, cutie, lege — with named exceptions that are masculine instead: arbore, cărbune, câine,
dinte, lapte, munte, iepure, nasture, perete, peşte, pieptene, sâmbure, sânge, soare, şarpe, şoarece,
vierme), nouns in -ă (apă, aţă, casă — exceptions tată, popă), nouns in accented -a (cafea, muşama,
stea), nouns in -î/-i (luni, zi). §73–75 cover gender-by-meaning overriding gender-by-ending: male
beings → masculine (bărbat, frate, lup, părinte, preot, rege — but occupational nouns like calfă,
gazdă, paşă, slugă are feminine by ending despite denoting people of either sex); alphabet letters,
month names, tree names → masculine (but sălcie "willow" is feminine); female beings, season names,
weekday/time-division/month-division names → feminine (but an "year" is masculine); fruit names →
feminine (but strugure "grape" is masc., măr "apple" is mixed); most inanimate-object and abstraction
nouns → mixed (chibrit, lucru, pat; adevăr, gust).

### §76–§77 — Definite article (postposed/enclitic)
The definite article attaches to the end of the noun, not before it. Singular (nom./acc.) forms:
masc./mixed nouns in -u take -l (socru→socrul, lucru→lucrul); masc./mixed nouns in a consonant
insert a supporting -u before -l (om→omul, adevăr→adevărul); masc. nouns in -e take -le (frate→
fratele, peşte→peştele); fem. nouns take -a (soră→sora, vară→vara); fem. nouns in -a or -i insert a
supporting -u before -a (cafea→cafeaua, stea→steaua, zi→ziua). In everyday speech, masc. -l is often
not pronounced but the supporting -u is still sounded ([lukru] not [lukrul]). §77: feminine nouns in
-ă (and sometimes -e) drop that final vowel before adding -a (casă→casa, cutie→cutia) — but exceptions
like carte→cartea, pâine→pâinea keep the stem vowel and simply add -a.

### §78–§81 — Indefinite article
Singular indefinite articles: un (masc.), o (fem.); both decline for genitive-dative (un om/unui om;
o casă/unei case). No true plural indefinite article exists — nişte ("some") substitutes. Common
uses: before a coordinated series of related nouns; with a predicate noun of title/trade/religion/
nationality modified by an adjective (footnoted exception: the article is often dropped when directly
answering "What is he?" — E profesor — but is regularly kept answering "Who is he?" — E un profesor).
Common omissions of the indefinite article: with an indeterminate-quantity direct object (Am
cumpărat pâine şi vin); after a preposition when the noun is unmodified; with a negated unmodified
noun; with a predicate noun naming matter/an individual/an object (Nu-i carne, e legumă); with a noun
in apposition (Am fost la Bucureşti, oraş mare şi frumos).

### §82–§83 — Negation and interrogation
Negative nu precedes the verb (Nu am, Nu este/Nu-e); stressed for emphasis (nu pot); nu repeats after
fără ("without," as conjunction) and after verbs of cessation (Nu se poate opri să nu ne scrie).
Questions rarely change word order — usually signaled by rising intonation or an interrogative word
(A plecat? De ce nu vine?); the subject pronoun may be added for clarity/emphasis with inversion
possible (Vine el sau nu?); inversion also occurs in compound-tense questions (rare: Făcut-am-eu
asta? alongside the regular Am făcut eu asta?), and regularly in sentences opening with an
interrogative pronoun or adverb (Cine sunt ei? Unde a fost el?).

### §84–§86 — Present indicative of a fi "to be" and a avea "to have"; pronoun/politeness usage
Full present paradigms given: fi = sunt, eşti, este/e/-i, este/e/-i, suntem, sunteţi, sunt, sunt;
avea = am, ai, are, are, avem, aveţi, au, au. Footnotes: sunt is replaced by îs sentence-initially in
casual speech, or contracted to -s after a vowel (Nu-s obosit); este is less common than e, especially
colloquially; -i is used for "is" when the preceding word ends in a vowel (Casa-i mică); 3rd-pl. is
sometimes spelled with -s after a vowel-final word (Casele-s frumoase); spelling sânt for sunt is
used by some writers. §85: subject pronouns (eu, tu, el, ea, noi, voi, ei, ele) are used rarely, only
for emphasis/contrast, and may stand alone as a complete answer (Cine-i acolo? Eu.). §86: the 2nd
person plural (without pronoun) is the ordinary polite form of address; Dumneata + 2nd sg. is a more
familiar-but-still-polite alternative; Dumneavoastră + 2nd pl. is the most formal/deferential register
— cross-referenced to Lesson XXIV for the full Dumneata paradigm, not yet reached in this chunk's
page range.

### §87 — Formation of the noun plural
(a) Masc. nouns in a consonant: add -i (prieten→prieteni, lup→lupi). (b) Masc. nouns in -clu, -cru,
-dru, -gru, -tru: drop supporting -u, add -i (cioclu→ciocli, socru→socri, codru→codri, tigru→tigri,
metru→metri). (c) Fem. nouns in -e: drop -e, add -ĭ (muiere→muieri, farmacie→farmacii). (d) Some fem.
nouns in -ă: drop -ă, add -e; others add -ĭ instead (casă→case; inimă→inimi) — the book notes both
patterns exist without giving a fully predictive rule for which applies when. (e) Some nouns (either
gender) in -a, -ea, -u: drop that ending, add -ale or -ele (muşama→muşamale; şosea→şosele; atu→atale).
(f) Mixed-gender nouns pluralize either by adding -e to the singular (scaun→scaune) or by adding -uri
(timp→timpuri). (g) A short list of nouns has an irregular/suppletive plural stem: cap→capete,
cuvânt→cuvinte, soră→surori, oaspe→oaspeţi, om→oameni. Footnote: the plural marker never shifts
stress (cf. §59's accentuation rules).
