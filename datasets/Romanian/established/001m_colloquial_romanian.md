## Grammar points

### Further uses of `pe`: before human-referring pronouns generally, not just stressed object pronouns — Unit 15, p. 192

Extends Unit 11's `pe`-before-human-objects rule: `pe` also precedes other pronouns (not just the
stressed direct-object set) whenever they refer to a person — `l-am chemat pe toți` "I summoned all
of them," `pe unul l-au dus la spital, pe altul la policlinică` "one they took to hospital, another
to the clinic." This confirms `pe` is tracking **humanness of the referent**, not a fixed closed
pronoun class.

### Further uses of `la`: with quantifying adjectives and numerals above "one" — Unit 15, p. 193

A small set of quantifying adjectives (`mulți` "many," `amândoi` "both") that do have their own
possessive/dative forms (`multor`, `amânduror`) **more often appear with `la` instead in colloquial
speech**: `la puțini copii le place să meargă la doctor` "few children like going to the doctor"
(lit. "to few children it-is-pleasing..."). `la` is also required to mark the dative with numerals
above "one": `au vândut bilete la patru studenți` "they sold tickets to four students" — i.e.
Romanian's rich case-marking system has a colloquial escape hatch into a prepositional strategy
once the possessor/recipient is quantified rather than a single definite noun.

### Sentence-adverbs `ca` + that-complement — Unit 15, p. 193

Adverbs that comment on the whole sentence (`bineînțeles`, `firește`, `desigur` — all roughly
"of course/naturally") take **`ca`** before a following clause: `bineînțeles că l-am vizitat` "of
course I visited him." When used as a stand-alone one-word reply (not followed by a clause), no
`ca` is needed: `i-ai telefonat? Desigur.` "Did you call him? Of course."

### The past historic tense — Unit 15, pp. 195–196 — **sixth distinct past-tense-family form, regionally marked**

A dedicated literary/narrative past tense, distinct from the compound past (Unit 3) and the
imperfect (Unit 14), formed by endings suffixed to the **past-participle stem**: `-i/-și/∅`
singular, `-răm/-răți/-ră` plural (`plecai, plecași, plecă, plecarăm, plecarăți, plecară` from `a
pleca`). Verbs with past participles in `-s` instead use `-ei/-eși/-e(se)` sg., `-erăm/-erăți/-eră`
pl. (`mersei, merseși, merse...` from `a merge`). `a fi` is doubly irregular, with **two parallel
past-historic paradigms** (`fui`/`fuși`/`fu`... and `fusei`/`fuseși`/`fuse`...). **Register/
geography note the source states explicitly**: the past historic "normally is used in written
narratives, such as the fairy-tale [extracted from this unit's Reading passage]... In the area of
Oltenia, in the south of Romania, the past historic replaces the compound perfect [i.e. the Unit-3
"am fost" type] in speech, but otherwise it is rarely used in standard spoken Romanian." This is a
genuine, source-marked regional/register distinction (written-narrative-only nationally, but a live
regional speech form in Oltenia) — a strong candidate for the mechanics-analysis phase's regional-
variation findings.
