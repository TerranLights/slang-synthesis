## Grammar points

### Comparison of adjectives and adverbs with `mai`/`decât` — Unit 9, pp. 103–105

Comparison is formed by placing invariant **mai** ("more") before the adjective or adverb, with
**decât** ("than") introducing the compared term: `mai mică` "smaller," `mai bun` "better,"
`Polonia este mai mare... decât România` "Poland is bigger... than Romania." The same `mai`
construction covers adverbs (`mai repede` "faster," `ei vorbesc mai prost românește` "they speak
Romanian less well"). No separate synthetic comparative morphology (no English-style `-er` suffix)
— the entire comparison system runs through this one invariant particle plus `decât`.

### Demonstratives `acest`/`acel` ("this"/"that") — full case paradigm — Unit 9, pp. 107–108

Demonstrative adjectives **precede** the noun and carry their own full case paradigm (nom/acc and
gen/dat, across gender and number) independent of the noun's own article state: `acest prieten`
"this friend" / `acestui prieten` "of/to this friend" / `acești prieteni` "these friends" /
`acestor prieteni` "of/to these friends"; parallel `acel`/`acei`/etc. for "that." **When the
demonstrative follows the noun instead of preceding it**, the noun takes the definite article and
the demonstrative itself shifts to a longer form (`acesta`, `aceasta`, `aceștia`, `acela`, etc.):
`prietenul acesta` "this friend" (lit. "the-friend this"), `prietenii aceia` "those friends." This
pre-/post-nominal alternation with a matching morphological shift on the demonstrative itself is a
genuinely distinctive Romanian structural feature.

### `alt` ("another/other") — Unit 9, p. 108

Behaves like a demonstrative for case-marking purposes: `alt om` "another man" / `altui om` "of/to
another man" / `alți oameni` "other people" / `altor oameni` "of/to other people"; feminine `altă
mașină`/`altei mașini`/`alte mașini`/`altor mașini`.

### Direct-object pronoun clitics — Unit 9, pp. 108–109

Full table of unstressed direct-object pronouns, which **precede** the verb in the present tense but
**follow** (as a reduced suffix) in the compound past: `mă` (me), `te` (you sg.), `îl` (him/it),
`o` (her/it), `ne` (us), `vă` (you pl.), `îi` (them m.), `le` (them f.). Present-tense example:
`îi invit la masă` "I am inviting him to a meal." Past-tense placement and reduction: `ei au
văzut-o la gară` "they saw her at the station" (o follows); other clitics reduce and attach as a
prefix-like element to the auxiliary: `mă` → `m-` (`el m-a luat cu mașina` "he took me by car"),
`îl` → `l-` (`ea l-a trimis acasă`), `ne` → `ne-` (`ei ne-au văzut la restaurant`). **After the
subjunctive particle `să`, `îl`/`îi` reduce to `-l`/`-i`** attached to `să`: `trebuie să-i invităm`
"we must invite them," `ea vrea să-l vadă` "she wants to see him" — contrasted explicitly with `ea
vrea s-o vadă` "she wants to see her" (o-reduction after să is `s-o`, not `să-o`). The source also
flags a minimal-pair trap: don't confuse the negator-reduction `n-` (`nu` before a vowel, Unit 3)
with the object clitic `ne` ("us") — `n-au luat trenul` "they didn't catch the train" vs. `ne-au
luat acasă` "they took us home."

### Reflexive verbs — Unit 9, pp. 109–111

Romanian has a large, productive class of reflexive verbs, including many with **no reflexive sense
in their English translation** at all — the source's own examples are `a se întreba` "to wonder"
(lit. "to ask oneself") and `a se duce` "to go" (lit. "to take oneself"). The 3rd-person reflexive
pronoun **se** ("himself/herself/itself/themselves") is what appears baked into the infinitive of
every such verb (`a se numi`, `a se duce`, `a se întreba`). Full present/past paradigms given for
`a se întreba` and `a se duce`, with the same clitic-reduction-before-vowel pattern already seen for
plain object pronouns (`m-am întrebat` "I wondered," `s-a dus` "he/she went," `v-ați dus` "you
went").

### Family-relation vocabulary network — Unit 9, pp. 111–113

The family-photo dialogue and its accompanying family-tree diagram (p. 112) lay out a fuller kinship
set than Unit 3's: `bunic/bunică` (grandfather/grandmother), `unchi`/`mătușă` (uncle/aunt),
`var`/`vară` (male/female cousin — note the gendered noun pair differs only in final vowel, unlike
the unrelated English "cousin"), `nepot`/`nepoată` (nephew/niece — the same noun pair is also used
for grandson/granddaughter, disambiguated by context only), and `geamăn`/`geamănă` (twin). The
dialogue's own usage of `nevastă` (colloquial "wife") alongside the more neutral `soție` (Units 3,
5) establishes a genuine two-tier register pair for "wife" worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis
phase.
