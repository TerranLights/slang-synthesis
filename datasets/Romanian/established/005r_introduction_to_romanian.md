## Grammar points

### §161 — Demonstrative adjectives and pronouns
Full declension tables for *acesta/acela* "this/that" (masculine and feminine, singular/plural) and
*cestălalt/celălalt* "this other/that other." Footnote: the possessive article *al* is used in the
genitive case of the demonstrative pronouns. In everyday speech, the shorter forms *ăsta/ăla* replace
*acesta/acela* and *ăstalalt/al(a)lalt* replace *cestălalt/celălalt*; a further, even-less-common pair
*ăst/ăl* also exists. The ironic/derogatory indefinite-article-plus-demonstrative-pronoun idiom (*un
ăla* "a man of bad reputation," *o aia* "a woman of bad reputation") is a genuinely rich finding for
later slang-mechanics synthesis — a grammatical demonstrative pronoun repurposed, via a fixed
syntactic frame, into a euphemistic insult construction.

### §162 — Special meanings and uses of demonstrative adjectives and pronouns
(a) *Acest*/*cestălalt* mark proximity to the speaker; *acel*/*celălalt* mark remoteness. (b) The
demonstrative adjective may precede or follow its noun, declining in either position; footnote: when
it precedes, the *-a* suffix is dropped (*omul acesta* but *acest om*). (c) When paired with another
adjective, the demonstrative comes first, is declined, and the noun/other adjective stays undeclined.
(d) General gender/number agreement rule, with example paradigm. (e) In a coordinated series, the
demonstrative may agree only with the first noun (*Aceşti oameni şi femeia se duc la băi* "These men
and (this) woman are going to the shore"). (f) The demonstrative pronoun preceded by *de* takes on the
sense "this kind of," e.g. *nu se mai vede de aceşti* "you don't see any more of this type" — a
genuinely useful generic/kind-marking use. (g) With two nouns of different gender/number, the
demonstrative is used only with the last noun. (h) The feminine singular forms *aceasta/asta* can be
used in a neutral sense referring to a general situation rather than a specific feminine noun, e.g.
*Asta e o chestie veche* "This is an old question"; *Asta-i cura lui* "That's his diet" — directly
parallel to the neuter-*o* pattern already documented for the clitic pronoun system in Lesson XII
§137, confirming this neutral/generic-reference strategy recurs across multiple pronoun paradigms in
Romanian.
