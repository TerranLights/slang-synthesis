## Grammar points

### `a durea` ("to hurt") is impersonal/defective; "mi-e..." dative idiom family extended — Unit 13, p. 160

`a durea` ("to hurt") is **only used in the third person** (singular `doare`/plural `dor`), always
with a dative/indirect-object pronoun marking the sufferer, mirroring the `mi-e foame`-type
construction from Unit 11: `mă dor ochii` "my eyes hurt" (lit. "me-hurt the-eyes," verb agrees with
the plural subject "eyes," not the person), `mă dor dinții` "I have toothache," `mă doare în gât`
"I have a sore throat." The reading-passage letter extends the "mi-e..." dative family further:
`mi-e greață` "I feel sick," alongside a a brand-new impersonal-verb pairing explained in the
grammar point below.

### Reduced dative pronoun before reflexive/impersonal `se`: `mi s-a spus`, `mi se pare` — Unit 13, p. 157

When the reduced dative pronoun (`mi`, `ți`, `i`, `ni`, `vi`, `li` — from `îmi`/`îți`/`îi`/`ne`/
`vă`/`le`) precedes the reflexive marker `se` in an impersonal construction (often English-passive
in sense), it takes this specific reduced shape: `mi se pare că...` "it seems to me that..." (lit.
"to-me it-seems that"), `i se pare curios` "it seems strange to him," `mi s-a spus că ai plecat` "I
was told that you had left" (lit. "to-me it-was-said that..."). The impersonal verbs that pattern
this way include `a se părea` ("to seem") and `a se întâmpla` ("to happen" — `mi se întâmplă` "it
happens to me").

### Double-clitic stacking: direct-object pronoun precedes indirect-object pronoun — Unit 13, pp. 157–158

When both a direct- and an indirect-object pronoun attach to the same verb, **the direct-object
clitic precedes the indirect-object one**, and both reduce together: `mi-l dă` "he gives it (m./n.)
to me," `ni-le dă` "he gives them (f.) to us," `li-i dă` "he gives them (m.) to them." The direct-
object pronoun `o` keeps the same reduced shape whether before the present or the past-tense
auxiliary: `mi-o trimite` "he sends it to me" / `mi-a trimis-o` "he sent it to me." This builds
directly on Unit 11's indirect-pronoun and Unit 9's direct-object systems into a combined paradigm.

### The imperative mood — Unit 13, pp. 163–166 — **major grammar point**

A detailed, rule-by-rule presentation of Romanian's command form: (1) the **plural** imperative is
always identical to the 2nd-person-plural present tense (`plecați!` "leave!"), with one irregular
exception (`fiți!` "be!"); (2) the **singular** imperative, for verbs without a direct object, is
usually identical to the 2nd-person-singular present (`taci!` "be silent!", `mergi!` "go!"); (3) for
verbs that *do* take a direct object, the singular imperative instead usually borrows the
**3rd-person-singular present tense** form (`bate-l!` "hit him!", `trimite banii!` "send the
money!") — **except** that all `-a`-infinitive verbs and verbs with 3rd-singular present endings in
`-ește`/`-aște`/`-oară` use the 3rd-singular present as their singular imperative regardless of
whether they have a direct object (`cântă!` "sing!", `coboară!` "get down!"); (4) a handful of
verbs have **irregular, suppletive imperative forms** distinct from any present-tense form: `a
zice` → `zi!`, `a face` → `fa!`, `a lua` → `ia!`, `a veni` → `vino!`, `a da` → `dă!`; (5) **negative**
imperatives use a completely different construction from positive ones: plural negative = plain
2nd-plural present preceded by `nu` (`nu plecați!`), but **singular negative uses the bare
infinitive (minus `a`) preceded by `nu`**, never the present-tense-derived positive form (`nu
pleca!` "don't leave!", not *`nu plecă!`); (6) **reflexive verbs** suffix their object pronoun onto
the imperative (`du-te!` "go!" is irregular; `duceți-vă!`/`uitați-vă!` regular plural; singular
`uită-te!`, often further reduced to `uite!`); (7) in **positive** imperatives, object/dative
pronouns are **suffixed after** the verb (`trimite-i-l!` "send it to him!" — direct-object `-l`
again precedes the dative `-i` as a suffix chain, mirroring Unit 13's own double-clitic rule above,
with `o` triggering loss of a preceding vowel: `invit-o!` not `*invită-o!`); in **negative**
imperatives, the pronoun instead **precedes** the verb as in ordinary sentences (`nu-l trimite!`
"don't send him!", `nu mi-o dați!` "don't give it to me!"). **`să` + subjunctive is also used to
express a milder command**, with the same preceding-pronoun placement as a negative imperative:
`să-i dați ziarul!` "give her the newspaper!"
