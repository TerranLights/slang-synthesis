## Grammar points

### §163 — Reflexive verbs
Two kinds: (a) conjugated with the unstressed accusative pronoun (e.g. *a se plimba* "to take a
walk"); (b) conjugated with the unstressed dative pronoun (e.g. *a-şi aminti* "to remember"). This
accusative-vs-dative split among Romanian reflexives is a genuinely important structural fact,
directly connecting back to the full clitic-pronoun system documented in Lesson XII.

### §164 — Present indicative and past indefinite of *a se plimba* and *a-şi aminti*
Full paradigms given for both verbs in both tenses, demonstrating the accusative-type (*mă plimb, te
plimbi...*) vs. dative-type (*îmi amintesc, îţi aminteşti...*) conjugation patterns side by side.

### §165 — Personal pronouns with reflexive verbs
(a) *Se*/*s'* are the 3rd-person accusative (direct-object) reflexive clitics. (b) *Îşi*/*şi* are the
3rd-person dative (indirect-object) reflexive clitics. (c) The stressed reflexive *sine* patterns
exactly like *mine*/*tine* as the object of a preposition (*Îl ia cu sine la târg* "He took him with
him to market"). (d) The accusative *pe sine* and dative *şie*/*şiesi* are used specifically to add
emphasis to the reflexive pronoun (*Se vede pe sine în locul ei* "He sees himself in her place"). (e)
Reflexive pronouns follow the same positional rules relative to the verb as the other object pronouns
already documented in Lesson XII.

### §166 — Reciprocal use of reflexive pronouns
With two or more persons, 3rd-person reflexive pronouns can carry a reciprocal ("each other") sense,
e.g. *El şi ea se iubeau* "He and she loved each other." To emphasize reciprocity explicitly, Romanian
adds *unul pe altul*, *unul altuia*, or *unul la altul* "one another" (plus feminine equivalents) —
e.g. *Nu-şi ziceau unul altuia nici o vorbă* "They didn't say a single word to one another."

### §167 — Impersonal use of reflexive
The 3rd-singular reflexive with the accusative clitic is frequently used impersonally, translatable
with English "one," "they," "you," or "people" as subject, e.g. *Se zice că a murit* "They say he
died." A genuinely important construction for understanding how Romanian expresses generic/impersonal
statements — directly relevant to register analysis, since impersonal-*se* constructions are a
common source of fixed colloquial phrases cross-linguistically.

### §168 — Passive voice
Formed with *a fi* "to be" plus the past participle of the main verb, e.g. *Casa a fost clădită de un
vecin al nostru* "The house was built by a neighbor of ours." Critically, the book flags that this
literal passive construction **is used rarely in Romanian** — the language strongly prefers two
alternative strategies instead: (a) active voice with unstressed accusative pronouns, functionally
equivalent to an English passive, e.g. *Băiatul m'a condus* "The boy led me" = "I was led by the
boy"; (b) the reflexive verb, especially where there's no clear real subject, e.g. *Aici se vorbeşte
franţuzeşte* "French is spoken here" (lit. "Here speaks-itself French"). This is a genuinely important
typological fact for later mechanics analysis — Romanian's avoidance of the literal passive in favor
of impersonal-reflexive and object-fronted-active constructions is exactly the kind of structural
preference that shapes how colloquial/slang registers phrase things. The passive past participle
agrees in gender/number with the subject (*Creionul a fost ascuţit*, *Casa a fost clădită*).
