## Grammar points

### Plural formation by noun-ending class — Unit 5, pp. 56–57

Expands the plural system sketched in Unit 2 into a fuller set of ending-based rules. Feminine
nouns ending in **-ă** replace it with **-e** (`portocală`→`portocale`) or, for a subset, **-i**
(`piersică`→`piersici`, `gară`→`gări` — the source doesn't give a rule distinguishing which -ă nouns
take -e vs. -i, just flags both patterns exist). Feminine nouns in **-e** take **-i**
(`roșie`→`roșii`); those in **-ură** take **-uri** (`prăjitură`→`prăjituri`); those in **-ie** take
**-ii** (`hârtie`→`hârtii`); those in **-ea** take **-ele** (`cafea`→`cafele`). Neuter nouns ending
in a consonant or **-i** take **-uri** (`chibrit`→`chibrituri`, `vin`→`vinuri`) or, for some,
**-e** (`pahar`→`pahare`); those in **-ou** take **-ri** (`metrou`→`metrouri`); those in **-iu**
take **-ii** (`fotoliu`→`fotolii`). **All masculine nouns pluralize in -i** regardless of their
singular ending — the one gender with a uniform rule (`munte`→`munți`, `membru`→`membri`,
`leu`→`lei`).

### Additive/continuative adverbs și, mai, tot — Unit 5, pp. 58–59

Three short adverbs carry overlapping "also/still/in addition" senses but are **not
interchangeable**, and their position diverges from English equivalents. `și` = "also, too" (and
also doubles as the conjunction "and" seen since Unit 1): `și eu aș lua tot o cafea` "I too would
also like a coffee." `mai` = "still, in addition, another": `mai vrei o cafea?` "do you want another
coffee?", `Irina mai doarme` "Irina is still asleep." `tot` = "also, still" with a continuative
sense distinct from `mai`: `George este tot la gară` "George is still at the station," `Ana tot
vorbește` "Ana keeps talking." The source's own minimal-pair-style dialogue line stacks two of them:
`și eu aș lua tot o cafea` ("I too would also like a coffee") — `și` scopes over "I" (also-me), `tot`
scopes over the drink choice (same-as-before).

### Conditional mood — Unit 5, pp. 58–60

A new periphrastic mood, the **conditional** ("I would..."), built from a dedicated set of auxiliary
forms + the bare infinitive stem (infinitive minus the `a` particle): `aș vrea` "I would want/like,"
`ai vrea`, `ar vrea`, `am vrea`, `ați vrea`, `ar vrea` (3rd pl. identical to 3rd sg.); likewise `aș
merge`/`ai merge`/`ar merge`/etc. "I would go." The conditional is most often paired with a `dacă`
("if") clause — the source notes explicitly that **Romanian's "if" clause does not shift to a past
tense the way English does**: `dacă am avea bani, am merge la mare` "if we had enough money, we
would go to the seaside" uses the *conditional* in both clauses, not a past tense in the "if" clause
as English "if we had" might suggest.

### Round-trip/circuit tickets and regional tourism vocabulary — Unit 5, pp. 60–62

Minor but concrete cultural-travel detail: `bilet (în) circuit` ("circuit/round ticket") is
explained in-dialogue as a ticket "valid for the whole of your journey" that lets the traveler
interrupt the trip at any point along a pre-set route — contrasted with a simple point-to-point
ticket. The dialogue's geography (Mamaia on the coast, the Danube Delta, Sinaia in the mountains)
reflects the three-way seaside/delta/mountain holiday pattern that recurs across this book's travel
dialogues.
