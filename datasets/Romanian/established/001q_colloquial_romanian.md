## Grammar points

### Past participles as adjectives and as nouns — Unit 19, pp. 245–246

Beyond their role in the compound past/passive (Units 3, 12), past participles function as ordinary
**adjectives agreeing with the noun** (`România este situată în sud-estul Europei` "Romania is
situated..." — already seen, but now named explicitly as this same participle-as-adjective
mechanism) and can be **substantivized as neuter nouns**: `fumatul interzis` "smoking prohibited"
(lit. "the-prohibited smoking"), `un tuns` "a haircut" (from `a tunde` "to cut hair"). **Past
participle + `de`** renders an English infinitive-of-purpose sense: `am multe lucruri de făcut` "I
have many things to do," `el are niște bani de schimbat` "he has some money to change" — this `de`
+ participle construction is distinct from the `ca să` purpose-clause (Unit 11) and from the
ordinary past-participle-as-adjective use.

### Presumptive statements ("may/might [have]") — Unit 19, p. 246

"May/might come" (present-oriented uncertainty) uses the **conditional of `a se putea`** ("to be
possible") + `să` + subjunctive: `s-ar putea să vină` "he/she might come." For **past** presumptive
sense ("might have come," "can he have come?"), Romanian instead uses the invariant phrase **`o fi`**
+ past participle: `o fi venit cineva azi?` "might someone have come today?," `el o fi vândut
mașina` "he might have sold the car." The same past-presumptive meaning can alternatively be
conveyed with the adverb **`oare`** (a general "I wonder" marker) plus an ordinary compound-past
verb: `oare nu ne-am mai întâlnit undeva?` "haven't we met somewhere before, I wonder?" — two
structurally distinct ways (`o fi` + participle vs. `oare` + ordinary past) to express the same
uncertain-past meaning.

### Result clauses with `așa de`/`atât de`...`încât` — Unit 19, p. 247

"So... that" is built from **`așa de`** or **`atât de`** (both ≈ "so") modifying the
adjective/adverb, with **`încât`** introducing the result clause: `George a plecat așa de repede
încât și-a uitat portofelul acasă` "George left so quickly that he forgot his wallet at home." The
related forms `atât`/`cât` ("as much/as") appear in a cluster of correlative constructions: `cu
cât... atât` ("the more... the more," `cu cât vorbea el mai repede, eu atât înțelegeam mai puțin`
"the faster he spoke, the less I understood"); `pe cât... pe atât` ("as much... as," `pe cât era de
frumoasă, pe atât era de proastă` "she was as silly as she was beautiful"); `după cât` ("as far as,"
`după cât știu...` "as far as I know...").

### Temporal conjunctions — Unit 19, pp. 248–249

Extends Unit 6's `până`-family with several more precise temporal subordinators: `ori de câte ori`
("whenever," lit. "or of however-many times," `ori de câte ori voia să vorbească cu ea` "whenever he
wanted to speak to her"); `până ce`/`până când` (near-synonyms of plain `până`, "until"); `până nu`
(used when the main clause is negative, or marking a deadline-before-which — "before," reinforcing
Unit 6's own rule); `până sa` ("by the time that," `până să vină pompierii, focul se stinsese` "by
the time the firemen came, the fire had gone out" — the "by the time" sense specifically paired
here with the pluperfect `se stinsese`).

### `de mult`/`de puțin` with present/imperfect vs. compound past/pluperfect: a completion-based split — Unit 19, p. 249

**Sharpens the rule from Units 7, 14, and 17.** `de mult`/`de puțin timp` + present or imperfect
tense is used specifically when **the action is still ongoing at the time of speaking**: `îl cunosc
pe Marian de trei luni` "I have known Marian for three months" (still know him now). **Once the
action is completed/no longer ongoing, the compound past or pluperfect is used instead**, even
though the English translation still looks continuous: `a ieșit la pensie de mult` "he retired a
long time ago" (compound past, not present/imperfect, because retirement-as-an-event is complete) /
`ieșise la pensie de mult` "he had long retired" (pluperfect, same completion logic one step further
back). This is the clearest statement yet in the book of exactly what conditions the present/
imperfect-for-"have been doing" substitution rule versus the ordinary past tenses.
