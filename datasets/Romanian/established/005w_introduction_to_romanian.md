## Grammar points

### §189 — Infinitive
(a) Almost always introduced by the preposition *a*, except after *a putea* "to be able" and (less
consistently) *a şti* "to know," and after *a avea* "to have" when followed by a pronoun or
pronominal adverb (*Are cui semăna* "He has someone to resemble"). (b) *Nu, cam, mai, tot*, and the
unstressed personal pronouns are usually placed between *a* and the infinitive. (c) *De* is inserted
before *a* with an infinitive after verbs of cessation (*Nu se oprea de a călători* "He did not stop
travelling"). (d) *De* is inserted before *a* and the infinitive after adjectives like *bun, gata,
vrednic*. (e) *De* is inserted before an infinitive depending on a noun. (f) The infinitive functions
as a noun via the *-re* suffix (the "long infinitive"), e.g. *schimbare* "change," *facere* "making,
doing," *simţire* "feeling," *avere* "fortune," *fire* "nature, way of being" — this long infinitive
retains genuine verbal force specifically in inverted conditional exclamations with the subjunctive/
conditional, e.g. *Dare-ar Dumnezeu!* "Would to God!" (g) Functions as the subject of a sentence. (h)
Functions as direct or indirect object. (i) When object of another verb, the infinitive is often
replaced by the subjunctive instead (*Spune-i să cânte* "Tell him to sing") — directly consistent
with the subjunctive's established role (Lesson XI) as the functional replacement for the infinitive
in most Romanian complement constructions; this lesson makes explicit that the infinitive itself still
exists and is used, but the subjunctive is the more natural/default choice in many of the same
contexts.

### §190 — Perfect infinitive
Formed with *a fi* plus the past participle of the main verb: *a fi schimbat* "to have changed," *a fi
avut* "to have had," *a fi fost* "to have been," etc.

### §191 — Supine (continued from Lesson XIII)
Three further use-categories, extending Lesson XIII §141's introduction: (a) after the auxiliaries *a
avea* and *a fi* to indicate obligation or anticipated action (*Am de schimbat cartea* "I have to
change the book," *E de câştigat un premiu* "There is a prize to be won") — note the structural
parallel to the *avea*-future grammaticalization already documented in Lesson XVII. (b) after
adjectives (*Case bune de locuit* "Houses good to live in"). (c) after verbs and the prepositions *de,
din, după, la, pe, pentru* (*Am isprăvit de mâncat* "I finished eating," *Umblau după furat* "They
were seeking to rob").

### §192 — Presumptive
A distinct named mood, formed with invariable *o fi* plus the present participle (gerund) of the main
verb, expressing an uncertain action suspected only by the speaker, e.g. *O fi având o boală gravă*
"He must be having a serious disease." This is the formal naming and completion of the epistemic *o
fi*-plus-participle construction first introduced as a future-tense extension in Lesson XVII §158 —
worth flagging that Romanian apparently treats this construction as significant enough to warrant its
own mood category rather than just a marked use of the future, reinforcing how central evidential/
epistemic marking is to this verb system (also seen in the conditional's hearsay use, Lesson XXI
§178b.2).

### §193–194 — Pronouns of identity (emphatic "self" pronouns)
Full N./G./D./A. declension given for all persons/numbers of the *însumi* "myself" family, built by
adding an identity-marking suffix to the stressed personal pronoun paradigm (*eu → eu însumi*, etc.).
Four use-cases: (a) before a noun, for emphasis (*Cucerit de înşişi soldaţii lui* "Conquered by his
very soldiers"). (b) after a stressed or subject pronoun. (c) the present-day tendency is to use only
the 3rd-person singular masculine/feminine forms (*însuşi, însăşi*) for all persons and numbers — yet
another documented live simplification in the pronoun system, alongside the possessive-article and
demonstrative-pronoun simplifications already flagged in earlier lessons. (d) *chiar* "even" and
*singur* "alone" are often used instead of the *însumi*-family forms for the same emphatic effect.
