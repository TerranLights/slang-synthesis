## Grammar points

### §183–184 — Preterite (simple past)
Formed from the past participle minus its final *-t*, plus endings *-i, -şi, —, -răm, -răţi, -ră*
(the 3rd singular ending is zero); stress stays fixed on the same syllable as in the participle. Full
regular paradigms given for *a schimba, a putea, a face, a simţi*. Irregular preterites given for *a
avea, a fi* (which has two parallel full series, *fui...* and *fusei...*), *a da*, *a sta*.

### §185 — Use of preterite
Functions as in other Romance languages — simple past action/state without reference to repetition or
continuity (*De ce făcură asta?* "Why did they do this?"). Critically, the book notes that **the first
person singular and the second-person forms (both numbers) are almost never used in actual
conversation** — only the 3rd singular, 1st plural, and 3rd plural preterite forms are commonly heard.
This is a genuinely important register/usage-frequency fact, directly relevant to understanding which
verb-tense morphology is actually productive in spoken Romanian versus merely textbook-complete —
worth flagging prominently for later mechanics work, since a form that exists in the paradigm but is
functionally dead in 1st/2nd person is exactly the kind of detail generic grammar summaries miss.

### §186–187 — Pluperfect indicative
Formed from the past participle minus *-t*, plus endings *-sem, -seşi, -se, -sem, -seţi, -se*
(footnote: plural forms alternatively take *-serăm, -serăţi, -seră*). Full paradigms given for
*a schimba, a avea, a fi, a da, a sta*, plus *a merge* "to walk/go" introduced specifically for this
paradigm demonstration. Used for a more remote past than the preterite (*Aflasem că a murit* "I had
heard that he died"), but the same time reference can also be expressed by either the preterite or the
past indefinite (Lesson XIII) — the pluperfect indicative is somewhat more common in conversation than
the preterite, consistent with §185's note about the preterite's restricted conversational range.

### §188 — Declension of *cât*
Full masculine/feminine, singular/plural paradigm for the interrogative/exclamatory adjective *cât*
"how much, how many" (G.-D. *cătui(a)/cătei(a)/câtor(a)*, footnote: possessive article used with the
genitive) — distinct from (though related to) the indefinite adjective *câtva* already documented in
Lesson XX §171.
