## Grammar points

### §88(h) — Nouns with two plural forms
A small irregular class has two valid plurals with no meaning distinction: aripă (aripi or aripe),
boală (boale or boli), cămin (cămine or căminuri), grup (grupe or grupuri), roată (roate or roţi),
stradă (strade or străzi), şcoală (şcoale or şcoli), tufiş (tufişe or tufişuri).

### §88 — Plural forms that shift meaning
A notable and lexically rich set of nouns change meaning between singular and plural — directly
relevant to later slang/semantic-drift analysis, since this is exactly the kind of lexicalized
number-contrast that can seed figurative extensions:
- **Masculine**: argint "silver" → arginţi "pieces of silver"; antic "antique" → Anticii "the
  Ancients"; ban "mil/cent" → bani "money"; lapte "milk" → lapţi "fish roe" / lăpturi "milk foods";
  ochiu "eye" → ochi "eyes" but ochiuri "fried/poached eggs".
- **Feminine**: bucată "piece" → bucăţi "pieces" but bucate "foods"; cadră "picture" → cadre
  "pictures" but cadruri "army ranks" (cadru, m., = picture frame); carne "meat" → cărni "meats" but
  cărnuri "kinds of meat"; coadă "tail" → coadele "pigtails (girl's)" / cozile "animals' tails"; iarbă
  "grass" → ierbi "grasses" but ierburi "herbs"; pânză "linen" → pânze "linens" but pânzeturi "things
  made of linen"; pară "mil" → parale "money"; răutate "badness" → răutăţi "badnesses" and also
  "calamities".
- **Mixed**: ac "needle" → ace "needles" but also "piercing pains"; cot "elbow" → coate "elbows" but
  coţi "a cloth measure" / coturi "river bends"; frig "cold" → friguri "colds" and "fever"; mijloc
  "middle" → mijlocuri "middle" but mijloace "means (money)"; nimic "nothing" → nimicuri "trifles";
  oţel "steel" → oţele "gun plates"; raport "written report" → raporturi "written reports" but
  rapoarte "relations (between people/things)"; schimb "exchange, change" → schimburi "changes of
  linen." A footnote adds a broader present-day drift: -i/-ĭ plurals are spreading to feminine nouns,
  -e plurals to mixed nouns, and -uri plurals to abstract/material nouns.

### §89–§91 — Declension system: five cases, N-A and G-D syncretism
Nouns, adjectives, articles, and pronouns decline for gender, number, and case. **Five cases**:
nominative, accusative, genitive, dative, vocative. Critically for this language's typology: **with
noted exceptions, nominative and accusative are formally identical in all genders/numbers, and
genitive and dative are likewise identical** — i.e. the five-case system collapses, in the default
pattern, into a two-way N-A/G-D syncretism (the book abbreviates these as N.-A. and G.-D.
throughout). Vocative is treated separately and omitted from the regular declension tables, with its
own endings given briefly: masc. sg. vocative adds -(u)le or -e to the undeclined form (domn→
domnule!, vecin→vecine!); masc. pl. vocative adds -lor (domni→domnilor!); fem. sg. vocative is either
identical to the undeclined form (soacră!) or drops -ă and adds -o (soacro!); fem. pl. vocative is
generally identical to the plural G.-D. form (femeilor!).

### §92 — Definite article, full declension paradigm
Masculine singular (consonant-final stems, e.g. domn): N.-A. -(u)l (domnul), G.-D. -lui (domnului).
Masculine plural: N.-A. -i (domnii), G.-D. -lor (domnilor). Masculine singular (-e stems, e.g.
frate): N.-A. -le (fratele), G.-D. -lui (fratelui); plural -i/-lor (fraţii/fraţilor). Feminine
singular (casă): N.-A. -a (casa), G.-D. -ei (casei). Feminine plural (case): N.-A. -le (casele),
G.-D. -lor (caselor). This table is the backbone of the postposed-article system introduced in §76
and is the first place the G.-D. syncretism is shown operating with the article attached.
