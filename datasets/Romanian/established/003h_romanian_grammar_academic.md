## Grammar points

### The verb's full grammatical dimensions (§4.1.1)

Romanian verbs mark mood (5 personal: indicative, imperative, subjunctive, optative-conditional,
presumptive; 4 non-personal: infinitive, participle, gerund, supine — repeating the ch.2 overview, now
with the full worked a cânta paradigm reproduced schematically as in file `003`), tense (indicative
alone has 8, split into simple one-word tenses (present, imperfect, simple perfect, pluperfect
indicative) and compound tenses built from an auxiliary + infinitive/past-participle), person, number,
gender (only for the passive past participle and the attributive gerund), and voice (active, passive,
reflexive). A set of three infixes (-ez-/-eaz-, -esc-/-eşt-, -ăsc-/-ăşt-) appears only in the 1st and
4th conjugations. Two independent parts of speech are repurposed as pure verbal morphemes: the
preposition `a` marks the infinitive, and the conjunction `să` marks the subjunctive (and the
subjunctive-based futures).

### Four conjugation classes, by infinitive ending (§4.1.2)

Romanian's four conjugations are identified purely by the infinitive's final vowel(s): 1st = -a (a
cânta, a lucra, a întârzia, a parca), 2nd = -ea (a vedea, a putea), 3rd = -e (a merge, a face, a umple,
a scrie), 4th = -i or -î (a fugi, a veni, a opri, a locui / a hotărî, a coborî). The -a and -i
conjugations are explicitly noted as the two largest/most productive classes.

### The infinitive: short vs. long form, past tense, negation (§4.1.3)

The "short infinitive" (a + bare stem) is the dictionary citation form and also feeds compound-tense
formation elsewhere in the paradigm (future 1, present optative-conditional, present presumptive all
use the short infinitive minus the `a`). A separate "long infinitive"/verbal noun form (ending in -are,
-ere, -ire, -âre) has fully nominalized as an ordinary feminine noun (cântare, vedere, pierdere, iubire,
hotărâre) — this is the same deverbal-noun-suffix pattern already documented from the noun-ending
table in file `003b`. The infinitive has its own rarely-used past tense (a fi + past participle: a fi
cântat "having sung"), and negates by inserting `nu` directly after the particle `a` (a nu cânta "not
to sing").

### The past participle: four suffix classes, with genuine unpredictability in the -e conjugation (§4.1.4)

Each conjugation has a (mostly) predictable past-participle suffix replacing the infinitive ending: -a
→ -at, -ea → -ut, -i → -it, -î → -ât. The 3rd conjugation (-e infinitives) is the outlier: its past
participle can take -ut, -s, or -t with **no reliable formal rule to predict which** — the source gives
a direct minimal contrast (a crede → crezut but a râde → râs) to make exactly this point. Before -s/-t
suffixes the stem's final consonant is generally dropped (a spune → spus, a sparge → spart), with a
cânta's irregular a rupe → rupt as an explicit exception that keeps the consonant. a fi's past
participle, fost, is fully irregular. Negation of the past participle (unlike every finite mood) is
derivational rather than periphrastic: prefix ne- (cunoscut → necunoscut), and some verbs simply lack a
negative past participle at all; where it exists, the negative form is independent-use only and cannot
itself feed further compound tenses. Past participles — affirmative or negative — can also function as
ordinary adjectives (agreeing, declining, taking degrees of comparison), and are the standard means of
forming the passive voice, where they take the subject's gender and number.

### Auxiliary verbs: a avea, a fi, a voi, and a closed set of other elements (§4.1.5)

A large, fully worked person×number auxiliary-conjugation table underlies most compound tenses/moods:
compound perfect (am/ai/a/am/aţi/au — from a avea), future 1 (voi/vei/va/vom/veţi/vor — from a voi),
future 2 (invariant o (să) for every person), future 3 (am/ai/are/avem/aveţi/au (să) — a different,
avea-based auxiliary set than future 1's voi-series), future perfect (voi fi.../ — future-1 auxiliaries
+ fi), future-in-the-past (aveam/aveai/avea/aveam/aveaţi/aveau (să) — imperfect of a avea), past
subjunctive ((să) fi, invariant across person), present/past conditional (aş/ai/ar/am/aţi/ar, +fi for
past), present/past presumptive (oi/oi/o/om/oţi/or, +fi for past). The source highlights genuine
structural economy and ambiguity here: several auxiliary forms are reused verbatim across multiple
mood/tense cells (fi marks every past-tense compound across subjunctive, conditional, presumptive, the
indicative future perfect, and the past infinitive; the future-1 auxiliary set doubles as the future-
perfect auxiliary set; the present-conditional and present-presumptive auxiliary sets each double for
their own past tense). Even within a single tense, identical auxiliary forms recur across different
persons (am for both 1sg and 1pl of the compound perfect; o uniformly across every person of future 2;
ar for both 3sg and 3pl of the conditional; oi for both 1sg and 2sg of the presumptive) — and several
auxiliary strings (am, ai, aţi, au, o) are flagged as genuinely homonymous *across different moods and
tenses entirely*, disambiguable only by what follows them in the sentence (am as compound-perfect 1st
person vs. am as future-3 1st-singular vs. am as conditional-present 1st-plural, all illustrated with
distinct worked example sentences by the source).

### Infixes -ez-/-eaz-, -esc-/-eşt-, -ăsc-/-ăşt- (§4.1.6)

A subset of 1st-conjugation (-ez-/-eaz-) and 4th-conjugation (-esc-/-eşt- for -i verbs, -ăsc-/-ăşt- for
-î verbs) verbs insert one of these infixes between stem and ending, specifically in the singular (all
persons) and 3rd-person plural of the present indicative, future 2, future 3, present subjunctive, and
the 2nd-singular affirmative imperative — i.e., the same five mood/tense cells across the board,
establishing infix presence/absence as a genuinely verb-class-defining property rather than a
free-floating irregularity. (This directly connects to Model-1-vs-Model-2 conjugation subtypes to be
detailed in the forthcoming present-indicative chapter.)

### Verbal homonyms: same form, different person/tense, disambiguated only by context (§4.1.7.1)

Because Romanian regularly omits the subject pronoun, several person/tense combinations collapse onto
identical surface forms within the *same* conjugation class, and the source catalogs each pattern
precisely: 1st-conjugation 3sg = 3pl present indicative (cântă = "he/she sings" or "they sing"); 1st-
conjugation (vowel-stem, no infix) 1sg = 2sg present indicative (întârzii "I am late" = "you are late");
1st-conjugation 2pl present = 2pl imperfect indicative (cântaţi "you sing" = "you were singing"); 1st-
conjugation 2sg imperfect = 1sg simple perfect (cântai "you were singing" = "I sang"); 2nd/3rd/4th-
conjugation 1sg present = 3pl present (văd "I see" = "they see," with the infixed 4th-conjugation
subtype as an explicit exception); 4th-conjugation vowel-stem (no infix) 3sg = 3pl present (coboară
"he/she climbs down" = "they climb down"); 4th-conjugation vowel-stem 1sg = 2sg present (bântui "I
haunt" = "you haunt," illustrated by the source with a small ghost-themed joke dialogue); and, across
*every* conjugation without exception, 1sg imperfect = 1pl imperfect (cântam "I was singing" = "we were
singing") and 3sg present subjunctive = 3pl present subjunctive (să cânte "he/she sing" = "they sing").

### Verbal homographs: identical spelling, different pronunciation (stress position) (§4.1.7.2)

A distinct phenomenon from homonymy: these forms are written identically but disambiguated by stress
placement, which print does not normally mark. 1st-conjugation (no infix) 3sg/3pl present (stem-stressed)
vs. 3sg simple perfect (final-vowel-stressed) — cântă/cântă — with an explicit noted exception when the
stem vowel is -a, since stressed -a and unstressed -a surface as different letters (-ă) entirely,
removing the ambiguity (îmbracă present vs. îmbrăcă simple perfect — not homographic at all). Parallel
patterns hold for 3rd-conjugation consonant+r/l-stem verbs (1sg present vs. 3sg simple perfect: umplu/
umplu) and 4th-conjugation -i verbs without the infix (2sg present vs. 3sg simple perfect: fugi/fugi;
and, within the imperative alone, 2sg affirmative vs. 2sg negative: fugi!/nu fugi!, again
distinguished only by stress).

### Negation: particle nu, with mood-specific placement and a true form-change only in the imperative (§4.1.8)

`nu` precedes the verb form in every mood/tense, but when the mood embeds a grammaticalized
conjunction/preposition (să for the subjunctive, a for the infinitive), `nu` is inserted *after* that
element rather than before the whole unit (să nu cânt, not nu să cânt). Vowel-initial verb/auxiliary
forms (beginning with a- or o-) optionally elide nu's final -u (n-am cântat), exactly paralleling the
elision facts already documented for personal-pronoun clitics in file `003e`. The singular imperative
is the sole mood/tense that cannot simply prepend nu to its ordinary form — its negative is built
instead from nu + the infinitive (nu cânta!). Past participle and gerund negation remain derivational
(ne- prefix), as already noted in §4.1.4.

### The interrogative: intonation only, with subject-verb inversion after question words (§4.1.9)

Romanian has no dedicated interrogative verb form or particle — a statement becomes a question purely
through rising intonation (Voi aveţi o casă în Bucureşti. → Voi aveţi o casă în Bucureşti?). When the
question is instead introduced by an interrogative pronoun or adverb (cine, ce, care, cum, cât, unde,
când, etc.), the subject moves to follow the verb (Andrei are o casă. → Ce are Andrei?).
