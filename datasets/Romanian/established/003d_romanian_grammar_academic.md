## Grammar points

### Adjectival agreement: post-nominal by default, four/three/two/one-form classes (§3.3.1)

Descriptive adjectives normally follow their noun and always agree in gender, number, and case; a
neuter-modifying adjective is masculine in the singular and feminine in the plural, mirroring the
noun's own neuter behavior. Romanian adjectives fall into four size classes by how many distinct
surface forms they have:

- **Four-form** (masc.sg / fem.sg / masc.pl / fem.-neut.pl, e.g. bun/bună/buni/bune) — the majority
  pattern, split by whether the masculine singular ends in a consonant (+ -ă/-i/-e) or in -u
  (→ -ă/-i/-e, with possible stem mutation). greu and rău are irregular four-form adjectives.
- **Three-form** — two subtypes collapse one pair of forms: (a) masc.pl = fem.pl for adjectives ending
  in -c/-g (mic, with explicit 4-form exceptions sărac, harnic, pribeag), -esc (românesc), -iu (auriu),
  and the irregular nou; (b) fem.sg = fem.pl for -tor adjectives (fermecător).
- **Two-form** — masc.sg = fem.sg AND masc.pl = fem.pl for -e-ending adjectives (mare, pl. mari for
  every gender); masc.sg = masc.pl for -i/-ui-ending adjectives (vechi); vioi is a distinct two-form
  pattern where instead masc.sg=masc.pl and fem.sg=fem.pl independently (vioi/vioaie).
- **One-form** (invariable across gender and number) — adverbial-origin adjectives (cumsecade) and a
  closed set of borrowed color terms (roz, kaki, gri, bleu, maro) plus a few Latinate/learned adjectives
  (eficace).

### Pre-nominal adjective placement: emphasis, and some fixed exceptions (§3.3.2)

Adjective-before-noun order is marked, not default, and signals heightened emotional involvement —
typically in exclamations (Ce casă frumoasă! ~ Frumoasă casă! "What a beautiful house!"). Word-order
inversion has systematic knock-on effects on articles: the indefinite article moves to precede the
fronted adjective (o actriţă remarcabilă → o remarcabilă actriţă); the definite article, since it is
enclitic, physically relocates onto the fronted adjective (actriţa excepţională → excepţionala
actriţă); and when a possessive pronominal adjective is also present, the full order noun+possessive →
adjective+possessive+noun shifts, with the article landing on the now-initial descriptive adjective.
Two adjective subclasses break the general freedom of word order: invariable color terms (roz, bej,
kaki) can *never* precede their noun, while biet "poor/unhappy" can *only* ever precede its noun.

### Adjectival declension mirrors the noun, with one exception (§3.3.3)

As with nouns (ch. 3.1), only feminine adjectives have a dedicated genitive-dative singular form
(identical to their own nominative plural). When a noun+adjective (or adjective+noun) group takes the
*indefinite* article, the article precedes the entire group regardless of word order; when it takes the
*definite* article, the article attaches to whichever element is first in the actual linear order
(noun, if post-nominal; the fronted adjective, if pre-nominal) — a full worked paradigm table across
both word orders and all four cases/both numbers is given in the source for actor talentat / roman
minunat / actriţă talentată.

### Comparative degree: superiority, equality, inferiority (§3.3.4.1)

Three comparative constructions, each a fixed adverbial-frame + adjective + comparative-preposition
template, with the complement noun/pronoun always in the accusative:
- **Superiority**: `mai` + adjective + `decât`/`ca` (+ accusative). `ca` is explicitly flagged by the
  source as more frequent than `decât` in colloquial speech — a genuine register distinction between
  two near-synonymous comparative markers, worth tracking into the slang/colloquial corpus. `mai` can
  itself be intensified by mult, cu mult, incomparabil, infinit.
- **Equality**: `tot aşa de` / `tot atât de` / `la fel de` + adjective + `ca`/`ca şi` (+ accusative).
- **Inferiority**: `mai puţin` + adjective + `decât`/`ca` (+ accusative), similarly intensifiable by
  mult/cu mult.

### Superlative degree: relative superiority/inferiority and absolute (§3.3.4.2)

The **relative superlative** reuses the demonstrative/adjectival article (cel/cea/cei/cele, see §3.2.2)
directly before the invariable `mai` (superiority) or `mai puţin` (inferiority) + adjective; cel/cea/
cei/cele agrees with the modified noun's gender/number. Word order again interacts with the definite
article: post-nominal placement requires the noun to carry the definite article (romanul cel mai
interesant), while pre-nominal placement of the whole cel-mai-adjective group causes the noun to lose
its article entirely (cel mai interesant roman) — both forms gloss identically in English but are
structurally distinct. The superlative's complement is marked by `dintre` when plural ("among the...",
denoting the comparison set) or `din` when singular ("in the...", denoting a location/group). The
**absolute superlative** simply intensifies the bare adjective with a preceding adverb; `foarte` "very"
is the neutral default, while `tare` is explicitly flagged by the source as colloquial-register, and
`extrem de` / `cât se poate de` are flagged as "emotionally marked" alternatives — three more explicit,
source-marked register distinctions within what looks like a single semantic slot.

### A closed set of non-comparable adjectives (§3.3.4.3)

A fixed list of adjectives (superior, inferior, prim, ultim, major, minor, perfect, desăvârşit,
anterior, ulterior, asemenea, optim, unic, suprem) cannot themselves take comparative or superlative
marking, since their lexical meaning is already inherently relative/absolute.
