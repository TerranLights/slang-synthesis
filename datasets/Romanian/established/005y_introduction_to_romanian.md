# Romanian — *Introduction to Romanian* (George O. Seiver, 1953) — Appendices I–V (Verb Paradigm Reference)

Continuation of files 005–005x. Covers printed pages 173–195 (DJVU 187–209): **Appendix I**
("Flexional Changes in Verbs," pp.173–176), **Appendix II** ("Conjugation of Regular Verbs," full
paradigm tables for *a schimba/a lucra, a putea, a face, a simţi/a vorbi/a scoborî/a hotărî*, across
every tense and mood, pp.177–181), **Appendix III** ("Auxiliary Verbs," full paradigms for *a avea*
and *a fi*, pp.182–184), **Appendix IV** ("Conjugation of Reflexive Verbs," full paradigms for *a se
plimba* and *a-şi aminti*, pp.185–188), and **Appendix V** (full paradigms for nine more irregular
verbs — *a bea, a da, a lua, a mânca, a rămânea, a scrie, a sta, a şti, a usca, a vrea, a voi, a
vroi* — pp.189–195). No marginalia; single-column print; tables throughout.

## Treatment note (why this file is a condensed summary, not a row-by-row transcription)

These five appendices are **pure paradigm-reference consolidation**, not new grammar content. Every
tense/mood form tabulated here (present, imperfect, past indefinite, preterite, pluperfect, future,
future anterior, present/past conditional, present/perfect subjunctive, imperative, gerund, past
participle) for every verb shown was already individually documented — with its governing rule
explained in prose — across Lessons VII, XI, XIII, XIV, XVII, XIX, XX, XXI, and XXIII of this same
book (005–005w). The appendices exist in the source purely so a student can look up a complete
paradigm in one place without flipping between lessons; they introduce no rule, construction, or
vocabulary not already captured. Per this project's coverage rule ("skip repeated drills/material that
doesn't introduce new vocabulary or grammar beyond what's already captured"), this file summarizes
the appendices' scope, flags the small number of genuinely new data points they contain, and does not
reproduce the paradigm tables cell-by-cell (which would be hundreds of near-duplicate rows of forms
already present in prior files' grammar-point prose).

## New data points found in the appendices (not previously captured)

- **Appendix I** (flexional-change rules governing stem alternations across all four conjugations) is
  itself mostly a cross-reference restating the stem-alternation patterns already documented per-verb
  in earlier lessons (the *d/s/t → z/ş/ţ* 2nd-singular shift from §103's footnote pattern; the
  accented-*e*-to-*ea* shift seen with *a începe* and others; the 1st-singular *-u* addition after
  *r/l/i* stems already noted in §103's footnote). It does, however, supply a long, previously-
  uncaptured **closed list of verbs exhibiting each specific alternation** — e.g. the ă→e stem-vowel
  list (*a apăra, a asemăna, a astâmpăra, a căpăta, a cumpăra, a curăţa, a legăna, a lepăda, a număra,
  a răscumpăra, a schiopăta, a semăna, a supăra*) and the *d/g/c/n/t → s* past-participle list (a very
  long roster including *a aduce, a ajunge, a alege, a atinge, a atrage, a conduce, a culege, a
  cuprinde, a curge, a deprinde, a deschide, a distruge, a drege, a duce, a închide, a întinde, a
  întoarce, a înţelege, a învinge, a merge, a ninge, a pătrunde, a plânge, a pretinde, a prinde, a
  produce, a pune, a răde, a răspunde, a recurge, a respinge, a retrage, a scoate, a scrie, a spune,
  a şterge, a stinge, a stoarce, a strânge, a surâde, a trage, a trimite, a ucide, a zice* — and a
  further irregular subset *a coace → copt, a fierbe → fiert, a întrerupe → întrerupt, a rupe → rupt,
  a sparge → spart*). These closed lists are a genuinely useful morphological-pattern resource — worth
  flagging for later mechanics work on which verbs cluster into the same irregular-formation class —
  but are reference data, not new rules, so captured here as a pointer rather than a full vocabulary
  table.
- **Appendix I** also documents two further irregular-stem verbs not given full treatment earlier: *a
  muri* "to die" (stems *mor*/*mur*, stressed/unstressed) and *a veni* "to come" (stems *vin*/*ven*,
  with an alternate 1st-singular *viu* alongside *vin*, imperative *vino*, and two parallel gerund
  forms *venind*/*viind*) — both new, specific irregular paradigms.
- **Appendix III** records that the present indicative of *a fi* "to be" has **four attested
  alternative forms for most persons** — 3rd singular *este*, *e*, or *-i* (enclitic); 1st/3rd plural
  *sunt*, *îs*, *-s* (enclitic), or *sânt* — a genuinely notable register/dialectal-variation data
  point not spelled out this explicitly anywhere in the lesson chapters themselves (Lesson VII's
  original *a fi* paradigm just gave the single standard form). Worth flagging prominently: *îs* and
  the enclitic *-s* in particular read as markedly colloquial variants of Romania's single most
  frequent verb, exactly the kind of high-frequency-verb register variation later slang-mechanics work
  would want to know about.
- **Appendix V** documents three further distinct verbs for "to want/wish" — *a vrea* (the standard
  verb, already seen as an auxiliary-adjacent irregular elsewhere), *a voi* (a more formal/literary
  doublet, fully regular 4th-conjugation-esc pattern: *voesc, voeşti, voieşte...*), and *a vroi* (a
  third, hybrid doublet blending *vrea*'s stem with *voi*'s -esc conjugation, explicitly marked with a
  footnote number suggesting the book itself flags it as the least standard of the three) — a three-
  way near-synonym doublet set worth flagging for register/formality analysis, parallel to the *a
  datora*/*a datori* and *a însura*/*a mărita* doublets already found in Lessons XVI and XXIII.
- **Appendix V** also documents *a usca* "to dry" as a 1st-conjugation verb with a stem-consonant
  alternation (*usuc, usuci, usucă...*) not given dedicated treatment in the lesson chapters.
