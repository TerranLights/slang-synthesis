## Grammar points

### §145 — Interrogative/relative pronouns *cine* and *ce*
*Cine* "who" declines across the full N./G./D./A. paradigm (*cine, al/a/ai/ale cui, cui, pe cine*);
*ce* "what" is invariable. *Ce* also functions as an interrogative adjective.

### §146 — Gender and number for *cine* and *ce*
Since neither shows gender/number on its own, Romanian expresses "the one(s) who" via *cel ce*/*cel
care* (masc. sg.), *cei ce*/*cei care* (masc. pl.), *cea ce*/*cea care* (fem. sg.), *cele ce*/*cele
care* (fem. pl.), with the demonstrative-emphatic variants *acel care*/*acei care*/*acea care*/*acele
care*. G.-D. of *cel* is *celui* (m.), *celei* (f.), *celor* (both plural).

### §147 — Uses of *cine* and *ce*
(a) *Cine* is used only for persons, as subject or object. (b) *Cine* can carry the indefinite sense
"someone" (*Nu-i cine să mă ajute* "There is no one on whom I can call for help"). (c) *Ce* expresses
the relative "what, that which." (d) *Ce* combines with prepositions *de, din, despre, la* to form
questions (*De ce nu vine?* "Why doesn't he come?"). (e) *Ce* generally refers to things or indefinite
actions/ideas. (f) In indirect discourse, *ce* practically always requires a subjunctive verb (*Nu
ştia ce să zică* "He did not know what to say") — consistent with the subjunctive's role as the
default complement-clause mood already documented in Lesson XI.

### §148 — Imperfect indicative
Formed by adding conjugation-specific endings directly to the infinitive stem: 1st conj. *-am, -ai,
-a, -am, -aţi, -au*; 2nd/3rd conj. *-eam, -eai, -ea, -eam, -eaţi, -eau*; 4th conj. consonant-stem
verbs take *-eam* etc. directly, vowel-stem verbs take *-iam, -iai, -ia, -iam, -iaţi, -iau*, and the
r-stem/-î-infinitive subclass (*a hotărî, a scoborî*) instead takes the 1st-conjugation endings
*-am, -ai, -a*, etc. — a genuinely systematic morphophonological conditioning pattern across all four
conjugations, worth flagging for later mechanics analysis alongside the similarly conditioned present-
subjunctive/indicative split from Lesson VII.

### §149 — Imperfect indicative of irregular verbs *a avea, a fi, a da, a sta*
Full paradigms given (captured above as vocabulary): *eram* "I was," *aveam* "I had," *dădeam* "I was
giving," *stăteam* "I was staying." Footnote references the Appendix for further irregular-verb
imperfect forms.

### §150 — Use of imperfect indicative
Expresses continuing, repeated, or habitual past action, and past-tense description — functionally
parallel to the imperfect in other Romance languages. Footnote: can sometimes substitute for the
conditional to express regret for something not done, e.g. *Dacă aveam, îi dădeam* "If I had, I would
give him" (lit. "If I had, I was-giving him") — a genuinely interesting mood-substitution pattern,
distinct from but structurally similar to the past-indefinite-for-future substitution already noted
in Lesson XIII §143. Footnote 2: the imperfect may correspond to three different English renderings
(*I was changing, I used to change, I changed*), all contained in the single form *schimbam*.
