## Grammar points

### §93 — Further uses of the definite article
(a) With substantivized adjectives, especially divine/sacred epithets (Atotputernicul "The All
Powerful," Preacurata "The Immaculata"). (b) With nouns following întreg "entire" or tot "all, each,
every" (Întreg oraşul s'a dus la târg; Toţi oamenii trebue să-şi apere ţara). (c) With nouns in
comparison after ca/cât/cum "as, like" (E atât de tare ca omul de acolo; Săracul nu dă atât de mult
cât bogatul; Cum e fata aşa-i şi băiatul) — a fi "to be" is obligatory with cum in this construction;
no article is used when ca instead means "in the sense of/as a" (Ca lucrător e foarte harnic). (d)
With weekday names when indicating a habitual action on that day (Duminica nu lucrăm "We don't work
on Sundays [as a rule]").

### §94 — Omission of the definite article
(a) When the noun is followed by an unstressed dative pronoun clitic standing in for a possessive
(Glasu-i ascuţit "His sharp voice"). (b) When the short 3rd-sg. present of a fi follows the noun
(Copilu-i bun "The child is good"). A shared footnote notes both constructions rest on the same
phenomenon: the supporting -u's following -l dropping before the semivocalic [j], a usage the book
flags as not recommended by contemporary grammarians even though attested.

### §94(c) — Definite article after prepositions
Generally omitted after a preposition (Şade la masă; Se duce în oraş), but the article IS used after
a preposition when: (1) the noun is modified by an adjective or prepositional phrase (Şade pe
scaunul bun; Şade pe scaunul de lemn); (2) often after cu "with," especially marking a particular/
habitual quality (Se va obicinui cu timpul) or a means of accomplishing something (Deschid uşa cu
cheia) — footnoted that article use after cu is in general optional; (3) with nouns for persons whose
official-ish occupation functions like a proper name (Am văzut pe primarul; M'am dus la doctorul).

### §95 — Declension of the noun: two systems
The Romanian noun inflects in two parallel systems. (a) With the **indefinite** article, case is
carried entirely by the article's own forms, the noun itself staying undeclined — full paradigm
given: masc. N.-A. un domn/nişte domni, G.-D. unui domn/unor domni; fem. N.-A. o doamnă/nişte
doamne, G.-D. unei doamne/unor doamne (footnoted: the feminine G.-D. singular happens to match the
plural form). (b) With the **definite** article, case is carried by the article suffixed to the noun
(§92's paradigm, repeated with domn/doamnă): masc. N.-A. domnul/domnii, G.-D. domnului/domnilor;
fem. N.-A. doamna/doamnele, G.-D. doamnei/doamnelor. From this point the book states it will show
only the definite-article declension by default, since the indefinite system is fully covered by the
article's own forms.

### §96 footnotes — Orthographic double-i convention
A dense but important set of transcription notes, directly relevant to this project's vision-reading
and transliteration concerns: when the plural definite article -i is added to a noun whose own
undeclined plural already ends in -i (domni→domnii, fraţi→fraţii), the grammar marks the second i as
ı (dotless/bold) purely to show the two i's don't form a diphthong — not a separate phoneme. The same
convention applies to nouns in -clu/-cru/-dru/-gru/-tru (metru→metri→metrii) and to nouns in -il with
an i in the root (copil→undeclined pl. copii [kopi]→declined pl. copiii [kopij]; similarly cal→cai→
caii [kaij]). A further footnote: the genitive case translates the English possessive 's (Câinele
fratelui meu or Câinele fraţilor mei, "my brother's dog"/"my brothers' dog") — cross-referenced
forward to Lesson XI, not yet reached in this chunk.

### §96 — Present indicative of irregular verbs a da "to give" and a vrea "to want, to wish"
a da: dau, dai, dă, dăm, daţi, dau. a vrea: vreau, vrei, vrea, vrem, vreţi, vreau. A footnote adds
that a vrea has alternate forms a voi/vroi, conjugated instead like a regular 4th-conjugation (-esc)
verb — cross-referenced to the Appendix (outside this chunk's page range).
