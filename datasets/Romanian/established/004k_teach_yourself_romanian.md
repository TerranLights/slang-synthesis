## Grammar points

### 1. Extended reflexive/impersonal constructions

Building on Units 7, 12, and 16's reflexive/impersonal material: (a) a verb may be made reflexive to
**avoid attributing an action/remark to a specific person** — an impersonal-passive-like strategy
(`Se zvonește că o să mai vină minerii` "It's rumoured that the miners will come again"; `Se vede
că...` "It's obvious that..."; `Se afirmă că...` "It is claimed that..."). (b) A verb often **becomes
reflexive when used without an object**, shifting an active/causative meaning to an
inchoative/passive-like one: `Au stins luminile` "They turned out the lights" vs. `Luminile s-au
stins` "The lights went out"; `Primul ministru a mărit lefurile` "The PM raised wages" vs. `Lefurile
s-au mărit` "Wages have been increased". (c) When **two or more people are simultaneously doer and
object** of an action, a reflexive form is obligatory (true reciprocal): `S-au întâlnit în fața
hotelului` "They met in front of the hotel"; `Radu și Dan se ceartă zilnic` "Radu and Dan quarrel
daily" (lit. "argue each other"). (d) Romanian reflexives frequently stand in for an **English
passive** with no overt agent: `Cheile s-au pierdut` "The keys have been lost"; `Mașina s-a vândut`
"The car has been sold" — pp.190–193.

### 2. Present participle ("-ind"/"-ând" form): "seeing", "writing", "wanting"

Formed by adding an ending to the verb's root, governed by the infinitive's final vowel: verbs in
`-i`, `-ia`, `-ie` take `-ind` (`a scrie→scriind` "writing", `a întârzia→întârziind` "being late", `a
citi→citind` "reading", `a dormi→dormind` "sleeping", `a fi→fiind` "being"); all other verbs take
`-ând` (`a lua→luând`, `a da→dând`, `a cânta→cântând`, `a pune→punând`, `a șterge→ștergând`).
**Reflexive verbs add `-u` to `-ind`/`-ând`** (`a se spăla→spălându-se` "washing (oneself)", `a se
trezi→trezindu-se` "waking up"); this same `-u` is added whenever the participle is directly
followed by `him/us/them` object clitics (`citindu-l` "reading it", `cumpărându-le` "buying them"),
**except** when the following clitic is `o` "her/it" (`trezind-o` "waking her up", `luând-o` "taking
her" — no `-u` inserted). A handful of verbs have unpredictable/suppletive participles: `a
avea→având`, `a bate→bătând`, `a cădea→căzând`, `a deschide→deschizând`, `a face→făcând`, `a
vedea→văzând`, `a se întoarce→întorcându-se`. Negation prefixes `ne-` directly onto the participle
(`neavând` "not having", `neștiind` "not knowing"). Functionally, the participle expresses a
simultaneous or causally-linked background action, much like an English "-ing" clause: `Neavând bani
de metrou, m-am dus acasă pe jos` "Not having any money for the underground, I went home on foot";
`Văzându-l pe Victor i-am dat plicul` "Seeing Victor I gave him the envelope" — p.192–194 (this
grammar point's page range — 192–193 — was sampled early during this file's page-offset
verification and is cross-checked against the p.194 continuation above).

### 3. "So...that" (result clauses) — cross-referenced from p.192

`așa de`/`atât de` + adjective/adverb + `încât` expresses "so...that" (`Era așa de frig încât au
renunțat să meargă la mare` "It was so cold that they decided not to go to the seaside"). `atât`/
`cât` recur with a range of correlative meanings ("as much as", "however much") and `atât` itself
agrees with its noun like an adjective (`Erau atâția oameni acolo încât nu vedeai scena` "There were
so many people there that you couldn't see the stage").
