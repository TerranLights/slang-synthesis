## Treatment note — curated/representative sampling, per dispatch instructions

Per the original dispatch instructions, this oversized two-column dictionary section is handled with
representative/curated sampling rather than exhaustive transcription, the same discipline already
used on this project's other oversized dictionaries. The glossary's own stated scope (its prefatory
note, captured below) confirms it is a closed, complete word-list drawn entirely from words already
used somewhere in the book's lesson text — meaning **every substantive entry in both glossaries has
already been captured, with full grammatical and contextual detail, in this book's per-lesson
vocabulary tables (files 005–005x)**. The glossary adds no new vocabulary beyond consolidating what
the lessons already introduced; its only genuinely new information is bibliographic/structural
(its own front-matter conventions) plus the cross-reference numbers tying each entry back to the
paragraph that explains it — useful as a navigation aid internal to the book itself, not as a source
of new content for this project's purposes.

### Glossary's own stated scope and conventions (paraphrased from its prefatory note, p.197)

All words used anywhere in the grammar appear in the Glossary. Nouns are listed in the undeclined
singular with gender marked; plurals shown in parentheses. Adjectives show masculine/feminine,
singular/plural. Verbs are listed in the infinitive, marked with *-ez* or *-esc* when they take that
augment (cross-referencing Lesson VII), and with an irregular 1st-singular present indicative shown
in parentheses when applicable. Bold paragraph numbers in parentheses point back to the exact grammar
section (§) where an item is fully treated — this is the same cross-referencing device already
visible throughout this project's own per-lesson files (e.g. "§161" citations). An italicized vowel
marks stress; a dot between two vowels marks absence of diphthongization. A standard abbreviation key
is given (*adj, App, art, aux, dem, f, m, mx, pl, poss, pron, var, vr*).

## Appendix VI (country names, p.196)

A single-page, two-column list of country names in Romanian and English — *Abisinia/Abyssinia* through
*Venezuela/Venezuela*, roughly 55 countries, alphabetical by Romanian form. Pure proper-noun reference
list with no grammar content; not reproduced in full per the coverage rule (a closed proper-noun list
with no morphological or register information beyond the bare Romanian-form-to-English-form mapping).
Worth noting only two items with minor translation interest: *Anglia or Marea-Britanie* "England or
Great Britain" (showing the Romanian preference for the specific-country name in casual use alongside
the formal political-union name) and *Statele-Unite* "United States" (already captured as vocabulary
in file 005h from Lesson VII).
