## Grammar points

### Impersonal reflexive constructions ("it is said," "it is rumored") — Unit 18, pp. 232–233

Romanian's reflexive `se`-marker (already met for true reflexives, Unit 9, and the passive, Unit
12) also forms **impersonal statements**, often English-passive in sense: `se pare că...` "it
seems that...," `se spune că...` "it is said that...," `se vede că...` "it is obvious that..."
(lit. "it sees itself that"). A related, productive pattern: **a normally transitive verb used
without its direct object automatically becomes reflexive** and shifts to a passive-like sense:
`eu am deschis ușa` "I opened the door" vs. `s-a deschis ușa` "the door opened" (lit. "itself-opened
the door"); `s-a trimis faxul` "the fax was sent"; `s-a spart gheața` "the ice was broken." The same
`se`-reflexive is also the standard way to express reciprocal "each other": `ne-am întâlnit la gară`
"we met each other at the station," `s-au văzut la hotel` "they saw each other at the hotel."

### Ordinal numbers — Unit 18, pp. 233–234 — full system

1st has **two competing forms**: `primul`/`prima` (the one normally used **before** a noun, and
the more frequent overall) and `întâiul`/`întâia` (used more often **without** an accompanying
noun). From 2nd onward, ordinals are built with the adjectival article `cel` (Unit 14) prefixed
to the cardinal number plus suffix `-lea` (masc./neut.) or the cardinal plus `-a` (fem.):
`al doilea`/`a doua` (2nd), `al treilea`/`a treia` (3rd), continuing regularly up through `al
unsprezecelea` (11th), `al douăzecilea` (20th), `al treizeci și unulea` (31st). **In compound
ordinals, only the final element takes the ordinal suffix**: `al o sutălea` (100th), `al trei sute
doilea` (302nd) — the hundreds/thousands part stays cardinal. `primul`/`prima` and most other
ordinals (but not `întâiul`) may precede **or** follow the noun (`a doua mașină` = `mașina a doua`
"the second car"). **A special use of `ca`** ("in respect of") appears with ordinals for ranking
comparisons: `Constanța este al doilea oraș ca mărime al României` "Constanța is Romania's
second-largest town (lit. 'second town in-respect-of size')." Distinct frequency-adverb forms
built on the same ordinal root: `o dată` "once" vs. `de două ori` "twice" vs. `prima oară` "for the
first time" vs. `a doua oară` "for the second time" vs. `de câte ori?` "how many times?" — four
structurally different ways of counting repetitions depending on whether it's a bare count, a
`de...ori` multiplicative, or an ordinal-marked "nth time."

### Further case-governing prepositions — Unit 18, p. 238

Extends Unit 12's list of prepositions requiring the genitive/dative (possessive/indirect-object)
case form of their object: `contra` ("against"), `înaintea` ("in front of," distinct from `în
fața`), `în fundul` ("at the back of"), `în jurul` ("around"), `în urma` ("after, in the aftermath
of"), alongside the already-gendered-noun-dependent `deasupra`/`în spatele`/`în dreapta`/`în stânga`
(feminine-looking `-a`-ending prepositions are treated as feminine nouns grammatically; others as
neuter). **When the object of such a preposition is a 1st/2nd-person pronoun, a possessive
adjective is used (agreeing in gender) instead of the case-marked pronoun; for 3rd person, the
ordinary personal pronoun (in its gen/dat form) is used**: `deasupra mea` "above me" (possessive)
vs. `în spatele lor` "behind them" (personal pronoun) — a genuine person-based split in how this
whole preposition class marks its object.

### The present participle — Unit 18, pp. 239–240

Formed by `-ind` on the root of `-i`/`-ia`/`-ie`-infinitives (`a vorbi` → `vorbind` "speaking") and
`-ând` on all other infinitives (`a pleca` → `plecând` "leaving"). Reflexive verbs add `-u` to the
participle and suffix the reflexive pronoun: `a se apropia` → `apropiindu-se` "approaching." The
same `-u`-suffixing happens whenever **any object pronoun except `o`** attaches to the participle:
`luându-l` "taking it (m.)" but `luând-o` "taking it (f.)," without the extra `-u`. Irregular
participles: `fiind` ("being," from `a fi`), `având` ("having"), `făcând` ("doing"), `bătând`
("beating"), `dând` ("giving"). Negation prefixes `ne-`: `neavând bani, George n-a putut cumpăra
cartea` "having no money, George couldn't buy the book." **Register note the source states
explicitly**: "present participles are rarely used in colloquial speech but you will need to be
able to recognize them in more formal usage, e.g. in a letter" — a direct source-marked formal/
written-register flag.
