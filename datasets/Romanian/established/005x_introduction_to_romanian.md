## Grammar points

### §195–196 — Ordinal numbers
Full series given from "first" through "thousandth." *Întâi(u)*/*întâia* "first" is irregular — not
built on the *al/a* + cardinal pattern that generates the rest of the series. All other ordinals from
"second" on follow the regular *al/a* + cardinal + *-lea/-a* pattern. Four use/position rules: (a)
only ordinals are used with names of kings, popes, etc. (*Pius al Doisprezecelea* "Pius the Twelfth").
(b) cardinal numbers (not ordinals) designate days of the month, except the first day. (c) *vreo, cam*
"about" indicate approximate numbers. (d) when placed before a noun, *întâi* itself takes on a full
declined-adjective paradigm (N.-A./G.-D., masc./fem., sg./pl.) — captured above as a representative
vocabulary entry rather than reproduced in full table form, per copyright discipline.

### §197 — Pronouns of polite address
A genuinely important social-register system, distinct from (and layered on top of) the plain *tu*/
*voi* system already documented in Lesson XVI. The book notes that *tu* is used considerably more in
everyday Romanian conversation than in other Romance languages, but the present-day tendency is to use
the 2nd-plural verb form *without* the subject pronoun *voi* as a politeness strategy — instead using
one of a dedicated set of polite pronouns: *dumneata* (abbreviated *d-ta*) for 2nd-singular polite
direct address, *dumneavoastră* (*d-voastră*, *dv.*) for 2nd-plural polite address, and for indirect
(3rd-person) reference, *dumnealui*/*dumneaei*/*dumnealor* (he/she/they) or the gender-neutral
*dumneasa*/*dumisale*. This is a substantial, previously-undocumented social-register system in this
extraction pass — directly relevant to any later work modeling formality/politeness tiers in derived
slang, since the abbreviated forms (*d-ta, dv., d-sa*) and the full forms likely carry different
register weight themselves.

### §198 — Impersonal verbs
Verbs used only in the 3rd person singular, in five categories: (a) a closed set of weather-phenomenon
verbs (*a bura, a fulgera, a grindina, a însera, a ninge, a ploua, a trage* [draft], *a trăsni, a
tuna*), regular in conjugation and never used with a subject pronoun — full list captured above. (b)
verbs almost always used impersonally (*a se cădea, a se cuveni, a se întâmpla, a se părea*). (c) *a
plăcea* "to like" preceded by the unstressed dative clitic, for both "to like to do something" and
"to like something" — with the important related note that love/liking of a *person* instead uses *a
iubi*, a genuine semantic-field split (liking things/activities vs. loving people) worth flagging. (d)
*a durea* "to ache, hurt" with the unstressed accusative clitic, an irregular two-stem verb (*dor*
stressed / *dur* unstressed, parallel to *a putea*'s *pot*/*put* split from Lesson VII). (e) a broad,
productive pattern: many ordinary verbs can be used impersonally when constructed with the reflexive
plus the unstressed dative pronoun (*Mi s'a spus* "It was said to me") — directly continuing the
impersonal-reflexive pattern already established in Lesson XIX §167, now generalized further.
