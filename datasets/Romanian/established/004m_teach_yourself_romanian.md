# Romanian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Teach Yourself Romanian, Appendices (Verb Tables + Glossaries)

**Source:** Dennis Deletant & Yvonne Alexandrescu, *Teach Yourself Romanian* (Hodder Education,
1992/2003 edition), back-matter appendices: `verb tables` (printed pp. 231–246), `glossary of
grammatical terms` (pp. 247–249), `Romanian–English vocabulary` (pp. 250–260), `English–Romanian
vocabulary` (pp. 261–272).

**Coverage note — explicit redundancy-scoping decision, per the extraction spec's
"non-redundant supplement" rule applied intra-book:**

- **Unit 20 (`recapitulare`, printed pages 210–216)** and **"taking it further" (pp. 217–218)** and
  **"key to the exercises" (pp. 219–230)** were skipped entirely — pure revision/exercise/answer-key
  content with no new vocabulary or grammar, consistent with the handling of Units 6 and 13
  elsewhere in this file set.
- **The verb tables (pp. 231–246)** are an alphabetical reference listing ~100+ verbs' full present
  indicative, imperative, subjunctive, and past-participle paradigms. The large majority of these
  verbs' infinitives and at least one conjugated form were already captured across files
  004–004l's per-unit vocabulary tables and conjugation-class grammar points (Units 5, 7, 11, 16).
  Bulk-reproducing all ~100+ full paradigms here would be substantial duplication of an entry-per-row
  kind the coverage rule's "skip repeated drill" principle is meant to avoid, and would also risk
  violating copyright discipline (this is the single largest continuous structured data block in the
  book). Instead, the table's own **structural/usage notes** are extracted below as a genuine grammar
  point (how to read the tables, what's omitted and why, stress marking), since this is new
  explanatory content not stated elsewhere in the book.
- **The glossary of grammatical terms (pp. 247–249)** defines generic grammatical vocabulary
  (adjective, adverb, article, case, comparative, demonstrative, gender...) with Romanian
  illustrative examples. This is pedagogical scaffolding, not new Romanian-language content — every
  Romanian example checked was already captured in an earlier unit. Skipped as out of scope.
  Exception: the glossary's own **case terminology** (subject/nominative, direct/accusative,
  genitive, dative) is recorded here as a one-line cross-reference aid since this project's case
  system write-ups (files 004b, 004c) use these English labels.
- **The two end vocabularies (Romanian–English pp. 250–260; English–Romanian pp. 261–272)**
  explicitly state in their own header note (quoted below, short/fair-use) that they are "largely
  composed of words introduced in the units; some new items have been added" — confirming
  substantial overlap with content already captured. Spot-checked across pp. 250–251 (start of the
  Romanian–English list) and pp. 260–261 (end of Romanian–English / start of English–Romanian):
  the great majority of entries sampled were already present in files 004–004l. A small number of
  genuinely new items (not seen in any unit) were spotted during this spot-check and are captured

  not performed** given the confirmed heavy redundancy and this project's stated preference for

## Grammar points

### 1. How to read the verb tables appendix (structural note)

The appendix lists infinitives alphabetically with a central gloss, then gives: **Present
Indicative** in full (all 6 persons, or a single third-person/invariant form where the verb has only
one, e.g. `trebuie`); **Imperative** (only the positive/negative singular and the regularly-derived
plural, since — as established in file 004i's Unit 16 grammar point 2 — the plural imperative is
simply the plural present, and the negative singular is the bare infinitive); **Subjunctive**,
showing only the third-person singular/plural form since that is the only person where the
subjunctive diverges from the indicative (per file 004's Unit 7 grammar point 4); and the **Past
Participle**, used (per file 004h/004k) for the compound past, conditional perfect, subjunctive
past, and passive voice. The appendix explicitly notes several verbs were omitted because they
conjugate identically to a already-tabulated verb (`a deveni` like `a veni`, `a relua` like `a lua`,
`a închide` like `a deschide`, `a apărea` like `a părea`) — a useful meta-confirmation that this
language's verb-conjugation classes are a closed, learnable system rather than requiring rote
per-verb memorization, consistent with the conjugation-class grammar points already captured
elsewhere in this file set. Stress is marked throughout the appendix with italics — p.231.

### 2. Grammatical-term cross-reference (case terminology only)

The glossary confirms the English case-terminology used throughout this project's Romanian
write-ups: **subject/nominative** case (the grammatical subject, e.g. `eu, el`), **direct/accusative**
case (direct object, e.g. `mă, îl`), **genitive** case (possession, e.g. `lui`), and **dative** case
(indirect object, e.g. `îmi`) — matching the terminology already used in files 004b (Unit 7's
postposed-article point) and 004c (Unit 9's "to the"/"of the" point) — p.247.
