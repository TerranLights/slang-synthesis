## Grammar points

### 14.1-14.2 Identifying people and things (pp. 172-174)

Covers identity, ownership (built on the possessive-article genitive paradigm of `cine` already
given in 5.7.1 — `A cui`, `Ale cui`, `Al cui`, `Ai cui`), and profession/occupation vocabulary,
which the book splits across several near-synonymous nouns for "job" (profesie, meserie, post)
without stating an explicit register difference, though `meserie` conventionally implies a manual/
trade skill versus `profesie`'s more white-collar connotation — worth flagging as an *implicit*
register distinction the grammar itself doesn't spell out, unlike the explicit annotations found
elsewhere in this source. Identifying things reuses the colloquial demonstrative-pronoun doublets
from 4.6.2 (ăsta/ăsta-series marked "(informal)" vs. the fuller acesta-series). **Telling time**
(14.2.1) shows Romanian using a subtractive construction for "quarter/minutes to" the hour
(`E ora unu fără un sfert`, lit. "it is the hour one, without/minus a quarter") rather than a
forward "to"-preposition construction, worth comparing against how other Romance languages in this
project's corpus handle the same semantic slot.

### 14.3-14.4 Asking for and offering information (pp. 175-176)

Practical travel/service-register phrases (train times, platform numbers, ticket purchases, hotel
rooms) — functionally parallel to a phrasebook's "at the station"/"at the hotel" sections, with no
further register annotation from the source beyond the ordinary informal/formal split already
established for direct address.

### 14.5 Reporting, narrating (pp. 176-177)

The book states explicitly that **Romanian indirect/reported speech does not backshift tense** —
the verb tense used in the reported clause is whatever tense the original speaker would have used
"live," unlike English's past-tense backshifting in indirect speech (this is the same free-
sequencing principle already described for conditional "if"-clauses in 7.5.2.1 and generalized in
7.14, now shown to extend to reported speech specifically). The chapter lists fourteen common
reported-speech verbs (a nara, a povesti, a spune, a raporta, a declara, a critica, a adăuga, a
întreba, a comenta, a afirma, a informa, a transmite, a asigura) each with one full example
sentence — collectively spanning a register range from neutral (a spune, a întreba) through
journalistic/official (a declara, a raporta, a transmite, a informa) to literary (a nara), which
is itself a useful register-graded sample of the "reporting verb" lexical field for the mechanics-
analysis phase.

### 14.6 Letter writing (pp. 178-180)

A clean three-way register ladder for both opening and closing formulae: informal (Dragă + first
name; closings Te pup., Cu drag,), a distinct **romantic/intimate** sub-register nested within
informal (Dragostea mea!, Iubirea mea! openers — notably not just "informal" but specifically
romantic, a register axis orthogonal to the formal/informal one), and formal (Stimate domnule/
Stimată doamnă openers; Cu stimă/Cu respect/Cu consideraţie closings). Two full example letters
are given (a formal hotel-cancellation letter and an informal postcard-style letter from a friend
abroad) — their content was paraphrased rather than quoted in full per copyright discipline, but
their structural pattern (date-first formatting, indented-paragraph body, formula-close) matches
standard Romanian business/personal letter convention as taught here.
