## Grammar points

### Invariable adjectives (color/style loanwords) with domain-restricted variants — Unit 16, p. 206

A productive subclass of Romanian adjectives (mostly recent/French-origin color and style terms)
**never inflect for gender or number**: `bej`, `gri`, `maro`, `mov`, `roz`, `șic` — contrasting with
the native inflecting color adjectives already met (`negru/neagră`, `galben/galbenă`, etc.).
**Domain-specific substitution rule flagged by the source**: `maro` is the general word for
"brown," but describing eyes specifically requires `căprui` and hair specifically requires
`castaniu` — a genuine lexical-domain restriction, not free variation (`el are ochii căprui` "he
has brown eyes" / `ea are păr castaniu` "she has brown hair," never `*ochii maro`/`*păr maro` in
the examples given).

### Interrogative/relative pronoun system: `cine`/`cui`, `care` — Unit 16, pp. 206–207 — **flagship case-system finding**

`cine` ("who?") has a reduced case paradigm: `cine` (subject), `pe cine` (object, `pe`-marked per
Unit 11's human-object rule), `cui` (dative "to whom?" — supported by the ordinary indirect pronoun
`îi`: `cui îi place mâncarea?` "who likes the food?"), and the possessive article + `cui` for
"whose?" (`al cui este fularul?` "whose scarf is it?," agreeing with the thing possessed, not the
possessor — exactly as the possessive article did in Unit 14). **`care`** ("who/which" as a
relative pronoun, "which (one)?" as a question word) has a fuller paradigm distinguishing singular
masc./neut. and fem. in the genitive (`al/a/ai/ale cărui` vs. `al/a/ai/ale cărei`) collapsing to one
plural form (`al/a/ai/ale căror`), with `cărui`/`cărei`/`căror` agreeing with the **possessor** (the
opposite agreement direction from the `al`/`a` possessive-article part of the same construction):
`studenții ale căror biciclete sunt afară...` "the students whose bicycles are outside" (`ale`
agrees with "bicycles," `căror` agrees with "the students").

### `cel`/`cea`/`cei`/`cele` used pronominally ("the one(s)") — Unit 16, p. 208

Beyond its adjectival-article role (Unit 14), `cel` can stand alone as a full pronoun meaning "the
one(s) who/that": `cel care vorbește este american` "the one who is speaking is American," `cei
care ascultă învață` "those who listen learn."

### `fiecare` ("each," "every") — Unit 16, pp. 208–209

Has distinct nominative and gen/dat forms by gender (`fiecărui` masc./neut., `fiecărei` fem.), but
the source notes explicitly that **"in colloquial usage the plural forms are replaced by `toți`,
`toate`, or `tuturor`"** (Unit 12's quantifier) — i.e. `fiecare` itself has no true plural and
colloquial speech reaches for `tot` instead when a plural sense is needed. When used pronominally
(standing alone, not before a noun), `fiecare` suffixes `-a` in its possessive/dative form:
`i-am spus fiecăruia să plece acasă` "I told each of them to go home."

### Present and past conditional revisited in a connected dialogue — Unit 16, pp. 209–212

No new tense morphology here, but the unit's "Planuri de viitor" dialogue is worth flagging as the
richest connected-discourse illustration yet of Units 5 and 14's conditional/past-conditional
system working together across several turns of real deliberative conversation (weighing
hypothetical life choices) rather than isolated example sentences — useful context for how these
forms actually co-occur in natural register.
