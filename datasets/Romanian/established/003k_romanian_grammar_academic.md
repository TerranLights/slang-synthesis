# Romanian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Imperative, Subjunctive, Optative-Conditional, Presumptive (Ch. 4.2.2–4.2.5)

**Source:** Dana Cojocaru, *Romanian Grammar* (academic reference grammar), 2003, pp. 145–159 (PDF

## Grammar points

### Imperative: only 2 persons, 2nd plural borrowed from the indicative, 2nd singular partly irregular (§4.2.2.1)

Romanian's imperative exists only for 2nd person singular and plural — no 1st or 3rd person imperative
forms exist at all (those meanings are instead carried by the subjunctive, see below). The 2nd-plural
imperative (affirmative and negative alike) is formally identical to the 2nd-plural present indicative;
only intonation signals the command. The 2nd-singular imperative is more complex and splits by verb
class and transitivity: for -a and -î conjugation verbs, 4th-conjugation -i verbs with the -esc- infix,
and transitive verbs generally in -e/-ea/-i, the 2nd-singular affirmative imperative is identical to the
**3rd-singular present indicative** (Aşteaptă! ~ el aşteaptă); for *intransitive* -e/-ea/-i verbs it is
instead identical to the **2nd-singular present indicative** (Mergi! ~ tu mergi) — meaning a handful of
verbs with both transitive and intransitive senses (a trece) actually have two different imperative
forms depending on which sense is meant. The negative 2nd-singular imperative is built differently from
every other negated mood: `nu` + the bare infinitive *without* the particle `a` (nu cânta!), not a
negated version of the affirmative imperative form. A closed list of verbs (a fi, a face, a desface, a
duce, a aduce, a conduce, a reduce, a traduce, a veni, a preveni, a reveni, a zice) has a fully
irregular 2nd-singular affirmative imperative unrelated to either indicative form (fă!, du!, zi!, etc.);
a fi is additionally irregular in the 2nd plural (fiţi!).

### Imperative clitic placement: post-verbal in the affirmative, pre-verbal in the negative (§4.2.2.2)

Unstressed personal/reflexive pronoun clitics attach *after* the verb with obligatory hyphenation in
the affirmative imperative (Ajută-mă! "Help me!") but move to *before* the verb, between `nu` and the
verb, in the negative (Nu mă ajuta! "Don't help me!") — a clean mirror-image of the ordinary clitic-
placement rule and the single largest word-order shift triggered by negation anywhere in the verb
system. When both a dative and accusative clitic combine on an affirmative imperative, they fuse into
one phonetic word attached after the verb with double hyphenation, dative first (Arată-mi-l! "Show it
to me!" from Arată-mi pantoful!) — in the negative the same double-clitic cluster instead sits between
nu and the verb (Nu mi-l arăta!).

### Subjunctive: să + a largely indicative-identical form, distinguished by 3rd-person flip (§4.2.3.1)

The present subjunctive reuses the present indicative's own forms for 1st/2nd person singular/plural
verbatim — only the preceding particle `să` (plus the clause's syntactic context) signals subjunctive
mood at all for those persons. The 3rd person alone genuinely changes form, via two linked rules: the
present-indicative 3rd-singular ending -ă flips to -e in the subjunctive and vice versa (cântă → să
cânte; vine → să vină), and 3rd-singular and 3rd-plural subjunctive forms are *always* identical to each
other (reusing the already-established present-indicative 3rd-sg=3rd-pl homonymy pattern from file
`003h`, but now extended to every verb regardless of conjugation). Verbs whose present-indicative 3rd
singular already ends in -ie keep that same form unchanged in the subjunctive. The infixes -eaz-/-eşt-/
-ăşt- from the present indicative are replaced by -ez-/-asc- specifically in the subjunctive's 3rd
person. Nine irregular verbs (a fi, a avea, a da, a lua, a sta, a mânca, a bea, implicitly others) have
dedicated subjunctive paradigms given in full by the source.

### Subjunctive usage: the default complement mood after a huge verb class, plus独立 modal uses (§4.2.3.1.2)

The subjunctive is the standard complement clause after verbs of wish/preference/permission/request/
advice (a vrea, a dori, a prefera, a lăsa, a ruga, a sfătui, a sugera, a recomanda, a cere, a interzice,
a permite, a se teme), after a putea and a şti, after impersonal predicates of necessity/value
judgment (trebuie, e necesar, e bine, etc. — including the specifically social/propriety-marked se
cuvine/se cade alongside the more neutral se poate/se recomandă), after a plăcea with a dative subject,
in a avea + interrogative constructions marking possession/absence of means ("Ai cu ce să te îmbraci?"
"Do you have [anything] to wear?"), and after a family of compound conjunctions (fără să, în loc să, ca
să, înainte să). When the subjunctive clause's subject differs from the main clause's subject and needs
explicit mention, plain să is replaced by ca să with the subject inserted between ca and să (Vreau ca tu
să stai acasă.). Used independently, the subjunctive carries modal/imperative force — notably supplying
the 1st- and 3rd-person "imperative" meanings the true imperative mood cannot express (Să mergem! "Let's
go!", optionally intensified with the interjection hai; Să plece imediat! "Have him leave immediately!")
and, in the 2nd person specifically, functioning as an even *stronger* command than the true imperative.
A closed set of fixed subjunctive-based social formulas round out this use — situational blessings/
leave-takings for specific social moments (a child stating their age, finishing a meal, wearing new
clothes, mentioning someone recently deceased) — captured in the vocabulary table above.

### Past subjunctive: a single invariant form across every person (§4.2.3.2)

`să fi` + past participle, with no person/number variation whatsoever — the most form-reduced tense in
the whole verbal system. It follows the past optative-conditional of subjunctive-governing verbs to
express past necessity/desire (Ar fi trebuit să fi rămas acasă. "We should have stayed home.") and, used
independently, expresses regret about a completed past action now seen as undesirable (Să fi rămas
acasă... "If only we had stayed home.").

### a putea + subjunctive/bare infinitive: free variation with clitic-placement quirks (§4.2.3.3)

a putea can take either the present subjunctive or the bare infinitive (without `a`) as its complement,
fully synonymously (El nu poate să vină. = El nu poate veni.). When the complement verb is reflexive or
takes a clitic pronoun, the clitic can instead attach to a putea itself in the infinitive-complement
construction (Pot să mă îmbrac singură. ↔ Mă pot îmbrăca singură.) — except when a putea is itself in
the compound perfect and the clitic in question is specifically the 3rd-singular feminine accusative
`o`, which must instead follow the *compound* verb as a whole (Am putut-o găsi uşor., not *Am putut-o
găsi uşor rearranged differently) — and except that negation `nu` always precedes whichever element is
leftmost regardless of which construction is chosen.

### Optative-conditional: a genuinely distinct 5th mood for desired/conditioned action (§4.2.4)

Romanian treats "would" as its own mood (not a tense of the indicative or subjunctive), explicitly
contrasted by the source against the indicative (real/accomplished action) and subjunctive (possible/
not-yet-accomplished action): the optative-conditional marks action framed as *desired* or as the
*condition* for another action. Present tense: aş/ai/ar/am/aţi/ar + bare infinitive (3sg=3pl always
identical, ar cânta). Past tense: the same auxiliary set + invariant fi + past participle (aş fi
cântat), again with 3sg=3pl identity.

### Present optative-conditional usage: desire, softened requests, unreal comparison, and the curse/endearment idiom family (§4.2.4.1.2)

Beyond plain desire/wish (Aş citi ceva. "I'd like to read something."), the present optative-
conditional softens/politens discourse after verbs of wish/necessity (a dori, a vrea, impersonal a
plăcea/a trebui) and, in the opposite direction, makes *requests* firmer/more polite when substituted
for the plain present with verbs like a ruga/a putea/a vrea (Ai putea să mă ajuţi? vs. Poţi să mă
ajuţi?). After ca şi cum/de parcă "as if," it marks an explicitly "unreal comparison." It also carries a
general value of potentiality/probability/uncertainty (N-aş zice că te-ai îngrăşat. "I wouldn't say
you've gained weight."). Most distinctively, a family of **fixed idioms built on inverted word order**
(verb-pronoun-auxiliary instead of the ordinary auxiliary-pronoun-verb order) spans the full emotional
range from affection to outright curse, all sharing one syntactic template: Mânca-te-ar mama! (literally
"May your mother eat you!", used affectionately — the source is explicit this expresses *positive*
feeling despite the predatory image), Lua-te-ar dracu! ("May the devil take you! / Go to hell!"),
Vedea-te-aş mort! ("I'd like to see you dead!"). A related but semantically distinct inverted-word-order
use (generally 1st person singular) marks doubt with verbs like a se mira/a se îndoi (Mira-m-aş. "I'd be
amazed" — i.e., "I doubt it").

### Past optative-conditional usage: unfulfilled past desire, reproach, and an imperfect-indicative substitute (§4.2.4.2.2)

The past form expresses an unfulfilled past desire/intention, again optionally with an impediment or a
conditional clause (dacă/de + past optative-conditional in the condition, matched by past optative-
conditional in the result — Dacă aş fi avut timp, aş fi citit un roman poliţist.). The ca şi cum/de parcă
unreal-comparison use and the potentiality/uncertainty use both extend straightforwardly into the past.
a putea's past optative-conditional + present subjunctive specifically forms **reproaches** (Ai fi putut
să-mi telefonezi! "You could have called me!"). The source notes that in practice the plain imperfect
indicative can substitute for the past optative-conditional to express the same unfulfilled-past-
condition meaning with a simpler construction (Dacă am fi luat biletele ieri... = Dacă luam biletele
ieri...).

### Presumptive: a dedicated mood for presupposition/doubt, with a unique progressive variant (§4.2.5)

A fifth distinct auxiliary set — oi/oi/o/om/oţi/or (1sg=2sg identical, the mirror of the ar-mood's
3sg=3pl identity pattern) — marks presupposition, hypothesis, hope, or doubt, and is explicitly
described as carrying genuinely modal force throughout. Present: auxiliary + bare infinitive (o fi
acasă "he/she might be home"). The present also has a **progressive sub-variant found nowhere else in
the Romanian verbal system** — auxiliary + fi + gerund (oi fi stând "I might be sitting") — used
specifically for a supposition about an action actually in progress right now, something the ordinary
present presumptive cannot distinguish. Past: auxiliary + fi + past participle (oi fi venit "I might
have come"). All three sub-forms share the same clitic-pronoun-placement and modal-adverb-insertion
machinery already seen across the compound tenses (clitics before the first auxiliary element, with the
3rd-singular feminine accusative o the systematic exception, placed after the final verbal element
instead).
