## Grammar points

### Four conjugations, each with an ending-set and (for 1st and 4th) an infix split (§4.2.1.1.1–5)

Each conjugation has its own fixed present-indicative ending set attached to the infinitive stem, with
predictable phonetic mutation at the stem-ending boundary:

- **1st conjugation (-a)**: -Ø/-u/-i (1sg), -i (2sg), -ă/-e (3sg), -ăm/-em (1pl), -aţi (2pl), -ă/-e (3pl
  — always identical to 3sg). Splits into **model 1** (no infix — four stem-final-sound subtypes:
  plain consonant; consonant+r/l taking -u in 1sg; -i-after-vowel with Ø 1sg/2sg and -e 3sg/pl; and two
  lexically-specific irregular outliers a întârzia and a continua) and **model 2** (infix -ez-/-eaz- in
  1st/2nd/3rd sg. and 3rd pl. — three stem-final-sound subtypes: plain consonant; velar c/g requiring
  the -che-/-ghe- spelling adjustment to preserve the palatal sound before the infix; and -i-stem with
  -em in 1pl and infix-initial -e- dropped in 3rd persons).
- **2nd conjugation (-ea)**: -Ø (1sg), -i (2sg), -e (3sg), -em (1pl, always stressed), -eţi (2pl, always
  stressed), -Ø (3pl — identical to 1sg). No infix split exists for this conjugation; a putea shows the
  most complex stem mutation (pot/poţi/poate/putem/puteţi/pot).
- **3rd conjugation (-e)**: identical endings to the 2nd conjugation except 1st/2nd plural are
  *unstressed* rather than stressed, and 1sg/3pl can take -u instead of -Ø after consonant+r/l or vowel
  stems. Four stem-final-sound subtypes are documented: plain consonant other than -n; -n-final stems
  (which drop the -n before 2sg -i: pune → pui); consonant+r/l stems (taking -u in 1sg/3pl: umple →
  umplu); and vowel-final stems (also taking -u in 1sg/3pl, with a double-i spelling in 2sg: scrie →
  scrii).
- **4th conjugation, -i**: -Ø/-i (1sg), -i (2sg), -e (3sg), -im (1pl), -iţi (2pl), -Ø/-e (3pl). Splits
  into **model 1** (no infix — three subtypes: consonant-other-than-n stem, with the lexically flagged
  irregular pair a oferi/a suferi; -n-final stem dropping -n in 2sg exactly like the 3rd-conjugation
  pattern; and vowel-final stem with identical 1sg=2sg) and **model 2** (infix -esc-/-eşt- in
  1st/2nd/3rd sg. and 3rd pl. — two subtypes: consonant stem; vowel stem, which inserts a linking -i-
  before the infix).
- **4th conjugation, -î**: -Ø (1sg), -i (2sg), -ă/-e (3sg), -âm (1pl), -âţi (2pl), -Ø/-ă (3pl). Splits
  into **model 1** (no infix, 3sg=3pl identical: coboară=coboară) and **model 2** (infix -ăsc-/-ăşt-,
  1sg=3pl identical instead: hotărăsc=hotărăsc) — note this is the *reverse* homonymy pattern from
  model 1, a direct parallel to the 4th-conjugation -i split documented structurally in file `003h`'s
  homonym catalog.

Across every conjugation, the 3rd-singular/3rd-plural present-indicative homonymy already catalogued in
`003h` (§4.1.7.1) is now visible as the direct, mechanical consequence of these ending tables rather
than a separate fact to re-derive.

### Eight irregular verbs with their own full paradigms (§4.2.1.1.6)

a fi, a avea, a da, a sta, a lua, a mânca, a bea, a vrea, and a şti each have a dedicated, non-
conjugation-model-conforming present-indicative paradigm, given in full by the source. a fi notably has
colloquial short/clitic-like alternate forms for 1sg and 3sg (sunt/-s; este/e/-i) not available to any
regular verb.

### Usage of the present indicative: broader than English present tense (§4.2.1.1.7)

Beyond ordinary present-tense uses (general truths, habitual/repetitive actions, in-progress actions,
imperative-like commands), Romanian's present indicative covers two ranges that English splits into
separate tenses: (1) an action or state beginning in the past and still ongoing is expressed with the
*present* indicative where English requires present perfect/present perfect continuous (Lucrez aici din
1994. "I have been working here since 1994."); (2) since Romanian has no dedicated
continuous/progressive verb form at all, the bare present indicative covers both English simple present
and present continuous indiscriminately (Eu predau italiană, dar acum predau şi spaniolă. "I teach
Italian, but now I am teaching Spanish too."). The present can also render a past event vividly as a
"historic present" (La 4 iulie 1776 America îşi declară independenţa.) and can stand in for the future
when the action is treated as certain, or for the imperative in a command sequence.
