## Grammar points

### 1. Time expressions: "have been doing" and "since"

Where English uses a perfect progressive ("I have been learning Romanian..."), Romanian uses the
plain **present** tense with `de mult`/`de puțin timp` or a specific duration (`Învăț românește numai
de puțin timp`); the imperfect covers the same pattern in a past-relative frame (`Locuiam de cinci
ani la Londra` "We had been living in London for five years"). The matching question word is `de
când?` "for how long?/since when?", itself paired with the present tense. Where the action is
**completed** (negative, "hasn't done X for..."), the compound past is used instead, with `de` +
duration or any of a cluster of since-phrases: `de astă iarnă`, `din iarna trecută`, `din 1989`,
`din aprilie`, `de ieri`, `de luni`, `de la 1 decembrie` — pp.176–177.

### 2. Further connective adverbs: `abia`, `cum`, `ori de câte ori`, `încă`

`abia` "just, hardly" (`Abia acum am reușit să termin` "I have only just managed to finish"); `cum`
in this specific use means "as soon as" (`Cum o văd te voi suna` "The moment I see her I will give
you a call") — a distinct sense from interrogative `cum` "how"; `ori de câte ori` "whenever" (Unit
11 also introduced this); `încă` "yet" in a negative context (`George n-a venit încă` "George hasn't
come yet") — p.177.

### 3. `până` family: "until"/"before"/"by the time that"

`până`, `până când`, `până ce` are largely interchangeable for "until"/"before" with a positive verb
(`Rămân aici până (ce) vine ea` "I'm staying here until she comes"). `până nu` is used when the verb
is **negative** (`Nu plec până nu-mi dă telefon` "I'm not leaving until he rings me") and can also
mean "before" in a time-limit sense (`Să-l găsim până nu pleacă la serviciu` "Let's find him before
he leaves for work"). `până să` means "by the time that" (`Până să căpătăm o viză, biletul de avion
nu mai era valabil` "By the time we obtained a visa the plane ticket was no longer valid") —
pp.177–178.

### 4. Further uses of `pe`

Extending Unit 10's `pe`-before-personal-object rule: `pe` also appears with other pronouns denoting
people as objects, including `cine` and `care` (`Pe cine să invităm la masa de seară?` "Whom shall
we invite to dinner?"; `Banii pe care îi am` "The money that I have") and with quantifier pronouns
like `unul`/`toți`/`aceștia`/`cei care` when they denote people-as-object (`I-am văzut pe toți la
recepție` "I saw them all at the reception") — p.178.

### 5. Further contexts requiring `să`

Beyond Unit 7's core cases, `să` is also obligatory: after verbs of command/instruction (`Doctorul
le-a spus copiilor să ia medicamentele`); after verbs of wanting/wishing beyond `a vrea` (e.g. `a
ține` "to be keen to"); after `trebuie`/`a putea`; in "it is easy/difficult to..." constructions
(`Mi-e greu să plec chiar acum`); introducing questions (`Cum să ajung la metrou?` "How shall I get
to the underground?"); after prepositions `fără` "without", `înainte (de)` "before", `în loc (de)`
"instead of" (`Fără să exagerez...` "Without exaggerating..."); and after `ca` to express purpose
(`Ca să fiu cinstit, el nu-mi place` "To be honest, I don't like him") — pp.179–180.

### 6. The infinitive (bare "to" form) as an alternative to `să`

With the prepositions `fără`, `înainte de`, `în loc de`, and purpose-`ca`, Romanian can alternatively
use the bare infinitive (preceded by `a`) instead of a `să`-clause, with no meaning difference:
`Pentru a fi cinstit...` = `Ca să fiu cinstit...` "To be honest...". This infinitive-vs-subjunctive
free variation is also available after `ce` in indefinite expressions: `Ai ce mânca?` = `Ai ce să
mănânci?` "Have you got something to eat?"; `N-avem unde sta` = `N-avem unde să stăm` "We've got
nowhere to stay" — pp.180–181.

### 7. Ordinal numbers

Ordinals behave as gender-agreeing adjectives with masculine/neuter and feminine forms, each having
a long and a short/abbreviated written variant: `primul`/`prima` (1-ul/1-a) "first"; `al
doilea`/`a doua` (2-lea/2-a) "second"; continuing regularly through `al zecelea`/`a zecea` (10th) and
beyond (`al cincisprezecelea`/`a cincisprezecea` 15th; `al douăzecilea` 20th; `al o sutălea` 100th;
`al o mielea` 1000th). `primul` and `ultimul` ("the last") always **precede** their noun; other
ordinals may precede or follow, and all can stand alone. When the ordinal precedes, the noun takes
the plain indefinite ("a") form; when it follows, the noun takes the definite ("the") form:
`primul lui pașaport` "his first passport" vs. `omul al treilea`/`al treilea om` "the third man". To
express "of the second"/"of the third" etc., `cel` (Unit 15) precedes the ordinal and agrees with
the noun (`începutul celei de-a doua curse` "the beginning of the second race"). A frequency
reading ("twice", "three times") instead uses the cardinal + `ori`: `Am vizitat România de două ori`
"I visited Romania twice" — pp.181–183.
