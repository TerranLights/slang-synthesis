## Grammar points

### 1. Ailments: `a durea` and the dative-experiencer construction

`a durea` "to hurt" behaves opposite to English "hurts" in terms of grammatical subject: the body
part that hurts is the grammatical subject, and the person is expressed via a direct-object clitic
(singular `mă/o/îl`) — `Mă doare capul` "I have a headache" (lit. "me hurts the-head"), `O doare
spatele` "Her back hurts" (lit. "her hurts the-back"). In the past tense the clitic still precedes
but the verb takes the past participle (`A durut-o spatele` "Her back hurt"); with plural subjects,
`pe` + stressed pronoun appears too (`Pe ei i-au durut mâinile` "Their hands hurt them"). `a ustura`
"to smart" follows the identical pattern (`Mă ustură ochii` "My eyes are smarting"). A related but
distinct dative-reflexive idiom covers color/visible change: `Mi se înroșesc ochii` "My eyes are
turning red" (lit. "to-me they-are-turning-red the-eyes") — pp.164–165.

### 2. The imperative mood — this project's fullest treatment so far

**Singular commands**: for most verbs without an object, the imperative equals the ordinary 2nd
person singular present (`Taci!` "Be quiet!", `Mergi!` "Go!"). Verbs that take an object instead use
the **he/she/it** present form as the singular imperative (`Trimite-l acasă!` "Send him home!").
`-a`-conjugation verbs and `-e/-oară`-type he/she forms derive the imperative from that same
he/she/it form (`Cântă!` "Sing!", `Vorbește!` "Speak!"). A short list of very common verbs has
irregular/suppletive singular imperatives: `Dă!` "Give!", `Fii!` "Be!", `Vino!` "Come!", `Ia!`
"Take!", `Fă!` "Do!", `Zi!` "Say!". **Plural commands** are simply the plural you-form of the
present (`Cântați!`, `Tăceți!`), with one flagged exception `Fiți!` "Be!" (not the expected
`*Sunteți!`). **Negative ("don't") commands** differ sharply by number: singular `nu` + bare
infinitive without `a` (`Nu veni!` "Don't come!"); plural `nu` + the ordinary plural present
you-form (`Nu veniți!`). **Reflexive verbs** append `-te` to the he/she/it present form in the
singular (`Uită-te!`, colloquially shortened to `Uite!`, "Look!") and `-vă` to the plural you-form
(`Sculați-vă!` "Get up!"); the idiomatic/suppletive `du-te` "go" ("Du-te dracului!" "Go to hell!")
is flagged as the paradigm example and as commonly used in swearing. Negative reflexive commands
place `te`/`vă` *before* the verb (`Nu te uita!`, `Nu vă uitați!`). Object/dative clitics
(`-l, -o, -mi,` etc.) **follow and attach to** a positive command (`Lasă-l!` "Leave him!",
`Lasă-mi banii!` "Leave me the money!") but **precede** a negative one (`Nu-l lăsa!` "Don't leave
him!") — the attachment-side flip is itself a notable, learnable mechanical rule — p.161 (recap),
165–168. As a courteous alternative to the imperative proper, a `să`-clause (Unit 7) can soften a
command (`Să nu faceți zgomot!` "Don't make any noise!").

### 3. `unul`/`altul`/`celălalt`: "one of", "another", "the other"

`unul`/`una` (+ case forms `unuia/uneia`, plural `unii/unele`, `unora`) expresses "one of"/"some
of": `Unul din noi este un mincinos` "One of us is a liar". These forms require `nu` before the verb
when used with a negative sense (`Nici un autobuz nu oprește` "Not one bus stops"). `alt`/`altă`
(pre-nominal) vs. standalone `altul`/`alta` both mean "other"/"another", sharing the same
case-inflecting pattern. "The other" (as opposed to plain "other") is `celălalt` — a fusion of `cel`
(Unit 15) + `alt` — used identically before a noun or standing alone, and (like `cel`) both
components agree with the noun: `cealaltă mașină` "the other car" — pp.168–169.

### 4. `fiecare` "each" and `amândoi` "both"

`fiecare` "each" declines through the same paradigm as `care` (Unit 14): `Fiecare copil are patul
lui` "Each child has his own bed"; `fiecărei infirmiere` "to each nurse" (dative). `amândoi` (m.) /
`amândouă` (f./n.) "both" is followed by the noun in its **definite-article** form: `Amândoi frații
au participat` "Both brothers attended" — p.170.
