## Grammar points

### Definite genitive/dative ("of the," "to the") — Unit 8, pp. 94–96 — **flagship case-system finding, completes Unit 7**

Just as the plain definite article is a suffix (Unit 2), the genitive/dative **definite** forms are
also suffixed directly onto the noun, giving a 4-way case contrast when combined with Unit 7's
indefinite forms. Worked examples: `magazinul` "the shop" → `magazinului` "of the shop";
`vânzătoarea` "the shop assistant" → `vânzătoarei` "to the shop assistant"; `paltoanele` "the
overcoats" → `paltoanelor` "of the overcoats." **The source states explicitly that the 'of the' and
'to the' forms are always identical** — genitive/dative syncretism holds for the definite forms
exactly as it did for the indefinite ones. Full paradigm given for all three genders across
singular/plural (masculine `muntele/muntelui/munții/munților`; feminine `doamna/doamnei/
doamnele/doamnelor`; neuter `trenul/trenului/trenurile/trenurilor`), giving this project's first
complete 3-gender × 2-number × 2-case (nom/acc vs. gen/dat, each in both indefinite and definite
forms) picture of a Romanian noun's full inflectional paradigm across Units 2, 7, and 8 combined.

### Multi-adjective agreement and adjective-class paradigms with case endings — Unit 8, pp. 97–100

Extends Unit 3's adjective-agreement system by showing that **multiple adjectives stack on one
noun, each agreeing independently** (`struguri negri, zemoși și dulci` "black, juicy, sweet
grapes" — three 4-form-class adjectives all agreeing with masc. plural "struguri"). The unit then
works through the full genitive/dative-inflected paradigm for each of the three established
adjective-agreement classes from Unit 3 (`bun`-type 4-form, `mare`-type 2-form, `mic`-type 3-form,
and the nationality `-esc`-type 3-form `românesc/românească/românești`) — i.e. adjectives inflect
for case in lockstep with the noun they modify, not just for gender/number.
