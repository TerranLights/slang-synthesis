## Grammar points

### §157 — Future indicative
Three distinct formation strategies, in descending order of formality: (a) the literary/formal
future, with invariable-stem auxiliary forms *voi, vei, va, vom, veţi, vor* placed before the bare
infinitive of all four conjugations — full paradigms given for *a schimba, a putea, a face, a
simţi*. Footnote: object pronouns are placed immediately before the auxiliary in this form. (b) In
everyday speech this is replaced by two further-reduced strategies: (1) the present indicative of *a
avea* "to have" (*am, ai, are, avem, aveţi, au*) plus the *să*-subjunctive of the main verb — a
genuinely striking grammaticalization path (possession auxiliary → future marker), parallel to how
French/Romance futures historically grammaticalized from "have to do" periphrases, worth flagging for
deeper diachronic-mechanics interest later; (2) the invariable *o* plus *să*-subjunctive, an even
further-reduced colloquial form that doesn't conjugate for person at all. Both colloquial strategies
place the object pronoun immediately before the main verb rather than before the auxiliary.

### §158 — Uses of the future indicative
Generally parallel to other Romance languages, except the *avea*-plus-subjunctive form (§157b.1) can
carry a sense of obligation rather than pure futurity, e.g. *Are să studieze lecţiile* "He has to
study the lessons" vs. the plain *Am să mănânc la două* "I shall eat at two." A further everyday
construction — *a fi* plus the present participle (*Va fi făcând un oarecare lucru* "He is probably
doing some sort of work") or its reduced form *o fi* plus participle (*O fi mâncând* "He must be
eating") — expresses doubt or probability about a present situation using future-tense morphology, a
genuinely interesting epistemic/evidential extension of the future tense, cross-referenced by the
book itself to Lesson XXIII for further treatment.

### §159 — Future anterior
Formed with the future of *a fi* ("to be," using the *voi*-series) plus the past participle of the
main verb: *voi fi schimbat* "I will have changed," *voi fi putut* "I will have been able," *voi fi
făcut* "I will have made," *voi fi simţit* "I will have felt."

### §160 — Uses of the future anterior
Used as in other Romance languages, to refer to a point of time that will be past from the vantage of
some future reference point, e.g. *Vi-l va da când va fi isprăvit* "He will give it to you when he
will have finished."
