## Grammar points

### 1. The imperfect tense ("I was working", "I used to work")

A second past tense, distinct from the compound past (Unit 9), used for continuous or habitual past
action. Formed by endings on the infinitive/`to`-stem, not by an auxiliary. `-a`/`-ea` verbs add
`-m, -i, (none), -m, -ți, -u` (`a lucra→lucram, lucrai, lucra, lucram, lucrați, lucrau`); verbs in
`-e`/`-ui` add `-am, -ai, -a, -am, -ați, -au` (`a merge→mergeam`; `a locui→locuiam`); verbs in `-î`
drop the `-î` first (`a urî→uram`); verbs in `-i` replace final `-i` with `-e` before the same
ending set (`a dori→doream`). Several high-frequency verbs are irregular: `a fi→eram`, `a sta→
stăteam`, `a da→dădeam`, `a bate→băteam`, `a face→făceam`, `a ști→știam`, `a vrea→voiam`, `a
trebui→trebuia`. Note the semantic-duration contrast `de mult` "for some time" vs. `de puțin` "for a
short time" used with this tense (`Căutam de mult un apartament` "I had been looking for a flat for
some time") — pp.109–112.

### 2. Indirect object ("to me", "to you") pronouns — the dative clitic system

A fourth distinct pronoun paradigm (after reflexive, direct-object unstressed, and direct-object
stressed), expressed by one word with no preposition needed: `îmi, îți, îi, își` (sg.) / `ne, vă,
le` (pl.) "to me/you/him-her(-self)/us/you/them". These precede the verb in the present but, as with
direct-object clitics, attach/contract with the past-tense auxiliary (`el îmi dă→el mi-a dat` "he
gave me") and, after `să`/`ce`/`nu`, attach with a preceding hyphen (`să-mi`, `ce-i`, `nu-ți`). `ne`
and `vă` are ambiguous with the direct-object paradigm (same forms serve "us"/"you" in both
systems). As with direct objects, these dative clitics also appear "in support of" a full noun
phrase with no literal English translation (`I-am spus lui George` "I told George", literally
"to-him I-told George") — pp.112–114.

### 3. `a trebui` and `a plăcea`: dative-subject ("experiencer") verbs

`a trebui` "to need" (English "I need an X") is expressed as `Îmi trebuie un X` (literally "to-me is
necessary an X") — `a trebui` keeps one fixed present-tense form regardless of person; an equivalent
synonym construction is `am nevoie de` "I have need of". `a plăcea` "to like" (English "I like X")
likewise inverts subject/object onto `Îmi place X` (literally "to-me X pleases"), with a distinct
plural present form `plac` when the liked thing is plural (`Îmi plac rochiile` "I like the
dresses") — p.114.

### 4. Dative/reflexive verbs: "to me/to you" fused onto the verb

A small closed class of verbs obligatorily take a dative-reflexive pronoun as part of their basic
meaning, not matching any reflexive sense in English: `a-și imagina`/`a-și închipui` "to imagine",
`a-și aminti`/`a-și aduce aminte` "to remember" — distinct from the ordinary reflexives of Unit 7.
Full present-tense paradigms given for all three — p.115.

### 5. The dative-experiencer idiom family: bodily/emotional states as "to me is X"

A systematic pattern distinct from English's subject-experiencer phrasing: physical/emotional states
are expressed with the dative pronoun + `e`/`i` (reduced `este`) + a noun, rather than "I am X":
`Mi-e foame` "I am hungry" (lit. "to-me is hunger"), `Mi-e sete` "I am thirsty", `Mi-e somn` "I am
sleepy", `Mi-e rău` "I feel ill", `Ți-e greu` "it is difficult for you", `Ne e frig` "we are cold",
`Vă e cald?` "are you warm?", `I-e frică` "he/she is afraid", `Mi-e dor (de)...` "I miss..." — p.115–116.

### 6. `tot`: "all"/"every"

`tot` is an adjective agreeing with its noun (which itself must be in the definite-article form):
`tot anul` "the whole year" (m.), `toată strada` "the whole street" (f.), `tot timpul` "all the
time" (n.); plural `toți englezii` "all Englishmen", `toate femeile` "all the women". `tot`/`toată`
can stand alone as a pronoun too (`Tot ce faci este greșit` "Everything you do is mistaken"; `toți
sunt eroi` "all are heroes"). A fuller genitive/dative form `tuturor` ("of all the"/"to all the")
occasionally occurs, though `a toată`/`la toată` + `tot`/`toată` more commonly cover those meanings
in practice — pp.116–117.
