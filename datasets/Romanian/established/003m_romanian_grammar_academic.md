## Grammar points

### Adverbs: invariable, often identical to the masculine-singular adjective (§5.1)

Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs and never inflect. Many adverbs are formally
identical to a corresponding adjective's masculine singular form, distinguished only by syntactic
function (clar "clear"/adj. vs. "clearly"/adv.). The productive suffix `-eşte` derives manner adverbs
from nouns denoting people/nationalities (bărbat → bărbăteşte, român → româneşte) — this is the same
derivational family already encountered as the adjective-derived adverb româneşte in file `003d`'s
grammar point on românesc. Indefinite adverbs layer the familiar ori-/-va apparatus (already seen for
pronouns in file `003f`) onto the basic interrogative adverbs (unde → undeva, oriunde), with a further
alt- layer for differentiation (altundeva). A small set of adverbs can even take diminutive suffixes
(-el/-or/-şor: uşor → uşurel), an affect-marking device not usually associated with adverbs cross-
linguistically.

### Adverbs with grammatical/morphological function (§5.2)

Beyond their lexical meaning, mai, puţin, and foarte are the structural building blocks of the
adjective/adverb comparative and superlative system already documented in files `003d` and below. A
separate small set of short aspectual adverbs (mai "again/still," şi "also," tot "continuing," nu prea
"not really") attach directly to the verb complex, placed before a bare verb form, after the negation
nu when the verb is negated, or inserted between an auxiliary and the main verb in compound forms —
mirroring exactly the modal-adverb-insertion slots already documented throughout the verb chapter
(files `003h`–`003l`). mai specifically can fuse directly into negated past-participle/gerund forms
(nemaivăzut "unseen before").

### Interrogative adverbs double as relative connectors (§5.3)

unde, când, cum, cât function as ordinary question words and, in subordinate clauses, as relative
adverbs linking main and subordinate clauses while playing a real syntactic role — directly paralleling
the interrogative/relative pronoun overlap already documented in file `003f`. Each can form a fixed
correlative pair with a demonstrative-flavored adverb in the main clause (atunci când, acolo unde, aşa
cum, atât cât).

### Adverbial phrases without an adverb at their core (§5.4)

A substantial inventory of multi-word phrases function adverbially despite containing no adverb
morpheme at all — preposition+noun(+adjective), preposition+pronoun, preposition+adjective,
preposition+supine, pronoun+verb+adverb, adjective+preposition, and reduplicated-word patterns — each
replaceable by a single synonymous adverb in discourse (fără îndoială ~ indubitabil/neîndoielnic; cu
totul ~ complet/totalmente; zi de zi ~ zilnic). The reduplicated-word subtype includes a genuinely
colorful onomatopoeic item, pâş-pâş "slowly, quietly," worth flagging for the slang/colloquial corpus
phase.

### Semantic classes of adverbs (§5.5)

The source catalogs adverbs by meaning into eight classes — place, time, manner, quantity, affirmation,
negation, reinforcement, restriction, and modality — each inventoried in the vocabulary table above.
Two items stand out as genuinely idiomatic, colorful, register-marked content rather than neutral
grammar vocabulary: `la dracu-n praznic` ("at the devil's feast," meaning "very far away" — directly
continuing the dracu/taboo-adjacent thread from file `003k`) under place adverbs, and `când o face
plopul pere` ("when the poplar tree bears pears," i.e. "never" — a folk-idiom structurally identical to
English "when pigs fly") under negation adverbs.

### Adverb comparison: identical machinery to adjectives, same register flags (§5.6)

Adverbs form comparative/superlative degrees with the exact same structural apparatus already fully
documented for adjectives in file `003d` (§3.3.4) — mai+decât/ca for superiority, tot aşa de/tot atât
de/la fel de+ca/ca şi for equality, mai puţin+decât/ca for inferiority, cel mai(+dintre/din) for the
relative superlative of superiority, cel mai puţin for the relative superlative of inferiority, and
foarte/tare/extrem de/cât se poate de for the absolute superlative. The source repeats, verbatim in
substance, the same two register flags already captured for adjectives: `ca` is more frequent than
`decât` in colloquial speech, and `tare` is specifically colloquial-register (here additionally noted
as carrying its own emotional coloring) among the absolute-superlative intensifiers. A closed list of
Latinate adverbs cannot form degrees of comparison at all (superior, inferior, major, minor, anterior,
ulterior, optim, ultim, suprem, perfect, desăvârşit, asemenea, unic) — the same list, functionally, as
the non-comparable adjectives already given in file `003d`.
