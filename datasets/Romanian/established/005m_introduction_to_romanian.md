## Grammar points

### §138 — Past participle
Formed with a fixed ending per conjugation: 1st → *-at*, 2nd → *-ut*, 3rd → *-ut*, 4th → *-it*
(regular) or *-ît* for the small r-stem subclass. Captured as structural pattern, consistent with
the present-tense conjugation-marker system already documented in Lesson VII.

### §139 — Past participles of the irregular verbs
Nine verbs (*a da, a bea, a lua, a mânca, a rămânea, a scrie, a sta, a şti, a vrea*) plus the
auxiliaries *a avea* and *a fi* — their irregular past participles captured above as vocabulary.
Footnote: these comprise practically all of Romanian's irregular verbs, a genuinely useful closed-set
fact. Footnote 2: *a rămânea* and many 3rd-conjugation verbs with a consonant-final stem (mostly c,
d, g, n) form their past participle in *-s* instead (*merge → mers* "walked"), flagged by the book as
a pattern it will mark per-verb in later vocabularies — worth watching for in subsequent lessons'
vocabulary notes.

### §140 — Agreement of past participle
Invariable in the active-voice conjugation (the past indefinite covered in §142 below). Only agrees
in gender/number with the subject when the verb is conjugated with *a fi* "to be" (i.e., the passive
voice, covered later).

### §141 — Past participle as supine
When preceded by a preposition (mostly *de*), the past participle functions like the Latin supine —
equivalent to an English infinitive, e.g. *E greu de făcut* "It is difficult to make." Other supine
uses deferred to Lesson XXIV.

### §142 — Past indefinite
Formed with *a avea*'s present-indicative forms (*am, ai, a, am, aţi, au*) as auxiliary plus the main
verb's past participle — structurally Romanian's compound past/present perfect, used as the default
past tense in speech. Full paradigm tables given for *a schimba, a putea, a face, a simţi, a avea, a
fi*.

### §143 — Uses of past indefinite
Indicates an action begun in the past and considered complete at the moment of speaking — Romanian's
functional equivalent of both the English present perfect and simple past, e.g. *Ai citit cartea
această? Da, am citit-o* "Have you read this book? Yes, I have read it." Can also substitute for a
simple past/preterite when the action isn't felt as remote from the present. Footnote: can even be
used instead of a future or future-perfect to vividly anticipate a completed action, e.g. *Scriu
scrisoarea şi m'am dus* "I write the letter and I have gone" (meaning: as soon as I finish writing,
I'll leave) — a genuinely interesting proleptic/vivid-future use of a nominally past tense, worth
flagging for later register/narrative-mechanics analysis as a marked, colloquial-feeling construction.

### §144 — Expressions for time of day
*Cât e ceasul?* "What time is it?" with answers built from cardinal numbers plus *şi* "and" (past the
hour) or *fără* "without/minus" (before the hour): *E trei şi jumătate* "It's three-thirty," *Patru şi
un sfert* "Four fifteen," *Cinci şi douăzeci* "Fifteen after five" (idiomatically, not literally
"twenty," interesting usage worth a second look if time permits later), *Opt fără zece* "Ten minutes
of eight," *Şapte punct* "Exactly seven."
