## Grammar points

### The infinitive as subject, attribute, and circumstantial — and its reflexive-passive instructional use (§4.3.1)

Beyond the uses already documented in file `003h` (dictionary form, compound-tense building block), the
infinitive preceded by `a` can function as a full sentence subject (A citi este marea ei bucurie.
"Reading is her biggest joy.") or, governed by a preposition, as an attribute or circumstantial of
time/purpose/modality (Plăcerea de a vorbi cu ea..., înainte de a gândi, pentru a mă întrista, fără a
comunica). A dedicated construction — present infinitive + reflexive pronoun `se`, with `a` — is the
standard register for written instructions, warnings, and prohibitions (A se păstra la rece. "Store in
a cold place."; A se feri de foc. "Keep away from open flame."; A nu se lăsa la îndemâna copiilor. "Keep
out of reach of children.") — a genuinely distinct register from ordinary spoken infinitive use, worth
flagging as the formal/institutional-instruction register. The bare infinitive (no `a`) substitutes for
the subjunctive after a putea and, rarely and regionally, after a şti — an explicit regional-register
note — and appears in a avea + interrogative-pronoun constructions marking the absence of means,
mirroring the subjunctive-based version already documented in file `003k`.

### Past participle as a fully productive four-form adjective (§4.3.2)

Reiterating and extending file `003h`'s §4.1.4: any past participle (e.g. apreciat "valued, appreciated"
from a aprecia) functions as an ordinary four-form adjective, fully agreeing in gender/number/case with
its modified noun.

### The gerund: suffix choice, clitic attachment, and negation (§4.3.3)

The gerund takes `-ând` (verbs in -a, -ea, -e, -î) or `-ind` (verbs in -i, plus a specific list of
exceptions ending in -chea/-ghea/-ia and a scrie's whole derivational family, which take -ind despite
not ending in -i). Nine irregular verbs have dedicated gerund forms (fiind, având, stând, dând, luând,
mâncând, bând, ştiind, vrând). Personal and reflexive pronoun clitics attach *after* the gerund with
obligatory hyphenation, triggering a final linking -u on the gerund itself (văzând → văzându-mă) —
except the 3rd-singular feminine accusative `o`, which attaches without the linking vowel (văzând-o).
Reflexive-pronoun attachment is notable because it gives the gerund — ordinarily a non-personal,
person-neutral mood — a way to still mark grammatical person (îmbrăcându-mă vs. îmbrăcându-te vs.
îmbrăcându-se, etc.). Double-clitic combinations (dative+accusative) attach together, dative first,
exactly paralleling the imperative's double-clitic rule from file `003k`. Negation is derivational, with
ne- prefixed directly onto the gerund (lucrând → nelucrând), and can itself host the adverb mai between
the negative prefix and the gerund stem (nevăzând → nemaivăzând "not seeing anymore").

### Gerund usage: supplemental predicate and circumstantial of manner/cause/condition/time (§4.3.3.2)

The gerund functions as a supplemental predicative element (L-am găsit lucrând. "I found him working.")
or a circumstantial clause of manner, cause, condition, or time — the source gives one worked example
per relation and glosses each with its full paraphrase (nesimţindu-mă bine = pentru că nu m-am simţit
bine "because I didn't feel well," exersând = dacă vei exersa "if you practice," etc.), plus a rare
attributive use that agrees with its noun (o mână tremurândă "a shaking hand") and a standard use after
perception verbs (Am văzut-o trecând. "I saw her passing by."). Romanian's gerund is never preceded by
a preposition (unlike the English gerund, which commonly follows one), and while its implicit subject
usually matches the main clause's subject, the two can differ (Te-am văzut ieşind din clădire. "I saw
you leaving the building.").

### The supine: past-participle form, nominal function, governed by prepositions (§4.3.4)

Formally identical to the past participle but always preposition-governed and functioning as a noun
(not an adjective, unlike the participle) — the Romanian grammatical-tradition analog of the English
infinitive/-ing-form-as-noun. Most frequently governed by `de` after a fi/a avea to express obligation
or anticipated action (Am de lucrat mult. "I have a lot to do."), but also after a specific closed set
of aspectual/completive verbs (a termina de, a se apuca de, a se pune pe [explicitly glossed "very
intensely"], a se pregăti de, a fi în stare de, a se sătura de), after adjectives/adverbs (Carnea e bună
de mâncat. "The meat is good to eat."), with an implied/elided repeated verb (De înţeles, înţeleg... "As
for understanding, I do understand..."), and — very productively — in noun+supine compounds naming an
instrument by its purpose (maşină de scris "typewriter," fier de călcat "iron," aparat de fotografiat
"camera," ochelari de citit "reading glasses") or, negated, forming a modal "cannot be X-ed" adjective
phrase (de neuitat "unforgettable," de neiertat "unforgivable").

### Reflexive voice: three semantic classes sharing one pronoun-attachment mechanism (§4.4.1.1)

Reiterating the reflexive-pronoun paradigm already given in file `003e` (§3.4.3), this chapter
classifies reflexive *verbs* (as opposed to merely reflexively-used pronouns) into three semantic
groups: (1) **subject-affecting** reflexives, where the action's result targets the subject itself (a
se bucura, a se gândi, a se îmbăta, a se odihni, a se îmbrăca, a se culca, a se scula, a se spăla, plus
a dative-reflexive subgroup a-şi aminti/a-şi dori/a-şi imagina/a-şi închipui/a-şi reveni); (2)
**reciprocal** reflexives, where two logical subjects each direct the action at the other (a se bate, a
se certa, a se cunoaşte, a se iubi, a se întâlni, a se împrieteni, a se înţelege, a se saluta, a se
vedea, plus dative-reflexive a-şi dărui/a-şi împărtăşi/a-şi povesti); and (3) **passive/impersonal**-
meaning reflexives (a se construi "to be built," a se vinde "to be sold," se spune "it is said," nu se
aude "it can't be heard").

### Semantic drift between active and reflexive forms of the same verb (§4.4.1.2)

Most verbs that have both an active and a reflexive form simply differ by which entity the action
targets (a spăla ceva "to wash something" vs. a se spăla "to wash oneself"; a îmbrăca pe cineva "to
dress someone" vs. a se îmbrăca "to dress oneself"; a întâlni pe cineva "to meet someone" vs. a se
întâlni cu cineva "to meet reciprocally"; a vinde ceva "to sell something" vs. a se vinde "to be sold");
a closed set of verbs (a se baza pe, a se căi, a se răzgândi, a se sfii, a se lamenta) exist **only** in
reflexive form with no active counterpart at all. More strikingly, a small set of verb pairs have
**genuinely diverged in meaning** between active and reflexive: a afla "to find out" vs. a se afla "to
be (located)"; a uita "to forget" vs. a se uita "to look, watch" (now one of the most frequent everyday
verbs in the language, entirely disconnected semantically from its active counterpart); and a găti "to
cook" vs. a se găti "to dress up, get dolled up" — the last pair explicitly marked by the source itself
as "(iron.)," i.e. carrying an ironic connotation, a genuine source-flagged register annotation.

### Passive voice: two mechanisms, periphrastic and reflexive-impersonal (§4.4.2)

Romanian forms the passive either (1) periphrastically, replacing the active transitive verb with the
corresponding mood/tense of a fi + the past participle (agreeing with the new subject's gender/number),
optionally naming the original agent via de/de către + noun — fully parallel to English "by" passives
— or (2) via the reflexive-impersonal `se` + 3rd person of the active verb, which the source
specifically notes carries an *impersonal* flavor distinct from the periphrastic passive (Conferinţa se
va organiza la Bucureşti. "The conference will be organized in Bucharest," with no sense of a specific
agent even implicitly available) — essentially two passives with a subtle agentivity/impersonality
contrast between them, not freely interchangeable stylistic variants.

### Impersonal and unipersonal verbs: a closed functional inventory (§4.5)

A cross-cutting class of verbs — active or reflexive, restricted to 3rd person singular (some, like a
ploua/a ninge, lack other-person forms altogether) — covers weather phenomena (a ploua, a fulgera, a
tuna, a ninge, a se înnora, a se însenina), time-of-day transitions (a se lumina, a se întuneca, a se
însera), and social-permission/custom/chance concepts (a se cuveni, a se cădea, a se întâmpla, a se
putea), plus a family of impersonal reflexive "people say" reporting constructions (se vorbeşte că, se
spune că, se zvoneşte că — the last specifically rumor-flavored) and apparent-evidence constructions (se
vede că, se pare că). The class additionally includes ordinary active verbs reflexivized in the 3rd
person to express a generic/impersonal "one, people" subject (În acest restaurant se mănâncă bine. "You
[people in general] can eat well in this restaurant.").
