## Grammar points

### Stressed indirect pronouns (dative emphasis forms) — Unit 12, pp. 146–148 — **flagship case-system finding, completes Unit 11's indirect-pronoun system**

Parallel to Unit 11's stressed direct-object forms (`pe mine` etc.), Romanian has a fully distinct
set of **stressed indirect-object (dative) pronouns**: `mie` (to me), `ție`/`dumitale` (to you),
`lui` (to him), `ei` (to her), `nouă` (to us), `vouă`/`dvs.` (to you pl.), `lor` (to them, both
genders). Unlike the stressed direct-object set, **these do not take `pe`** and almost always
co-occur with their unstressed counterpart rather than replacing it, purely for emphasis: `tu
le-ai scris lor?` "have you written to THEM?" The reflexive stressed form `sie` is "rarely used,"
with `lui`/`ei`/`lor` substituting instead (`el i-a cumpărat Anei o batistă, iar lui și-a luat o
cravată` "he bought Ana a handkerchief, and for himself bought a tie"). **Two additional uses
flagged explicitly**: (1) these stressed forms are required after the three near-synonymous
prepositions `datorită`, `grație`, `mulțumită` (all ≈ "thanks to/because of"): `mulțumită vouă am
reușit să prind trenul` "thanks to you I managed to catch the train"; (2) **masculine proper names**
take `lui` to mark possession or dative case where a feminine name just inflects directly:
`i-ai cerut părerea lui Călin?` "did you ask Călin's opinion?" vs. `nu i-am scris Adrianei` "I
haven't written to Adriana" (feminine name inflects on its own, no `lui`-equivalent particle
needed). The source also notes that when two recipients of differing or same gender are referred to
by one clitic, **the clitic often just agrees with the nearer noun** rather than pluralizing
strictly (`spuneți-i și lui Radu și lui Carmen să vină` "tell both Radu and Carmen to come" — singular
`-i` despite two addressees).

### The quantifier `tot` ("all," "every") — Unit 12, p. 149

`tot` precedes a definite-article-bearing noun and inflects for gender/number (`tot`/`toată`/
`toți`/`toate`), but **collapses to a single invariant plural form `tuturor`** in the possessive/
indirect-object case regardless of gender: `le-am spus tuturor copiilor adevărul` "I told all the
children the truth." `toată lumea` ("everyone," lit. "all the world") is given as a fixed idiom.

### Passive voice — Unit 12, pp. 149–150

Formed periphrastically from the appropriate tense of `a fi` ("to be") plus the past participle,
which **agrees with the subject in gender/number like an adjective** (not left invariant): `sunteți
invitați` "you [masc. pl.] are invited," `ocazia trebuie să fie sărbătorită` "the occasion [fem.
sg.] must be celebrated" — `sărbătorită` takes the feminine ending to agree with `ocazia`, distinct
from the masculine-plural `invitați` earlier in the same dialogue. The agent of a passive is
introduced by `de` ("by"): `pachetul a fost trimis de el` "the parcel was sent by him."
