## Grammar points

### 1. Emphatic ("stressed") dative pronouns

Parallel to the stressed direct-object pronouns of Unit 10, Romanian has emphatic counterparts to
the unstressed dative/indirect-object clitics of Unit 11: `mie, ție, dumitale, lui, ei, (nouă, vouă,
dumneavoastră, lor, lor)`. These are optional, add emphasis, and always co-occur with the unstressed
clitic rather than replacing it — they may stand before or after the verb (`Mie îmi place...` "As
for me, I like..."). The emphatic reflexive `sie` is usually replaced in practice by `lui`/`ei`.
Three prepositions — `datorită`, `mulțumită`, `grație`, all roughly "because of"/"thanks to" — govern
this same emphatic dative set (`Datorită mie au pierdut trenul` "They missed the train because of
me") — pp.123–124.

### 2. Impersonal verbs with the dative clitic standing in for a grammatical subject

Certain verbs (`a se întâmpla` "to happen", `a se părea` "to seem") are **structurally impersonal**
in Romanian — they take no personal subject at all, and the experiencer is expressed only via the
dative clitic: `mi se pare` "it seems to me" (not a literal "I seem"), `mi se întâmplă` "it happens
to me". The same pattern extends to a wider idiom family covering sudden-onset physical states in
the past (`i s-a făcut foame` "he/she got hungry", `ni s-a făcut rău` "we became ill") — building
directly on the Unit 11 `mi-e foame`-type dative-experiencer pattern but in impersonal-reflexive
form. The same impersonal dative-clitic strategy is also used to avoid naming an agent/attributing a
statement to a specific person, corresponding to an English passive (`Mi s-a spus că au sosit` "I
was told that they had arrived", lit. "to-me it-was-said") — pp.125–126.

### 3. The conditional mood ("I would")

Formed with a dedicated auxiliary set placed directly before the bare infinitive (without `a`): `aș,
ai, ar, am, ați, ar` + infinitive (`aș da` "I would give", `ar vinde` "he/she would sell", `aș
vorbi` "I would speak"). Common after `dacă` "if" — and the source flags an explicit tense mismatch
with English here: English `if` + past tense pairs with Romanian `dacă` + **conditional**, not a
past form (`Dacă aș avea timp aș merge la birou` "If I had time I would go to the office"). The same
reduced dative-clitic forms from Units 11–12 combine with this conditional exactly as with the past
tense (`Dacă ar cere o mână de ajutor, l-am ajuta` "If he asked for help, we would help him") —
p.127.

### 4. "I would have": conditional perfect

Formed from `aș fi` "I would be" + past participle: `aș fi dat` "I would have given", `ar fi vândut`
"he/she would have sold", `am fi vorbit` "we would have spoken". **In colloquial conversation, the
imperfect tense can substitute for this entire construction** — another register-conditioned
simplification pattern (cf. the numeral/pronoun/demonstrative clippings already catalogued):
`Puteai să le scrii?` "You could have written to them?" stands in for the fuller conditional-perfect.
Conversely, the source notes English `would` in reported speech sometimes maps onto a plain Romanian
**future**, not a conditional, when the original statement used a future (`Am spus că o să vin` "I
said that I would come") — pp.127–128.

### 5. The negative-pronoun/adverb system: `nimic`, `nimeni`, `niciodată`, `nici`, `nicăieri`

A small closed paradigm — `nimic` "nothing", `nimeni` "nobody", `niciodată` "never", `nici`
"neither/nor", `nici un/o` "not one", `nicăieri` "nowhere" — all of which **require accompanying
`nu` before the verb** (true double/multiple negation, unlike standard English): `Eu nu aud nimic`
"I hear nothing" (lit. "I don't hear nothing"), `Nu l-am găsit nicăieri` "I couldn't find him
anywhere", `Nimeni nu ne iubește` "Nobody loves us" — p.129.
