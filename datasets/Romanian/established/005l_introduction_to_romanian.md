## Grammar points

### §134 — Forms of the unstressed pronouns
Full declension tables (captured above as vocabulary) for the accusative (direct object) and dative
(indirect object) clitics, singular and plural, including every phonologically-conditioned allomorph:
pre-verb vs. pre-vowel forms (*mă*/*m'*), î-initial "full" forms used clause-initially or after a
word ending in a consonant (*îmi, îţi, îl, îi*) vs. reduced forms used elsewhere, and distinct forms
used specifically before another clitic pronoun. This allomorphy is conditioned by adjacent sounds in
a genuinely systematic way — a rich seam for later phonotactic/contraction-pattern mechanics
analysis, since colloquial contraction of clitic clusters is exactly the kind of process that tends
to drive slang-register sound change.

### §135 — Position and uses of object pronouns
(a) The î-initial full forms (*îmi, îţi, îl, îi*) are used clause-initially, and when the preceding
word ends in a vowel and the following word begins with a consonant — but never immediately before
another pronoun. (b) Object pronouns precede the verb in all tenses except as noted in (c). (c)
Object pronouns follow the gerund and the affirmative imperative, with a supporting vowel *-u* added
to the gerund before attachment (*dându-mi* "giving to me") — but not before the feminine accusative
*o*, which instead follows the whole verb in compound tenses (d) (*am văzut-o* "I saw her"), a
genuine irregularity singled out as its own subrule. (e) When two object pronouns combine, dative
precedes accusative (*i l-am dat* "I gave it to him"). (f) The adverbs *cam, mai, prea* can separate
a clitic pronoun from its verb (*Îi cam jicnesc* "I sort of vex them"). (g) A dative clitic placed
immediately after a noun or adverb (hyphenated) can express possession instead of its usual indirect-
object sense, e.g. *Părul-mi cade mereu* "My hair is constantly falling" (lit. "the-hair-to-me falls
constantly") — a genuinely interesting possessive-via-dative-clitic construction, distinct from the
Lesson VIII possessive-adjective-replacement pattern, worth flagging for mechanics analysis.

### §136 — Repetition of unstressed personal pronouns
The dative clitic is commonly doubled/repeated with its own coreferent: (a) with a noun object in the
accusative or dative, whether that noun opens the sentence or precedes the verb (*Copiilor le place
îngheţata* "Children like ice cream," lit. "To-the-children to-them is-pleasing the-ice-cream") — a
genuine clitic-doubling phenomenon, directly relevant to understanding colloquial emphasis patterns.
(b) Across coordinated verbs sharing one object. (c) In compound tenses when the auxiliary itself is
repeated. (d) With an interrogative or relative pronoun (*Cui i-ai spus...* "To whom did you tell...").

### §137 — Neuter pronoun *o*
Romanian renders the English neuter "it" (referring to a preceding or following fact/situation, not a
specific noun) with *o*, e.g. *Se plângea cât de rău a nimerit-o cu măritişul fetei* "He was
complaining about how badly he had done with his daughter's marriage." Distinct in function from the
feminine accusative clitic *o* covered in §134/§135d, though formally identical — worth flagging as a
homograph with two distinct grammatical functions.
