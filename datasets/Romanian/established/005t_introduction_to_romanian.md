## Grammar points

### §169 — Cardinal numbers (continued from Lesson X)
Full series from 20 upward (captured above as vocabulary), with the *şi* "and" conjunction joining
tens and units from 20 on (distinct from the compound-without-conjunction 11–19 "-sprezece" forms
documented in Lesson X). *Sută* "hundred" and *mie* "thousand" decline as regular nouns. Numbers 20+
require the partitive linker *de* before the noun they modify, but *sută*/*mie* skip *de* when
directly followed by a number 1–19 (though not when followed by a multiple of ten) — a fairly
intricate but systematic rule. Footnote: approximate numbers use *vreo* "some" plus *până la* "up to."

### §170 — Uses of cardinal numbers
(a) For centuries, either as a bare cardinal (*secolul douăzeci* "the twentieth century") or with the
possessive-article-plus-ordinal construction (*secolul al douăzecilea*) — a genuine doublet worth
flagging. (b) For days of the month (*zece Mai* "May tenth"); when *lună* "month" precedes the month
name, it takes the possessive article *al* (*Treizeci ale lunii Iunie* "The thirtieth of the month of
June").

### §171 — Indefinite adjectives
A closed list the book itself claims comprises practically all indefinite adjectives in Romanian:
*alt* "other," *atare*/*cutare* "such, someone," *atât* "so much, so many," *câtva* "a few, a little,
some," *destul* "enough," *fiecare*/*fiece* "each, every," *mult* "much, many," *oarecare*/*oricare*/
*orişicare*/*orice*/*orişice*/*oricine*/*orşicine* "any...at all / anyone," *puţin* "little, few,"
*tot* "all, any, entire." Full declension tables given for each (captured in condensed form above as
vocabulary rather than reproduced verbatim, per copyright discipline). Several share a structural
pattern worth flagging as a recurring Romanian morphosyntax rule: when the N.-A. singular form of an
indefinite adjective like *mult, puţin, destul* may precede or follow its noun, but the G.-D. plural
is always placed before the noun — the same word-order-conditions-declension pattern already seen
with demonstratives in Lesson XVIII. *Fiecare*/*ce* and *oarecare*-family words are declined exactly
like the interrogative/relative pronouns already documented (*care* in Lesson XV, *ce* and *cine* in
Lesson XIV), a nice confirmation of how productive those declension patterns are across word classes.
*Tot* has three special rules: it always triggers full declension of its noun; it's repeated before
every noun in a series rather than appearing once; and it's replaced by *întreg* "entire" specifically
when the noun carries an indefinite article.
