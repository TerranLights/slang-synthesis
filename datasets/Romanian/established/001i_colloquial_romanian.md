## Grammar points

### Indirect-object ("to me/you/him...") pronoun paradigm — Unit 11, pp. 130–134 — **flagship case-system finding**

A dedicated set of unstressed indirect-object pronouns, distinct from both the subject pronouns
(Unit 1) and the direct-object clitics (Unit 9): `îmi` (to me), `îți` (to you sg.), `îi` (to him/
her/it — same form for both genders), `ne` (to us — identical to the direct-object form), `vă` (to
you pl. — identical to direct-object), `le` (to them — covers both genders, unlike direct-object
`îi`/`le` split). The reflexive counterpart is `își`. **Five distinct use-cases the source lays
out explicitly**: (1) with impersonal `a trebui` ("to need") and `a plăcea` ("to please/like" — the
source's note: Romanian "I like X" is structurally "X is pleasing to me," `îmi plac munții` "we
like the mountains" lit. "the mountains are pleasing to us," with a genuine singular/plural verb
agreement contrast, `place`/`plac`); (2) as a **possession-marking alternative to possessive
adjectives**, often preferred in natural speech (`mi-am pierdut ceasul` "I've lost my watch," lit.
"to-me-self I-lost the-watch" — note this literally uses the reflexive-reduced form, not plain
`îmi`, when the possessor is also the subject performing the action, contrasted with `ei i-a pierdut
biletul` "he lost his [someone else's] ticket" using plain `i-`); (3) in support of/doubling a
full noun indirect object for emphasis (`mamei vreau să-i dau o farfurie` "I want to give mother a
plate" — `-i` doubles `mamei`); (4) with a small closed set of verbs that specifically require it,
e.g. `a-și permite` "to afford" (lit. "to permit to oneself"); (5) in a family of **impersonal
dative-subject idioms** for physical/emotional states: `mi-e somn` "I am sleepy" (lit. "to-me-is
sleep"), `mi-e foame`, `mi-e sete`, `mi-e frig`, `mi-e cald`, `mi-e frică`, `mi-e rău` — a highly
productive construction (seen already once in Unit 10's "mi-e foame," now given its full paradigm).
Before a vowel-initial verb, these pronouns reduce (`îmi`→`mi-`, `îți`→`ți-`, `îi`→`i-`, `își`→
`și-`), and after `să`/`nu`/`ca`/`și` they reduce the other direction, losing the initial `î`
(`-mi`, `-ți`, `-i`, `-și`): `el nu-mi dă cheia` "he doesn't give me the key."

### Stressed object pronouns and `pe`-marked differential object marking — Unit 11, pp. 135–137 — **flagship typological finding**

Romanian direct-object pronouns have a **second, stressed/emphatic form**, built by prefixing `pe`
to the ordinary subject pronoun: `pe mine` (me), `pe tine`/`pe dumneata` (you), `pe el`, `pe ea`,
`pe noi`, `pe voi`/`pe dvs.`, `pe ei`, `pe ele`, reflexive `pe sine`. These normally co-occur **with**
the ordinary unstressed clitic rather than replacing it, for added emphasis: `pe mine mă doare
piciorul, nu pe el` "MY foot hurts, not his." **The genuinely striking typological fact**: `pe` is
not just an emphasis marker — it is the obligatory marker for a **definite human direct object**
even with a full noun (not just a pronoun), a pattern called differential object marking, unusual
among Romance languages: `îl întreb pe Vasile` "I ask Vasile," `l-am văzut pe domnul Predescu` "I
saw Mr Predescu." `pe` here behaves like a preposition, and — mirroring the prepositional-definite-
article-dropping rule from Unit 2 — **the definite article is omitted after `pe` on an unqualified
noun** (`la vamă i-au controlat pe turiști` "they checked the tourists," not "pe turiștii") **but
restored once the noun is modified** (`pe turiștii americani` "the American tourists"). This
`pe`-marking-of-human-objects pattern is genuinely rare for a Romance language and a strong
candidate for the eventual mechanics-analysis phase.

### `să`-clauses as polite commands and verb-complementation — Unit 11, pp. 138–139

Beyond the want/can/must constructions already seen (Units 2, 4), `să` + subjunctive also expresses
a **polite indirect command**: `să te îmbraci mai repede` "get dressed more quickly," `vă rog să
începeți` "please begin." A specific list of verbs is flagged as taking `să`-complementation: `a
trebui`, `a plăcea`, `a încerca` ("to try"), `a continua` ("to continue"), `a îndrăzni` ("to dare"),
`a începe` ("to begin"). `ca să` (rather than plain `să`) specifically marks **purpose**: `Dumitru
s-a dus la Roma ca să vadă o prietenă` "Dumitru went to Rome (in order) to see a girlfriend."
