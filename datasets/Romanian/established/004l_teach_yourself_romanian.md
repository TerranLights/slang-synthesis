## Grammar points

### 1. The pluperfect tense: "I had seen", "I had slept"

Formed from the past participle (Unit 9), dropping the final `-t` where applicable, and adding a
dedicated ending set: `-sem, -seși, -se, -serăm, -serăți, -seră`. Examples across verb classes:
`cântasem` "I had sung", `făcusem` "I had done", `scrisesem` "I had written", `tăcusem` "I had kept
silent", `mersesem` "I had gone", `coborâsem` "I had descended". Several forms are modelled directly
on the irregular past participles of Unit 9: `fusesem` "I had been" (from `fost`), `dădusem` "I had
given" (from `dat`); `a avea` has **two parallel pluperfect stems**, `avusem` and `avusesem`, both
glossed "I had had". Two usage notes: `după ce` "after" is followed by the ordinary **past** tense in
Romanian where English often uses a pluperfect (`După ce am stat zece ore în tren...` "After we had
spent ten hours on the train..."); and Romanian uses the pluperfect **less often than English** in
reported speech, where a plain past tense often suffices (`Au crezut că am plecat fără umbrelă`
"They thought I had left without my umbrella") — pp.201–203.

### 2. The presumptive mood: "I might be going", "I might have gone", "I wonder if"

Two strategies express present/past possibility. (a) A conditional form of `a se putea` "to be able"
+ `să`: `S-ar putea să ajungem mâine` "We may/might arrive tomorrow"; "might have" uses `să fi` + past
participle: `S-ar putea ca George să fi vrut să schimbe niște dolari` "George might have wanted to
change some dollars". (b) A dedicated **presumptive mood**, built from future `voi fi` + present
participle (Unit 18) for "might be doing" (`voi fi mergând` "I might be going") and from `voi fi` +
past participle for "might have done" (`voi fi mers` "I might have gone") — a full person paradigm is
given (`voi/vei/va/vom/veți/vor fi mergând`/`mers`). **In colloquial speech `voi` reduces to `oi`**
throughout this paradigm (`va→o`, `vom→om`, `vor→or`) — another register-conditioned clipping
pattern in the same family already catalogued across several units. With `a fi` itself, the reduced
`o fi` (not `o fi fiind`) is the idiomatic choice, and doubles as **"I wonder"**: `Unde o fi Ana?`
"I wonder where Ana is?" (lit. "where might-be Ana"), equivalent to `Mă întreb unde este Ana?` or, with
the dedicated wonder-particle **`oare`**, `Oare unde este Ana?` — pp.203–205.

### 3. Position/cause prepositions governing the genitive ("of the") form

A substantial list of position and causal prepositions take their following noun in the "of
the"/genitive form (Unit 9): `în fața` "in front of", `în spatele` "at the back of", `în dosul` "at
the back of", `în dreapta`/`în stânga` "to the right/left of", `în fundul` "at the bottom of", `în
jurul` "around", `în urma` "following", `din cauza` "because of", `deasupra` "above" (already noted
in file 004c), `de-a lungul` "along", `în locul` "in place of", `lupta contra`/`lupta împotriva`
"the fight against". When the "of"-object would be *me, you, our* etc. rather than a full noun, the
matching **possessive adjective** form is used instead and must agree with the expression's own
(somewhat arbitrary) grammatical gender — expressions ending `-a` count as feminine, those ending
`-le`/`-ul` as neuter (`în fața mea` "in front of me", `în dreapta noastră` "on our right"); for
*him/her/them*, the invariant personal-possessive pronouns `lui/ei/lor` are used instead (`în fața
lor` "in front of them") — pp.205. `în stânga`/`în dreapta` also extend metaphorically to the
political "Left"/"Right" (`un partid de stânga` "a party of the Left") — p.205.
