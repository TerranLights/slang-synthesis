## Grammar points

### §116 — Adjective (mixed gender note)
Since there is no special adjective form for mixed-gender nouns (masculine in singular, feminine in
plural), adjectives are simply either masculine or feminine; they follow the noun's actual gender in
that number.

### §117 — Gender of the adjective
(a) Masculine adjectives share endings with masculine nouns in -u or a consonant (*albastru, bun*).
(b) Feminine is formed by adding -ă to the masculine, dropping a final -u (*albastră, bună*). (c)
Adjectives in -iu form feminine in -e (*grăsuliu* → *grăsulie*). (d) Adjectives in -ciu/-chiu form
feminine in -ce/-che (*dibaciu* → *dibace*; *vechiu* → *veche* — footnote: the -u of *vechiu* is kept
specifically to distinguish it from the masculine plural *vechi*, both pronounced [vek]). (e)
Adjectives in -tor form feminine in -toare (*folositor* → *folositoare*). (f) Diminutives in -el form
feminine in -ică, less often -ea (*mititel* → *mititică*/*mititea*). (g) A closed list of adjectives
ending in -e are invariable for gender in the singular (full list captured above as vocabulary). (h)
*greu, nou, rău* form feminine irregularly: *grea, nouă, rea*.

### §118 — Plural of adjectives
(a) Masculine plural in -i/-î (*albastru* → *albaştri*; *bun* → *buni*). (b) Feminine plural in -e
(*albastră* → *albastre*; *adâncă* → *adânce*). (c) Feminine adjectives in -toare are unchanged in
the plural (*săptămâna viitoare* → *săptămânile viitoare*). (d) Feminine diminutives in -ică/-ea drop
that ending and add -ele (*mititică/mititea* → *mititele*). (e) *greu, nou, rău* plural irregularly:
masc. *grei, noi, răi*; fem. *grele, nouă, rele*.

### §119 — Declension and position of adjectives
(a) A preceding adjective is declined and agrees with the noun; the noun itself stays undeclined —
full masculine and feminine N.-A./G.-D. paradigms given for *bunul copil*/*frumoasa casă* patterns.
(b) A following adjective stays undeclined (but still agrees in gender/number); the noun is declined
instead (*copilului bun*, *caselor frumoase*). (c) When preceded by an indefinite article or
demonstrative adjective, neither the adjective nor the noun declines — only the article/demonstrative
does (*un bun copil* → *unui copil bun*; *acest bun copil* → *acestui copil bun*). (d) **Semantic
split by position**: an adjective before the noun tends to carry a figurative sense, one after the
noun a literal sense — the book's worked example is *Săracul om!* "The poor man!" (to be pitied,
figurative) vs. *Omul sărac* "The poor man" (literally lacking money) — a genuinely useful register/
semantics distinction for later mechanics analysis, not just a word-order rule. (e) Most adjectives
follow the noun by default, but closed semantic classes may precede without a meaning shift: badness,
foolishness, frightfulness, goodness, intelligence, unhappiness, wonderfulness — full lists captured
above as vocabulary (note: *biet* is idiosyncratic within this system — always placed before its
noun, per the book's own footnote, not just optionally fronted like its classmates). (f) With two
adjectives modifying one noun, the less characteristic one precedes and the more significant one
follows, e.g. *Marea operă nouă de filozofie a profesorului nostru* "Our professor's great and new
philosophical work." (g) Alternatively, two adjectives can both follow the noun, joined by *şi*
"and," e.g. *O operă mare şi nouă* "A great new work."

### §120 — Noun used as adjective
A noun functioning adjectivally follows the noun it modifies and is introduced by *de*, e.g. *O
pălărie de paie* "A straw hat" — the Romanian equivalent of an English noun-adjunct compound.

### §121 — Agreement of adjective
(a) Agrees in gender and number with its noun in the general case. (b) When one adjective modifies
two nouns of different genders, the adjective takes masculine plural, e.g. *două fete şi doi băieţi
inteligenţi* "two intelligent girls and boys." (c) The invariable -e adjectives from §117g agree only
in number, not gender, e.g. *un băiat mare*/*băieţi mari* vs. *o fată mare*/*fete mari*.
