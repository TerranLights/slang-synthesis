## Grammar points

### Unit 63 — *To...*, *for...* and *so that...* (purpose)

Purpose-marking infinitive (both action-purpose and existence/possession-purpose senses), the
noun+*to...* "available/required action" construction, the categorical *for*+noun vs. *to*+verb
split (with the *What...for?* question frame and the *for ~ing* design-purpose variant), and the
three specific licensing conditions that force *so that* over a plain infinitive (see Vocabulary).

### Unit 64 — Adjective + *to...*

Four distinct adjective-plus-infinitive constructions sharing the same surface shape but different
functions: the object-raising evaluative-property construction (*he is difficult to understand*,
with its object-gap restriction), the character-judgment construction (*it was kind of you to...*),
the emotional-reaction construction (*I was surprised to hear...*), the ordinal/exclusivity
construction (*the first to know*), and the epistemic-confidence construction (*sure/bound/likely
to...*) — see Vocabulary for the full adjective sets licensing each.

### Unit 65 — *To...* (*afraid to do*) and preposition + ~ing (*afraid of ~ing*)

Three genuine minimal-pair polysemy splits hinging on infinitive-vs-preposition+gerund choice after
one surface word (*afraid, interested, sorry* — see Vocabulary for each), closing with the book's
own cross-reference summary of every other preposition-choice-changes-meaning pair introduced
earlier (Units 53–54, 61).

### Unit 66 — *See somebody do* and *see somebody doing*

The complete-action (bare infinitive) vs. partial/in-progress (gerund) contrast after perception
verbs (*see, hear, notice, feel, find*, and others), its active/passive infinitive-marker asymmetry,
and the cases where the distinction neutralizes under habitual/experiential use.

### Unit 67 — ~ing clauses (*Feeling tired, I went to bed early*)

Three distinct functions of the free-standing participial (~ing) clause: marking simultaneity (plain
~ing, main clause usually first), marking strict temporal sequence (*having (done)*, or plain ~ing
for two short sequential actions, clause usually first), and marking a reason/cause (plain ~ing or
*not having (done)*, clause usually first, paraphrasable with *because*) — all three flagged by the
source itself as more characteristic of written than spoken English.
