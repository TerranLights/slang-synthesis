## Grammar points

### Unit 120 — *At/on/in* (time)

The three-way clock-time/calendar-day/extended-period preposition system, a closed set of fixed
*at*-time idioms, the parts-of-day *in*→*on* preposition switch triggered by naming a specific day,
the zero-preposition rule before deictic time determiners, and the dual future-point/completion-
duration senses of *in* + a time span.

### Unit 121 — *On time*/*in time*; *At the end*/*in the end*

Two structurally similar-looking but semantically and grammatically unrelated idiom pairs: *on
time* (punctuality) vs. *in time* (margin before a deadline), and *at the end* (a bounded period's
terminal point) vs. *in the end* (the eventual outcome of a process) — the latter a genuine
false-friend trap despite sharing most of their surface wording.

### Units 122–123 — *In/at/on* (place) (1) and (2)

The core three-way spatial-preposition system (enclosure/*in*, point-landmark/*at*,
surface-contact/*on*), demonstrated through worked minimal triples on identical locations, extended
across two units' worth of fixed collocation sets for lines/printed-media/abstractions (*in*),
sides/floors/lists/geography (*on*), and two genuinely idiosyncratic lexically-conditioned
preposition switches (*corner*, and the three-way *front/back* split) that resist the general rule
entirely.
