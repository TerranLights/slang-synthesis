**Non-redundant-supplement note.** Checked `007_cmos_ch05_grammar_and_usage_pt07.md`, which catalogs
the modal auxiliaries (*can/could, may/might, must, ought, shall/should, will/would*) with brief
structural/register glosses (§5.143–5.150) — e.g. CMOS notes *can* for permission is "colloquial" and
that *should* "does not actually function as a true past tense of shall." That is the full extent of
CMOS's modal coverage: no epistemic-strength scale, no past-modal (*must have/can't have/should
have/could have*) system, no speech-act taxonomy (request/offer/permission/invitation), and no

## Grammar points

### Units 26–27 — *Can, could, (be) able to*; *could (do)* and *could have (done)*

*Can/could* for ability and possibility, with the general-vs-particular-occasion ability split (see
Vocabulary) and the hedged-suggestion/possibility sense of bare *could*. *Could have (done)* for
unrealized past possibility — near-misses and "would have been able to" readings.

### Unit 28 — *Must* and *can't* (epistemic)

A sense of *must*/*can't* distinct from obligation: logical certainty from evidence, both present
(*must/can't be*) and past (*must/can't have been*), with *couldn't have* as an accepted
*can't have* variant.

### Units 29–30 — *May and might* (1) and (2)

Present/past epistemic possibility (*may/might (have) been*), the *might not have* vs. *couldn't
have* logical-scope distinction (see Vocabulary), future possibility, the *might*-only unreal-
condition restriction, the *may/might be ~ing* continuous and its tentative-plan reading, and the
*might/may as well* idiom.

### Units 31–32 — *Must and have to*; *Must, mustn't, needn't*

The book's central modal-nuance teaching point: *must* (speaker's personal judgment of necessity)
vs. *have to* (external/impersonal necessity) — plus *must*'s defective paradigm forcing *have to*
into perfect/infinitival/questioned slots. The *mustn't* (prohibition) vs. *don't have to*/*needn't*
(no obligation) false-friend pair, and the *needn't have (done)* vs. *didn't need to (do)* hindsight
contrast (see Vocabulary for both).

### Units 33–34 — *Should* (1) and (2)

Advice/opinion *should*, deviation-from-expectation *should*, past-regret *should have (done)*, and
*ought to* as its formal synonym (Unit 33). Unit 34 covers the mandative/subjunctive-adjacent
*should* after directive verbs and evaluative adjectives, the hedging *if...should...* construction,
and first-person advisory *I should...* (see Vocabulary for the full set).

### Unit 35 — *Had better*; *It's time...*

*Had better*'s situation-specific, consequence-implying advisory force, contrasted with *should*'s
general/no-consequence advisory force; the *it's time (for X) to do Y* / *it's time X did Y*
overdue-action construction and its intensified *about time*/*high time* variants.

### Unit 36 — *Can/Could/Would you...?* (requests, offers, permission, invitations)

A full speech-act taxonomy built on the modal verbs already covered: requesting action, requesting
an object, requesting/granting permission, offering, and inviting — each with its own
conventionalized modal-verb choice and a formal/informal register gradient (*could* > *can*; *may* >
*could/can*). This systematic mapping from modal verb to speech act has no counterpart anywhere in
CMOS/Oxford's register-note-level treatment of the same modal verbs.
