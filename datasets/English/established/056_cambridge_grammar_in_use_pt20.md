## Grammar points

### Unit 107 — Superlatives: *the longest*/*the most enjoyable* etc.

The superlative morphology rule (mirroring Unit 104's comparative rule), the obligatory *the*
requirement, the family-restricted *eldest*/*oldest* pair, the *in*(place)/*of*(time) preposition
choice, the superlative+present-perfect collocation, and a wholly separate non-comparative "very"
sense of *most* + adjective sharing the identical surface form.

### Unit 108 — Word order (1): verb + object; place and time

The strict verb-object adjacency rule (barring any intervening word, even an adverb) and the default
place-before-time ordering of adverbials, with time frontable to the sentence's start.

### Unit 109 — Word order (2): adverbs with the verb

The full mid-position-adverb placement system: before a one-word ordinary verb but after *be*,
immediately after the first auxiliary in a multi-word verb phrase, with the quantifiers *all/both*
occupying the identical slot, and the same slot used again before an ellipted/anaphoric bare
auxiliary.

### Unit 110 — *Still*, *yet* and *already*; *Any more*/*any longer*, *no longer*

A four-way temporal/aspectual adverb system: *still* (unchanged continuation, mid-position),
*not...any more/any longer* (end-position change-of-state) and its mid-position synonym *no longer*,
*yet* (expectation of an eventual event, polarity-restricted, end-position), and *already*
(sooner-than-expected, mid-position) — plus the pragmatically-stronger *still...not* variant of plain
*not...yet*.

### Unit 111 — *Even*

*Even*'s core function marking an unusually extreme/unexpected instance, its mid-verb-position
placement (parallel to Unit 109's system), its comparative-intensifying use, the three fixed
concessive connectives built on it (which categorically require the connective word — bare *even*
cannot stand alone as one), and the *even if*/*if* minimal-pair contrast in conditional commitment.
