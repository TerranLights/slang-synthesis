## Grammar points

### Unit 136 — Phrasal verbs (*get up/break down/fill in* etc.)

The closed particle set combinable with verbs to form phrasal verbs, the distinction between
literal/compositional movement uses of a particle and fully idiomatic non-compositional meanings
(often sharing identical surface form with the literal use, as the triple polysemy of *get on*
across this unit demonstrates: board a vehicle / perform-fare / have a good relationship),
three-word phrasal-verb-plus-preposition combinations, and — the unit's single genuine syntactic
rule — the separable-phrasal-verb object-placement system: a full noun-phrase object may go in
either position relative to the particle, but a pronoun object is obligatorily placed between the
verb and the particle, never after it. This pronoun-forces-separation constraint is unique to this
construction among everything else catalogued across the whole book and is worth flagging for later
cross-linguistic mechanics comparison (a word-class-sensitive, not just semantically-sensitive,
syntactic placement rule).
