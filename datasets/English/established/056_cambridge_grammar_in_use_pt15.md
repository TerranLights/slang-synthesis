## Grammar points

### Unit 85 — *No/none/any*

The built-in-negative determiner/pronoun set and its ban on verb-negation stacking, the *no*+noun
vs. bare/*of*-governed *none* complementation split, and the shared singular-grammar/plural-anaphor
pattern with *nobody/no one*.

### Unit 86 — *Much, many, little, few, a lot, plenty*

The core count/mass quantifier split, the dual-countability *a lot/lots/plenty of*, the
register-based preference for *a lot (of)* over bare *much/many* in positive statements, and the
book's sharpest quantifier minimal pair — bare *little/few* (insufficiency) vs. *a little/a few*
(adequate amount), including the *only a little/few* implicature-reversal.

### Unit 87 — *All/all of, most/most of, no/none of* etc.

The bare-quantifier-plus-noun pattern (with bare *most*, never "the most"), the parallel *of*+
definite-phrase construction (with its obligatory-definiteness and optional/obligatory-*of*
sub-rules), and the generic-vs-specific minimal pair for *all* vs. *all (of) the*.

### Unit 88 — *Both/both of, neither/neither of, either/either of*

The dedicated two-referent quantifier triad, its *of*-construction (structurally parallel to Unit
87's many-referent system), the correlative-conjunction extensions (*both...and*, etc.), and the
book's own explicit statement of the two-item/many-item paradigmatic parallel with *any/none/all*.

### Unit 89 — *All, every and whole*

The restricted distribution of bare *all* against the default universal pronouns
(*everybody/everything*), the singular-grammar/plural-anaphor pattern for *every-* compounds, the
*whole*/*all* countability-conditioned contrast, and the frequency (*every*) vs. duration (*all
day*/*the whole day*) minimal pair.

### Unit 90 — *Each and every*

The book's central distributive minimal pair (individuating *each* vs. collective-generalizing
*every*, including the two-member and frequency-expression exceptions), their differing
stand-alone/*of*-complementation rules, *each*'s unique word-order flexibility (including the fixed
per-unit-price idiom), and the *everyone*/*every one* orthographic-semantic split.
