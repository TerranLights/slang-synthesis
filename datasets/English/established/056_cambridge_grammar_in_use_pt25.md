## Grammar points

### Unit 128 — Noun + preposition (*reason for, cause of* etc.)

Five fixed noun-plus-preposition collocation sets (*for, of, in, to*, and the *with*/*between*
complementation split), the last being the unit's most structurally interesting point: the same
relational-noun meaning requires a different preposition depending on whether the two related
parties are both named within the phrase or not.

### Units 129–130 — Adjective + preposition (1) and (2)

A large system of fixed adjective-plus-preposition collocations spanning *of, to, about, with,
at/by, for, in, on, from*, including several genuinely notable patterns: the *nice...of...to...*/
*nice...to...* complementation split sharing one adjective set across two distinct frames, the
*angry about*/*angry with...for* dual-complement split, the three-way polysemy of *sorry
about/for/for*, the lexically-arbitrary near-synonym splits (*full of* vs. *crowded with*), the
morphologically-related-antonym preposition flip (*dependent on* vs. *independent of*), and the
dialect-flagged *different from/to* (British) vs. *different from/than* (American) variation.
