## Grammar points

### Units 68–69 — Countable and uncountable nouns (1) and (2)

Core countable/uncountable article-and-quantifier behavior (see Vocabulary), the genuinely
semantic — not just syntactic — countable/uncountable doublet pattern (a closed set of nouns whose
two countability forms carry different meanings, not the same meaning in two guises), the beverage-
noun metonymic countability flip, and the closed cross-linguistically-mismatched-uncountable list
(a frequent, well-known learner-error source Murphy names explicitly).

### Unit 70 — Countable nouns with *a/an* and *some*

The classificatory function of *a/an* (including profession-naming, and the possessed-body-part
exception that bars *the*), and the two distinct senses of *some* before a plural countable noun
(unspecified-quantity vs. genuine-subset), with its categorical exclusion from true generic
statements.

### Unit 71 — *A/an* and *the*

The book's central discourse-based account of article choice: first-mention *a/an* vs.
subsequent-mention *the* once the referent is established as identifiable, extended to situationally
unique referents (a closed list of fixed situational nouns) and the unrelated distributive-rate use
of *a* (*once a week*).

### Unit 72 — *The* (1)

The uniqueness-triggers-*the* rule and six specific lexicalized exception/extension patterns:
fixed-*the* abstract-location nouns vs. bare cosmic *space*, the fixed *the same* idiom, the
three-way cinema/theatre/radio/television article idiosyncrasy, the bare-meal-noun-except-with-an-
adjective rule, and the zero-article noun+number exception to uniqueness-implies-*the*.
