## Grammar points

### Unit 1 — Present continuous (*I am doing*)

Marks an action in progress around the moment of speaking — started, not yet finished — including
actions not literally happening at this instant but ongoing "these days" (*I'm reading an
interesting book at the moment*). Also covers temporary periods around now (*today/this week*) and
ongoing changes (*the population of the world is rising*). CMOS's treatment of the progressive
aspect is purely structural (how it's formed); Murphy's contribution is the pragmatic test —
"in-progress, unfinished" — and the "changes happening now" use, neither stated in CMOS.

### Unit 2 — Present simple (*I do*)

Marks general/habitual/permanent truths, independent of whether the action is happening at the
moment of speech — repeated actions, facts, and frequency adverb constructions (*how often...?*).
Covers the performative-verb use (*I promise, I suggest, I apologise, I insist, I agree, I refuse*)
where the present simple itself performs the speech act — a pragmatics point (illocutionary present)
not found in CMOS's structural tense/mood discussion.

### Unit 3 — Present continuous and present simple (1)

Direct side-by-side contrast: continuous for the temporary/in-progress, simple for the
general/permanent, applied to matching verb pairs (*the water is boiling* vs *water boils at 100°C*;
*I'm living with friends until I find a flat* vs *my parents live in London*). Introduces the
"always ~ing" complaint-idiom contrasted with neutral "always do" (see Vocabulary).

### Unit 4 — Present continuous and present simple (2)

Establishes the stative-verb restriction (see Vocabulary) and three exception/nuance cases: the
*think* belief/consider polysemy split, the *see/hear/smell/taste* perception-verb restriction (used
with *can* rather than continuous: *can you hear something?*), the *seeing* = "meeting with" sense
shift, the *be being* behavior-vs-trait distinction, and the *look/feel* dual-form flexibility. None
of this granularity is present in CMOS/Oxford.

### Unit 5 — Past simple (*I did*)

Regular *-ed* and irregular past forms (full irregular-verb list deferred to Appendix 1, covered
separately in a later part of this dispatch); *did/didn't* as the question/negative auxiliary,
including the double-*do* trap (*what did you do*, not *what did you*); *was/were* as *be*'s own
past, which — unlike every other verb — forms its own questions/negatives without *did*.

### Unit 6 — Past continuous (*I was doing*)

Marks being "in the middle of" a past action at a specified time, parallel to the present continuous
but shifted to past reference. Its chief teaching point is the interrupted-action pattern: past
continuous (ongoing backdrop) + past simple (interrupting event) — *Tom burnt his hand when he was
cooking dinner* — versus two past simples in sequence denoting consecutive completed actions (*I was
walking along the road when I saw Dave. So I stopped...*). Also contrasts *when Karen arrived, we
were having dinner* (dinner already in progress) against *when Karen arrived, we had dinner* (dinner
began only after her arrival) — a minimal-pair distinction CMOS's structural tense/aspect discussion
does not draw. The Unit 4A stative-verb restriction carries over to past continuous (*we knew each
other well*, not "were knowing").
