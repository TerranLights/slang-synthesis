## Grammar points

### 13.6 Question-word questions (2): 'When?', 'Where?', 'Which?', 'Whose?'

**13.6A 'When...?' and 'Where...?' as question-words.** *When* asks about time (present, past, or
future), typically answered with a time adverb or prepositional phrase. *Where* asks about place, with
answers ranging from a full sentence to a single word; *where...from* specifically asks about origin,
p.198.

**13.6B 'Which...?' as a question-word.** *Which* presupposes a limited set of options — it can
combine with a noun, stand alone, take *of* for two-or-more items, combine with a comparative/
superlative, combine with *day/month/year*, or form *which way* for precise directional questions
(more exact than plain *where*), p.198.

**13.6C 'Whose?'.** Asks about possession; the possessor is always a person, and the expected answer
is a name + *'s* or a possessive pronoun. The noun after *whose* can be dropped when the possession is
a thing/substance ("Whose [umbrella] is this? — It's mine") but is normally kept when the possession is
a person ("Whose son is he? — Kate's"); *whose* can also combine with a following noun phrase in a
second question pattern ("Whose is this umbrella?"), p.199.

### 13.7 Question-word questions (3): 'Why?', 'How?'

**13.7A 'Why...?' as a question-word.** Answered with *because* or a to-infinitive (never a bare
"*Why*"). *What...for?* can substitute for *why*; *why don't/doesn't...?* makes a suggestion; *why*/
*why not* + bare infinitive makes a subjectless suggestion, p.200.

**13.7B 'How...?' as a question-word; 'How much?'/'How many?'.** *How* basically asks about manner and
combines productively with *much* (uncountable quantity or cost), *many* (plural count number), and
with adjectives/adverbs, p.200.

**13.7C 'How' + adjective or adverb: 'How far?'.** *How* + adjective substitutes for *What* + the
corresponding noun: "How long is that skirt?" = "What length is that skirt?," p.200.

**13.7D 'How long...(for)?' and 'How long ago?'.** *How long (for)* asks about duration; *How long
ago* + past tense asks for a point in time, p.201.

**13.7E Social uses of 'How...?'.** Four fixed social-formula functions, each with its own
conventional answer — see the Vocabulary table: introductions (*How do you do?*, echoed back rather
than answered with "Fine, thanks"), health inquiries (*How are you?*/*How have you been?*), personal
reactions (*How was...?* = "What was...like?"), and offers/suggestions (*How about...?* = "What
about...?"), p.201.

### 13.8 Subject-questions: 'Who?', 'What?', 'Which?', 'Whose?'

**13.8A Subject or object?.** A subject-question asks for the identity of the sentence's subject and
uses the *same* word order as the underlying statement — no inversion — unlike an object-directed
*who(m)*-question, which does invert [>13.5B]. Answers to subject-questions typically echo the
question's own auxiliary, or supply *do/does/did* when none is present. *What, which, whose*, and
*how much/how many* can all combine with a noun to form a subject-question, p.202.

### 13.9 Questions about alternatives; emphatic questions with 'ever'

**13.9A Questions about alternatives (1): 'Did you laugh, or cry?'.** A question offering two
alternatives joined by *or* can drop the repeated subject and auxiliary from the second alternative:
"Did you laugh, or did you cry?" → "Did you laugh or cry?," p.204.

**13.9B Questions about alternatives (2): 'Did you take it, or didn't you?'.** An affirmative/negative
alternative about the same action can likewise avoid repeating the verb in full, via any of three
equivalent phrasings: "Did you take it, or didn't you?" / "Did you or didn't you take it?" / "Did you
take it, or not?" (never "*or no*"), p.204.

**13.9C Emphatic questions with 'ever', etc.** *Ever*, written as a separate word after a question-word
(distinct from the fused *-ever* compounds like *wherever*/*however*/*whatever*, which function
differently), expresses admiration, anger, concern, and similar heightened reactions; it follows every
question-word except *which* and *whose*, and often carries heavy stress. *Ever* can get a subject or
object ("What ever made you late?"), appear in a short response ("What ever for?"), or be replaced/
supplemented by a phrase like *on earth* for extra emphasis ("How on earth did you know?"), p.205.
