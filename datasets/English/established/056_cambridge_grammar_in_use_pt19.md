## Grammar points

### Unit 101 — *So* and *such*

The core *so*+adjective/adverb vs. *such*+(adjective+)noun complementary pair, their shared
*...that...* result-clause construction, a second deictic-degree (non-intensifying) sense shared by
both words, and a closed set of lexically-fixed exceptions (*so long* vs. *such a long time*, etc.)
where the expected intensifier choice is overridden by idiom.

### Unit 102 — *Enough* and *too*

The word-class-dependent placement rule for *enough* (post-adjective/adverb, pre-noun) with its
*too*/*too much/too many* negative-degree counterparts, the *for*/*to*-infinitive complementation
system, and the implicit-object-deletion rule after *too...to...*.

### Unit 103 — *Quite* and *rather*

The moderate-intensifier senses of both words (with *rather*'s polarity-sensitivity and its distinct
"surprisingly" reading when paired with positive words), their collocational tendency with
positive/negative-leaning adjectives respectively, and the wholly separate "completely" sense of
*quite* restricted to a closed set of absolute adjectives — a genuine lexically-conditioned polysemy
within one surface word.

### Units 104–106 — Comparison (1), (2), (3)

The comparative morphology rule (*-er* vs. *more...*, with a flexible middle class and an irregular
set), the scale of comparative-degree-grading modifiers (large-to-small), the *any/no* + comparative
negative construction, the reduplicated continuous-change construction, the *the...the...*
correlative-dependency construction, the family-restricted *elder/eldest* alternative forms, and the
full positive/negative equative system (*as...as*, *not as...as*/*not so...as*, *less...than*, *the
same as*) together with the pronoun-case register rule after *than*/*as*.
