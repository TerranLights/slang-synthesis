## Grammar points

### Error 54: Agree to vs. Agree with (p. 143)

*Don't say* "agree to their arguments"; *say instead* "agree with their arguments." "Agree to"
introduces an infinitive and means to consent to a course of action; "agree with" introduces a noun
and means to be in alignment with a viewpoint.

### Error 55: Differ with vs. Differ from (p. 144)

*Don't say* "differed from the Trumbles on their right..."; *say instead* "differed with the
Trumbles..." "Differ from" means to be unlike (physical/qualitative difference); "differ with" means
to disagree with an opinion or position.

### Error 56: Different from vs. Different than (pp. 144–145)

*Don't say* "my invitation looks different than hers" (a direct two-item comparison); *say instead*
"...different from hers." "Different from" is universally acceptable; "different than" is accepted by
less-conservative speakers only when followed by a full clause, and rejected outright by conservative
speakers in any context. It is never acceptable for a quick, bare two-item comparison.

### Unidiomatic and superfluous prepositions: errors 57–62 (pp. 146–149)

Six entries addressing prepositions added where none is needed, or paired illogically: *"centers
around"* (logically impossible — a center cannot be around anything, error 57); *"where...at"*
(redundant locative, explicitly flagged as a stigmatized "tip-off" of careless speech despite being
common, error 58); *"as to"* as a pretentious-sounding preposition substitute, as distinct from its
legitimate emphatic-fronting use (error 59); *"off of"* (unneeded "of," unlike the required "of" in
"out of," error 60); *"over with"* used in a neutral-completion context rather than its genuine
eagerness-for-completion sense (error 61); and *"type of a"* (unneeded article, error 62).
