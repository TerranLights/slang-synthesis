## Grammar points

### Unit 116 — *Like* and *as*

Three distinct senses of *like* (resemblance preposition; "for example"; and — by exclusion — never
the clause-introducing function) set against *as*'s two distinct, unrelated senses (manner-identity
before a clause, including a family of fixed epistemic-stance idioms; and genuine
role/capacity/function, sharply contrasted with *like*'s mere-resemblance sense in the book's
clearest minimal pair of the unit, *as a hospital* vs. *like a hospital*).

### Unit 117 — *As if*

The near-synonym pair *as if*/*as though* after perception-copula verbs (with an impersonal *it*
variant that additionally admits informal *like*), the extension to ordinary action verbs for
manner-comparison, and the past-tense-for-present-irrealis mechanism inside *as if* clauses — the
third documented instance in this book (alongside Units 38–39) of a formally-past verb marking
present-time counterfactual meaning, confirming it as a systematic cross-construction strategy
rather than an isolated idiom.

### Unit 118 — *For*, *during* and *while*

The duration-marking preposition *for* ("how long") set against the point-marking preposition
*during* ("when, within what period") and its complementation-class counterpart *while* (+ finite
clause) — the same during/while pair participating in the broader complementation-contrast pattern
this section repeatedly surfaces (*in spite of*/*although*, *like*/*as*, now *during*/*while*).

### Unit 119 — *By* and *until*; *By the time...*

The sharp *by* (single-event deadline) vs. *until* (continuing-state endpoint) contrast on identical
clock times, and the *by the time (+ clause)* construction anchoring one event's completion to
another's occurrence, in both future-predictive and past-narrative forms, with its discourse-
anaphoric *by then*/*by that time* shorthand.
