## Grammar points

### Bookend expressions (correlative conjunctions): errors 63–68 (pp. 151–155)

Correlative conjunctions are word pairs that jointly structure a sentence's comparison or contrast;
errors here are of two types — dropping the second half entirely, or pairing the first half with an
improper substitute for its true mate. Error 63 (*not only...but/but also*) and error 66 (*neither...
nor*) are drop-the-second-half cases. Error 64 (*on the one hand...on the other hand*) and error 67
(*as...as*) are mismatched-second-half cases; error 64 additionally notes the pair is strictly
two-sided — a third "hand" is never grammatical regardless of how many points are being made (excess
points must be grouped under one of the two hands instead). Error 65 (*either...or*) is a padding
case — adding unneeded words ("or else," "or maybe") rather than dropping or mismatching anything.
Error 68 (*the reason...was because*) is a distinct redundancy case: "the reason" and "because" both
independently signal a cause-effect relationship, so combining them duplicates that signal; the fix
is either "the reason...was that" or dropping "the reason" and keeping "because" alone.

### Imprecise, pretentious, or needless connectors: errors 69–75 (pp. 157–160)

Errors 69–75 are explicitly framed by the source as not grammatically wrong so much as "fuzzy" —
imprecise enough to blur meaning or sound pretentious. Error 69 (*where* in a definitional noun
clause, e.g. *"A home run is where..."*) misapplies "where" to a defining role standard English
reserves for "means that." Error 70 (*per*) is flagged as actively ambiguous business jargon once
stretched past its narrow ratio sense, genuinely risking reader misunderstanding, not just style.
Error 71 (*plus* joining full clauses) extends a legitimate noun-joining use of "plus" into a
nonstandard clause-joining one. Error 72 (*as to whether*), 73 (*in the event that*, for "if"), and 74
(*owing to the fact that*, for "because") are all padding/wordiness cases with no real register
dispute — just unnecessary bulk. Error 75 (*as* for "because"/"since") is a genuine ambiguity case:
"as" also means "while," so a causal "as"-sentence can be misread as a temporal one; "because"/"since"
avoid the ambiguity entirely.
