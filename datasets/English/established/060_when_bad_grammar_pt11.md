## Grammar points

This chapter addresses near-synonym pairs/triples whose subtle meaning differences are lost in casual
speech (contrast Chapter 8's sound-alikes and Chapter 9's look-alikes) — see the Vocabulary table
above for each set's distinguishing gloss and page citation. Three entries stand out for being framed
as live register disputes rather than flat errors: error 125 (*kind of/sort of* for "rather") is
explicitly called a minor, register-level infraction rather than a real error; error 130 (*quote* as a
noun) is framed as accepted by most educated speakers but rejected by conservative holdouts, with
"quotation" reserved for only the most formal contexts; and error 134 (*utilize* for "use") gets the
book's bluntest anti-pretentiousness verdict — "use" is always safe, "utilize" is defensible only when
drawing a genuine good-use-vs-mere-use distinction. Error 132 (*service* as a verb applied to people)
is a distinct case: the objection isn't a definitional mismatch but an inadvertent collision with two
other established senses of "service" as a verb (machine maintenance; a stud animal's breeding act),
making the construction unintentionally comedic/crude rather than simply wrong.
