## Grammar points

### Unit 37 — *If I do...* and *If I did...*

Establishes the two-way present/future conditional split: real-possibility (*if + present, will*)
vs. imagined/not-expected (*if + past, would*) — with the past-tense form in the second type carrying
no real past-time meaning. States the categorical *would*-never-in-the-if-clause rule and its one
narrow formal exception (see Vocabulary).

### Unit 38 — *If I knew...*, *I wish I knew...*

Extends the unreal-present conditional to *wish*: both constructions use a past-tense verb for
present-time imagined/regretted content. Introduces the optional *were* (for all persons) in both
constructions, and reiterates the *would*-ban (now also applying after *wish*).

### Unit 39 — *If I had known...*, *I wish I had known...*

Shifts the same real/unreal machinery one step further into the past: unreal-past conditional
(*if + had done, would have done*) and *wish + had (done)* for past regret. Draws the *would (do)*
vs. *would have (done)* present-vs-past-result distinction, and grades counterfactual certainty via
*would have / could have / might have* (see Vocabulary).

### Unit 40 — *Would*; *I wish...would*

Covers *would*'s three non-conditional uses — bare hypothetical, reported-speech past-of-*will*, and
past-habitual-action (near-synonymous with *used to*, Unit 18) — then isolates a third, structurally
distinct *wish* construction (*wish + would*) restricted to actions/changes rather than states, with
its own minimal-pair test against the *wish + past simple* construction from Unit 38.
