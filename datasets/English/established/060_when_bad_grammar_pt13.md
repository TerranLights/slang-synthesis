## Grammar points

### Errors 142–148: wasteful/redundant constructions (pp. 221–224)

Unlike most chapters, these errors are not meaning confusions but excess-wordage problems: extra
words sprinkled in that add nothing (or actively create redundancy). *A half a* (142) doubles the
article around "half." *And et cetera* (143) redundantly stacks "and" onto a Latin phrase that already
means "and other things." Filler *"like"* (144) is flagged purely as a stigmatized marker of
unpolished speech, distinct from the earlier clause-linking "like" misuse covered in Chapter 10. *The
field of* (145) is clutter unless "the field" itself is genuinely the sentence's subject. *Needless to
say* (146) gets the chapter's most nuanced treatment: the source affirms it has a real, useful
discourse function (flagging a shared assumption, seriously or ironically) and objects only to its use
as filler around a trivial opinion — explicitly recommending a test ("ask yourself whether it's doing
any meaningful work") rather than banning the phrase outright. *Time period* (147) redundantly pairs
two words that both carry a sense of time. *Party for "person"* (148) is a register-restriction case:
correct only in legal-brief or hospitality contexts, and a marker of straining for formality anywhere
else.
