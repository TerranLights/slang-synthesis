## Grammar points

This chapter is organized entirely as a sequence of 15 near-homophone/sound-alike confusable pairs,
each following the same Don't-Say/Say-Instead/Here's-Why structure (see the Vocabulary table above
for each pair's distinguishing gloss and page citation). The chapter's unifying theme, stated in its
own introduction, is that these errors arise specifically because the confused words sound nearly
identical in speech ("one little letter can make a big difference"), unlike Chapter 9's look-alike
confusions or Chapter 10's related-meaning confusions. Error 90 (*could of* for *could have*) is the
chapter's clearest phonetically-motivated case: the source explicitly traces the error to how
contracted "have" ('ve) sounds like "of" in ordinary speech, generalizing the same pattern to "would
of," "should of," "may of," and "might of." Error 94 (*jive* for *jibe*) is notable for the source's
own explicit identification of "jive" (in its correct sense) as itself a slang term for a music/dance
style and its associated talk — a rare case in this chapter where one half of a confusable pair is
itself flagged as slang vocabulary rather than simply a different standard word.
