## Grammar points

### Unit 57 — Verb + ~ing or *to...* (3): *like/would like* etc.

Three-way verb-class split for affect verbs (gerund-only, dual-complement, infinitive-only — see
Vocabulary), the genuinely subtle British-English *like doing* vs. *like to do* register
distinction, and the *would like to have done* retrospective-regret perfect infinitive.

### Unit 58 — *Prefer* and *would rather*

Two parallel but non-interchangeable preference constructions (*prefer...to...ing* vs. *prefer to do
...rather than*), the infinitive-only restriction on *would prefer*, *would rather*'s bare-infinitive
and *than*-clause syntax, and the distinct *I'd rather you did* construction for preferences about
someone else's action.

### Unit 59 — Preposition + ~ing

States the governing rule (any preposition + verb → gerund), works through four specific
preposition-plus-gerund functions (temporal *before/after*, instrumental *by*, exclusive *without*),
and flags the central homograph trap: *to* as infinitive-marker vs. *to* as ordinary preposition —
the single rule that explains why *looking forward to seeing* takes a gerund while *decided to see*
takes a bare infinitive.

### Unit 60 — *Be/get used to something*

Extends the Unit 59C preposition-*to* insight to the "accustomed to" construction, distinguishes the
change-of-state (*get used to*) from the settled-state (*be used to*) reading, and explicitly
contrasts the whole construction against the unrelated past-habitual *used to* (Unit 18) — two
superficially near-identical constructions with different syntax (preposition+gerund vs. defective
modal+bare infinitive) and opposite temporal orientation (present familiarity vs. discontinued past
habit).

### Unit 61 — Verb + preposition + ~ing

Two large fixed-collocation inventories (verb+preposition+~ing, and verb+object+preposition+~ing —
see Vocabulary for both full lists), with their passive-voice behavior and the
object-insertion extension noted.

### Unit 62 — Expressions + ~ing

A set of fixed idiomatic frames (*it's no use/no good, there's no point in, it's (not) worth, have
difficulty, a waste of time/money, spend/waste time, be busy*) that all categorically require a
following gerund.
