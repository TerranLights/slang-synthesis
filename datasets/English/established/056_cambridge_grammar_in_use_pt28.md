## Grammar points

### Appendix 1 — Regular and irregular verbs

A morphological classification of the English verb paradigm: the default *-ed* regular pattern; a
three-way irregular-verb classification by how many of the three principal forms (infinitive, past
simple, past participle) remain distinct from one another (all-identical; past-simple-merges-with-
participle; all-three-distinct, frequently via vowel ablaut); and a small closed set of verbs
straddling the regular/irregular divide with a British-preferred irregular variant. The single
past-participle form's dual grammatical role (perfect-tense auxiliary complement and passive-voice
complement) is the appendix's own unifying observation tying this morphological material back to
the tense (Units 7–16) and voice (Units 41–43) systems already covered.

### Appendix 2 — Present and past tenses

A pure cross-referencing summary table restating the eight tense/aspect combinations already taught
in full across Units 1–16 (present simple, present continuous, present perfect simple, present
perfect continuous, past simple, past continuous, past perfect, past perfect continuous), each with
one fresh worked example sentence per form built around a single running scenario (Ann and tennis).
No new grammar rule beyond what Units 1–16 already establish; not re-extracted here.

### Appendix 3 — The future

A pure cross-referencing summary table restating the full future-expression system already taught in
Units 19–25 (present continuous, present simple, *(be) going to*, *will*, future continuous, future
perfect, and the present-tense-in-future-subordinate-clauses rule), organized around one running
example sentence (*I'm leaving tomorrow*, in each of its grammatical variants). No new grammar rule
beyond what Units 19–25 already establish; not re-extracted here.

### Appendix 4 — Modal verbs (*can/could/will/would* etc.)

A pure cross-referencing summary table restating the modal-verb system already taught in Units
21–40, organized as two parallel grids: one comparing the ability/permission/obligation-flavored
modals (*can, could, will/won't, would, shall, should/ought to, must/mustn't, needn't*) and their
past-tense (*could have*, etc.) counterparts around one running example (*go out tonight*); the
other comparing the epistemic-certainty-flavored modals (*will, would, should/ought to, may/might/
could, must, can't*) and their past-tense counterparts around a second running example (*she...be
here*). No new grammar rule beyond what Units 21–40 already establish; not re-extracted here.
