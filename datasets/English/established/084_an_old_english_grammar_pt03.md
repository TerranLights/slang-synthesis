**Source:** Randolph Quirk and C. L. Wrenn, *An Old English Grammar* (Methuen, 1955/1957), Ch. II "Inflexions" — Verbs: General Notes (§§68–69), Consonantal Type (§§70–73), Vocalic Type (§§74–84), Irregular Verbs (§§85–93), pp.40–58.

**Coverage note:** Continues `084_an_old_english_grammar_pt02.md`. Vision-read directly from the scanned source; no handwritten marginalia found on any page in this range. OE verb morphology is fusional (stem + person/number/mood/tense endings), so the standard morpheme-breakdown convention (for agglutinative languages) does not apply here — instead, full present/preterite paradigm tables are reproduced as structured grammar-point data, and every distinct verb lexeme explicitly glossed in this range is captured in the Vocabulary table. Given the sheer density of "so too" verb lists in this chunk (dozens of verbs per conjugation class), vocabulary rows below are comprehensive of every verb given its own gloss; verbs cited only as bare paradigm-form examples without a fresh gloss (i.e. already covered) are not re-listed. ## Grammar points

### §68–69. Overview of OE verb morphology

Almost three-quarters of the verbs a student meets are **consonantal** ("weak") conjugation — the productive, still-growing type, encompassing essentially all new verbs formed/adopted since earliest OE and since (matching Mod.E. *-ed* weak verbs continuing to be productive). About one-quarter belong to the **vocalic** ("strong") conjugation, with preterite differentiated by internal vowel change (gradation) rather than a dental suffix. A very small residue, around one-fiftieth, are **irregular** verbs of several types — but despite their tiny number, these are disproportionately the commonest verbs in the language (*be, will, can* and their OE equivalents), so must be learned thoroughly.

General notes on verb inflexions (§69): (1) present indicative singular has distinctive person forms — 1 p.sg. *-e*, 2 p.sg. *-st*, 3 p.sg. *-ð*; (2) preterite indicative singular 1st/3rd person are identical (*-e* consonantal, no ending vocalic), with a distinctive 2nd person form (*-est* consonantal, *-e* vocalic); (3) present indicative plural and preterite indicative plural, and the present/preterite subjunctive singular and plural, show no person distinctions; (4) present indicative plural almost always ends in *-að*, preterite indicative plural always in *-on*; (5) consonantal and most irregular verbs have a dental suffix in all preterite forms (*herian→herede*), vocalic verbs form the preterite by stem-vowel change (*bindan→band→bundon*); (6) present and preterite subjunctive singular are in *-e*, plural in *-en*; (7) there are in general two imperative forms, 2 p.sg. in *-e, -a,* or no ending, and 2 p.pl. in *-að*; (8) infinitives end in *-(i)an*, present participles in *-(i)ende*; past participles normally have the prefix *ge-* and end in *-ed* (consonantal) or *-en* (vocalic). The whole paradigm of a verb can be inferred from selected items called **principal parts**: infinitive (and pres.indic. 3 p.sg.), pret.indic. 1 and 3 p.sg., 1–3 p.pl. (vocalic verbs only), and past participle.

### §70–73. Consonantal (weak) verbs

**Class I** — stem of the infinitive almost always has a mutated vowel; two sub-classes: **(a)** short vowel + double consonant or *-ri-* (*fremman* 'perform', *nerian* 'save'); **(b)** long vowel (or short vowel + consonant cluster other than doubling) (*dēman* 'judge'). **Class II** — infinitive vowel *-ian* (*lufian* 'love'), vast majority with unmutated stem vowels. Full present/preterite indicative, subjunctive, imperative, and participle paradigms are given for *fremman, nerian, dēman, lufian* (§70); dual pronouns *wit, git* take the same verb forms as *wē, gē*. There is a rare 1 p.pl. imperative in *-an, -on* (§70, note).

Class I(a) verbs: lengthened-consonant j-presents like *fremman, trymman* (and *cnyssan, dynnan, settan, cnyttan, lettan, lecgan, derian, erian, ferian, spyrian, werian*) show West Germanic consonant-lengthening (§184) before the original *-j-* of the infinitive suffix. Class I(b) verbs (long-vowel stem, not subject to consonant-lengthening): *bærnan, ālȳsan, dǣlan, drǣfan, fēdan, fēran, flȳman, gȳman, hǣlan, hȳran, lǣfan, lǣran, (ge)lȳfan, nȳdan, rǣran, rȳman, wēnan*; further sub-patterns with *-d-*→*-t-* devoicing in the preterite (*cȳðan→cȳdde*, *(ge)cwīdan→cwīdde*), with syncope/assimilation (*fyllan→fylde*, *cennan→cynde*, *cyrran→cyrde*, *sendan→sende*, *andwyrdan→andwyrde*, *nemnan→nemde*), with *-t-* preterites after dentals/long syllables (*bētan→bētte*, *ȳcan→ȳhte*, *grētan→grētte*, *mētan→mētte*, *swencan→sweahte*, *wǣtan→wǣhte*, *cyssan→cyste*, *(ge)lǣstan→lǣste*).

**Class II** (§73): *endian→endode* model; common verbs listed: *andswarian, āscian, bēotian, bismrian, blissian, bodian, clipian, eardian, ebbian, fandian, folgian, gaderian, geǣmetigan, heri(ge)an, hȳrsumian, lōcian, leornian, losian, macian, scēawian, trūwian, weorðian, wunian, wundian*. A few contracted verbs (*frēo(ga)n* 'love', *frēo(ga)n*/*fēon* 'hate', *smēagan* 'think', *twēo(ga)n* 'doubt') have preterites in *-ode* instead of *-odon*. Class II verbs frequently have preterite participle in *-od* instead of *-on*. In Late OE, several Class II verbs tended to be used with Class I inflexions, and new Class II verbs like *fremian, trymian* arose beside *fremman, trymman* — a sign of this merger process.

### §74–84. Vocalic (strong) verbs — seven classes by gradation pattern

There are **seven classes** distinguished by differences of stem-vowel between present and preterite, with several classes also distinguishing pret.sg. from pret.pl./past pple. The changes of vowel in Class VII are of obscure origin; Classes I–VI arise by **gradation** (ablaut, see §182f). Model paradigms are given for *drīfan* 'drive' (straightforward, Cl.I), *cēosan* 'choose' (Cl.II, affected by Verner's Law consonant shift), *hebban* 'raise' (Cl.VI, with consonantal-type present), and *sēon* 'see' (Cl.V, contracted).

**Class I** (§75): infin. *ī*, pret.sg. *ā*, pret.pl./pple *i*: *drīfan–drāf–drifon–-drifen*; so too *blīcan, hrīnan, mīðan, rīpan, scīnan, stīgan, swīcan*. Also with 3sg.pres. in *-tt*: *gewītan, glīdan, rīdan, slītan, wlītan, wrītan*. Affected by the second consonant shift (Verner's Law, §§180f): *līðan→lidon*, *snīðan→snidon*. Contracted verbs also so affected: *lēon→lāh→ligon→-ligen*; other contracted verbs are *tēon* 'accuse', *þēon* 'prosper' (originally Class III, later reflected adjectivally as *geþungen* 'excellent'), *wrēon* 'cover'.

**Class II** (§76): infin. *ēo*, pret.sg. *ēa*, pret.pl./pple *u/o*: *cleofan–clēaf–clufon–-clofen*; so too *brēowan, hrēowan, smēocan*; *drēogan, flēogan, lēogan* have 1/3 sg. pret. in both *-g* and *-h*; *bēodan–bēad–budon–-boden*, also *brēotan, flēotan, gēotan, grēotan, scēotan, sēoðan*. Contracted verbs affected by the second consonant shift: *seoðan→sēaþ→sudon→-soden*; like *cēosan* are *drēosan* 'fall', *(for)lēosan* 'lose', *frēosan* 'freeze', *hrēosan* 'fall'. Contracted: *flēon* 'flee'; also *tēon* 'draw'. The "aorist-present" pattern: *brūcan–brȳcþ–brēac–brucon–-brocen*; so too *būgan (bēag, bēah), dūfan, lūcan, lūtan, scūfan*. *Ābrēoðan* 'perish' is irregular (unlike *sēoðan*) in extending *-ð-* to the pret.pl. and past pple: *ofhrēowan→ofhrēow(þ)* 'pity'.

**Class III** (§77–79): the majority have (a) *i* + nasal + another consonant (pret.pl./pple *-u-*) or (b) *e/eo* + liquid + another consonant (pret.pl./pple *-o-*). (a): *drincan–dranc–druncon–-druncen*; so too *climban, gelimpan, onginnan, sincan, singan, springan, pringan, swimman, swincan, winnan*. Similar 3 sg. pres.: *findan, bindan*. Two verbs metathesized (§193): *byrnan* 'burn', *yrnan* 'run' (cf. Gothic *brinnan, rinnan*). (b): *helpan–healp–hulpon–-holpen*; so too (with diphthongisation, §§201ff) *belgan, swelgan, swellan, meltan, sweltan*; with palatal-consonant diphthongisation (§204) *gyldan, gyllan*; also *weorpan–wearp–wurpon–-worpen*, with similar diphthongisation *beorgan, ceorfan, feohtan, hweorfan, steorfan*. Two aorist-presents: *murnan* (pret. *murnde*, beside *mearn*), *spurnan*. Affected by the second consonant shift: *weorðan–wearð–wurdon–-worden* 'become'. Irregular: *berstan–bærst–burston–-borsten* 'burst'; similarly *perscan* 'thresh'; further *bregdan, stregdan* with alternative forms from loss of *g* and lengthening; with *æ* in 1/3 sg. pret.: *frignan(frīnan)* 'ask' (pret. *frægn/frān/fræʒn*; pret.pl. *frugnon*; past pple *-frugnen*; there was also an infin. *fricgan* and past pple *-frigen* on the Cl.V model).

**Class IV** (§80): few verbs, mostly *e* + *r/l* in infin.: *beran–bær–bǣron–-boren*; so too *brecan, helan, stelan, teran*; *sceran* 'cut' has forms affected by palatal consonant diphthongisation (§204): *scieran, scear, scēaron*. Two irregular aorist-presents: *cuman–cōm–cōmon–-cumen* 'come', *niman–nōm–nōmon–-numen* 'take' (also with *cwōm(on), nam, nāmon*).

**Class V** (§81): infin. with *e* + single consonant (other than liquid/nasal): *sprecan–spræc–sprǣcon–-sprecen*; so too *drepan, metan, swefan, tredan, wefan, (fr)etan, wrecan*. Two verbs with *-i-/-y-/-ie-* variant forms and palatal-consonant diphthongisation: *gifan* 'give', *(on)gytan* 'catch, perceive'. *Cweðan* 'say' is affected by second consonant shift. The defective verb *wesan* 'be' belongs here (§87). Contracted verbs affected by both second consonant shift and velarised-consonant diphthongisation include *gefēon* 'rejoice'; *sēon* 'see' (pres. *seah, sāwon*, pple *-sewen*, alternatively *sægon/segon*, pple *-segen*). A few verbs have present forms of the consonantal type: *biddan* 'pray', *bæd, bǣdon, -beden*; *licgan* 'lie, recline', *læg, lǣgon, -legen*; so too *sittan* 'sit' (pret. *sæt*), *fricgan* (cf. *frignan*) 'ask', *þicgan* 'receive'.

**Class VI** (§82): typical vowel *a* in infin.: *faran–fōr–fōron–-faren*; so too *bacan, dragan, galan, grafan, hladan, wadan, wascan(waxan), scacan, scafan*; also the verb "stand" with *-n-* throughout present and pret.pl.: *standan–stōd–stōdon–-standen*. *Wæcnan* 'awake' has pret. *wōc, wōcon*. Contracted, affected by the second consonant shift: *slēan–slōh–slōgon–-slagen* 'strike'; so too *lēan* 'blame', *þwēan* 'wash'. A few verbs have consonantal-type present forms: *swerian–swōr–swōron–-sworen* 'swear'; *hebban–hōf–hōfon–-hafen* 'raise'. Similarly, *hlihhan* 'laugh' (pret. *hlōg*, or *-h*); *scieppan/scyppan* 'create' (pret. *scōp*), *steppan* 'step' (pret. *stōp*).

**Class VII** (§83–84): best considered by their preterite vowel, which is *ēo* or *ē*; the signs of reduplication (compare Lat. *currō–cucurri*) are meagre in OE: *leolc* 'played' (*lācan*), *heht* 'called' (*hātan*) are nearly the only vestiges. (a) preterite *ēo*: *feallan–fēoll–fēollon–-feallen*; so too *fealdan, healdan, wealcan, weallan, weaxan*. (b) preterite *ē*: *lǣtan–lēt–lēton–-lǣten*; so too *ondrǣdan, rǣdan, slǣpan* (3sg.pres. *slǣpþ*; these three also have consonantal preterites in *-dde, -pte*); *hātan–hēt–hēton–-hāten*; *lācan–lēolc–leolcon–-blenden* [sic — the book cross-refers past pple of *blandan*]; *blandan–blend–blēndon–-blenden* 'mix'. Contracted, affected by the second consonant shift: *fōn–fēng–fēngon–-fangen* 'seize'; *hōn–hēng–hēngon–-hangen* 'hang'. *Sc(e)ādan* has an alternative pret. *scēad*.

### §85–93. Irregular verbs

Most of these are high-frequency and should be learnt completely; grouped three ways, all but *bēon* having consonantal preterites: **(1) the "have" group** (*habban, libban, secgan, hycgan*, §86) — full paradigms given, with many recorded variant forms (*hafast, hafað* for *hæfst, hæfð*; *lifast, -að* for *leofast*, etc.; a negative form of *habban*: *nabban, næbbe, næfde*, etc.). **(2) anomalous verbs** (*bēon/wesan* 'be', §87; *willan* 'will, wish', *dōn* 'do', *gān* 'go', §88) — *bēon/wesan* has two present-tense roots (cognate respectively with Lat. *esse* and *fui*) and a preterite/subjunctive/imperative all drawn from *wesan* (vocalic Cl.V); Mercian/Northumbrian texts show pres.indic.pl. *(e)arun*; negative forms (*neom, næs*, etc.) occur for all parts beginning with *w* or a vowel; *willan* shows confusion between *wile* and *wille* (the former originally 3sg.pres.indic.), frequent negative forms (*nylle, nolde*, etc.), and a preterite from a wholly different root corresponding to *gān* (not itself recorded with a present form). **(3) preterite-present verbs** (§§89–93) — formed because an old vocalic preterite assumed present meaning in Gmc, so a *new* consonantal preterite had to be built: *witan* 'know' (new pret. *wiste/wisse*, related in form to *drīfan*, §§74–75); *sculan* 'have to, be obliged to'; *cunnan* 'know, be able', *unnan* 'grant'; *magan* 'be able'; *þurfan* 'need', *(ge)munan* 'remember'; *dugan* 'avail, be profitable', *āgan* 'have, own', *mōt* 'I am allowed'; a further defective pret.-pres. verb *-neah* 'is ample' is recorded only in 3sg.pres.indic. (with pl. *-nugon*, pres.subj. *-nuge*, pret. *-nohte*). Full present/preterite paradigms for all are tabulated (§§90–93); negative forms occur freely throughout this whole group (*nāt, nyton, nyste*, etc.).
