## Grammar points

### Appendix 5 — Short forms (*I'm/you've/didn't* etc.)

The positive-auxiliary contraction set and its two genuinely ambiguous members (*'s* = is/has, *'d*
= would/had, each resolvable only by context — a real design-relevant case of permitted surface
ambiguity under contraction), the lexicalized exception *let's*, the extended distribution of
contraction onto question words/*there*/*here*/ordinary nouns, the ban on contraction in
stressed sentence-final position, and the full negative-contraction set (including the free-
variation choice between contracting *be* or contracting *not* for *is/are*).

### Appendix 6 — Spelling

Five spelling-change rule clusters governing English's core inflectional endings (*-s/-es*,
*y*→*ie*/*i*, *ie*→*y*, final-*e* deletion/retention, and consonant-doubling with its
stress-sensitive and British-specific sub-rules) — a systematic treatment of English orthographic
alternation that CMOS/Oxford's style-guide-level spelling chapters never attempt at this
morphological-rule level.

### Appendix 7 — American English

The book's own comprehensive, unit-by-unit British/American grammatical-and-spelling contrast
table — not a vocabulary-difference list (that territory belongs to other sources) but a genuine
grammar-and-morphology minimal-pair set, spanning tense-choice, lexical-collocation, modal-usage,
mandative-mood, article-presence, subject-verb-agreement, preposition-choice, complementation, verb
irregularity, and spelling. Kept in full as this project's most systematic British/American
grammar-contrast resource to date for English.
