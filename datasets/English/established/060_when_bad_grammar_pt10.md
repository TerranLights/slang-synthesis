## Grammar points

This chapter follows the same structure as Chapter 8, but collects confusable pairs/triples that are
mixed up because they *look* similar in spelling rather than because they sound alike (see the
Vocabulary table above for each pair's distinguishing gloss and page citation). The chapter's own
framing explicitly contrasts it with Chapter 8's sound-alike confusions. Error 113 (*sensuous/
sensual*) stands out for its genuine etymological content: the source reports that John Milton coined
"sensuous" in 1641 specifically to strip the sexual connotation out of "sensual" for one of his poems —
and then separately observes that, because "sensual" so frequently carries a sexual association,
"sensuous" itself has been drifting toward that same sexual reading in modern usage, which the source
generalizes into a broader semantic-change principle: "whenever a word becomes associated with a
sexual meaning, that meaning tends to become the dominant one." This is a directly citable, named
mechanism for this project's later slang/semantic-drift mechanics analysis.
