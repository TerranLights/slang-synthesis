## Grammar points

### §25–49. Noun declension system: overview and five learner groupings

OE nouns fall into three grammatical genders — **masculine, neuter, feminine** — marked by agreement on determiners (*se, þæt, sēo*), adjectives, and pronouns; gender is purely grammatical, not tied to real-world animacy (*bōc* 'book' is feminine, *wīfmann* 'woman' is neuter). Statistically, of the nouns a student will meet in reading: **45% are masculine** (4/5 of these have gen.sg. *-es*/nom.acc.pl. *-as*; ~1/5 have both gen.sg. and nom.acc.pl. *-an*; a small residue are irregular); **30% are feminine** (5/6 of these have gen.sg. *-e*/nom.acc.pl. *-a* or *-e*; under 1/6 have both *-an*); **25% are neuter** (almost all gen.sg. *-es*/nom.acc.pl. *-u* or no ending). Regardless of gender, nouns have gen.pl. in *-(r)a* and dat.pl. in *-um*, except that nouns with gen.sg./nom.acc.pl. in *-an* have gen.pl. in *-(e)na*, and nouns with nom.sg. ending in a long vowel/diphthong have dat.pl. in *-m*.

For learning purposes the grammar organizes nouns into five groups: **(A) General Masculine Declension**, **(B) General Neuter Declension**, **(C) General Feminine Declension**, **(D) The *-an* Declension**, **(E) Irregular Declensions**.

**(A) General Masculine Declension** (§26) — typical paradigm *cyning* 'king':

| | sg. | pl. |
|---|---|---|
| nom. | se cyning | þā cyningas |
| acc. | þone cyning | þā cyningas |
| gen. | þæs cyninges | þāra cyninga |
| dat./instr. | þǣm, þȳ cyninge | þǣm cyningum |

Sub-variants within Group A: (§27) stems in *-lh, -rh* (e.g. *wealh, mearh*) lose *-h-* before endings, with monosyllables showing *-a-* in nom.sg. but *-e-* in pl.; (§27, note) a few stems ending in vowel+*h* (*scōh, hōh*) undergo loss of *h* with contraction and lengthening of an originally short stem-vowel; (§28) dissyllables like *heofon* often weaken the second vowel to [ə] (spelt *-e-*) in inflected forms, or syncopate it entirely for dissyllables with a long first syllable (*dryhten* → gen.sg. *dryhtnes*) — some short-first-syllable dissyllables (*fugol, nicor*) show syncope too, though inflected forms without syncope also occur; (§29a) nouns of 2+ syllables ending in *-e* (agent nouns in *-ere/-end*, and dissyllables like *ende, hyrde, hwǣte*) have pl. *-eras/-era/-erum*; (§29b) a further group, historically a distinct declension, has double consonant throughout the plural (*bite→byte(ras)*, etc; *hyse, mete, mere, slege, white* and others pattern this way); (§30) the old *stede*-type paradigm shows n.a.pl. identical to the sg. (*stede/stede*); some nouns (*wine*, and notably racial/tribal names like *Dene, Engle, Myrce, Seaxe*) retain archaic *-e* plurals.

**(B) General Neuter Declension** (§31–35) — differs from masculine chiefly in that n.a.pl. may be in *-u* (especially short-stemmed monosyllables: *scip→scipu*) or have no ending (especially long-stemmed monosyllables: *land→land*). Paradigm *scip*:

| | sg. | pl. |
|---|---|---|
| n.a. | þæt scip | þā scipu |
| g. | þæs scipes | þāra scipa |
| d.i. | þǣm, þȳ scipe | þǣm scipum |

Where the n.a.sg. stem vowel is *ǣ*, we find *-a-* in the pl. (*fǣr, fæ(e)ras*); *geat* 'gate' has *-ea-* in the pl. Dissyllabic neuters in long vowel + single consonant + *-e* (*wite, ærende, fiðere, rīce, spere, yrfe*) are a distinct type (§34); some neuters of this type that were originally *ja*-stems have alternative palatal-vowel forms in the plural before endings (*rīce*: n.a.pl. *rīc(i)u*, g.pl. *rīc(e)a*). Dissyllabic neuters show variation between retaining/losing the second vowel before inflexions and between *-u*/no ending in n.a.pl.; short-first-syllable types tend to decline like *land* uninflected in n.a.pl. (*werod, reced*); nouns with long first syllable + single consonant normally show syncope in g.d.i.sg. and pl. except before *-u*, giving n.a.pl. forms mostly with the second vowel preserved (*hēafodu*).

**(C) General Feminine Declension** (§36–39) — many feminine nouns (especially short-stemmed) have n.sg. in *-u*, while others (especially long-stemmed) have n.sg. ending in a consonant. Paradigm *talu* 'tale':

| | sg. | pl. |
|---|---|---|
| n. | sēo talu | þā tala |
| a. | þā tale | þāra tala |
| g. | þǣre tale | þǣm/þām talum |
| d.i. | þǣre tale | — |

N.a.pl. of both types sometimes ends in *-e*; g.pl. (especially of short-stemmed nouns) is often *-(e)na*. Long-stemmed nouns in *-u* (*andswaru, c(e)aru, cwalu, sacu* and others; also *cȳððu, fæhðu, gesælðu, strengðu* type, originally with uninflected sg.) show alternative sg. forms without ending and alternative a.g.d.i.sg. forms in *-e*. Nouns with a final double consonant always have the double consonant throughout the paradigm (*benn, hell, sibb, wynn* and derivatives in *-en(n)/-nes(s)*); abstract derivatives in *-ung* (§§164, 170) have alternative forms in *-a* for g.d.i.sg. A distinctive paradigm (§39) applies to nouns like *dǣd* which have n.a.pl. in *-e*: *dǣd, dǣde, dǣde, dǣde* (sg.) / *dǣde, dǣda, dǣdum* (pl.) — includes *bēn, cwēn, fyrd, miht, nȳd, tīd, wēn, wyrd* and several others.

**(D) The *-an* Declension** (§40–41) — masculines in *-a* and feminines in *-e* (plus two neuters in *-e*: *ēage, ēare*) decline alike: paradigm *guma* (masc.)/*byrne* (fem.)/*ēage* (neut.) all take *-an* throughout the oblique singular and the whole plural except nom.sg.; nom.pl. is *-an* for all three genders. A few nouns in this class have gen.pl. in *-na* (notably *willa*: *wilna*); *oxa* 'ox' has gen.pl. *oxna*, dat.pl. *ox(n)um*, n.a.pl. *oxan, exen* (the latter the ancestor of Mod.E. "oxen"). A small subgroup of masculines/feminines ends in a long vowel or diphthong in the nom.sg. (*gefā, gefēa, frēa, Swēon* (pl.) 'Swedes', *wēa*; fem. *bēo, flā, tā*) — inflected by adding *-na* to the nom.sg. form for gen.pl., *-n* for other cases; *lēo* 'lion' shows the *-n-* of Latin oblique cases (*lēonan, lēonum*) plus a d.i.sg. form *lēone*.

**(E) Irregular Declensions** (§42–49) — four sub-types by plural formation: **(a) *-a* plurals**: masc. *sunu, wudu*; fem. *duru, nosu, hond*; plus many nouns with some forms like *sunu/hond* alongside General Masc./Fem. forms (*meodu, sidu, eard, feld, ford, sumor, weald*; fem. *flōr, cweorn*). **(b) *-ru* plurals**: neuter *ǣg→ǣgru*, *brēadru, cealf→cealfru, cild→cildru, lomb→lombru* (*cild, cealf, lomb* also appear with General Neuter or General Masc. inflexions). **(c) uninflected plurals**: three sub-groups — (i) masculine agent nouns in *-end* (*rīdend→rīdend*, pl. also attested in *-as* and sometimes *-r-* throughout the pl.); (ii) a few nouns with medial *-ð-* before inflexions (*hæleð* 'hero', *mōnað* 'month', fem. *mægeð* 'maiden', neut. *ealu* 'ale' — *ealu* is defective, with gen.d.i.sg. and only pl. form *ealeða*); (iii) kinship terms (*brōðor, mōdor, dohtor* unchanged in n.a.pl., with alternative pl. forms in *-(e)ru, -ra, -ra/-dra, -ðra* and sometimes mutation in gen.sg., e.g. *dohtor*'s dat.sg. *dehter*; *fæder* follows the General Masc. pattern). **(d) mutated plurals**: masc. *fōt→fēt, tōð→tīð, fēond→fīend, frēond→frīend, mann→menn*, neut. *scrūd* (patterns partly like *land*, partly mutated like *mann*); fem. *gōs→gēs, bōc→bēc, brōc, āc→ǣc, burg→byr(i)g, lūs→lȳs, mūs→mȳs, hnutu→hnyte*; *cū* 'cow' has gen.sg. *cū, cūe, cȳ, cūs*, n.a.pl. *cȳ(e)*, gen.pl. *cū(n)a*, dat.pl. *cȳna*; *furh* 'furrow' loses *h* before inflexions.

### §50–54. Adjectives: the two declensions

Every adjective (with the stated exceptions) has **two parallel sets of inflexions**: an **indefinite** declension (§51–53, on usage see §116) and a **definite** declension (§54, on usage see §117). Exceptions always indefinite: *eall, fēa(we), genōg, manig, ōðer*; always definite: *ilca* 'same', ordinal numerals (except *ōðer*), comparatives, and (mostly) superlatives.

**A. Indefinite declension** — typical paradigm *trum* 'firm':

| | masc. | neut. | fem. |
|---|---|---|---|
| sg.n. | trum | trum | trumu |
| a. | trumne | trum | trume |
| g. | trumes | trumes | trumre |
| d. | trumum | trumum | trumre |
| i. | trume | trume | trumre |
| pl.n.a. | trume | trumu | truma |
| g. | trumra | trumra | trumra |
| d.i. | trumum | trumum | trumum |

The *-u* of n.sg.fem./n.a.pl.neut. is sometimes replaced by *-o*; late texts often have n.a.pl. *-e*; occasionally a weakened ending (spelt e.g. *-an*) replaces *-um* in d.sg.masc.neut./d.i.pl. all genders. Short-stemmed monosyllables (*gram, til*) and compounds in *-lic/-sum* (*lāðlic, lufsum*) follow this same pattern. Adjectives with stem-vowel *ǣ* (*glæd, hwæt, bald*) have *-a-* before an inflexional vowel (a.sg.fem./a.sg.masc.neut. *glade* beside *glædne*). Adjectives with *-e* in n.sg.masc./n.a.sg.neut. (*āðele, dēore, grēne, mǣre, rīce, swēte*) differ from the *trum* type only in those two cells. Adjectives with *-u* in n.sg.masc./n.a.sg.neut. (*gearu* 'ready') insert *-w-* before *-e-* and *-a-*, *-o-* before consonants (*gearwes* g.sg.masc./neut., *gearowe* g.sg.fem.; *-w-* sometimes before *-um* too); fem. form *fēa* ('few', n.pl. only) is often invariable, and g.pl. is *fēa(we)ra*, d.i.pl. *fēa(we)um/-an*. *Cucu(cwicu)* 'living' is defective, recorded with *-u* for n.sg.fem. and n.a.pl. all genders only; *frēo(h)* 'free' has varied forms (n.sg.masc. *frēone, frigne*; a.sg.fem. *frēo, frige* and similar pairs). Long-stemmed monosyllables (§52) differ from *trum* in being uninflected in n.sg.masc./n.a.sg.neut. (*blind, dēad, eald, lāð*) — compound adjectives in *-isc, -lēas, -weard* and other long-syllabled elements pattern here too, as do present/past participles used adjectivally. Adjectives whose uninflected stem ends in *-h* lose the *h* in inflected forms (§189): *þweorh* 'perverse'; *fāh* 'hostile', *hēah* 'high', *nēah* 'near' double the consonant in the endings *-ne, -re,* and *-ra*.

**B. Definite declension** (§54) — typical paradigm *trum* on this pattern:

| | masc. | neut. | fem. |
|---|---|---|---|
| sg.n. | truma | trume | trume |
| a. | truman | trume | truman |
| g.d.i. | truman | truman | truman |
| pl.n.a. | truman | truman | truman |
| g. | trumra | trumra | trumra |
| d.i. | trumum | trumum | trumum |

Late texts sometimes show g.pl. *-ena*, and n.sg.masc. sometimes *-an* (*se forman dæg* 'the first day'). Adjectives like *hēah* (§52) have one n.sg. form for all genders, adding *-n* etc. for oblique cases; adjectives with *-w-* in the indefinite paradigm (§51) or with syncope before certain inflexions (§53) have these throughout the definite paradigm too, except before the *-ra* g.pl., which matches the indefinite declension's conditions.

### §53. Dissyllabic/trisyllabic adjectives in *-ig, -en, -el, -ol, -er, -or*

Where the stressed syllable is short (*manig* 'many', *swutol* 'clear'), the n.sg.fem. and n.a.pl.neut. are almost always uninflected; in such forms syncope is often resisted (*maniges, swutoles*), though it is frequent with short-stemmed forms like *miclum, yflum*. Where the stressed syllable is long (*lȳtel* 'little', *hālig* 'holy'), n.sg.fem./n.a.pl.neut. are usually in *-u* (*lȳtlu, hāligu*); syncope is usual before endings beginning in a vowel, with the exception that it tends to be resisted before *-u* (*hāl(i)gu, hālgum*) and is not frequent with adjectives in *-en* (*cristenes*).

### §55–59. Comparison of adjectives and adverbs

The comparative ends in *-ra* and is declined on the **definite** pattern (§54); the superlative ends in *-ost(a), -(e)st(a)* and is also declined on the definite pattern except that n.sg.masc./fem. and n.a.sg.neut. may follow the **indefinite** pattern. The common pattern: *earm* 'poor' → *earmra* → *earmost* 'poorest'; *heard* 'hard' → *heardra* → *heardost*; *lēof* 'dear' → *lēofra* → *lēofost*. Where the stem vowel of a monosyllabic adjective is *ǣ*, the superlative has *-a-* (*glæd* → *glædra* → *gladost*). Several adjectives with suffix-*i*-mutation (originally taking different suffixes, §209) record their superlative as *-(e)st(a)*: *eald→yldra→yldest*, *feorr→fyrra→fyrrest*, *geong→gingra→gingest*, *grēat→grȳtra→grȳtest*, *hēah→hȳrra→hȳhst*, *lang→lengra→lengest*, *sceort→scyrtra→scyrtest*, *strang→strengra→strengest*. Beside *hȳrra*, *hēah* also has variant forms *hēahra, hēaxt, hȳhst, hēah(e)st*; *nēah* has comp. *nēahra* or *nēarra*. A small group has comp./superl. from a different root than the positive (suppletive): *gōd* 'good' → *betra/sēlra* → *betst/sēlest*; *lȳtel* 'little' → *lǣssa* → *lǣst*; *micel* 'great' → *māra* → *mǣst*; *yfel* 'evil' → *wyrsa* → *wyrst*. A few more comp./superl. have no corresponding positive at all: *ǣr* 'before' → *ǣrra* 'earlier' → *ǣrest* 'first'; *ēast* 'eastwards' → *ēasterra* → *ēastmest* 'most easterly'; *inne* 'inside' → *innerra* → *innemest* 'inmost'; likewise *nord/sūð/west* with *-erra* and (for *ēast* etc.) adverbial comp./superl. in *-mest*, an old superlative suffix (cf. Lat. *primus, optimus*) surviving in *forma* 'first'.

**Adverbs** (§59) have comp. in *-or*, superl. in *-ost* or *-est*: *oft→oftor→oftost*; *hraðe→hraðor→hraðost*; *luflīce→luflīcor→luflīcost*. A few exceptional forms: *lȳt*'little'→*lǣs*→*lǣst*; *micle*'much'→*mā*→*mǣst*; *wel*'well'→*bet/sēl*→*betst/sēlest*; *yfle*'ill'→*wyrs*→*wyrst*. *Seldan* 'seldom' has comp. *seld(n)or*, superl. *seldost*; i-mutation variants also occur (*lange*'long'→*leng*→*lengest*; *nȳr* beside *nēar* 'nearer'; *sēft* 'more easily', and more).

### §60–62. Numerals

The grammar gives a full cardinal/ordinal table from 1 to 1000 (*ān, twēgen, þrȳ, fēower, fīf, syx, seofon, eahta, nigon, tȳn, endleofan, twelf, þrēotȳne, twentig, þrītig, hundseofontig, hundeahtatig, hundnigontig, hundteōntig/hund(red), hundendleofantig, hundtwelftig, tū hund(red), þūsend*). Ordinals 14–19, the *-tig* decades 22–29/40–60/130–190/300, and 200/1000 are inferable from the structure of *hund* and *þūsend*; periphrases like *æftemest ōn þǣm twǣm hundredum* 'last in the two hundreds' (i.e. "two-hundredth") are used where no ordinal form is recorded. All ordinals follow the **definite** declension except *ōðer*, which always has the indefinite inflexions. *Ān* 'one' can have both indefinite and definite inflexions (the latter meaning 'alone'), except that it has only acc.sg.masc. *ǣnne*; in the plural it means 'only, unique'. *Twēgen* has n.a.pl.masc. *twā* (with alternative neut. form *tū*); gen.pl. *twēg(r)a*, dat.pl. *twǣm* or *twām*. *Þrȳ* is also the n.a.pl.masc.; the n.a.pl.neut. and fem. is *þrēo*, gen.pl. *þrēora*, dat.pl. *þrim*. *Bēgen* 'both' patterns like *twēgen*. Cardinals 4–19 are not usually declined when used attributively, but sometimes when they stand alone take endings: n.a.masc./fem. *-e*, neut. *-u*, gen. *-a*, dat. *-um* (*fīf menn* 'five men' vs. *ic sēo fīfe* 'I see five'). Numerals in *-tig* are sometimes declined as neuter nouns (*þrītiges mīla brād* 'thirty miles wide'), more frequently with adjectival inflexions agreeing with the items counted, but frequently also with no inflexion at all; *hund* and *þūsend* are either invariable or declined as neuter nouns. Partitive-genitive idioms like *syxa sum* 'one of six' and ordinals-with-*healf* (*ōðer healf* 'one and a half', *þridde healf* 'two and a half', *fīfte healf hund* 'four hundred and fifty') are noted as special constructions.

### §63–67. Pronouns

**Personal pronouns** (§63) have distinct singular/dual/plural forms for the 1st and 2nd persons (*ic/wit/wē*, *þū/git/gē*), with case-marked nom./acc./gen./dat.-instr. forms; the 3rd person has masc./neut./fem./common(pl.) forms (*hē/hit/hēo/hī*) and no dual. Early texts sometimes show distinctive acc.sg. forms for 1st/2nd person (*mec, uncit, ūsic, þec, incit, ēowic*); 3rd person forms show considerable orthographic variety (*hīe/hīo* for *hēo*, *heom* for *him* d.i.pl.).

**Possessive pronouns** (§64) are formed from the genitives of the personal pronouns, plus (to a small extent) the 3rd-person form *sīn* 'his/her/their', an old reflexive. 1st/2nd person possessives and *sīn* take the indefinite adjective inflexion; *his, hire, hira* (3rd person genitives used possessively) are **not declined**.

**Demonstratives** (§65) — two series, *se/þæt/sēo* and *þes/þis/þēos*, each fully declined by gender/case/number (paradigms given in full, §65a–b). The alternative forms *þām* for *þǣm*, *þon* for *þȳ*, *þisse* for *þisse*, with *-y-* for *-i-* etc., are noted as common variants.

**Interrogatives** (§66) — *hwæðer* 'which (of two)' and *hwelc/hwylc* 'which (of many)' take the indefinite adjective inflexion; *hwā* 'who' has only masc./neut. singular forms (n. *hwā*/*hwæt*, a. *hwone*/*hwæt*, g. *hwæs*, d. *hwǣm*, i. *hwȳ*). Beside instr. *hwȳ* (or *hwī*), the form *hū* 'how' also exists; other alternative forms include *hwām, hwæne* for *hwone*.

**Indefinite pronouns** (§67) — the interrogatives *hwā, hwæðer, hwelc* can be used indefinitely, 'any(one), any(thing)'; many compound indefinites are built around these three (*āhwā, āhwæðer, āhwelc* 'anyone'; *gehwā, gehwylc* 'each one'; *ǣghwā, ǣghwylc* 'each one'; *swā hwā swā* 'whoever'; *hwæthwugu, hwelcwugu* 'something/someone'; *nāthwā, nāthwelc* 'someone', lit. "I don't know who"). Other indefinites: *ǣlc* 'each', *ǣnig* 'any', *man* 'one' (see §§120e, 131), *swelc(swylc)* 'such', *wiht* 'anything' (and its compounds, e.g. *āwiht*), and several others. *Hwā, hwæðer, hwelc* take the indefinite adjective inflexions; *ǣlc, ǣnig,* and *(ge)hwylc(hwyslic)* also take the indefinite inflexions, though these forms are invariable; *wiht* has *-es* and *-e* endings in adverbial function, 'at all'.
