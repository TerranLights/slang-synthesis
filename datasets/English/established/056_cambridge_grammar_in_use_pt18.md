## Grammar points

### Unit 97 — Adjectives ending in ~ing and ~ed (*boring*/*bored* etc.)

The systematic cause-adjective (*-ing*)/experiencer-adjective (*-ed*) split applying productively
across a large derived-adjective-pair set, with the explicit rule that a person described with the
*-ing* form is characterized as the SOURCE of that effect on others, not as experiencing it
themselves.

### Unit 98 — Adjectives: word order; adjectives after verbs

The opinion-before-fact adjective-stacking rule, the fact-adjective internal ordering hierarchy
(with the colour-coordination exception), the fixed *the first two days*-type ordinal+number word
order, and the copular/linking-verb-licenses-adjective rule contrasted with the verb-describing
adverb requirement.

### Unit 99 — Adjectives and adverbs (1): *quick/quickly*

The default *-ly* adverb-derivation rule (with its lookalike-adjective exception set), the core
noun-describing/verb-describing functional split, and adverbs' further role modifying adjectives,
other adverbs, and past participles.

### Unit 100 — Adjectives and adverbs (2): *well/fast/late*, *hard/hardly*

Four distinct irregularity patterns in the adjective/adverb system: the unrelated-word-form pair
*good/well* (plus *well*'s unrelated health-state sense), the zero-derivation class *fast/hard/late*
(identical adjective and adverb forms), and two false-friend *-ly* traps (*lately* ≠ "late" used
adverbially; *hardly* ≠ "hard" used adverbially) where the expected derived form exists but carries
an unrelated, often near-opposite meaning.
