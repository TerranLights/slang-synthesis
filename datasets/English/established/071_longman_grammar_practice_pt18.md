## Grammar points

### 16.1 The bare infinitive and the to-infinitive

**16.1A Forms of the infinitive.** The bare infinitive (base verb form, no *to*) is distinguished from
the to-infinitive (always preceded by *to*); the bare infinitive's most common use is after a modal
verb. A full active/passive paradigm exists across present, present progressive, perfect/past, and
perfect/past progressive infinitive forms (the progressive forms have no passive equivalent), p.226.

**16.1B 'Let', 'make', 'would rather/sooner' and 'had better'.** *Let's* + bare infinitive makes
suggestions. *Let* as a full verb (= "allow") always takes a noun/pronoun object + bare infinitive.
*Make* (= "compel") takes a bare infinitive in the active but a to-infinitive in the passive. *Would
rather (not), would sooner (not), had better (not)* all take the bare infinitive, p.226.

**16.1C The infinitive with or without 'to' after 'help' and 'know'.** Both verbs allow either a bare
or to-infinitive, with the to-infinitive being more formal; *to* is normally kept after *not* with
*help*. *Help* can appear without a noun/pronoun object; *know* always takes one, and its passive
always keeps *to*, p.227.

### 16.2 The bare infinitive or the '-ing' form; the to-infinitive

**16.2A Verbs of perception: 'Watch him draw/drawing'.** A set of perception verbs (*feel, hear,
listen to, look at, notice, observe, perceive, see, smell, watch*) take a noun/pronoun object plus
either the bare infinitive (for a complete action, start to finish) or -ing (for an action caught in
progress). Four of these (*hear, observe, perceive, see*) are also commonly used in the passive + -ing
or to-infinitive, p.228.

**16.2B ''Have' + object: 'Have the next patient come in''.** Causing someone to do something for the
speaker uses *have* + object + bare infinitive (extending to *have* + *know*/*believe*); using *have* +
object + -ing instead shifts the meaning to an intended result or an unintended consequence, p.228.

**16.2C ''(Not) to', 'so as (not) to', 'in order (not) to''.** *To, so as to,* and *in order to* all
express purpose; the bare *to*-infinitive (not the other two) can alternatively mark a following event
in a sequence. *Not to* marks a contrasting alternative purpose; *so as not to*/*in order not to*
express negative purpose (never "*to not*"). Many ordinary verbs (*appear, arrange, hope, 'd like,
want*) simply take a to-infinitive directly; a key negation-scope contrast exists between "didn't tell
X to" and "told X not to," p.229.

### 16.3 Verb (+ noun/pronoun) + to-infinitive

**16.3A ''He can't afford to buy it''.** A set of verbs (*afford, aim, apply, fail, hasten, hurry,
manage, offer, prepare, refuse, seek*) takes only a to-infinitive — never -ing, never *that*, p.230.

**16.3B ''I want (you) to speak to him''.** A different verb set (*ask, beg, choose, expect, hate,
help, like, love, need, prefer, want, wish*) takes a to-infinitive either directly or with an
intervening noun/pronoun object — but never "*I want that you...*," p.230.

**16.3C ''He advised me to take out a loan''.** A further verb set (*advise, allow, entitle, forbid,
invite, order, remind, (it) takes, teach, tell, warn*) always requires an object before the
to-infinitive, p.230.

**16.3D ''I know him to be an honest man''.** A verb set referring to people/things (*believe,
consider, discover, find, imagine, know, prove, suppose, think, understand*) allows *that* or a
to-infinitive interchangeably, p.231.

**16.3E ''I agreed to accept their offer''.** A second, distinct verb set (*agree, arrange, claim,
choose, decide, expect, hope, pretend, promise, swear, threaten, wish*) likewise allows *that* or a
to-infinitive interchangeably, p.231.

### 16.4 Adjectives and nouns + to-infinitive

**16.4A Adjective + 'to': ''It was kind (of him) to help us''.** Adjectives of personal judgement
(*clever, foolish, generous, good, polite, right/wrong, rude, selfish, silly, wicked*) combine with
*of* + a personal subject, or with *it*, or with *as*. A separate adjective class (*afraid, anxious,
ashamed, careful, curious, eager, fit, free, frightened, glad, keen, sorry*) takes only a personal
subject, never *it*. A third class (*agreeable, amusing, boring, difficult, easy, hard, impossible*)
takes a personal subject or *it*, but never *of him*, p.232.

**16.4B Adjectives with 'too/enough': ''too weak/not strong enough to''.** *Too* + adjective expresses
excess (implying inability); adjective + *enough* expresses sufficiency (to the necessary degree) —
together these combine two underlying ideas into one construction, p.232.

**16.4C Noun + to-infinitive: ''My decision to wait was wise''.** Nouns related to to-infinitive-
taking verbs may share the verb's form (*wish to* → *my wish to*) or differ from it (*decide to* →
*my decision to*); nouns related to to-infinitive-taking adjectives usually take a different form from
the adjective (*eager to* → *my eagerness to*), p.233.
