**Source:** Carole Hough and John Corbett, *Beginning Old English* (Palgrave Macmillan, 2007),

**Coverage note:** three distinct kinds of content. (1) *Concluding Remarks* is a one-page authorial
envoi with no new grammar/vocabulary content — summarized briefly rather than tabulated. (2) The
*Glossary of Technical Terms* defines the book's own metalinguistic/literary-critical vocabulary
(Case, Mood, Kenning, Litotes-adjacent terms, metrical terminology, etc.) — this is PDE scholarly
terminology *about* Old English, not Old English vocabulary itself, so it is captured as a
consolidated reference list in Grammar points rather than the Vocabulary table (which is reserved
for actual target-language — i.e. Old English — lexical items per the project's column conventions).
(3) The **Appendix: Old English Paradigms** is pure grammatical paradigm data (pronoun, noun,
determiner, adjective, and verb declension/conjugation tables) — the single highest-value grammar
reference in the entire book, since it's the systematic tabulation that every preceding chapter's
piecemeal in-context paradigms built toward. These tables are reproduced in full here, as factual
grammatical data (not creative/narrative prose), consistent with how this project treats paradigm

## Grammar points

### Glossary of Technical Terms: consolidated reference (p.235–240)

The source's own closing glossary defines the book's full set of grammatical and literary-critical
terminology. Most terms were already introduced and used in context across pt01–pt12; this is the
book's own systematic consolidation. Worth recording as a single reference list for this project's
own cross-file indexing, grouped by domain:

- **Grammatical categories:** Case (Nominative/Accusative/Genitive/Dative), Gender
  (masculine/feminine/neuter — the source stresses OE gender is "more arbitrary" than PDE's
  biological-sex-based "natural" gender, cf. pt02's discussion), Number (singular/plural), Person
  (1st/2nd/3rd), Tense, Mood (indicative/subjunctive/imperative), Voice (active/passive),
  Declension/Paradigm (used interchangeably for both "the full case-table of a noun" and "a class of
  nouns sharing an inflexional pattern").
- **Word classes:** Adjective (the source formally defines OE's weak/strong adjective split here,
  cf. the Appendix tables below), Adverb, Conjunction, Determiner, Noun, Preposition, Pronoun, Verb
  (formally defining the strong/weak verb split, cf. pt04).
- **Clause-level terms:** Clause, Subordinate clause, Subject, Object, Infinitive, Inflexion,
  Participle (present participle pairs with 'be', past participle pairs with 'have' — cf. pt04's
  duration and perfect-construction discussion), Stem.
- **Sound/phonology terms:** Consonant, Vowel (including the long/short vowel-duration distinction,
  with a Scottish-accent example — 'agreed' vs. 'greed' — the source's own illustrative pair),
  Voiced/Unvoiced (of consonants, e.g. the two senses of 'th'), Syllable, Stress, Metathesis
  (cf. pt02).
- **Poetic-device terms (consolidating Chapter 6's full catalogue):** Alliteration, Half-line
  (a-verse/b-verse), Headstave, Kenning (defined here via a non-Beowulf example, "whale's road" for
  the sea — a kenning type not otherwise attested in this book's own worked texts, worth noting as an
  independent confirmation that this device was genuinely productive beyond the specific examples
  already catalogued), Formula, Variation, Circumlocution, Euphemism, Ambiguity, Prosopopoeia (OE
  riddles' first-person-object convention, cf. pt05/pt11), Crux (a textual puzzle — cf. this
  project's own repeated crux discussions: *ealu-scerwen* pt08, *ofer-mōde* pt09, Wistan's patronymic
  pt10).
- **Genre terms:** Riddle, Elegy, Epic, Heroic poetry (defined via the "heroic code," cf. pt06's
  comitatus discussion), Ballad (defined with its characteristic rhyme/meter scheme, relevant to
  pt06's translation-chapter discussion of Lumsden's ballad-measure Beowulf).
- **Metrical terms (consolidating Chapter 7's translation-analysis vocabulary):** Foot, Measure,
  Stanza, Couplet, Iambic, Anapaest, Anacrusis, Thesis, and the Greco-Latin line-length names
  Trimeter/Tetrameter/Pentameter/Heptameter — all cross-referenced to the specific translators'
  metrical choices discussed in pt06 (Hall's Browning-influenced four-beat line, Morris & Wyatt's
  anapaestic tetrameter, Lumsden's heptameter ballad-couplets).
- **Compounding:** Compound word (defined with a non-book example, 'gift-giver', distinct from any
  of the dozens of genuine OE compounds already catalogued across pt01–pt12), Synonyms.
- **Textual-scholarship terms:** Dialect, Gloss (as a verb, 'to explain a word' — distinct from its
  noun use already established as a vocabulary item in pt01).

### Appendix: Old English Paradigms — the book's systematic grammar reference (p.241–245)

This is the single densest concentration of pure grammatical data in the book, consolidating
paradigms only partially shown in running-text form across Chapters 2–5 and Part II. Reproduced here
in full as factual grammatical data (paradigm tables are not creative/copyrightable content in the
same sense as narrative prose).

**1. Pronouns — the complete case/number/person paradigm** (expands the partial tables given across
pt02–pt04; this is the authoritative full version):

| | Nom. | Acc. | Gen. | Dat. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1sg | ic | mē | mīn | mē |
| 2sg | ðū | ðē | ðīn | ðē |
| 3sg masc. | hē | hine | his | him |
| 3sg fem. | hēo | hīe | hire | hire |
| 3sg neut. | hit | hit | his | him |
| 1pl | wē | ūs | ūre | ūs |
| 2pl | gē | ēow | ēower | ēow |
| 3pl (all genders) | hīe | hīe | hira | him |

**2. Nouns — the three strong-declension genders, plus the weak ('-an') declension:**

Masculine strong (*cyning* 'king'): Nom./Acc. sg. *cyning*, Gen. sg. *cyninges*, Dat. sg. *cyninge*;
Nom./Acc. pl. *cyningas*, Gen. pl. *cyninga*, Dat. pl. *cyningum*.

Feminine strong (*heall* 'hall'): Nom. sg. *heall*, Acc./Gen./Dat. sg. *healle*; Nom./Acc./Gen. pl.
*healla*, Dat. pl. *heallum*.

Neuter strong (*scip* 'ship'): Nom./Acc. sg. *scip*, Gen. sg. *scipes*, Dat. sg. *scipe*; Nom./Acc.
pl. *scipu*, Gen. pl. *scipa*, Dat. pl. *scipum*. The source notes neuter nouns with a "long"
syllable (long vowel, or short vowel + 2+ consonants) take **no ending** in Nom./Acc. plural — the
direct historical source of PDE endingless plurals like *deer*, *sheep*.

Weak/'-an declension' (all genders, masc. Nom. sg. in *-a*, fem./neut. in *-e*; example *draca*
'dragon'): Nom. sg. *draca*, Acc./Gen./Dat. sg. *dracan*; Nom./Acc. pl. *dracan*, Gen. pl.
*dracena*, Dat. pl. *dracum*.

**Irregular nouns:** kinship terms (*fæder* 'father', *mōdor* 'mother', *brōðor* 'brother', *dohtor*
'daughter', *sweostor* 'sister') typically take no case endings at all except Gen. pl. *-a* and Dat.
pl. *-um*. A separate small class marks case/number by **internal vowel change** rather than suffix
— modeled on *tōð* 'tooth': Nom./Acc. sg. *tōð*, Gen./Dat. sg. *tēð*; Nom./Acc. pl. *tēð*, Gen. pl.
*tōða*, Dat. pl. *tōðum* — the direct ancestor of PDE's irregular vowel-change plurals *foot/feet*,
*goose/geese*, *tooth/teeth*. The source notes OE *bōc* 'book' was originally of this type too, but
has since regularized to the standard *-s* plural in PDE (*book/books*, not *book/beek*).

**3. Determiners — full paradigms for *se* 'the' and *ðes* 'this':**

*se* 'the': Nom. sg. masc./fem./neut. *se*/*sēo*/*ðæt*; Acc. sg. *ðone*/*ðā*/*ðæt*; Gen. sg.
*ðæs*/*ðǣre*/*ðæs*; Dat. sg. *ðǣm*/*ðǣre*/*ðǣm*; all-gender pl.: Nom./Acc. *ðā*, Gen. *ðāra*, Dat.
*ðǣm*.

*ðes* 'this': Nom. sg. masc./fem./neut. *ðes*/*ðēos*/*ðis*; Acc. sg. *ðisne*/*ðās*/*ðis*; Gen. sg.
*ðisses*/*ðisse*/*ðisses*; Dat. sg. *ðissum*/*ðisse*/*ðissum*; all-gender pl.: Nom./Acc. *ðās*, Gen.
*ðissa*, Dat. *ðissum*.

**4. Adjectives — weak vs. strong declension, modeled on *gōd* 'good':**

Weak (used when preceded by a determiner, e.g. "the good..."): heavily syncretic, dominated by *-an*
across most cells — Nom. sg. *gōda*/*gōde*/*gōde* (masc./fem./neut.), remaining singular cells and
the entire plural almost uniformly *-an* (Gen. pl. *gōdra*, Dat. pl. *gōdum* the only exceptions).

Strong (used with no determiner, e.g. "good..." alone): more differentiated — masc. sg. Nom. *gōd*,
Acc. *gōdne*, Gen. *gōdes*, Dat. *gōdum*; fem. sg. Nom./Acc. *gōd*/*gōde*, Gen./Dat. *gōdre*; neut.
sg. Nom./Acc. *gōd*, Gen. *gōdes*, Dat. *gōdum*; plural (all genders) Nom./Acc. *gōde* (neut. *gōd*),
Gen. *gōdra*, Dat. *gōdum*. This formalizes the weak/strong adjective distinction used informally
throughout Chapter 3 and Part II's running glosses.

**5. Verbs — full indicative/participle and subjunctive/imperative paradigms, modeled on weak
*wunian* 'to live, dwell', strong *feohtan* 'to fight', and irregular *bēon* 'to be':**

*Indicative/participle table:*

|---|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | wunian | feohtan | bēon |
| Pres. participle | wuniende | feohtende | wesende |
| 1sg pres. | wunie | feohte | eom |
