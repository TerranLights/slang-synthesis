## Grammar points

### Unit 94 — Relative clauses (4): "extra information" clauses (1)

The book's core restrictive/non-restrictive ("Type 1"/"Type 2") relative-clause distinction, with
comma-marking treated as a genuine grammatical signal rather than mere punctuation style, and the
categorical (not preferential) restrictions each type places on *that*-use and pronoun omission.

### Unit 95 — Relative clauses (5): "extra information" clauses (2)

The formal fronted-preposition pattern specific to non-restrictive clauses, the large and
productive partitive-quantifier-plus-relativizer construction (generalizing Units 87–88's
partitive-quantifier system into relative syntax), and the whole-clause relativizer *which* (never
*what*) referring back to an entire preceding proposition.

### Unit 96 — ~ing and ~ed clauses

The active/passive participial reduced-relative-clause pair: *~ing* clauses for ongoing actions or
permanent properties, *~ed* clauses for passive-equivalent descriptions (including irregular
participles and the fixed idiom *left*), both frequently combined with existential *there is/there
was*.
