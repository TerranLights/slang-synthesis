## Grammar points

### Chapter framing: pronunciation vs. grammar/usage (p. 227)

Unlike every other chapter, this one addresses pure pronunciation rather than grammar or word-choice
errors, and the source explicitly notes it has no written test, since "a written test can't tell you
much about how well you're pronouncing a word" — readers are instead told to practice the correct
forms aloud. All 27 entries follow a minimal Don't-Say/Say-Instead format without the fuller "Here's
Why" explanatory paragraphs used elsewhere in the book; most just give a brief phonetic tip (which
syllable/letter to watch) rather than a usage rationale.

### Recurring error patterns across errors 149–175

Several mispronunciations cluster into recognizable sub-patterns: (1) **dropped consonants/syllables**
— *libary* (153, dropped "r"), *reconize* (154, dropped "g"), *stricly* (155, dropped "t"),
*goverment* (158, dropped "n"), *temperment* (160, dropped "a"), *histry* (164, dropped "tor"),
*accidently* (169, dropped "tal"), *representive* (170, dropped "ta"), *artic* (173, dropped "c"),
*anartica* (174, dropped "t" and "c"); (2) **added/extra sounds** — *heighth* (156, added "th" by
false analogy with "width"), *athaletics* (157, added "a"), *mischevious*/*grevious* (162–163, added
"ee," explicitly linked by the source as the same pattern), *disasterous* (168, added "ter"); (3) a
**"per-"/"pre-" confusion cluster** spanning three separate entries — *perscription* (166, wrongly
"per-" for "pre-"), *prespiration* (167, wrongly "pre-" for "per-," the mirror-image error), and
*preform* (171, wrongly "pre-" for "per-" again); and (4) one **dialectal variant** rather than a true
error — *histry* (164) is explicitly flagged by the source as a legitimately British pronunciation,
not simply wrong, just non-American. Error 161 (*lightening* vs. *lightning*) is the chapter's one
genuine two-word meaning confusion (paralleling Chapters 8–10) rather than a pure articulation slip.
