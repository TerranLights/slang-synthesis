## Grammar points

### Lesson 26 (ふたが開かないんです。 — "The Lid Won't Open"), pp. 75–84
Goals: basic understanding of the intransitive/transitive (自動詞/他動詞) verb distinction; communicating a problem and requesting a repair.

**自動詞と他動詞 (core grammar point, pp. 82–83)** — the lesson's explicit pedagogical framework, stated as six numbered learning targets rather than a single rule:
1. Japanese verbs fall into two groups: 自動詞 (intransitive) describes the subject itself changing/moving; 他動詞 (transitive) describes the subject acting on/changing something else.
2. Particle rule: intransitive-verb subjects take が (or は if topicalized); transitive-verb objects (the thing acted upon) take を.
3. Explicit pedagogical caution: the morphological shapes of intransitive/transitive pairs are NOT reliably rule-governed (unlike, say, a regular conjugation pattern) — the book states outright that each pair must be memorized individually rather than derived from a formula.
4. High-frequency everyday pairs are to be learned anchored to their textbook scene/context rather than as an abstract list.
5. Not every verb has a transitive/intransitive partner — some verbs exist only as one or the other.
6. Pedagogical habit-forming goal: learners should get in the habit of asking "is this verb transitive or intransitive?" whenever they encounter a new verb going forward.

Three teaching sub-lenses are explicitly given for organizing the 15 verb pairs drilled in this lesson (with an explicit warning that this three-way split is a teaching convenience, not a fixed semantic category the student should treat as rigid — the same event can be framed through more than one lens depending on which verb pair is picked, e.g. 入る/入れる can be viewed either as a 7–12-type "acted-upon-object" pair via おふろに入る/おふろに入れる, or equally validly via 荷物がかばんに入る/荷物をかばんに入れる):
- Pairs ①–⑥: lowest-inference pairs, illustrating "does the object move/change by itself, or does a person have to touch it?"
- Pairs ⑦–⑫: pairs viewed through "is the focus on the act of a person affecting an object, or on the resulting state/change of the object?"
- Pairs ⑬–⑮: pairs where the entity acted upon is itself a person/animal (not an inanimate object), viewed through "does the entity act on its own volition, or is it caused to act by another?" (起きる/起こす is this book's worked example.)

Additional nuance embedded in the dialogue annotations (not separately numbered 文型, but genuine comparative/usage commentary):
- **Negated intransitive vs. negated potential-form minimal pair**: ドアが開かない ("the door won't open," intransitive negative) locates the cause of failure in the object itself (something's wrong with the door) — contrasted with ドアが開けられない ("I can't open the door," potential-form negative), which locates the cause in the subject's own inability/circumstance (e.g. can't reach the doorknob). This is flagged as a genuine source of confusion since intransitive-verb negation can semantically resemble potential-form negation.
- **搜していた物が見つかった時のテンス (tense on finding a sought object)**: once a previously-sought item is located, Japanese switches to the past tense (ありました, not あります) even though the utterance describes the present moment — an idiomatic present-via-past usage flagged explicitly as something to confirm with learners.
- **故障した車は「動けない」にならない**: a broken-down car takes the intransitive negative 動かない, not the potential-form negative 動けない — because the car's failure to move has an external cause (mechanical failure) rather than reflecting the car's own "ability."

1. **〜ても (concession, "even if/even though")** — restricted in this lesson to the case where the premise is met but the expected natural consequence from と/ば/たら conditionals (cross-referenced to L12, L22, L31) fails to occur, i.e. 〜ても is taught as the systematic negation/counter-case of those already-taught conditionals (砂糖を入れると甘くなります ↔ 砂糖を入れても甘くなりません). The extended sense of 〜ても describing an inherent personal trait/constitution regardless of effort (夜遅く寝ても、早く起きられる — "even if I stay up late, I wake up early [it's just how I am]") is introduced only as a brief aside/recognition item, explicitly to avoid confusing it with the main conditional-negation sense drilled here; the frequency/repeated-effort-failure sense (いくら読んでもわかりません) is deferred to L35.
2. **〜んじゃなくて／〜のではなくて** — corrects a listener's apparent misunderstanding/assumption ("it's not X, it's Y"). Connects to basic plain-non-past form for verbs, but to the dictionary form + じゃなくて for nouns (asymmetric connection rule flagged for attention). Practiced with both present- and past-tense antecedents.
3. **〜ことがあります (possibility, "it can happen that…")** — a second, distinct sense of ことがある from the "past experience" sense taught at L22 (which required a past-tense antecedent); here the antecedent is non-past and the meaning is "there's a possibility this occurs," explicitly restricted in this lesson's own drilling to warning/caution contexts (電池の方向を間違えると、こわれることがあります — "if you insert the battery backwards, it can sometimes break").
