Section-heading terms (e.g. "Music," "Comedy," "Fashion") are included as ordinary vocabulary
rows since the book itself gives them full romaji+script treatment like any other entry. Full
phrase-template entries (e.g. "What bands do you like?") are included with Part of Speech `phrase`
since the book presents them identically to single-word entries (bold English / romaji / script),

dialect markers, polite *-masu* forms, etc.).

## Grammar points

This chapter is a slang/vocabulary dictionary section with no formal grammar exposition. Two
recurring morphosyntactic patterns are worth noting as cross-cutting observations rather than
full grammar points:

### "~kei" (系) as a productive subculture/genre-naming suffix

Attested on at least 10 fashion- and music-subculture terms in this chapter alone (janizu kei,
bijuaru kei, abakuro kei, mōdo kei, bikei, gyaru kei, konsaba kei, onē kei, miritari kei, goshikku
kei, gosurori kei). The suffix 系 (lit. "system/lineage/type") attaches to a clipped loanword or
descriptor to name a recognized fashion or music "scene" — a highly productive pattern for coining
new subculture labels.

### Sentence-final-particle register marking

Several example phrases in this chapter carry sentence-final particles or Kansai-dialect markers
that mark informal/masculine or regional register distinctly from the neutral glosses given
(e.g. *ze* in "…kikō ze," Kansai *yanen*/*omoroi*/*aho* across the comedy section's tsukkomi/boke
examples). These are the clearest explicit register/regional annotations this chapter provides and
are flagged per-row above rather than only noted here.
