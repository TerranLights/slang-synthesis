## Grammar points

### Slang intensifier stacking (maji, chō, mecha)
p.41–44 show three competing slang intensifiers — *maji* ("seriously"), *chō* ("super/ultra"), and
*mecha* ("really") — used interchangeably and even stacked (*maji chō hima dayo*, "I'm totally
bored stupid," p.41) ahead of an adjective or verb, a hallmark of contemporary casual/youth
register.

### Masculine rough-speech endings (-ze, -zo, -nē)
Several fill-in templates use blunt masculine sentence-final particles: *-ze* (*shiyōze*, "c'mon,
let's..."; *nomō ze*, "let's drink") and *-zo* (*ageru zo*, *ōru suru zo*) add brash emphasis,
while *-nē* substituting for standard *-nai* (*shitakunē* for *shitakunai*) marks rough/masculine
pronunciation of a negative form (p.39–43).

### Mimetic (giongo/gitaigo) words for drunkenness
The chapter's "fucked-up-edness" progression (p.53–54) is built on a ladder of mimetic/reduplicated
words marking increasing intoxication: *fura fura* (wobbly/tipsy) → *beron beron* (smashed) →
*guden guden* (trashed) → *chō deisui jyōtai* (three sheets to the wind), illustrating how Japanese
mimetic vocabulary scales expressive intensity through reduplication and intensifier prefixing
rather than distinct root words.
