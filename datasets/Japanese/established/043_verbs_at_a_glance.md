## Grammar points

### Three conjugation groups and their cross-textbook terminology (pp. 11–14)

The book's own three-way "Group 1/Group 2/Group 3" terminology is explicitly cross-referenced
against the terms used by other Japanese textbooks and dictionaries, which this project's other
established Japanese files do not currently record in one place:

- **Group 1** (ends in plain-form -u): also called *godan dōshi* 五段動詞 ("godan verbs"), *shiin
  gokan dōshi* 子音語幹動詞 ("consonant-stem verbs"), *kyōhenka dōshi* 強変化動詞 ("strong verbs"), or
  simply "-u verbs."
- **Group 2** (ends in -eru or -iru, single conjugation pattern): also called *ichidan dōshi*
  一段動詞 ("ichidan verbs"), *boin gokan dōshi* 母音語幹動詞 ("vowel-stem verbs"), *jakuhenka dōshi*
  弱変化動詞 ("weak verbs"), or "-ru verbs."
- **Group 3** (irregular, exactly two verbs: kuru 来る "to come," suru する "to do"): also called
  *fukisoku dōshi* 不規則動詞 ("irregular verbs"); kuru is specifically the *ka-gyō henkaku katsuyō
  dōshi* カ行変格活用動詞 ("ka-row irregular verb") and suru the *sa-gyō henkaku katsuyō dōshi*
  サ行変格活用動詞 ("sa-row irregular verb").

This terminology cross-reference is useful because this project's other Japanese grammar sources
(028, 035) each use only one naming convention (Class I/Class II in 028; unnamed "two verb classes"
in 035d) without cross-mapping to the godan/ichidan terminology that appears in many other Japanese
pedagogical and dictionary sources a later synthesis phase might encounter.

### Group 1 verbs organized by final-mora phonological category (pp. 12–13)

Distinct from 028's and 035d's class-based explanations, this book tabulates Group 1 verbs by the
specific consonant (or vowel, for the smaller "-u preceded by a vowel" subset) immediately preceding
the final -u: -u (vowel-preceded), -ku, -gu, -su, -tsu, -nu (only one verb, shinu, in the whole
language), -bu, -mu, -ru. This is a genuinely different organizing principle from the "Class
I/vowel-stem vs. Class II/consonant-stem" binary used elsewhere in this project's Japanese files,
and makes explicit that the -nu category is a closed set of exactly one verb — a fact not stated
in 028 or 035.

### Group 1 verbs that look like Group 2 verbs, and vice versa (p. 13)

The book flags that some Group 1 verbs end in -iru or -eru (the standard Group 2 shape) — iru 要る
"to be required," kaeru 帰る "to return," kiru 切る "to cut," shiru 知る "to know," hairu 入る "to
enter," hashiru 走る "to run" — meaning the -iru/-eru ending alone cannot reliably predict
conjugation group membership; a learner must know these specific exceptions. This is a sharper,
explicitly-flagged caveat than either 028 or 035 state about the two-class system.

### The /-masu/ stem as a named intermediate form (pp. 16, 20)

The book adopts its own notational convention — writing the removed -masu ending within slashes and
struck through (e.g. nomi/̶m̶a̶s̶u̶/) — to name the bare stem that many other suffixes (covered in
Chapters 2–4) attach to. This "/-masu/ stem" terminology recurs as the base form cited throughout
the rest of the book's suffix tables (see 043b–043e) and is recorded here because this project's
other Japanese sources don't use this exact naming convention, even though the underlying
morphological fact (the -masu-stripped stem is a genuine attachment site for many suffixes) is
already established in 035d/035e.

### The -te form's phonological sandhi, tabulated by named rule number (pp. 16–17)

The book tabulates the Group 1 -te-form sandhi rule exhaustively by final-mora category with an
explicit rule-numbering scheme (e.g. "-au = a/̶u̶/ + tte," "-ku = /̶k̶u̶/ + ite," "-gu = /̶g̶u̶/ + ide,"
"-su = /̶s̶u̶/ + shite," "-tsu = /̶t̶s̶u̶/ + tte," "-nu = /̶n̶u̶/ + nde," "-bu = /̶b̶u̶/ + nde," "-mu =
/̶m̶u̶/ + nde," "-ru = /̶r̶u̶/ + tte"), explicitly flagging iku → itte as the one irregular exception
within the -ku category (expected ikite, actual itte). This matches the sandhi rule already
paraphrased in 028's "Class II stem-final consonant assimilation before -t- suffixes" section, but
this book's version is organized as a literal lookup table by rule label rather than prose, and
explicitly calls out the iku exception by name (028 mentions iku's irregularity elsewhere but not
at this specific juncture).

### Copula forms cross-referenced to each verb conjugation category (pp. 13, 15, 17, 19, 21)

Though not itself a verb, the copula da だ is given a parallel form at each conjugation juncture
introduced in this chapter: formal desu です (present), deshita でした (past), de wa nai ではない
(negative, informally contracted to ja nai じゃない), de で (-te form), datta だった (informal past).
This is consistent with — and slightly more complete than — the scattered copula notes in 028 and
035, which don't tabulate the full copula paradigm against every verb-conjugation category in one
place.
