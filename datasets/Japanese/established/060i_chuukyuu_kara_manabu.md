## Grammar points

### Public-notice register: bare imperative/prohibitive forms vs. softened paraphrase

Lesson 7's 速読 passage contrasts the blunt command register typically used on public signs
(short imperatives like 払え "Pay!", and the plain-form prohibitive 〜な attached to a verb stem,
as in 「花はとるな」「ゴミは落とすな」「入るな」) with longer, politer paraphrases that convey the
same instruction less abruptly (e.g. a mountain trail's own posted sign reading「とるものは写真だけ、
残すものは思い出だけ」instead of a blunt "do not pick flowers," or 「100ｍ向こうでわたれます」instead
of 「わたるな」). The passage's own argument is that the softer phrasing produces more genuine
compliance than the terse command form. The comprehension-check section (B, question 9) asks
students to draft their own softened paraphrase of 「駐車禁止」as a follow-on exercise — this is
drill content and was not separately captured as vocabulary, but is noted here as it's directly
tied to the grammar point.

### 〜（さ）せられる-type causative-passive used for rhetorical effect

Lesson 9's 速読 passage uses the construction 「右か左かとなかされる人」("people who are made to cry
over [whether a restroom door is marked] right or left") — a causative-passive built on 泣く "to
cry," used for comic/rhetorical effect to describe travelers frustrated by unclear
pictogram-only signage. Illustrative of how the causative-passive can carry an exaggerated or
humorous tone beyond its literal "forced to do X" meaning, consistent with the construction's
standard grammar (covered more formally elsewhere in the main textbook's own grammar sections,
not reproduced here).
