## Grammar points

### Kanji-reading traps: 長音・濁音・促音・撥音 (p. 7)

The book's opening strategy page for 問題Ⅰ (kanji-reading questions) teaches four specific confusion axes test-takers must check for in any kanji compound's reading: whether a vowel is long or short (長音), whether a consonant is voiced or voiceless (濁音, marked with ゛), whether a geminate/double consonant (っ) is present, and whether a moraic ん is present. The book's own worked minimal-pair set is 後悔(こうかい)/郊外(こうがい)/国会(こっかい)/今回(こんかい) — four words that look superficially similar in romanization but differ along exactly these four axes. This is a genuinely useful phonological-contrast framework not present in the Kanzen Master series (which teaches grammar patterns, not kanji-reading test mechanics).

### Kanji-writing traps: shared phonetic components and near-synonym verb families (p. 9)

For 問題Ⅲ (choosing the correct kanji to write a given reading), the book identifies two systematic trap types: (1) kanji that share a phonetic component and therefore the same on'yomi reading but differ in meaning (e.g. the 交 component shared by 郊/効/校/絞, all read こう), and (2) near-synonym verb families where several kanji attach to similar meanings but differ in nuance (e.g. 揺/振/震/翻, all describing some kind of physical motion/shaking but with distinct specific senses: 振る = to shake/wave, 震える = to tremble, 翻る = to flip over). A companion drill (問題Ⅳ, same page) extends this to same-reading-different-kanji pairs disambiguated purely by context (更ける/老ける both ふける; 解ける/溶ける both とける; 積む/摘む both つむ; 責める/攻める both せめる; 乗る/載る both のる).

### Sentence-final/concluding-expression checklist, pp. 34–37 (「〜といい〜といい」 through 「〜を余儀なくされる」)

This is a compact alphabetized reference list of ~25 fixed grammatical patterns typically appearing at or near a sentence's conclusion, each with a single illustrative example sentence — functioning as a quick-reference companion to the book's problem-type explanations rather than a full grammar-point lesson. Patterns already captured in the Kanzen Master N1 extraction (044/044b/044c) and not re-explained here: 〜にひきかえ (044b pt. 71), 〜を禁じ得ない (044b pt. 83). Patterns genuinely new to this project's corpus, worth naming even without full re-explanation given the list's compactness:

- **〜ときたら** — exasperated/critical topic-marking ("as for ~, [complaint follows]"); example: 山田さんときたら、シャツは裏返し、ズボンはしわくちゃ…
- **〜とばかりに** — "as if to say ~" (describing an action that silently but clearly conveys an attitude); example: 道ばたに品物と粗末な料金箱が置いてある、持っていきたいやつは勝手に持っていけとばかりに。
- **〜ともなると** — "once it reaches the stage of ~" (a threshold is crossed and consequences follow); example: 子供も中学生ともなると親の言う通りにはならない。
- **〜ないまでも** — "even if not (quite) ~, at least ~"; example: わざわざ謝りに行かないまでも、せめてお詫びの手紙ぐらい書くものだ。
- **〜なくして(は)** — "without ~" (a necessary precondition, formal register); example: 経済的自立なくして、一人前の大人とは言えない。
- **〜なしに** — "without ~ing" (an expected accompanying action is missing); example: 今回の失敗に対する反省なしに次に進んでも、また失敗するだけだ。
- **〜ならでは(の)** — "uniquely characteristic of ~, something only ~ could produce"; example: ユーモアあふれる物語は、この作家ならではのものです。
- **〜なり** — "no sooner than ~, immediately upon ~ing"; example: よほど疲れていたのだろう。布団に入るなり寝てしまった。
- **〜なりに** — "in one's own way, to the degree appropriate for ~"; example: たとえ不合格になっても自分なりに精一杯努力したので悔いはない。
- **〜にあって** — "in the midst of/under the circumstance of ~" (formal); example: 冬山での遭難という極限状態にあって冷静に行動できる人は少ない。
- **〜にして** — "even at/only at ~ (a notably early/late point)"; example: 彼は10歳にして天才ピアニストと呼ばれ注目を集めている。
- **〜に即して** — "in accordance with/true to ~"; example: 事実に即して書かれた小説だけあって描写が生々しい。
- **〜にひきかえ今年は** — used here in a "compared to last year" sense distinct from 044b's basic contrastive gloss; example: 昨年にひきかえ今年は農作物の収穫がよくない。
- **〜にもまして** — "even more than ~"; example: 70歳になるというのに、祖父は以前にもましてボランティア活動を熱心に行っている。
- **〜にあたらない** — "is not (surprising/worth) ~ing" (downplaying a seemingly notable fact); example: 晩婚化が進んでいるといっても驚くにあたらない。
- **〜にかたくない** — "is easy to ~" (said of imagining/guessing); example: 宇宙開発が人類の可能性を押し上げることは、想像にかたくない。
- **〜ばそれまでだ** — "if ~, then that's the end of it (nothing more can be done)"; example: 論文の締切まで、全力でやってみるが、間に合わなければそれまでだ。
- **〜までだ／〜までのことだ** — "all one can do is ~; it's simply a matter of ~"; example: 失敗したら、やり直すまでのことだ。あまり神経質になることはない。
- **〜までもない** — "there's no need to go so far as to ~"; example: こんな簡単な用件ならわざわざ来るまでもなかった。
- **〜を余儀なくされる／〜を余儀なくさせる** — "to be forced/compelled into ~ (by circumstance beyond one's control)"; example: 会社の倒産で突如、失業を余儀なくされた。

### Reading-comprehension question taxonomy: "what does the author think" vs. "why/what does it mean" (pp. 26–27)

The 読解・文法 section's opening pages (sampled here for boundary-checking; full treatment deferred to 056b per the file split) teach a two-way split for reading questions about authorial stance: (1) questions asking "how does the author think" are answered either by finding a condensed restatement of the author's point (often near the first/last paragraph of a long passage) or, when the author never states their view directly, by inferring it from why the passage was written at all; (2) a second question type, "それはなぜか／どのような意味か" ("why is that / what does that mean"), asks the reader to unpack an indirect or figurative statement using surrounding context — illustrated with the 自閉症的な耳 (self-absorbed/non-attentive "ear") passage discussed above.

### Listening-section strategy patterns: spatial description and "flip-back" dialogues (pp. 14–24)

Beyond the vocabulary captured above, the 聴解 (listening) section teaches two reusable comprehension patterns: (1) for map/diagram questions, build a mental inventory of landmarks mentioned (a tree, a church, a star) before trying to track relative position, since position-describing phrases (〜の角をまがる, 〜の向かい, etc.) only make sense once the landmarks are anchored; (2) for "two speakers converge on a decision" dialogues, expect a characteristic shape where several options are proposed and rejected in turn before the conversation loops back to the very first option (the ひっくり返し pattern) — don't assume the last-mentioned option before the final line is necessarily the answer.
