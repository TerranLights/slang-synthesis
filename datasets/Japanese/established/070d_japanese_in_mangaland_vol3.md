## Grammar points

### Colloquial/disdainful register marked explicitly in an answer key (Lesson 48)
One of this chunk's most valuable findings: exercise 8's answer key, for the translation "Although
she is an adult, she wears those kinds of clothes," states outright that **"the connotation is
colloquial and disdainful, even pejorative."** This is a rare case of the book flagging a sentence's
*social attitude*, not just its grammar — the adversative construction itself (～のに, "even though")
is being used here specifically to carry judgmental/critical force, a pragmatic nuance this project's
register taxonomy work should treat as a real, source-confirmed register signal distinct from mere
formality level.

### それでは → それじゃ, a farewell-phrase contraction (Lesson 48)
A second answer-key-only finding: それじゃ is explicitly derived from それでは ("now / well then"), with
the book noting it has been repurposed to mean "see you later" in a farewell context — a
meaning-narrowing contraction (not just a phonological shortening) worth comparing against the
～てしまう → ～ちゃう purely-phonological contraction documented from Vol. 2.

### A three-tier formality ladder for "but" (Lesson 49)
The book makes explicit a formality cline for a single conjunctive meaning ("but, although") that
exactly parallels the register minimal-sets found in Vol. 1's index: けれども (very formal) > けれど
(formal, but less than けれども) > けど (most informal). The lesson additionally notes a pragmatic
function for the informal end of this cline: けど is used to **soften a request by trailing off**,
letting the listener infer the unstated remainder rather than stating a demand outright — a politeness
strategy via *ellipsis* rather than via lexical choice, distinct from (but complementary to) the
register-ladder finding itself.

### Productive disease-naming suffixes: 炎 (-itis) and 病 (disease) (Lesson 51)
Two distinct, productive compounding suffixes for naming illnesses: 炎 *-en* ("inflammation/-itis,"
attaching to the affected body part: 肺炎, 扁桃腺炎, 胃炎) and 病 *-byō* ("disease," attaching to a named
condition or even a loanword stem: 心臓病, アルツハイマー病). The book explicitly calls out this second
example as a "good example of medical jargon," and separately highlights the giving/receiving verb
～てもらう appearing in the doctor's own speech (入院させてもらいます) to politely frame hospitalization as
a favor the patient receives, consistent with the uchi/soto giving-verb frame documented in Vol. 2.

### Excessive degree ～すぎる (Lesson 54)
A auxiliary verb 過ぎる ("to exceed") attaches to a verb root (働く → 働きすぎる, "to work too much") or to
an adjective stem with regular -i/-na adjective rules (広い → 広すぎる; 静かな → 静かすぎる), with the one
flagged irregular case being いい ("good") → よすぎる rather than *いすぎる. Presented in the same lesson
as a full comparative/superlative paradigm table (AはBより… "A is more … than B," Aが一番… "A is the
most …," etc.), treating "too much" as adjacent to, but grammatically distinct from, the
comparative/superlative system proper.
