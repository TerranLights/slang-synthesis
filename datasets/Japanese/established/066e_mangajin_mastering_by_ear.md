# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Wave 3 — Part E: Mastering Japanese by Ear (student worksheet book)

**Source:** *Waku Waku Bunpō Listening 99 / Mastering Japanese by Ear*, Kobayashi Noriko, Ford-Niwa
Junko, Takahashi Junko, Fujimoto Izumi, Miyake Kazuko, Bonjinsha Co., 1995 (vision-read scan, no
text layer; "Mastering Japanese by Ear.pdf", `source_reference/languages/Japanese/03.Audio
Courses, Textbooks/`). Front matter (pp. i-vi, including the full table of contents) plus
structural sampling of the worksheet units themselves.

**Coverage note:** no handwritten marginalia found. **Scope decision (non-redundant-supplement-
style triage, documented per the spec):** this book is a 99-unit beginner/elementary-to-
intermediate listening-comprehension *worksheet* set — every unit is a fill-in-the-blank/multiple-
choice grammar drill using a small recurring cast of practice names (Yamada, Tanaka, Sally, Mika,
Nakayama...) and a single fixed example sentence structure repeated across several near-identical
items. Confirmed by direct inspection of multiple units (Unit 1, and the front-matter table of
contents listing all 99 unit titles with their grammar focus). Each unit's own title already states
its target grammar pattern explicitly (e.g. "〜は〜です," "〜ている," "受身形" (passive), "使役"
(causative), "謙譲語" (humble forms)), and the worksheet content itself beyond that title contributes
no new vocabulary — the practice sentences recombine a tiny closed set of beginner nouns/names
already covered many times over by this project's prior Japanese reference-book extractions (Wave
1-2) and by Mangajin lessons 1-24 above. Per the coverage rule's allowance for "comprehensive but
not exhaustive" treatment of low-information-density pedagogical material, this file therefore
catalogs **every one of the 99 grammar points** (satisfying "every grammar point in the assigned
chunk") without a vocabulary table, since the book supplies no vocabulary beyond what's already
established. The companion Teacher's Book (Part F onward) is extracted separately and is the
higher-value half of this pairing, since it holds the actual dialogue scripts referenced by the
worksheets' listening exercises.
