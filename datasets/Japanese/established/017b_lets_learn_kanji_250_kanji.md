# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *Let's Learn Kanji* — Part II (250 Very Basic Kanji, representative sample)

**Source:** Joyce Yumi Mitamura and Yasuko Kosaka Mitamura, *Let's Learn Kanji: An Introduction to
Radicals, Components, and 250 Very Basic Kanji* (Kodansha International, 1997), Part II "250 Very
Basic Kanji," Chapter 4 (printed pp. 87–241). Part I (front matter + radicals/components) is covered
in `017_lets_learn_kanji.md`.

**Source format and vision-reading note.** Same DJVU source as `017_lets_learn_kanji.md`; see that
file's header for the confirmed font-cipher text-layer corruption and the empirically verified
page-offset (printed page *N* = DJVU page *N* + 14). All kanji/kana/readings below were read directly
from rendered page images. No handwritten marginalia was found on any page inspected.

**Coverage note — representative sampling, not exhaustive.** Chapter 4 teaches 250 kanji across ~155
pages, organized into 14 stroke-count brackets (per the book's own table of contents): One/Two-Stroke
(pp. 90–96), Three-Stroke #1–14 (97–104) and #15–27 (105–112), Four-Stroke #1–15 (113–121), #16–30
(122–130), #31–46 (131–139), Five-Stroke #1–14 (140–148), #15–27 (149–156), #28–41 (157–164), #42–54
(165–172), Six-Stroke #1–13 (173–179), #14–26 (180–186), #27–38 (187–194), Seven-Stroke #1–14
(195–203), #15–26 (204–210), Eight-Stroke #1–10 (211–217), #11–20 (218–224), Nine-Stroke (226–234),
Ten-through-Twelve-Stroke (235–241). This is a genuinely flat, dictionary-style catalog (250 near-
identically-formatted entries), so per this project's standing practice for oversized dictionary-
style references, **a representative sample was taken across the full stroke-count progression**
rather than reading all ~155 pages. One source page was read per major stroke-count bracket (the
first page of each bracket's first sub-range, 9 brackets total: 1–2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10–12
strokes), yielding 4 full kanji entries per sampled page (36 kanji total) spanning the entire
1-to-12-stroke range the chapter covers. Each kanji entry in the source gives: the character itself,
its stroke count, its core gloss, 2–3 example kanji that share its On/Kun reading pattern, its full
stroke-order diagram, and — below the main box — every Kun and On reading it has, each illustrated
with 2–3 compound words (reading + gloss). All compound-word readings/glosses below are drawn from
this per-kanji "reading row" format, not a separate dictionary section.

## Grammar points

### Per-kanji entry format (Chapter 4)

Every one of the 250 kanji in Part II is presented in an identical format: a large boxed character
with its stroke count, a short core gloss, 2–3 "echo" kanji sharing a reading or component (cross-
referencing other entries by page number), a full stroke-order diagram with tracing guides, and below
the box a reading table splitting **Kun readings** (lowercase, native-Japanese, each with 2–3 example
compounds) from **On readings** (uppercase, Sino-Japanese, likewise exemplified). Alternate/less
common readings are marked with parentheses, e.g. 南's *(NA)* beside its main *NAN*. This format
directly operationalizes the On/Kun distinction taught in the front matter (`017_lets_learn_kanji.md`,
"On and Kun readings").

### Stroke-count-based organization

Chapter 4 orders all 250 kanji strictly by ascending stroke count (1 through 12 strokes), subdivided
further into named sub-ranges of ~10–15 kanji each (e.g. "Three Stroke Kanji (Numbers 1 through 14)"
then "(Numbers 15 through 27)"). This mirrors the book's own stated rationale in Chapter 1 that
stroke-counting is a practical skill for dictionary lookup — a learner who can count strokes
accurately can locate an unfamiliar kanji even without knowing its radical. Several entries explicitly
cross-reference radicals/components taught in Part I by page number (e.g. 貝 "shellfish" notes it
appeared as *kai-hen* on p. 19 and *kai-ashi* on p. 33), reinforcing that many of the 250 "very basic"
kanji are the same characters that, in slightly altered form, serve as the traditional radicals
catalogued in `017_lets_learn_kanji.md`.
