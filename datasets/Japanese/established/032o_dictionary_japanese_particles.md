## Grammar points

### to-mo (とも) and to-mo-are (ともあれ) — emphasis and topic-deprioritizing combination particles
Paraphrased from p.220–221. "To-mo" is an untranslatable emphatic marker on the preceding noun. "To-mo-are" downplays the importance of a topic ("putting aside the destination for now..."), and in the fixed "nani wa to mo are" pattern means "in any case".

### to-shite (として), to-shite-mo (としても), to-shite-wa (としては) — role/position combination particles built on "suru"
Paraphrased from pp.222–224. "To-shite" marks a role or position ("chosen as the representing athlete"), sets aside a topic for later, and — with a following negative — asserts there are no exceptions ("not even one student could solve this problem"). "To-shite-mo" extends a stated position to the speaker as well ("as for me, too...") or builds a hypothetical ("even if I were to agree..."). "To-shite-wa" simply marks "as for..." a given position.

### to-wa (とは) — quotative topic-introducing combination particle
Paraphrased from p.225 (entry continues in part 16). Introduces a term as a topic for definition/discussion ("what exactly is the thing called a black hole?"); a second, emphatic "together with" sense is introduced here and detailed further in the next file.
