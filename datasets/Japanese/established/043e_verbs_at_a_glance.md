## Grammar points

### Ni kakawarazu: "regardless of whether or not" (p. 132)

The same verb appears twice, first in the plain affirmative then the plain negative, before ni
kakawarazu — "ryokō ni iku ikanai ni kakawarazu," "whether or not you go on the trip [please listen
to the explanation regardless]."

### -Nai uchi wa: "until..." (p. 136)

Literally "while the action does not occur," better rendered "until" in English — appears in the
well-known proverb "Nikkō o minai uchi wa kekkō to wa ienai" ("you can't say 'Splendid!' until
you've seen Nikkō"), which the book glosses with cultural background: Nikkō is the location of
Tōshōgū, the 17th-century shrine and burial place of Tokugawa Ieyasu, and the proverb plays on the
rhyme between Nikkō and kekkō ("splendid").

### Ni wa oyobanai: "there's no need to" (pp. 136–137)

Follows the plain form; indicates something is unnecessary, superfluous, or not important enough to
warrant an action.

### -Ppanashi: action abandoned mid-course or left ongoing (p. 137)

Attached to the /-masu/ stem; indicates an action begun but then abandoned/forgotten partway, or
simply an ongoing unresolved state — "mado ga akeppanashi," "the window was left open."

### Shiyō ga nai: "there's no way of doing it" (p. 138)

Follows a noun and the particle no; indicates there is no means or way of doing something.

### Sae ... -ba: "if only X, then..." (p. 138)

A verb's /-masu/ stem, a noun, or a pronoun precedes the particle sae, which in turn precedes a verb
in the -ba conditional form — "iki sae sureba ii n' da," "all you have to do is go [and everything
will be fine]."

### Shika nai: emphatic "only" (p. 139)

Follows the plain form; an emphatic way of saying there is only one course of action left — "yaru
shika nai," "there's nothing to do but [go ahead and] do it."

### Ni shitagatte / ni tsurete: "as X happens, Y happens too" (pp. 139–140)

Both follow the plain form. Ni shitagatte tends to describe cause-and-effect dependent on human
will; ni tsurete tends to point to one natural phenomenon leading to another. The book notes the two
are nonetheless often interchangeable in practice.

### Ni suginai: "no more than, only" (p. 140)

Preceded by a plain form, informal past tense, or noun; means "no more than" — "ichi-pāsento ni
suginai," "it's no more than one percent."

### Tabi ni: "every time, whenever" (p. 141)

Follows the plain form — "kono machi o tazuneru tabi ni, kodomo no koro o omoidasu," "every time I
visit this town, I remember my childhood."

### -Ta ka to omou to / -ta totan: "immediately after" (pp. 141–142)

Both mean "immediately after" and follow the informal past tense, functionally overlapping with the
near-identical Chapter 2 -ta totan pattern (see `043b`).

### -Tara kaette: result opposite to what was expected (p. 142)

The -tara conditional form followed by kaette かえって; the resulting construction indicates the
action's result was the reverse of what was hoped for — "kono kusuri o nondara kaette onaka ga
itaku natta," "I took this medicine, but it gave me a stomachache instead of making me feel better."

### -Te wa irarenai: "can't continue doing X" (p. 143)

The -te form followed by wa irarenai はいられない; used when nervousness, or the demands of the
moment, make it impossible to continue a given action — "nombiri o-cha nado nonde wa irarenai," "we
can't just sit around calmly drinking tea [with guests about to arrive]."

### -Te tamaranai: unbearable condition or urge (pp. 143–144)

The -te form of verbs or adjectives followed by tamaranai たまらない; expresses that a condition or
urge is unbearable — "nodo ga kawaite tamaranai," "my throat is so dry I can hardly stand it."

### -Te hajimete: "only after X does one realize Y" (p. 144)

The -te form followed by hajimete 初めて — "jibun ga kodomo o motte, hajimete oya no arigatami ga
wakaru," "you feel truly grateful to your parents only after you have children of your own."

### -Te bakari wa irarenai: reinforced "can't afford to only do X" (p. 144)

Near-identical to -te wa irarenai (p. 143), with bakari ばかり ("only," "nothing but") added to
reinforce that the subject is doing something inappropriate under the circumstances — "asonde
bakari wa irarenai," "[with exams approaching] I can't afford to keep just fooling around."

### -Te mo ... -te mo: "no matter how much X, not enough" (p. 145)

The same verb appears twice in the -te form — "kaite mo kaite mo umaku ikanai," "no matter how much
I write [and rewrite], it just doesn't turn out right."

### O tsūjite: "by means of, through, throughout" (p. 146)

Follows a noun — "ichinen o tsūjite," "throughout the year"; also "aihara-san o tsūjite," "through
[an introduction by] Ms. Aihara."

### Yō ni: mild imperative (p. 146)

Follows the plain form (affirmative or negative); used to form mild imperative-like admonitions —
"jiko o okosanai yō ni ki o tsukete kudasai," "please be careful not to cause an accident."

### -Yō to mo -nai / -yō to ... mai to: "regardless of whether or not" (pp. 147–148)

Both consist of the informal volitional form of one verb followed by to (mo), then a (negative) form
of another verb (or the same verb repeated with -mai to). Mean "no matter what is done/said, it
doesn't change the outcome" — "dare ga nan to iō to mo, keshite kikanai," "no matter who says what
to her, she never listens"; "ikō to iku mai to, watashi wa iku tsumori da," "whether others go or
not, I intend to go."

### Ya ina ya: literary "as soon as" (p. 148)

Follows the plain form; explicitly glossed by the book as "somewhat literary" — "densha ga tomaru ya
ina ya, man'in no jōkyaku ga soto ni afuredeta," "no sooner had the train stopped than the packed
passengers spilled out."

### -Zaru o enai: literary "must, have no choice" (p. 148)

Replaces the final -nai of the informal negative with -zaru (kuru → kozaru, suru → sezaru);
explicitly glossed as "a literary expression meaning 'must'" — "shusseki sezaru o enai darō," "we
have no choice but to attend."

### -Zu ni: "without doing" (p. 149)

Replaces the final -nai of the informal negative with -zu (kuru → kozu, suru → sezu); functionally
equivalent to -nai de — "nani mo iwazu ni kyōshitsu o dete ikareta," "[the teacher] left the
classroom without saying a word."
