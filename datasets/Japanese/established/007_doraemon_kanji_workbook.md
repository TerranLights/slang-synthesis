## Grammar points

This source is a pure kanji-writing reference (character-by-character stroke order, reading, and
radical drill) rather than a running-prose grammar textbook, so it carries no narrative grammar
explanations of its own. Two book-wide structural conventions are worth recording here since they
recur across every entry above and will matter for later mechanics analysis:

### Kun-yomi vs. on-yomi marking convention

The book visually distinguishes a kanji's native Japanese reading (訓読み, kun-yomi) from its
Sino-Japanese borrowed reading (音読み, on-yomi) with a black "くん" tag vs. a red "オン" tag beside
each entry, and marks okurigana (送り仮名, the inflectional kana tail after a kanji's root, e.g. the
「む」in 休む) in red within the kun-yomi reading itself. Readings the book judges low-frequency or
advanced for this grade are shown parenthesized (e.g. 貝's オン reading, 学's くん reading まな-ぶ) —
a real, explicit register/frequency-style annotation worth treating as source-marked data (p.21, "この
本の見方" explanation page).

### 成り立ち (etymological formation) notes

For a subset of entries (confirmed in this chunk: 休, 字, 森, 耳, 林, 男, 竹, 四), the book adds a
small "なりたち" aside breaking the kanji into its pictographic/compositional origin (e.g. 休 = 人
"person" + 木 "tree," a person resting against a tree; 林 = two 木 "tree" side by side vs. 森's three,
visually encoding a woods→forest intensity gradient). This is the closest thing this source has to a
morpheme-style breakdown and is noted per-entry above rather than pulled into a separate section,
since it's sparse (not given for most entries) rather than a systematic per-kanji feature.
