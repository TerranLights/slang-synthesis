## Grammar points

### de (で) — locational/instrumental/causal particle
Paraphrased from pp.17–26, the book's largest early entry (13 numbered senses). Core static-location use contrasts with directional "ni"/"e" (*gakkoo de benkyoo-suru* "study at school" vs. *gakkoo ni iku* "go to school"). Extends through superlative domain ("fastest runner in the class"), time-of-occurrence (including age and time limits, e.g. *nijup-pun de dekimasu ka* "can you do it in 20 minutes?"), method/means (*jitensha de kayotte-imasu* "commute by bicycle"), price and rate/unit, material composition (*purasuchikku de dekita hako* "a box made of plastic"), manner/intention, reason/cause (*fuchuui de kaidan kara ochita* "fell down the stairs due to carelessness"), source/basis of information or evaluation ("according to the weather forecast"/"depending on size"), and finally the agentive "done by" sense (*kazoku de kimeta koto desu* "a decision made by the family").

### demo (でも) — "even"/concessive particle
Paraphrased from pp.26–28. From the baseline "even" sense (*sensee demo wakara-nai deshoo* "even the teacher won't understand"), through the "don-na...demo" universal-concession pattern, the after-interrogative all-inclusive sense ("wherever/whatever"), a casual-suggestion use ("coffee or something"), a wishful-thinking use ("if only I had even a little savings"), to the "manzara...demo nai" double-negative softener ("not all untrue").

### dokoro (どころ) / dokoroka (どころか) — extreme-case negation particles
Paraphrased from pp.28–29. Both particles take an extreme case and negate its plausibility to emphasize a point, "dokoroka" additionally foregrounding the contrasting clause that follows with a "never mind X, in fact Y" reading (*benkyoo-suru dokoroka, ichi-nichi-juu asonde bakari iru* "never mind studying — he plays all day").

### domo (どども) — concessive repeated-verb particle
Paraphrased from p.29–30. Placed after a (typically doubled) verb to mean "(even) though", e.g. *ike domo ike domo, suna bakari no dai-sabaku datta* ("though we went on and on, [we saw] nothing but sand").

### e (へ) — directional particle
Paraphrased from pp.30–34. Marks direction/destination/goal ("to"/"toward"), the recipient of an action ("to"/"for"), and in the fixed pattern "...tokoro e" describes the situation in place when another event interrupts (*shukudai ga owatta tokoro e tomodachi ga asobi ni kita* "I had just finished my homework when my friend came over"). Mostly interchangeable with "ni", except immediately before "no" where only "e" works.

### ga (が) — subject particle and much more
Paraphrased from pp.34–41, the book's longest entry to this point. Section I covers "ga" as subject/topic marker: existence sentences (with the animate/inanimate "iru"/"aru" distinction), desire/ability/opinion predicates, post-interrogative use, adjective-modified nouns, five-senses expressions, and intransitive-verb subjects. Section II covers non-subject functions: a surprise-toned conjunction after "sore ga"/"tokoro ga"; "ga gotoku"/"ga mama ni" ("as if"/"exactly as"); an introductory "as for..." clause; the adversative conjunction "but"; the "...u ga...mai ga" ("whether or not, it doesn't matter") pattern; counterfactual wishful thinking (compared to "noni", p.167); a softening sentence-final particle for indirect requests; a sentence-final exclamatory particle the book explicitly flags as **men's language**, used scornfully/disapprovingly (e.g. "kono usotsuki-me ga!" "You liar!") — a genuine register/gender-usage annotation worth preserving; and finally a puzzlement/surprise particle ("I thought I'd put it here, but...").

### ga-na / ga-naa (がな/がなあ) — wishful-thinking combination particle
Paraphrased from p.42. Combines "ga" + "na(a)" for wishful thinking about an unlikely-to-happen situation ("I hope..., though...").

### gatera (がてら) — "incidentally, while doing X" particle
Paraphrased from p.42 (entry continues into the next page range). Indicates a second action carried out incidentally alongside a primary one ("kill two birds with one stone"), e.g. taking the kids to school and shopping along the way.
