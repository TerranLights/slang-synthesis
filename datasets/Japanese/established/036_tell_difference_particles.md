## Grammar points (continued)

### Group IV — Particles that Indicate Direction (pp. 45–51)
Four particles, largely re-deploying Group I's time particles for spatial direction:
に／へ, から, から…まで, より. に and へ are freely interchangeable as goal markers; から
indicates a spatial starting point; より is from's more formal counterpart, used for the same
spatial-starting-point function as から but in official/formal contexts (parallel to its temporal
use in I-6).

### Group V — Particles that Indicate a Question or Uncertainty (pp. 53–59)
Five particles/combinations: か (two senses), かな, かしら, の, って. Central discriminations:
- **か (1a, sentence-final) vs. か (1b, mid-sentence)**: the sentence-final use has a politeness
  register appropriate with strangers/superiors; mid-sentence か simply marks embedded
  question/doubt within a larger sentence (p. 54).
- **かな vs. かしら**: functionally identical tentative-question markers ("I wonder"), split by
  speaker gender — かな is typically masculine, かしら typically feminine (p. 54-55).
- **の (rising intonation) vs. か (1a)**: の is a softer, more casual functional equivalent of か for
  forming a question (p. 55).
- **って**: casual, spoken-register "is it true that...?", identical in core meaning to だと (XIV-2)
  but softer/more typically used by women; rising intonation = question, falling intonation = a
  flat statement meaning "I hear that..." (p. 56).

### Group VI — Particles that Indicate a Reason or Cause (pp. 61–65)
Five particles: て-form, で, から, ので, もので. Register/directness cline from most direct to
most polite: から (most direct, can sound blunt/risk offense) → ので (softer, often preferred in
polite conversation) → もので (most polite variant of ので). て-form can mark reason or "and"
(III-9), disambiguated only by context — the author explicitly flags this as a point requiring
attentive reading, not a formal grammatical marker distinguishing the two uses (p. 61). で marks a
noun-phrase reason directly.

### Group VII — Particles that Indicate a Condition or Supposition (pp. 71–77)
Six particles: ば, たら, なら, ところで, ても/でも, と. **The author's own opening caveat for this
group**: these particles overlap extensively and a full account of every overlap is beyond the
book's scope — she undertakes only to describe each particle's *core* usage (p. 71), an explicit
methodological disclosure worth preserving. Key discriminations:
- **ば vs. たら**: both general conditionals ("if"), but ば cannot (with exceptions) have a second
  clause expressing intention/desire/command/request, while たら has no such restriction and is
  more typical of spoken Japanese (p. 72).
- **なら**: like ば/たら but places emphasis on the preceding word, often implying the speaker
  suspects the condition is already true (p. 72).
- **ところで vs. てもでも**: both present a condition whose fulfillment won't yield favorable
  results, both followed by a negative verb, but ところで is more emphatic and takes a plain-past-
  tense verb before it (p. 73).
- **と**: like ば/なら/たら but can indicate an invariable "when...then" relationship and, like ば,
  cannot express intention/command/question/request/desire (p. 74).

### Group VIII — Particles that Indicate a Limitation or Maximum (pp. 79–85)
Seven particles/combinations: しか, だけ, だけしか, のみ, きり, きりしか, のみしか. **Author's own
cross-reference**: she explicitly flags that this group's particles closely parallel Group XV's
emphasis particles and that the sample sentences in each group should be compared for fuller
understanding (p. 79) — a direct authorial signal of a structural parallel across the book's own
organization. Organizing axes: affirmative-verb-taking (だけ, のみ) vs. negative-verb-taking (しか
and all its combinations); and a formality cline from colloquial (きり) through neutral (だけ/しか)
to formal/written (のみ). The "しか/だけしか/きりしか/のみしか" cluster intensifies emphasis roughly
in that order, with のみしか being the most formal of the emphatic combinations.

### Group IX — Particles Indicating, or Providing Information about, the Subject of a Clause or Sentence (pp. 89–97)
Six particles: は (three senses), が, で, も, として, には. **Author's own framing note**: the core
distinction between は and が is topic-marker vs. subject-marker (p. 89). Key discriminations:
- **は (1a/1b) vs. が (2)**: は marks a topic or a subject already known to speaker and listener; が
  introduces a subject the listener is hearing of for the first time (p. 90-91).
- **は (1c), contrast**: one of は's most frequent functions is creating a contrast between two
  items, replacing を in the process (p. 90).
- **が after interrogatives**: a flagged exception/rule — が, never は, follows interrogatives like
  誰/何/どれ/どこ/どんな, both in the question and in a straightforward answer (p. 91).
- **が with idiomatic する-expressions**: nouns like 音/匂い/気持ち combined with する ordinarily take
  が unless は is needed for contrast (p. 92).
- **で (manner)**: marks how a subject performs an action (e.g., 一人で, "by oneself") (p. 93).
- **も (IX-4) cross-references III-2–3**, viewing the same "also/too" subject-marking function from
  a different angle.
- **として vs. には**: として marks the role/status in which a subject functions ("as"); には marks
  not the grammatical subject but the party to whom a stated judgment applies ("for") (p. 94-95).

### Group X — Particles that Indicate Objects of Desire or Wishes (pp. 101–105)
Three particles: を, が, に/へ. を is the default object marker for たい/たがる (desire) forms; が is
required (not を) with 欲しい and is interchangeable with を for extra emphasis with たい — both
欲しい and たい can only express the *speaker's own* wishes, not other people's (たがる is used for
third-party wishes instead) (p. 102). に/へ marks the destination of desired movement, identical in
function to IV-1.

## Grammar points

### Group I — Particles Indicating Time (pp. 11–22)
Ten particles/particle-combinations covering points, intervals, starting points, deadlines, and
approximations of time: に, から, まで, までに, から…まで, より, ほど, くらい/ぐらい, ころ/ごろ,
ばかり. Key discriminations the author draws explicitly:
- **に vs. から**: both can mark a starting point, but から places more emphasis on the point of
  departure itself (p. 12).
- **まで vs. までに**: まで states a fact of continuation to an end point ("until"); までに sets a
  limit/deadline ("by") — the author recommends keeping the two straight via their distinct English
  equivalents (p. 13).
- **から vs. より**: near-synonyms for "from," but より carries a more formal/official register
  (p. 14).
- **ほど vs. くらい/ぐらい**: both mean "approximately," but ほど implies the figure given is a
  *maximum* and has a formal ring, while くらい/ぐらい implies a *minimum* and sounds more casual
  (p. 14).
- **ころ/ごろ vs. に vs. くらい/ぐらい**: ころ/ごろ approximates a *point* in time; に is exact; くらい/
  ぐらい approximates an *amount* of time (p. 15).
- A noted exception class: certain time words (seasons: 春/夏/秋/冬; some day/week/month terms)
  either optionally or obligatorily drop に (p. 13).

### Group II — Particles Indicating the Place Where an Action Takes Place or Where Something Is (pp. 23–29)
Four particles: で, に (three subtypes 2a–2c), の, へ/に.
- **で vs. に (2a)**: で marks the location of a *dynamic* action; に (with ある/いる) marks the
  location of something *static*/existing (p. 24).
- **に (2b)**: marks a goal with inward-movement verbs; へ can substitute, but に is more common in
  practice.
- **に (2c) vs. で**: a closed set of verbs (座る, 置く, 住む, 勤める, 積もる) take に for location even
  though the action looks dynamic — で is the default for most other verbs (p. 26).
- **Explicit author note**: when the grammatical subject is an event/happening (a parade, a
  fireworks display) rather than a person/thing, で is used for location even with ある/いる, which
  normally pattern with に (p. 25) — a genuinely non-obvious exception worth flagging.
- **の (place)**: combines with locational nouns (上/下/横/中/そば/まえ) to build compound location
  phrases (p. 26).
- **へ/に (direction)**: freely interchangeable with 行く/来る/戻る/帰る (p. 27).

### Group III — Particles Showing Connections between Words (pp. 31–39)
Eleven particles/combinations: と, も, も…も, や, や…や…など, に, とか, やら, て, たり…たり (two
senses), ては. Central organizing distinction across this whole group: **closed/complete lists**
(と, も…も, に, て, たり…たり-10a) vs. **open/partial lists that imply more could be added** (や,
や…や…など, とか, やら). Within the partial-list cluster, register and connotation differentiate
further:
- と is the default neutral "and" for a closed list of nouns; に is a more formal variant with the
  same closed-list semantics (p. 31, p. 34).
- や vs. や…や…など: identical in partial-list function; など makes the partiality explicit (p. 33).
- とか vs. やら: both present partial lists of nouns/adjectives/verbs and often pattern with する, but
  やら specifically connotes a confused/disorganized state of affairs that とか does not (p. 35).
- とか vs. だの (cross-referenced forward to XI-3): だの similarly connects a partial list casually,
  but the items listed under だの often carry a negative nuance (p. 34) — flagged here as an explicit
  forward cross-reference the author herself draws.
- て-form vs. たり…たり vs. ては: all three connect multiple verbs/adjectives, but with different
  completeness/repetition semantics — て/たり…たり(10a) present full or partial lists of actions with
  the *same* grammatical role; たり…たり(10b) specifically marks *repeated alternating* (often
  opposite) actions/states; ては marks two actions whose repetition is more tightly bound in time or
  causality than たり…たり (pp. 36–38).
