## Grammar points

### keredomo (けれども) and its colloquial shortenings — adversative/softening conjunction
Paraphrased from pp.69–73. As a mid-sentence conjunctive particle it is the default adversative ("even though this dish is delicious, it's a hassle to make"), softens an apologetic remark ("though there's only a little here, please have some"), or simply links two loosely related statements. Sentence-finally it expresses wishful reversal ("if I understood Japanese better, but..."), or — in the "doose...keredomo" pattern — a cynical, hopeless tone that isn't directly translatable, or an indirect lead-in ("excuse me, but..."). A separate, unrelated conjunction use (placed after a complete sentence) introduces a conflicting follow-up ("however") or agrees first before offering an alternative ("however/yet").

### kiri (きり) — limiting/unchanging-state particle
Paraphrased from pp.73–75. Marks a hard quantity limit ("just the two of us"), an unchanged ongoing state ("confined to lying down continuously"), or — followed later by a negative — "after X, nothing has happened since" ("he called once, and I haven't heard from him since"). The reduplicated "marukkiri" + negative gives "(not) at all".

### koso (こそ) — emphatic particle
Paraphrased from pp.75–77. Emphasizes the preceding word/phrase ("indeed"), gives emphatic reasons in "kara koso"/"ba koso" ("precisely because..."), concedes a point before adding more in "koso sure" ("does do X, but..."), and intensifies "sore koso" ("that would be disastrous").

### koto (こと) — women's-language sentence-final particle (with one exception)
Paraphrased from pp.77–79. The source explicitly flags this whole entry as women's language except sense No. 6. Senses 1–5 soften an opinion, express mild surprise ("what a wonderful painting!"), accompany a casual question, form an exclamatory rhetorical question ("isn't that wonderful!"), or soften a suggestion after a negative verb ("how about going to a movie?"). Sense 6 breaks the women's-language pattern entirely, instead transforming a statement into a strict, gender-unmarked command ("make sure you pay back borrowed money").

### kurai / gurai (くらい/ぐらい) — approximation/comparison particle
Paraphrased from pp.79–81. Covers rough quantity approximation, a belittling "something like" sense (deprecating the difficulty of a task), extent ("to the extent that the whole sky turned crimson"), an untranslatable comparison-basis use ("there's nothing scarier than an earthquake"), and a "rather than doing X" dispreference sense.

### kuse-ni (くせに) — critical/resentful combination particle
Paraphrased from pp.81–83. Combines "kuse" (habit) with "ni" to flag that the actual situation contradicts an expected norm, carrying built-in criticism ("even though he's in college, he still has his mother do his laundry"). Sentence-finally, expresses a slighter, less translatable resentment ("you got an upset stomach the other day [from overeating], remember?").
