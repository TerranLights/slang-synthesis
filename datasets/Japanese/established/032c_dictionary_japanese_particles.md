## Grammar points

### hodo (ほど) — approximation/extent/comparison particle
Paraphrased from pp.43–44. Covers approximation after a quantity (interchangeable with "kurai/gurai"), degree/extent evaluation ("to the extent that just seeing him makes you feel bad for him"), negated standard of comparison ("there's nothing I look forward to more than summer vacation"), and the proportional "the more...the more" construction shared with "ba" (*furukereba furui hodo kachi ga aru* "the older it is, the more value it has").

### hoka (ほか) — exclusive-negation particle
Paraphrased from p.45. Followed by a negative, excludes every possibility but the one named ("nothing except for..."), functionally close to "shika" (p.182).

### i (い) — men's-language emphatic/interrogative particle
Paraphrased from pp.45–46. The source explicitly marks this as used mostly in men's speech and not translatable; functions as a sentence-final emphatic ("That toy is mine!") or to reinforce a question ("Why don't we play mahjong tonight?").

### ka (か) — the core interrogative/uncertainty particle
Paraphrased from pp.46–51, one of the dictionary's largest entries. The first cluster of senses covers embedded uncertainty ("whether"), doubtful conjecture ("perhaps because..."), disjunctive listing ("...or..."), and approximate-number pairing ("two or three times"). The much larger sentence-final cluster covers plain interrogatives and rhetorical questions, confirmation-seeking, rebuttal/objection, a scolding tone, the emphatic "wasn't it so that..." rhetorical-negative-question pattern, casual suggestions ("why don't we"), the softened "...tara doo ka" command form, and finally a reflective self-address use with an emotional/reminiscent overtone.

### ka-i (かーい) and ka-na/ka-naa (かな/かなあ) — combination particles built on "ka"
Paraphrased from pp.51–53. "Ka-i" marks an interrogative or, emphatically, opposition to a prior statement. "Ka-na(a)" softens doubt into "I wonder...", serves as a self-directed suggestion ("I think I'm going to make coffee..."), and in the "...nai kana" pattern expresses wishful thinking ("I wish Dad would come home soon").

### kara (から) — "from"/"because" particle, the book's largest entry
Paraphrased from pp.53–65. As a post-nominal particle, "kara" marks a human or spatial source ("from"), a starting time, a "from...to" range, iterative succession ("one after another" — fireworks shot up "one after another"), material composition (interchangeable with "de"), and approximate quantity ("as many as a thousand visitors"). As a post-predicate particle, it is the default causal connective ("because/since"), extended to a viewpoint-marking sense ("judging from...") and an emotion-sourced-cause sense ("out of a sense of responsibility, he resigned"). Three further fixed patterns are covered: "...kara to itte" ("even though", a warning against over-generalizing from a cause), "...kara ni wa" ("now that...", expressing resolve), and "...kara ni" ("just by doing...").

### kara-shite (からして) — emphatic combination particle
Paraphrased from pp.65–66. Cites one representative characteristic to make an emphatic claim about the whole ("starting with his face, he is a little strange"), or serves as a more emphatic causal connective equivalent to "node".

### kashira (かしら) — women's-language "I wonder" particle
Paraphrased from pp.66–68. The source explicitly marks this as found in women's language. Functions include posing a question after an interrogative pronoun, softening doubt or an interrogative sentence (both not translatable), making a casual self/other-directed suggestion, and — after a negation — either wishful softened requests ("could you come right away?") or worried uncertainty ("I wonder if that suitcase isn't too heavy").

### ke (け) — colloquial reminiscence/confirmation particle
Paraphrased from p.69 (entry continues beyond this file's range). Explicitly marked colloquial; typically follows the copula "da" in forms like "dakke"/"dattakke". Used to reminisce about a past event ("I remember...") or to actively solicit confirmation from the listener ("when was it that...?").
