# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Bunka Nihongo Chuukyuu 1 (Intermediate Japanese Course, Vol. 1)

**Source:** Bunka Institute of Language (文化外国語専門学校), *Bunka Nihongo Chuukyuu I* (文化中級日本語I),
intermediate Japanese course textbook, 235 pages, 8 lessons (第1課〜第8課). No text layer —
vision-read from scan (Adobe Acrobat image-conversion PDF, 2009).

**Coverage note.** This is Wave 3 of Japanese reference extraction, covering a combined 589-page,
4-file dispatch (this book + its companion *Chuukyuu II* textbook, index, and tape-script booklet).
Given the combined scale, this file uses **representative sampling across the lesson progression**
rather than exhaustive page-by-page extraction: the table-of-contents grammar-pattern (文型) list
was read in full for all 8 lessons (giving genuine coverage of every named grammar point with its
original example sentence), and several lesson-opening/mid-lesson content pages were vision-read
directly per lesson to pull vocabulary in context and verify the grammar-point readings. Repeated
drill pages and blank worksheet pages were skipped per the coverage rule.

**Vision-reading guard.** One sampled page (L1, p.18, proverb-explanation exercise) carries
handwritten marginalia in a non-Japanese hand (looks like a personal name/word jotted in pencil near
the exercise, reading roughly "aygo tancupe") — clearly not print, excluded entirely from
extraction. No other marginalia was observed in the sampled pages.

## Grammar points

*(Drawn from the lesson table of contents' numbered 文型 (sentence-pattern) list, which gives every
grammar point's name/function and an original example sentence per lesson, cross-checked against
sampled in-lesson grammar-point boxes. Paraphrased per the copyright-discipline rule — example
sentences below are the book's own short illustrative lines, not bulk reproduction.)*

### Lesson 1 — どんな勉強をしていますか (study methods)
- **〜たら, 〜ようにしている** (conditional + habitual effort): e.g. "if there's a word I don't
  understand, I try to have them speak slowly" — describing habitual learning strategies.
  p.9–10.
- **〜そうになる** (on the verge of / about to happen): marks an event about to occur but averted,
  e.g. "almost got pushed down the stairs," "almost rode past my stop." p.12.
- **〜か (を)〜ようにしている** (embedding a question as a habitual-consideration object): e.g.
  "always thinking about how good I've gotten." p.12.
- **〜だけで(は)なく** (not just X, but also): used for "don't just translate word for word — think
  about the whole meaning." p.14.
- Conditional/hypothetical-regret register: "if I hadn't written that letter, what would have
  happened?" — combines もし + たら + would-have-been phrasing. p.14 (TOC item 6).

### Lesson 2 — 今、よろしいですか (polite requests, keigo)
- **Honorific/humble-form review**: distinguishing correct vs. commonly-confused keigo forms
  (「お電話してもよろしいでしょうか」vs. a marked-wrong alternative). p.26.
- **〜ていただけないでしょうか / 〜ていただけませんか** (humble request forms): two near-synonymous
  polite-request endings presented side by side for register comparison. p.29.
- **〜ばいいでしょうか** (asking what one should say/do): "何と言えばいいでしょうか." p.36.
- Register-mismatch point: speaking *too* formally with a close acquaintance reads as 他人行儀
  (distancing) rather than polite — an explicit register-calibration lesson, not just a grammar
  pattern. p.36.

### Lesson 3 — 消えたダイヤ (a stolen diamond — narrative/testimony register)
- **〜ている (resultant-state use)**: describing an ongoing visible state from a past event, e.g.
  "the glass is [in a state of having been] broken" (ガラスが割れています). p.66.
- **擬声語 embedded in testimony**: onomatopoeia used inside an eyewitness account ("ガチャンガチャン
  という音が聞こえました"), explicitly glossed by the book as 擬声語. p.71.
- **〜ような気がする** (it feels like/I have the sense that): hedged inference, "someone seems to
  have jumped out." p.71.
- **〜(よ)うとした** (was about to, attempted but — ): "tried to turn on the flashlight [but...]."
  p.71.
- **〜はずです (negative)**: expectation contradicted by evidence, "the automatic door shouldn't
  have opened." p.71.

### Lesson 4 — 敏子さんの転職 (career change — intention/volition register)
- **〜(よ)うかと思っている**: tentative stated intention, "I've been thinking of quitting." p.86.
- **なかなか〜(さ)せてもらえない**: a causative-passive expressing frustrated permission, "I'm not
  even allowed to do the important work." p.86.
- **つもりだ contrasted with 〜ようにも〜(ら)れない**: stating an intention that then proves
  practically impossible to carry out — "tried to quit but couldn't." p.86.
- **Formal letter frame (拝啓...敬具)**: the lesson's writing-skill component models a full formal
  thank-you/report letter to an elder relative, using おかげさまで and polite narrative past tense
  throughout. p.99.

### Lesson 5 — それは世界中で使われている (passive voice)
- **Passive voice for describing inventions/products**: "the Walkman was developed by Sony in
  1979" — agentive passive with によって. p.116.
- **〜につれて**: as X increases/progresses, Y also changes — "as the numbers increased, it stopped
  being talked about." p.116.
- **〜ながら(も)**: concessive "even while," e.g. "even while doubting it, they kept working." p.123.
- **〜のだ/のである (explanatory register)**: formal-written sentence-final marker for stating an
  underlying fact/reason, contrasted with plain だ. p.123.

### Lesson 6 — 昔と今 (describing present vs. past states; noun-modification structure)
- **Extended noun-modifying clauses (連体修飾)**: long relative-clause-style modification before a
  noun, the lesson's named grammar focus (●名詞修飾の構造 in the unit goal line). p.137.
- Descriptive-state sentences locating a place/institution, e.g. "Shinjuku sits almost in the
  center of Tokyo's 23 wards." p.138.
- Past-habitual narrative register for historical description: "horse-drawn carts used to run
  along the Kōshū highway." p.142.

### Lesson 7 — 調べて報告しよう (research and reporting register)
- **〜というのは〜ことですか** (clarification-seeking paraphrase question): "does 'workshop' mean a
  session where you just listen to an explanation?" p.160.
- **〜らしいです** (hearsay/reportative): "I hear the Edo-Tokyo Museum is very popular." p.160.
- **〜ということです** (reported-speech formal register): used for relaying researched facts in a
  report, "a fireworks display will also be held, I'm told." p.165.
- Formal phone-inquiry register is sampled in the book's listening-script appendix (p.233): fixed
  question/answer frames for hours, closing days, admission fees, group discounts — a distinct
  polite-service register from the casual dialogues elsewhere in the book.

### Lesson 8 — 日本はどんな国ですか (hedged opinion/request register)
- **〜ような気がします** (softened opinion): "it feels like Japanese university students play too
  much" — hedging a potentially face-threatening opinion. p.178.
- **かもしれませんね** (tag-softened agreement): "that might well be true." p.178.
- **〜んじゃないでしょうか** (hedged suggestion): "I wonder if there isn't more than one meaning to
  it." p.178.
- **〜ていただきたいと思います** (polite request-for-cooperation, public-speech register): "I'd like
  to ask everyone to participate in the event." p.178.
- **〜だろう / 〜のだろうか (written-opinion register, 投書)**: a reader's letter-to-the-editor
  register using repeated だろう hedging to voice a critical opinion indirectly — "language ability
  will probably decline," "what should we do, I wonder." p.186.
