## Grammar points

### Lesson 19 (迷子 — "Lost Child"), pp. 14–22
Goals: describing someone's clothing; explaining/apologizing for one's own carelessness; giving and understanding directions.
1. **服装のことば** — vocabulary set pairing clothing nouns with their verbs (帽子をかぶる, スーツを着る, etc.), pre-taught before 文型6 uses the 〜ている form of these pairs.
2. **〜てみる** ("try doing") — p.19: covers both literal "try it on" (〜てもいいですか) and a broader "try [doing]" sense with verbs like 聞く/捜す (ask/search).
3. **〜てしまう** (regret/unintended-event sense) — see Vocabulary table; distinguished from the "completion" sense taught at L35.
4. **〜間／間に** — the book's clearest use/non-use minimal pair: continuous vs. punctual predicates; subject-marking shift (が) when subjects differ across clauses — flagged as a common learner error point (students mis-mark the subordinate-clause subject with は).
5. **〜はずです** — confident inference from evidence, but the judged fact remains formally unconfirmed; textbook restricts the preceding form to plain non-past only (pedagogical simplification — past/〜ている/adjective/noun forms exist but aren't introduced here).
6. **〜ている (clothing-state)** — teaching note on not double-stacking 〜ていて when listing a person's full outfit.
7. **知っている／知りません** — irregular negative (知りません, not ×知っていません).
8. **〜と〜 (route directions)** — idiomatic direction-giving chain; the "must convert the first clause to 〜て before と" error point is explicitly flagged with a model incorrect sentence.

### Lesson 20 (もう進路を決めましたか — "Have You Decided on a Path Yet?"), pp. 23–30
Goals: describing future plans with volitional expressions; discussing post-graduation plans; basic knowledge of exam procedures for international students.
1. **意志形+と思っている** — standardized over 意志形+と思う; strong pragmatic warning against asking others' intentions with this pattern outside a clear superior→subordinate relationship.
2. **〜つもり** — same pragmatic register warning as above; negative 〜ないつもり; textbook omits the "as if/pretend" extended sense of つもり.
3. **〜かどうか** — uncertainty for yes/no questions, deliberately taught in contrast with 文型4.
4. **疑問詞+か** — uncertainty for wh-questions; same contrastive teaching strategy.
5. **〜なくてはいけない** — obligation; flagged pragmatic risk of sounding interrogative/accusatory in question form, and of signaling dissatisfaction if overused. A footnote contrasts this with the near-synonym 〜なければならない, framed as leaning toward describing rules/regulations rather than personal obligation — though the book treats them as equivalent for its own purposes.
Additional nuance (in dialogue annotations, not a numbered 文型): つもり/意志形 (individual intention) vs. 予定 (a plan already settled, beyond personal will alone) — directly contrasted via 父が来る予定です.

### Lesson 21 (訪問 — "Visiting"), pp. 31–41
Goals: Japanese home-visiting etiquette; set expressions used when visiting.
Etiquette content (非文型, cultural-knowledge dialogue, not grammar per se, but explicit and extractable): call ahead to arrange a time; never arrive earlier than the agreed time; ring the bell/chime, never let yourself in; take your shoes off and square them after stepping up, not before; hand over flowers/fresh food at the genkan but other gifts only once inside; it's fine to leave disliked food if offered; always thank the host both on leaving and again at the next meeting (先日はどうもありがとうございました).
1. **〜ないで〜** (attendant circumstance) — "accompanying circumstance" sense only; the contrastive sense (doing Y instead of X) is out of scope for this book.
2. **〜たほうがいいです** — advice construction; strong register warning (implicit "I know better than you," rude toward superiors unless solicited); negative 〜ないほうがいい; near-synonym 渡すほうがいい reads as comparison, not advice, so the book avoids it.
3. **〜なくてもいいです** — "not obligatory," contrasted directly with L20's 〜なくてはいけない; same "don't phrase this as a question" pragmatic caution as other obligation/permission patterns in this book.
4. **お〜ください** — more formal register of 〜てください (L9); explicit double-honorific-avoidance example with 召し上がってください vs. ×お召し上がりください.
5. **名詞修飾 (noun-modifying/relative clauses)** — が for clause-internal subjects (not は); pedagogical note explaining the book's deliberate avoidance of a word-order-transformation teaching method in favor of an "adding descriptive information" framing, because transformation drills confuse learners whose L1 has different base word order.
