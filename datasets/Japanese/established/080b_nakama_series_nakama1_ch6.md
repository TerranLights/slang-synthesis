## Grammar points

### I. Using the particles と and に

**と** has two related but distinct senses: (A.1) association, "with/together with" (私はチョイさ
んとべんきょうしました "I studied with Ms. Choi"), and (A.2) exhaustive listing of nouns only, "and"
(そうじとせんたくをしました "I did cleaning and laundry"). と can never connect two sentences or
verb/adjective phrases — only nouns; the て-form or そして must be used for sentence/phrase
connection instead (an explicit source-marked constraint). **に** here marks purpose (verb stem +
に いきます／きます／かえります, "go/come/return in order to [verb]") and goal/receiver. (p. 222–223)

### II. Commenting about the past, using polite past adjectives and the copula です

Past-tense formation differs by adjective type. **い-adjectives**: replace い with かったです
(affirmative) or くありませんでした／くなかったです (negative); いい is irregular → よかったです／
よくありませんでした. **な-adjectives and です**: です → でした (affirmative past),
じゃありません → じゃありませんでした／じゃなかったです (negative past). Illustrative exchange:
上田：パーティはどうでしたか。／リー：とてもにぎやかでした。 ("How was the party?" / "It was very
lively.") — this "どうでしたか + evaluative past adjective" pattern recurs as the textbook's default
small-talk register. (p. 227–229)

### III. Connecting verb/adjective phrases with the て-form; making requests using the て-form

The て-form is formed by replacing ます with て for る-verbs/irregular verbs (たべます→たべて), and
via five sound-conditioned patterns for う-verbs (はなします→はなして, ききます→きいて,
およぎます→およいで, はいります→はいって, よみます／よびます／しにます→よんで／よんで／しんで);
いきます→いって is an explicit lexical exception.

**Explicit three-tier politeness scale for requests**, directly from the source and high-value for
this project's register/slang-mechanics work — the *same* underlying request semantics realized at
three distinct social-distance levels:

| Form | Register | Addressee |
|---|---|---|
| 〜て下さい | most forceful/direct (a command form) | social inferior (e.g., teacher → student) — source states it would be rude to use upward |
| 〜て下さいませんか | more polite, indirect (negative-question softening) | social superior |
| 〜てくれませんか | less formal than 下さいませんか | peer / equal social status or similar age |

This is an explicit, source-marked social-register gradient built into ordinary grammar (not slang),
directly relevant to later mechanics-analysis work on how Japanese politeness levels constrain or
seed informal/slang register shifts. (p. 230–233)

### IV. Connecting phrases with the て-form ("and")

The て-form also chains phrases without itself encoding tense — the sentence-final verb carries the
tense for the whole chain (テレビをみて、ねます "watch TV and [will] go to bed" vs. 〜みて、ねました
"watched TV and went to bed"). The adjective て-form ("and," connecting predicates) replaces い(です)
with くて (い-adjectives) or です with で (な-adjectives/copula); いい → よくて (irregular). Chained
て-form phrases can imply either simple chronology or an implicit cause-effect relationship,
left ambiguous by the grammar itself — e.g. 田中さんがいて、にぎやかでした ("Mr. Tanaka was
there, and/so it was lively") reads as causal despite no explicit causal marker. (p. 234–236)

### V. Extending an invitation using ませんか

The negative-question form 〜ませんか (lit. "won't you...?") is the standard way to extend an
invitation ("why don't we..."), often paired with いっしょに ("together"): いっしょにジョギングを
しませんか. Explicit cultural/pragmatic notes from the source, directly relevant to politeness-
register mechanics:

- Speakers typically pre-check the listener's availability/interest before actually inviting
  (ジョンソン：今度の休みはいそがしいですか。→いいえ、いそがしくありません。→じゃあ、およぎに
  いきませんか。) — explicitly framed as avoiding a situation where the listener would have to give
  an outright rejection.
- Japanese speakers are described as avoiding explicit "no" to an invitation; the idiomatic way to
  decline is to apologize and give (or deliberately withhold) a reason, using one of two fixed
  softening phrases: すみません、ちょっとつごうがわるくて or すみません、ちょっとようじがあって
  (both captured above as vocabulary entries, tagged colloquial/fixed-expression).
- Fixed acceptance phrases: いいですね。／ええ、ぜひ。／ええ、いいですよ。

(p. 237–238)
