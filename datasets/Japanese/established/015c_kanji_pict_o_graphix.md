## Grammar points

### Multi-entry "sentence chains" across adjacent numbers

Several spreads in this sample show the author building one continuous mnemonic sentence across
2–3 consecutive entries rather than giving each kanji its own independent sentence — e.g. #601 左
"left" / #602 右 "right" ("Work with your left hand... eat with your right"), #692→693 (shared
altar-themed opener), #1191→1192 (街 + 行, "Which road... goes into town?"), and most elaborately
#1220→1221→1222 (遭/曹/槽, a three-entry joke sentence ending "...for our Hot Tub Encounter Group").
This is a structural mnemonic device, not a grammar rule of Japanese itself, but is worth flagging
because it means a single row's "Notes" field is sometimes only fully interpretable alongside its
immediate neighbor's row — both are retained in this file for that reason.

### Same gloss, two unrelated characters

This sample surfaces at least one case (暖 entry #605 vs. 温 entry #7, both glossed "warm") where
the book covers two graphically and etymologically distinct kanji under the same English keyword,
in different chapters, without cross-referencing each other. Confirms this is a real feature of the
dictionary's organization (by mnemonic/thematic chapter, not by consolidated semantic field) rather
than a sampling artifact — worth checking for again if any later extraction pass samples further
into this book.
