# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Bunka Chuukyuu Nihongo II Kyoukashoyouteepu Genkoutsuki (tape-script booklet)

**Source:** Bunka Institute of Language (文化外国語専門学校), *Bunka Chuukyuu Nihongo II
Kyoukashoyouteepu Genkoutsuki* (教科書用テープ原稿付き, "textbook cassette-tape script booklet"),
companion to `068b_...md`'s textbook, 34 pages. No text layer — vision-read from scan. Per its own
front-matter note (p.2), this booklet collects the full scripts for every audio-only
listening/speaking component (本文 readings, 聞く練習, 聴解, 活動 model conversations) — i.e. it
contains real spoken-register dialogue and news-broadcast-style narration that does not appear as
printed text anywhere else in the textbook.

**Coverage note.** Given the combined-dispatch scale, this file samples across the booklet's lesson
range (confirmed covering at least Lessons 1, 4, and 5 directly vision-read) rather than reading all
34 pages end to end; the booklet's content is organized by lesson in the same 8-lesson order as the
rest of this book's materials. The sampled pages span three distinct registers worth flagging: (1)
formal news-broadcast narration (L1), (2) casual peer-to-peer spoken dialogue (L4), and (3) a
semi-formal spoken lecture/monologue register (L5) — a register range not otherwise directly
represented in the textbook's printed 本文 pages, which lean toward written-prose register. This is
exactly the kind of register-contrast content the extraction spec flags as high-value.

**Vision-reading guard.** No handwritten marginalia observed on any of the sampled pages.

## Grammar points

### Formal news-broadcast register (NHK-style narration)
The booklet's L1 聴解 (listening) script opens with a fixed news-bulletin frame: "次のニュースです。
総務庁がまとめた推計によりますと…" — a formulaic lead-in ("here is the next news item, according to
a tally compiled by the Management and Coordination Agency…") that recurs with minor substitution
across each subsequent item in the same script (p.2–3). This formulaic opening-frame pattern — fixed
scaffolding language wrapped around variable content — is itself worth noting as a register feature:
formal spoken Japanese news narration relies on a small set of interchangeable frame phrases rather
than free composition.

### Disfluency transcription convention
Per the booklet's own stated convention (front matter, p.2: "言いよどみの部分はカタカナで表記した"),
hesitation sounds in spoken-register scripts (lecture monologues, unscripted-feeling dialogue) are
deliberately spelled out in katakana rather than smoothed into normal prose — e.g. エーエー, ウーン.
This is a source-level methodological note worth preserving: it means this booklet is one of the few
sources in this project's Japanese corpus that explicitly, intentionally represents spoken
disfluency rather than cleaning it up, useful later for any mechanics analysis of how hesitation/
filler markers behave in formal vs. casual registers.

### Casual-register particle/construction contrast with formal textbook prose
The L4 dialogue sample (p.17, electronics-shop scene and a casual peer conversation about work/leisure
balance) shows several constructions and interjections (へぇ, そうかもしれないけど, 〜ってわけにはい
かない used in casual rather than formal phrasing) that confirm grammar points taught formally
elsewhere in the Chuukyuu II syllabus (see `068b_bunka_nihongo_chuukyuu.md`) also occur naturally in
unscripted-feeling casual speech — a useful cross-check that the textbook's "formal" grammar points
are not artificially restricted to written register.
