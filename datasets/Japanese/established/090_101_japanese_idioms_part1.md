# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: 101 Japanese Idioms (Idioms 1-50 of 101)

**Source:** Michael L. Maynard & Senko K. Maynard, *101 Japanese Idioms: Understanding Japanese
Language and Culture Through Popular Phrases* (Passport Books/NTC Publishing Group, 1993; 1994
printing), pp. 1-108 (idioms 1-50 of 101; Section One "Relating to Nature," Section Two "Creatures
Large and Small," and the first half of Section Three "The Human Body").

**Coverage note:** every one of idioms 1-50 (each book entry = one romaji headword + kanji/kana
orthography + literal translation + idiomatic meaning + a prose explanation + a two-register
("Style:") sample dialogue given in romaji, Japanese script, and English). This half of the book
covers Section One in full (24 idioms: "Things That Grow," 15 idioms about plants/growth; "Water,
Wind, and Clouds," 9 idioms), Section Two in full (22 idioms: "Birds and Beasts," 12 idioms; "Fish,
Frogs, and Others," 10 idioms), and the opening 4 idioms of Section Three ("The Human Body").
Idioms 51-101 continue in `090b_101_japanese_idioms_part2.md`. Sample dialogues are paraphrased/
summarized rather than bulk-quoted per copyright discipline — the book's own two-line sample
exchanges are its main illustrative content and are described rather than transcribed in full.

**This source has no usable text layer** (`pdftotext` returns almost no characters despite
`pdfinfo` reporting a real text layer — confirmed before extraction) and was vision-read from a
two-printed-pages-per-scanned-image spread. All content below is print, not marginalia; no
handwritten annotation was found on any page read. **Vision Reading Confidence: verified** for
every entry — each idiom's headword/kanji is independently cross-confirmed between the chapter's
own title block and its restatement inside the English explanation and bilingual sample dialogue.

**Register note:** this book glosses each idiom with a "Style:" tag on its sample dialogue (e.g.
"spoken/casual/male," "written/formal") — a genuine source-marked register annotation per the

## Grammar points

This source is a vocabulary/phrase reference, not a grammar textbook — it introduces no new
grammar rules. The one recurring structural pattern worth noting: nearly every entry's sample
dialogue is given in three parallel forms (romaji, Japanese orthography, English translation) and
tagged with an explicit `Style:` register annotation (e.g. "spoken/casual/male," "written/formal")
specifying the idiom's conversational register and, where relevant, the speaker's gender — a
genuine source-marked sociolinguistic annotation distinct from the `Usage Tier` column above. See
`090b_101_japanese_idioms_part2.md` for a fuller per-entry capture of these Style tags, which become
more systematically recorded partway through the book.

### Idiom structure: literal image vs. idiomatic sense

Every entry in this book follows the same three-layer structure: (1) a literal, often vivid
concrete image (an animal, a plant, a body part, a natural phenomenon), (2) an idiomatic figurative
meaning divorced from that literal image, and (3) a short cultural/etymological note bridging the
two. This is a productive, systematic slang/idiom-formation pattern in Japanese worth flagging for
later mechanics analysis: concrete sensory image → abstracted social/emotional meaning, frequently
drawing on animals (cats, birds, insects, fish), food, or plants as the vehicle.
