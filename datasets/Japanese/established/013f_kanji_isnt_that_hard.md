## Grammar points

### "More Beginning Kanji" — a flat stroke-count-ordered reference list, not a new rule

The book states its own organizing logic directly (p. 128): this section exists because the core
24-rule system only worked through 192 example kanji (8 per rule × 24 rules), and many more
beginning-level kanji remain uncovered. Rather than inventing new rules, the book simply applies the
already-taught units/stems/signs to ~230 further kanji, with entries ordered first by ascending
stroke count, then (within equal stroke count) by on-yomi あいうえお order — explicitly designed so a
reader who already knows a kanji's reading can locate it quickly via the book's closing Kanji Index
(pp. 162–168). The book also explicitly defines its own English terminology here for the first time
in writing (though the terms were used informally throughout Rules 1–24): "stem" (ステム/基本部分) and
"sign" (サイン/付随部分), with "sign" explicitly equated to the standard dictionary term *bushu* (部首,
"radical" in English kanji dictionaries) — confirming that this book's pedagogical stem/sign
framework is its own simplified gloss on conventional radical-based kanji analysis, not an unrelated
invented system. (p. 128)
