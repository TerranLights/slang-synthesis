**Coverage note.** Each of the five lessons in this chunk follows the workbook's fixed structure:
第N課 速読 (speed-reading passage, 1–2 pages) → 内容チェック (comprehension-check A/○× + B/short-answer
drill, 1 page) → 第N課 聴解I (tape-dictation fill-in-the-blank drill, 2–3 pages) → 聴解II (second
tape-dictation fill-in-the-blank drill, 2 pages). **All 内容チェック and 聴解I/II pages in this
chunk are pure drill material** (multiple-choice/○×, short-answer prompts referencing the reading
passage, or blank-line dictation sheets meant to be filled in from an audio cassette this project
does not have access to) and were skipped per the coverage rule — they recombine/test
already-captured passage content and introduce no recoverable new vocabulary (the dictation blanks
hide their own target words, including one quoted proverb in the Lesson 22 聴解II drill that
cannot be recovered without the audio). **Extraction is therefore limited to each lesson's 速読
reading passage**, selectively quoted/paraphrased per the project's copyright discipline (short
phrases only, full passages and the two poems quoted in Lesson 23 are paraphrased rather than
reproduced). Because this workbook has no formal 新しい言葉 (word-box) section of its own — unlike
the main textbook — every vocabulary item below was independently identified from passage text as
notable (new/idiomatic/thematically load-bearing) rather than drawn from a pre-made list; basic
grammar-reference words already well covered by the main textbook's extraction (`060`–`060a` and
sibling files) were not re-extracted.

Lesson-by-lesson passage summary:
- **Lesson 21** (速読, untitled theme label): first-person essay on environmental consciousness —
  attending a "let's protect our earth" meeting, learning about desertification, CO₂/CFC-driven
  warming, acid rain, and a personal shift from apathy ("one person can't change anything") to
  small-scale civic engagement, closing on the idiom 山動く ("the mountain moves").
- **Lesson 22** (旅/travel theme): essay contrasting televised nature-documentary "armchair
  travel" with real travel, arguing real journeys offer irreplaceable encounters, partings, and
  sensory experience that a screen cannot convey.
- **Lesson 23** (詩/poetry theme): narrative about an amateur poet (山下光二) who struggles to
  capture fleeting sensory/emotional experience in verse and draws strength from the real poet
  立原道造 (Tachihara Michizō, d. age 26); the passage quotes two short poems in full — by 山村暮鳥
  (Yamamura Bochō) and by 立原道造 — which are **not reproduced here** per copyright discipline;
  only the poets' names, the poems' subjects, and a short paraphrased quote from Tachihara's own
  stated artistic wish are given below.
- **Lesson 24** (脳死・臓器移植/brain death & organ transplant theme): two first-person newspaper
  reader letters (framed as excerpted from 『毎朝新聞「脳死を考えよう」』) — one from a mother whose
  son received a heart transplant, one from a teacher opposing organ transplantation on grounds
  that brain death does not settle the ethical status of the body and that transplant markets
  could exploit the poor.
- **Lesson 25** (ふるさとの変化/hometown-change theme): first-person essay about returning to a
  rural hometown transformed by a large confectionery-industry factory, contrasting the narrator's
  brother's small family candy business (and his dream of passing the craft to his sons) against
  the sons' actual choices (one becomes a city ad-agency employee, one joins the factory's research
  lab) — closing with a reflection on this being a common pattern across Japan's "economic
  superpower" growth.

poetry is said to outlive its author.
