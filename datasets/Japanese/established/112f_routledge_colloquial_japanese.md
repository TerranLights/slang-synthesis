## Grammar points

### The -masu verb paradigm (first verb conjugation in the book)
Unit 6 introduces the book's first true verbs — ikimasu "go", kimasu "come", kaerimasu "return" —
and with them the polite -masu conjugation paradigm that recurs for every verb henceforth: stem +
masu (non-past affirmative), stem + masen (non-past negative), stem + mashita (past affirmative),
stem + masendeshita (past negative). The stem itself (iki-, ki-, kaeri-) never changes; only the
masu-ending inflects — a structurally simpler system than the i-adjective/na-adjective inflections
of Unit 5. p. 70.

### Direction marker e + go/come/return
To state a destination: noun(person) + wa + noun(place) + e + verb(go/come/return) — e marks the
place being moved toward (Watashi wa shiyakusho e ikimasu "I will go to the city hall"). doko
substitutes for the place noun to ask "where?" p. 70.

### Means of transport/method: de
The particle **de** (distinct from the location/means-counting de elsewhere) marks how a trip is
made, inserted as an added noun2-de slot before the destination: noun1-wa noun2(method)-de
noun3(place)-e verb (Watashi wa basu de uchi e kaerimashita "I went home by bus"). Two means — "on
foot" (aruite) and "running" (hashitte) — are irregular exceptions that do NOT take de. p. 73-74.

### Companion marker: to
The particle **to** ("with [a person]") inserts a companion noun before the destination: Watashi wa
Lī-san to toshokan e ikimasu "I go to the library with Miss Lee." The interrogative "with whom?" is
dare to. Note a particle-overlap gotcha: when specifying a headcount of companions rather than a
named person, **de** is used instead of to (hitori de "alone", futari de "as two people") — de thus
carries at least two distinct grammatical functions in this unit (means-of-transport and headcount-
of-companions), worth flagging to avoid conflating them in any downstream slang/conlang modeling.
p. 74.
