## Cross-level difficulty comparison (direct observation across the 3 sampled books)

- **Furigana density.** *Basu* (Level 1) glosses **every** kanji compound on every page, including
  extremely common ones (「毎日」まいにち, 「今日」きょう, 「正雄」まさお appear fully glossed on every
  single occurrence, even the 10th+ repetition). *Gon Gitsune* (Level 2) still glosses nearly every
  kanji but is visibly less exhaustive — a few very high-frequency items repeat without re-glossing
  within the same spread. *Yuki Onna* (Level 4) glosses only less-common readings/compounds
  (六十歳 ろくじゅっさい, 一人暮らし ひとりぐらし, 途中 とちゅう) and leaves ordinary-frequency kanji
  (男, 山, 木, 二人, 母, 村, 川) completely bare — the title itself, 雪女, carries no furigana at all
  on the cover, in sharp contrast to バス's fully-glossed title.
- **Sentence-final style / copula register.** *Basu* narrates almost entirely in polite です/ます
  form (「鈴木さんは、二十歳です」「バスを運転します」). *Gon Gitsune*'s narration is a mix, still
  leaning です/ます for narration but introducing more descriptive density per sentence. *Yuki Onna*
  narrates in plain/literary style with the である copula and plain-past いた/した forms
  (「二人は山で木を切って、それを売って生活していた」「ある村に、茂作と巳之吉という二人の男がいた」),
  plus the explanatory のだ nominalizer (「川のこちら側まで運んでもらうのだった」) — a register never
  seen in the Level 1 sample at all.
- **Sentence length / clause complexity.** *Basu*'s sentences are short, single-clause, heavily
  paratactic (one idea per sentence, frequent full stops). *Yuki Onna*'s sentences chain multiple
  clauses with て-form linking and subordinate clauses (e.g. the single sentence describing the two
  woodcutters' living arrangement spans three linked clauses: age, profession, and living
  arrangement, joined without a sentence break).
- **Dialogue register.** All three books use colloquial sentence-final particles in character
  dialogue regardless of level (なあ, よ, ぞ in *Basu* and *Yuki Onna* alike) — this is a dialogue-
  realism feature independent of the graded narration-prose difficulty, worth noting since it means
  even Level 1 carries some colloquial-particle vocabulary despite its otherwise highly simplified
  narration.
- **Onomatopoeia.** Both *Basu* (キキキキキーッ, brake-screech) and *Gon Gitsune* (ドボン, splash)
  use full-katakana sound-effect onomatopoeia integrated directly into the narration line, not set
  off typographically — common to both lower levels in this sample.

## Grammar points

### Polite narrative register (です/ます) as the Level-1 default (*Basu*)

*Basu*'s entire narration — not just dialogue — is carried in polite です/ます form throughout (e.g.
「鈴木正雄さんは、二十歳です」p.2-3; 「バスは、止まりました」p.10-11). This is a structural
simplification choice for Level 1: narration itself speaks directly to the reader in the polite
register rather than the plain/literary past tense more typical of adult prose narration. No plain
form (だ/である, plain past -た without です) appears anywhere in the sampled narration lines, only in
a few colloquial interjections inside quoted dialogue.

### Colloquial sentence-final particles inside dialogue, independent of narration level

Even at Level 1, quoted internal monologue/dialogue carries colloquial sentence-final particles that
never appear in the surrounding です/ます narration: なあ (「早く寝たいなあー」p.6-7, wistful/self-
directed), ぞ (*Gon Gitsune* 「散歩ができるぞ！」p.3, assertive/self-declarative). This confirms a
split: narration-level register scales with the graded-reader level, but dialogue realism (natural
colloquial particle use) does not get simplified away even at the easiest level.

### Literary plain-style narration with である and のだ (*Yuki Onna*, Level 4 only)

*Yuki Onna*'s narration departs from all two lower-level samples by using the plain/literary copula
である (「ある冬の寒い日のことである」p.6) and the explanatory のだ nominalizer (「運んでもらうのだった」
p.6) — both grammar points entirely absent from the Level 1 and Level 2 samples. This is consistent
with the official difficulty table's description of Level 4 as 中級 ("intermediate") versus Levels
1–2 as 初級 ("beginner"): the grammar inventory itself expands at Level 4, not just the vocabulary
count.

### Honorific/affectionate noun-register code-switching (おばあさん → おばあちゃん)

*Basu* uses the neutral/formal おばあさん in narration throughout but switches to the affectionate,
informal おばあちゃん the moment the driver recognizes his dead grandmother in dialogue
(「お、おばあちゃん?!」p.12-13). This narration-vs-dialogue register split mirrors real Japanese usage
where the informal/affectionate kinship term surfaces specifically at a moment of emotional
recognition, even though the surrounding simplified-reader narration stays neutral.

### Potential form みえる/できる pattern used for exclamatory capability statements

*Gon Gitsune*'s 「散歩ができるぞ！」(p.3) uses the potential form of する (できる) to express "I'm able
to..." as an excited exclamation about the weather clearing — a simple but genuine potential-form
construction appearing even in a Level-2-simplified adaptation of a 1932 literary original.
