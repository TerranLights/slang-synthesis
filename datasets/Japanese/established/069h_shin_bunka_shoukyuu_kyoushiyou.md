## Grammar points

### Lesson 30 (もう少し召し上がりませんか。— "Won't You Have a Little More?"), pp. 111–121 — 敬語 (keigo/honorific system) capstone lesson
Goals: handle simple honorific-register telephone interactions; understand how intimacy (親疎) and social hierarchy (社会的立場での上下関係) govern which register (尊敬語/謙譲語/plain) to use, and speak appropriately using 待遇表現 (politeness-level expressions).

Core framework (stated explicitly as the organizing principle before any 文型 is drilled): the use of honorific language is NOT determined by a fixed property of a word alone, but by the relationship — intimacy level and relative social standing — between speaker and the person being discussed, termed 敬意対象者 ("the person to whom respect is directed") in this book's own terminology. The clean worked minimal pair establishing this: a mother describing her own daughter (家族/in-group) to an outside caller uses plain/humble-leaning forms (由美は今おりませんが), while the same caller later addresses the daughter directly with full honorifics (由美さんはいらっしゃいますか) — same referent, different register, purely a function of the speaker's own relationship to her.

1. **尊敬語 (honorific register, for the addressee's/a respected third party's own actions)** — used when the action/state described belongs to the 敬意対象者 (the person being shown respect). Two formation types flagged explicitly: (a) suppletive — an entirely different verb replaces the plain one (食べる→召し上がる), (b) morphological — a productive お〜になる wrapping pattern (撮る→お撮りになる). Irregular-conjugation warning: なさる (honorific of する) irregularly conjugates to なさいます, not the expected ×なさります pattern — flagged as needing explicit teacher correction. Teacher instructed to pre-drill both honorific and humble paradigms from the companion textbook's summary tables (pp. 122–123, not part of this source) before entering sentence-level practice, and to have students identify for every example sentence: who the 敬意対象者 is, what the relationship (intimacy/hierarchy) is, who performs the action, and who the respect flows from/to.
2. **謙譲語 (humble register, for the speaker's own actions that affect/concern a respected person)** — used specifically when the speaker (a) acts directly upon the respected person or their belongings, or (b) imposes/inconveniences the respected person in some way (both conditions illustrated: お荷物お持ちしましょうか "may I carry your bag" — type (a); (お宅に)9時に参ります "I'll come [to your place] at 9" — type (b)). Explicit learner-error warning via a marked-wrong model sentence: a student describing a mundane, non-respect-laden personal action to a teacher using humble 拝見する ("(誤)昨日テレビを3時間拝見しました" — "I humbly watched TV for 3 hours yesterday") is flagged as wrong — 拝見する is reserved for actions that actually concern/involve the respected person, not just any statement made in their presence; overusing humble-register verbs for ordinary personal actions is a real documented learner pitfall. Fine-grained near-synonym distinction given for two humble "go/come" verbs: 参る (general humble "go/come") vs. 伺う (humble "go/come," but specifically implying visiting/approaching a respected person) — worked example: a student responding to a teacher's in-classroom "come over here and help" with 伺います is marked wrong (×) because the simple act of walking across the same room to help, even though addressed to a teacher, doesn't carry 伺う's "visiting" implication; 参ります is correct. The book explicitly tells the instructor that for THIS lesson's own purposes, 参る and 伺う are to be taught as synonymous (the finer distinction above is presented as a caveat for the teacher's own awareness, not something students are drilled on at this level). Irregular negative flagged: 存じております (humble "to know") negates irregularly to 存じません, not a compositional form — students are told to memorize this as a fixed unit. Prefix-choice irregularity for the ご/お〜なさる honorific-する pattern: which of ご or お attaches is lexically determined per verb and not rule-derivable at this level (ご試着なさいます vs. presumably お-prefixed alternatives for other verbs) — alongside a note that some ご/お〜なさる verbs take an obligatory を (お仕事をなさいます) while others take an optional/absent を (ご電話(を)なさいます), a distinction the book explicitly declines to teach systematically at this level. Parallel note for ご/お〜する (humble self-action, treated by the book as equivalent in meaning to ご/お〜いたす): some verbs exist in both a bare and お/ご-prefixed form (紹介いたします ≈ ご紹介いたします) while some お〜する forms are themselves restricted or simply unused for certain verbs (explicitly: お帰りする is not a real/used form, despite お〜する being otherwise productive) — again flagged as an irregularity for the teacher to watch for rather than a rule students can mechanically apply.
3. **〜ていただけませんか (polite request)** — treated as synonymous with 〜てくださいませんか (introduced alongside, for recognition) in this lesson; 〜てもらえませんか (a third near-equivalent) is explicitly named as outside this textbook's scope, offered only as an aside the teacher may introduce at their own discretion.

Semantic-narrowing note on an honorific-formation side effect (practice-section aside, not a numbered 文型): 持つ ("to have/hold"), when honorifically wrapped as お持ちになる, additionally narrows in meaning to specifically "to bring/carry (with oneself, in a coming-or-going context)" rather than just abstractly "to possess" — flagged as a case where the honorific transformation isn't semantically neutral, a genuine and non-obvious wrinkle for learners expecting honorific wrapping to be purely a register shift with no meaning change.

A supplementary reference page is noted ("いろいろな敬語のページ," p. 121 of the student textbook) collecting miscellaneous honorific expressions beyond the main dialogues/文型, for recognition only; and 名詞+にする ("to decide on/settle on [noun]," e.g. 和食にします) is noted as having its first appearance here, with the related 動詞+ことにする deferred to L31.
