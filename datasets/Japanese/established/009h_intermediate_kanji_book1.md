## Grammar points

### Review of single-kanji and partial-component phonetic markers, continued from Review 1 — Review 2 §①–②
This review explicitly continues and cross-references Review 1's two phonetic-marker categories
(a full standalone kanji used whole as a phonetic marker, vs. only part of a more complex kanji
serving that role). New full-kanji phonetic-marker families given: 永(エイ→泳,詠), 貫(カン→慣),
其(キ→基,期,旗,棋), 士(シ→仕,志,誌), 召(ショウ→召,招,紹,照,昭,沼,詔 — with 超チョウ explicitly
flagged as an exception that looks similar but does NOT follow this reading), 申(シン→神,伸,紳),
成(セイ→盛,誠,城— with 誠/城 both flagged as carrying irregular ジョウ-family readings rather than
plain セイ), 象(ゾウ→像), 則(ソク→測,側), 奴(ド→努,怒), 忍(ニン→認), 皮(ヒ→彼,疲,被,披 — with 波/破
flagged as a reading exception despite the same visual phonetic component, both read ハ), 包(ホウ→
抱,胞,飽,砲,泡), 亡(ボウ→忙,忘,望), 憂(ユウ→優). New partial-component phonetic-marker families:
哉(シキ/ショク→織,識,職), 开/开(ケイ/カイ→形,型,刑 / 開), 僉(ケン→験,険,検,倹,剣), 冓(コウ→講,購,
構,溝), 艮(コン/ゲン/ギン→根 / 限 / 銀,眼 — a single visual component spawning three different
on-readings across its derived characters), 㕣(ソウ→窓,総), 畐(フク/フ→福,副,幅 / 富), 复(フク→復,
複,腹 — 腹 and 履(リ) flagged as exceptions within this family).

### Standalone vs. phonetic-marker reading shifts, continued — Review 2 §③
Further worked examples of the same phenomenon introduced in Review 1 (a kanji's reading when used
alone differs from its reading when serving as a phonetic marker inside another character): 以
(alone=イ, 以上; as marker=ジ, 類似), 君(alone=クン, 君主; as marker=グン, 大群/郡部), 軍(alone=グン,
海軍; as marker=ウン, 運転), 占(alone=セン, 独占; as marker=テン, 代理店/点字), 曽(alone=ソウ, 曽祖父;
as marker=ソウ/ゾウ, 下層/増加/贈答 — here the marker reading shifts between two different values
depending on the specific derived character), 直(alone=チョク, 直営; as marker=チ, 位置/価値).

### Phonetic markers that cue multiple distinct on-readings depending on the word — Review 2 §④
Extending Review 1's exceptional-reading-family concept, this section catalogs markers whose
derived characters split across MORE than two on-readings rather than a single-exception pattern:
各→カク(各地,各国)/ガク(額→税額)/キャク(客→旅客, which itself has a secondary variant reading
リョカク noted in-source)/ラク(落→段落; 絡→短絡); 未→ミ(味→味方)/マイ(妹→姉妹); 予→ヨ(序→予防— note:
予 itself is read ヨ)/ジョ(序→序論)/ヤ(野→平野); 列→レツ(行列,列車)/レイ(例→具体例); 青→セイ・ショウ
(青春; 緑青 uses the ショウ branch); 静→セイ・ジョウ(動静; 静脈 uses the ジョウ branch); 成→セイ・
ジョウ(成年; 成仏 uses the ジョウ branch, a Buddhist-register reading); 盛→セイ・ジョウ(全盛期;
繁盛する uses the ジョウ branch); 亡→ボウ・モウ(亡父; 亡者 uses the モウ branch); 忙/忘→ボウ only
(多忙,備忘録); 望→ボウ・モウ(願望; 所望する uses the モウ branch); 容→ヨウ(内容). The review frames
this as the book's final, most advanced application of the形声文字 (phonetic-compound) decoding
method taught across both review chapters: a reader who has internalized these marker families can
predict the probable on-reading of a never-before-seen kanji, while knowing to treat single-member
exceptions (超,誠,城,波,破,腹,履, etc.) as memorized irregulars rather than counterevidence against
the general method.
