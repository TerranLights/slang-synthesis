## Grammar points

### Family terms: in-group vs. out-group vocabulary
Japanese has two distinct words for most family members: one plain/humble form used for one's own
family (chichi, haha, ane, ani, imōto, otōto, sobo, sofu, kazoku, ryōshin) and one honorific form
used when referring to someone else's family (otōsan, okāsan, onēsan, onīsan, imōtosan, otōtosan,
obāsan, ojīsan, gokazoku, goryōshin). This in-group/out-group split recurs across many areas of
Japanese politeness and is one of the clearest, earliest-taught instances of it. pp. 23.

### Numbers 11-99
Built compositionally from the 0-10 set (Unit 1): 11-19 = jū + 1-9 (jūichi, jūni...); multiples of
ten = digit + jū (nijū "20", sanjū "30"...); and combined numbers = tens-digit + jū + ones-digit
(nijūgo "25" = 2×10+5, rokujūhachi "68" = 6×10+8). p. 24.

### Age with -sai
"Years old" attaches as the counter suffix **-sai** directly to a number (29-sai "29 years old").
Questions use nan-sai "how old?"; yes/no confirmation just adds ka. p. 24.

### Also: mo
**mo** ("also/too") replaces the topic marker **wa** to assert the same predicate holds for the new
topic (Tanaka-san wa 29-sai desu. Watashi mo 29-sai desu. "Mrs Tanaka is 29. I am also 29."). p. 26.

### Date and month structure
Months use suffix **-gatsu**, dates use suffix **-nichi**, with month preceding date (1-gatsu
28-nichi "January 28th") — opposite of English order. Three months have irregular readings that
don't follow the expected numeral: April is shi-gatsu (not yon-gatsu), July is shichi-gatsu (not
nana-gatsu), September is ku-gatsu (not kyū-gatsu). p. 27.

### Days of the month 1st-31st — a genuinely irregular subsystem
Days 1st-10th, plus 14th, 20th, and 24th, use a fully separate, non-compositional set of native
Japanese readings (tsuitachi, futsuka, mikka, yokka, itsuka, muika, nanoka, yōka, kokonoka, tōka;
jūyokka "14th", hatsuka/nijū-nichi "20th", nijūyokka "24th") rather than number+nichi. All other
days (11th-13th, 15th-19th, 21st-23rd, 25th-31st) are regular: tens+ones+nichi (e.g. jūichi-nichi
"11th", nijūgo-nichi "25th"). This is the single most irregular vocabulary subsystem found in Unit
1-2 and worth flagging for conlang slang-synthesis purposes as a model of "a core, high-frequency
domain that resisted regularization." p. 28.

### itsu "when?" vs. nan-gatsu nan-nichi
Both ask about timing, but **nan-gatsu nan-nichi** specifically requires a month+date answer, while
**itsu** is broader and can be answered with a date, month, time, or year (or a relative expression
like ashita "tomorrow"). p. 28-29.

### Days of the week: -yōbi suffix
Built from a kanji-reading set unrelated to the numeral series: nichi-yōbi (Sun), getsu-yōbi (Mon),
ka-yōbi (Tue), sui-yōbi (Wed), moku-yōbi (Thu), kin-yōbi (Fri), do-yōbi (Sat); nan-yōbi "which day
of the week?". p. 31.

### Relative time vocabulary: a 3×3 grid
Day/week/month/year each have a last/this/next triad: kinō/kyō/ashita (day), senshū/konshū/raishū
(week), sengetsu/kongetsu/raigetsu (month), kyonen/kotoshi/rainen (year) — a clean, systematic
pattern (sen- "previous", kon- "this", rai- "next" + the time-unit noun) except for the day triad,
which uses entirely suppletive forms. p. 31.
