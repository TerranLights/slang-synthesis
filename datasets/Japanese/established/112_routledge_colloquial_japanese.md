## Grammar points

### Basic Japanese structure: X wa Y desu
"X is Y" equivalent, with topic-comment word order reversed from English: the topic (X) comes
first, marked by the particle **wa**, followed by the comment (Y) and copula **desu**. p.10.

### Politeness prefix "o-"
Certain nouns (okuni, onamae, oshigoto, osushi, osake, omizu, ohashi) take an **o-** prefix to sound
more polite. Exception: when referring to one's *own* country or name, the bare form is used
(watashi no kuni, watashi no namae), not the o- form — the honorific prefix is reserved for
referring to the listener's or a third party's things. p.12, p.13.

### Possessive marker no
**no** marks possession, equivalent to English "of" or "'s": watashi no namae "my name", Yamada-san
no (o)shigoto "Mr Yamada's occupation". p.15.

### Japanese names and titles: -san and -sensē
Japanese names are surname-first. **-san** attaches after a surname, first name, or both
(Yamada-san, Yamada Takeshi-san) and is never used for one's own name or one's own family members.
**-sensē** ("teacher," also used for doctors, lawyers, politicians) can be used the same way as -san
but, unlike -san, can also stand alone as a form of address. p.15.

### Yes/no questions with ka
Unlike English, Japanese doesn't invert word order for questions — the sentence-final particle
**ka** alone converts a statement into a yes/no question (Yamada-san wa ginkōin desu → ...desu ka).
Because Japanese commonly addresses a known listener by name rather than "you" (anata), such a
question can be ambiguous between "are you X" and "is [named person] X" depending on who's being
addressed. p.16.

### Interrogative questions with nan/nani
Japanese "what?" has two allomorphs: **nan** (before desu and some consonant-initial words: nan desu
ka, nan-ban, nan-sai) and **nani** (elsewhere: nani-jin, nani o shimasu ka). The interrogative word
slots into the position of the missing information rather than moving to sentence-initial position,
with ka added at the end (Yamada-san no shigoto wa nan desu ka). Answers to such questions use
neither hai nor īe, just the information itself (e.g. Ginkōin desu). pp.16-17.

### Numbers 0-10 and dual readings
Japanese numerals 0-10: zero, ichi, ni, san, yon/shi, go, roku, nana/shichi, hachi, kyū, jū. Two
numbers have two interchangeable readings (4: yon/shi; 7: nana/shichi), though a specific one is
sometimes required contextually (e.g. shi-gatsu "April", shichi-gatsu "July" always use the shi/
shichi readings, never yon-gatsu/nana-gatsu). p.19.

### Telephone numbers: X wa Y desu applied to numbers
Telephone numbers are stated with the same X wa Y desu topic-comment structure (Watashi no
denwa-bangō wa 0123-45-6789 desu), with the hyphen read aloud as "no" — this is a separate,
semantically empty use of "no" distinct from (but homophonous with) the possessive marker. p.19.
