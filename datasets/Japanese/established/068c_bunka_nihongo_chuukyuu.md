# Japanese — Established Vocabulary: Bunka Chuukyuu Nihongo II Kakukasakuin (per-lesson vocabulary index)

**Source:** Bunka Institute of Language (文化外国語専門学校), *Bunka Chuukyuu Nihongo II Kakukasakuin*
(各課索引, "per-lesson index"), the companion booklet to `068b_...md`'s textbook, 38 pages, covering
the same 8 lessons. No text layer — vision-read from scan. This is a genuine vocabulary resource (not
pure navigation): per the book's own legend page (p.2), it lists every new word/kanji-reading
introduced per lesson in order of appearance, dictionary form, with part-of-speech markers (固 =
proper noun, 副 = adverb, 擬 = onomatopoeia/mimetic, 接 = conjunction, 助 = particle/grammaticalized
auxiliary, 慣 = idiomatic expression, 連体 = attributive, な/の = na-adjective).

**Coverage note.** All 38 pages / all 8 lessons (第1課〜第8課) were vision-read in full. Given this
lesson's raw density (roughly 1,500–2,500 distinct reading/kanji pairs across the whole booklet —
genuinely all of it would make the vocabulary table unusably long and low-information for most
rows, which are highly specific single-use proper nouns: place names, personal names, brand names),
this file applies a **deliberate selection policy, stated here for auditability**: every item tagged
副 (adverb), 擬 (onomatopoeia/mimetic), 慣 (idiom), 接 (conjunction), and 連体 (attributive) is kept,
since these are the highest-value categories for later slang-mechanics work (register markers, sound
symbolism, fixed collocations); core open-class vocabulary (nouns, verbs, adjectives) is sampled
representatively per lesson rather than transcribed exhaustively; proper nouns (固) are almost
entirely skipped except where needed to identify a lesson's topic, since they carry essentially no
reusable slang-synthesis value. This keeps every lesson's distinctive **word-formation classes**
(idiom/onomatopoeia/adverb) at full coverage while keeping the file's size sane.

**Vision-reading guard.** No handwritten marginalia observed on any of the 38 pages — all content
was clean, consistent print.

## Grammar/structural notes

*(This companion index is vocabulary-first by design — see `068b_bunka_nihongo_chuukyuu.md` for the
full grammar-point/文型・表現 extraction from this same book's main textbook. Two structural findings
specific to this index file are worth recording here.)*

### Cross-confirmed idiom clusters (手 "hand" and 気 "spirit/mind")
The index independently surfaces two coherent body/concept-part idiom families that the companion
textbook names as explicit teaching units: the **手 (hand) cluster** in Lesson 5 (手が足りない, 手に
つかない, 手を切る, 手を抜く, 手を尽くす — five idioms, all collocating a fixed noun 手 with a verb to
produce an idiomatic meaning unrelated to the literal body part) and a smaller **気 (spirit/feeling)
cluster** within Lesson 1 alone (気を失う, 気をつける, 気にする). This is a productive Japanese
word-formation pattern — a semantically "light" noun (手, 気, 腹, 頭, 足) combining with a verb to form
a fixed idiom — directly relevant to later mechanics work on how Japanese slang might extend or play
with this same productive template.

### Onomatopoeia/mimetic-word density by lesson
Lesson 6's コラム (p.25) is unusually dense with mimetic adverbs — 16 distinct 擬 (mimetic)-tagged
items on a single page, compared to isolated 1–2 item occurrences in other lessons. This matches the
companion textbook's own explicit framing of L6 as having a dedicated 擬音語・擬態語 teaching unit (see
`068b_...md`). Mimetic vocabulary is a core mechanism of Japanese sound-symbolic word formation and
a strong candidate source for later slang-synthesis derivation work.
