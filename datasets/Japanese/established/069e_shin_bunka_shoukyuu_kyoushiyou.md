## Grammar points

### Lesson 27 (引っ越し — "Moving House"), pp. 85–93
Goals: learn the practical bureaucratic steps involved in moving house; use 〜ている to flag a problem or voice a complaint to someone.
1. **自動詞＋ている (discovered-problem state)** — marks a newly-noticed, unwanted resultant state (壁が汚れています), used specifically to flag an issue to someone or lodge a mild complaint; sentence-final よ recommended when drawing the listener's attention to the problem. Explicit pitfall: a service-request use of 〜をお願いしたいんですが is distinguished from this descriptive use. Also explicitly distinguished from the unrelated "fixture/equipment included" sense of 〜がついている used earlier in the same lesson's dialogue (エアコンもついています, L27 本文1②) — flagged as a likely source of learner confusion between two superficially similar でも semantically distinct 〜ている uses within the same lesson.
2. **〜ておく (preparatory action)** — doing something in advance in consideration of a later state; explicitly distinguished from the "resulting state" auxiliary 〜てある (deferred to L36); invitation/request framings (〜ておきませんか／〜ておいてください) emphasized as the practical register this is taught in.
3. **〜ように (purpose, "so that")** — restricted to antecedents the subject cannot directly/consciously control, broken into five illustrated sub-types: (1) an intransitive verb describing an object's own change (割れないように包む), (2) a potential-form antecedent (起きられるように目覚し時計をかける), (3) a negated-involuntary/unconscious-action antecedent (忘れないようにメモする), (4) a case where the actor of the purpose clause differs from the actor of the main clause (子供が触らないように高いところにしまう), (5) 〜やすい-based antecedents (食べやすいように切る). Classroom drilling is explicitly focused on sub-types (1) and (2) only, though all five are presented to the teacher. Cross-referenced against the superficially similar ために purpose-marker, deferred to L33 (ために takes a volitional/controllable antecedent, the complementary case to ように's non-volitional antecedent — though this complementarity is only implicit in the cross-reference, not spelled out as a rule in this lesson).
4. **〜まま (unchanged state persisting)** — "doing Y while a resulting state from X continues unchanged / without reverting," drilled here only in the 基本体過去+まま form (中身を入れたまま). A negative counterpart (〜ないまま, 用件を聞かないまま電話を切ってしまった) is noted as existing but not drilled, due to a shortage of good example sentences. Explicitly and carefully distinguished from 〜ないで (L21): 〜ないで describes an action proceeding in the absence of another action, while 〜まま describes a state persisting unchanged/neglected; the book notes the two are NOT always freely interchangeable, with a flagged example where substituting ないで produces an unnatural sentence (立ったままコーヒーを飲みました vs. the marked/awkward ?座らないでコーヒーを飲みました).
5. **〜て (causal, "too X to do Y")** — で causal/reason use of 〜て specifically restricted here to a "state X as reason → can't do Y" pattern (重くて持てません); the book notes in its teacher-commentary that beyond its own い-adjective example set, the same pattern extends naturally to stative verbs (いる, ある) and 〜すぎる-derived predicates (辛すぎて食べられません; 人がおおぜいいて、中に入れません) — offered to the teacher as supplementary examples, not drilled in the student book itself. The distinct "後件に感情がくる" (resultant-clause-expresses-emotion) use of causal 〜て is deferred to L34.
Additional register note (practice-section aside, not a numbered 文型): 〜でしょう? used to seek the listener's agreement (そうでしょう?, rising intonation) is explicitly flagged as imposing the speaker's own opinion on the listener and therefore impolite toward a social superior — paralleling the same register constraint already seen on other opinion-soliciting patterns earlier in the book (L20's 意志形+と思いますか, L21's たほうがいいです).
