## Grammar points

### The "want to" pattern (VERB STEM + tai-N-desu ga ...)

Introduced at p.181-182 (Ch.15, placed here since it recurs constantly through the travel chapters):
tai attaches to a verb stem for "want to," combined with the explanatory/softening -N-desu, and
typically trails off with the softening conjunction ga ("...but") rather than completing the
sentence outright — explicitly identified by the book as a politeness strategy (leaving a sentence
grammatically "incomplete" invites the listener to fill in or negotiate the rest, softening a
direct request). This same ga-softening device recurs independently in the "SumimaseN ga..."
construction already noted in the Part 3 file — a consistent pragmatic strategy across this book,
not a one-off.

### Euphonic/irregular counter alternations are pervasive and systematic, not isolated exceptions

Across the number/time/date/duration tables in this Part (minutes, hours, days, weeks, months,
years, money), the book consistently bolds a recurring small set of consonant-mutation patterns
(e.g. ichi+hyaku → hyaku but saN+hyaku → saN-byaku; roku+hyaku → rop-pyaku; hachi+hyaku →
hap-pyaku) wherever a counter beginning with h-/f- combines with certain preceding numerals. This
is the same general rendaku/sequential-voicing-adjacent euphonic alternation appearing over and
over across completely different counter classes (hyaku "hundred," -fuN "minutes," -haku "nights,"
-shūkaN "weeks," -kagetsu "months" all show some version of it) — worth flagging as a systematic
phonological rule governing numeral-counter combination, not a list of unrelated irregular words.
