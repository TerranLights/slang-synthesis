# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Moons, Months and Seasons (日本のくらし12か月) — Front Matter & January–March Vocabulary List

**Source:** APRICOT (国際日本語研究所編, International Japanese Language Research Institute, ed.),
*日本のくらし12か月 — Moons, Months and Seasons: A Pre-Intermediate Japanese Reader* (APRICOT
publisher; n.d., estimated 1990s based on content/design). Vocabulary List (語彙リスト), pp. 84–111
(PDF pages 85–112 of the scanned DjVu; offset verified empirically: printed page = PDF page − 1).
This file covers the front-matter explanation (p. 84) and the January–March vocabulary entries
(pp. 86–92 / PDF 87–93).

**Source format note:** This is a 12-chapter graded reader (one chapter per calendar month, ~800
characters of running Japanese prose per chapter, each accompanied by furigana-reading notes, an
English translation, and a chapter-topic vocabulary list reproduced in the book's table of contents).
The book's single largest vocabulary resource is its consolidated back-of-book **Vocabulary List**
(語彙リスト, pp. 84–111): every word from the main text judged difficult for a
pre-intermediate/post-beginner learner, listed in the order it appears in each month's chapter, with
kanji/kana headword, romanized reading, and English gloss (dictionary forms given parenthetically for
conjugated verb forms). This is the highest-density, most systematically exhaustive vocabulary
resource in the book, so this dispatch extracts it in full rather than sampling — split across
multiple files (105, 105b, 105c, 105d) by month-groups to avoid one unbounded file, per the
extraction spec's chunking rule.

**No text layer:** `djvutxt` produced zero output for this DjVu — confirmed vision-only (OCR/text
layer absent), so every entry below was read directly from rendered page images. All vocabulary-list
pages are clean, single-column, large-print typeset text (no multi-column scrambling risk, no
handwritten marginalia observed on any page checked — this is a clean, apparently-unused library/
institutional scan). Legibility was uniformly high; no `low_confidence` entries were needed in this
batch. Font is a clear Japanese textbook typeface throughout; no font-substitution cipher issues
(this is image-only, not a corrupted text layer, so the cipher gotchas don't apply here).

**Coverage note:** every distinct vocabulary entry from the January, February, and March sections of
the Vocabulary List is captured below — genuinely all of it, not a sample. Page/line reference
columns from the original ("P. ℓ.") are folded into the Notes column where given. Cross-reference
arrows in the source (e.g. "→P. 10") pointing to fuller treatment of a term in the main chapter text
are preserved in Notes as `(see main text p. N)`. No handwritten marginalia was found on any page in
this batch.

## Grammar points

This source is a graded reader, not a grammar textbook — it presents and glosses vocabulary and
cultural content rather than teaching grammatical structures directly. However, the Vocabulary
List's own front-matter (p. 84, paraphrased) establishes several consistent morphological/
romanization conventions worth noting as implicit grammar information:

### Verb citation form and conjugated-form listing

Vocabulary entries for verbs are given in the inflected form that actually appears in the main text
(e.g. the -masu form, a te-form, or a passive form), with the plain dictionary form supplied
parenthetically immediately after — except for two specific cases the source explicitly carves out:
(1) when a verb's te-form is used adverbially (e.g. そろって "together," まとめて "all at once"), and
(2) when a verb's ta-form is used adjectivally (e.g. はなやいだ "merry," すぐれた "superior" —
the latter not found in Jan–Mar), the dictionary form is deliberately omitted because the form in
context is functioning as a different part of speech, not as a verb per se.

### Compound-word spacing in romanization

The source's own romanization system (detailed on p. 84) distinguishes word-internal compounding
from phrase-level spacing: a verb's -masu form is always written as one unbroken word; a te-form
followed by an auxiliary like いる/くる gets a space before the auxiliary; a Sino-Japanese
noun+する verb gets a space before する; and compound verbs (e.g. 咲き始める "to begin to bud,"
浮かび上がる "to rise up") get a space between their two verbal elements. This is reflected directly
in several entries above (e.g. 咲き始め "saki hajime," not "sakihajime").

### Honorific prefix hyphenation

Nouns with the honorific prefix お- or ご- are hyphenated in this source's romanization (お‐とそ
"o-toso," お‐ぞうに "o-zōni," お‐寺 "o-tera," お‐彼岸 "o-higan," お‐世話 "o-sewa," お‐墓参り
"o-hakamairi") — except where the source judges the hyphen-free spelling more readily understood
or already lexically fused (its own examples: jukensei, mayonaka, otoshidama — all of which appear
unhyphenated even though they carry an honorific-adjacent or prefixed element).
