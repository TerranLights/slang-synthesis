# Japanese — Established Vocabulary: Zakennayo! Chapters VII-IX

**Source:** Philip J. Cunningham (illus. Kim Wilson Brandt), *Zakennayo! The Real Japanese You
Were Never Taught in School* (undated single edition), Chapter VII "Hookers and Gangsters"
(printed pp. 68-75: Kabuki-Cho, Red Light, Mama-san, Time to Run), Chapter VIII "Disco City"
(printed pp. 76-87: Roppongi, Beauties Sluts and Bimbos, Bodicon Dancers, Sugar Daddy, Bottoms
Up!), Chapter IX "Foreign Drinking Holes" (printed pp. 88-95: Gaijin Bar, Bimbo Heaven, Curfew
Time). PDF pages 38-51 of the scanned file, vision-read (no text layer).

**Coverage note:** every distinct romaji vocabulary/slang item from the vocabulary lists on this
page range, plus slang terms surfaced inline in the illustrated dialogue scenes and in speech
bubbles drawn into the cartoon illustrations. Illustrated dialogue scenes (bilingual Japanese/
English skits) are paraphrased/summarized rather than transcribed, per copyright discipline — the
book's own "Vocabulary" sections (which are short headword+gloss lists, not extended prose) are
captured in full since that is the source's own designed reference content. This source covers
red-light-district, gangster/yakuza, and nightlife-pickup vocabulary; tagged honestly as
`taboo`/`slang` where that is what the source itself documents, without sanitizing.

**No handwritten marginalia found** on this page range — all content is printed typeset text and
printed illustrations with lettered speech-bubble text (itself typeset/inked as part of the
original artwork, not reader annotation). Some pages show faint ghost show-through of the facing
page's text bleeding through the paper under scan lighting; this is a print/scan artifact, not
separate content, and was not transcribed.

## Chapter VII — Hookers and Gangsters (pp. 68-75)

### Kabuki-Cho (intro + character vocabulary, p. 68-69)

Kabuki-cho, on the east side of Shinjuku Station, is framed as Tokyo's major red-light/
entertainment district, run by criminal syndicates. The author gives a short "how to stay out of
trouble" list (don't go there; keep your mouth shut; pretend not to speak Japanese; walking away
from a fight is more honorable) and notes recognizable *yakuza* markers: a cheek-slashing hand
gesture, punch-perm hair, tattoos, a duck-like gait, and the diminutive nickname *yat-chan*. The
phrase *Zakennayo!* itself is given as a (risky) recommended fighting word.

## Chapter VIII — Disco City (pp. 76-87)

### Roppongi (intro, p. 77)

Roppongi is introduced as the "designated" foreigner nightlife district near the former U.S.
military compound and embassy, contrasted with more "Japanese"-oriented districts (Akasaka,
Ginza, Kabuki-cho) whose nightlife the author frames as catering to Japanese men's needs rather
than foreigners. No new vocabulary items on this page beyond the chapter title itself.
