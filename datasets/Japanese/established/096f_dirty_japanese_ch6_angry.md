## Grammar points

### Dragged-vowel / contracted rough-register verb endings

Multiple entries in this chapter (uzē/uzendayo, ussendayo, shitsukendayo, ki ni kuwane, tsukaenē)
show a consistent sociolinguistic pattern: standard negative/emphatic verb forms (urusai →
"usee"/"ussee"; shitsukoi → "shitsukee"; kuwanai → "kuwanē"; tsukaenai → "tsukaenē") get their
final vowel lengthened/dragged and fronted, marking rough, masculine, confrontational speech —
the Japanese equivalent of an English speaker drawling an insult. This is a productive register
marker across the whole "angry/confrontational" register, not a one-off feature of any single
word.

### "Maji ___" intensifier + abbreviation productivity

"Maji" (seriously/for real) functions as a general-purpose slang intensifier (maji mukatsuku,
maji de sutōka, maji kirai-equivalent constructions). The chapter's "MM" gloss for "maji
mukatsukū" demonstrates that this intensifier+adjective combination is frequent enough in speech
to spawn its own alphabetic shorthand/abbreviation, a notable slang-compression mechanism worth
tracking alongside more typical phonological clippings.
