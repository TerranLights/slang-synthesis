# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Kanji in Context — A Study System for Intermediate and Advanced Learners (Reference Book)

**Source:** Koichi Nishiguchi & Tamaki Kono (Inter-University Center for Japanese Language Studies,
Yokohama), *Kanji in Context — A Study System for Intermediate and Advanced Learners*, The Japan
Times, Ltd., 1994 (preface dated September 1994). This PDF is the **reference book** half of the
two-part system (reference book + workbook); it is a 163-page scan with no text layer, condensing
the printed book's pages 2 (front matter)/4 (preface) through 277 at two printed pages per PDF page.
The reference book entries run from kanji entry #1 through at least entry #1947 (the printed page
reached in this scan), organized into six "水準" (proficiency levels, 第1〜第6水準) by frequency/
difficulty, followed by a final "特殊" (special) section for kanji with non-standard/limited usage
(numeral-substitute characters, unit-of-measure characters, etc.). Per the preface, the full
reference book presents roughly 9,000 vocabulary items (covering most of the JLPT Level 1 kanji
vocabulary) built on an inventory of kanji ordered by component/phonetic-family structure rather than
by raw frequency alone.

**Coverage note — representative sampling (oversized flat dictionary-style reference).** This is a
flat, entry-numbered dictionary of kanji + compound vocabulary (not a lesson-structured textbook),
confirmed via inspection of the table-of-contents/structure pages (pp. 1-33 of the PDF) and
cross-checked against the back of the book. Per this project's standing practice for oversized
dictionary-style references (applied to several sibling Wave 1 sources), **full exhaustive
extraction of all ~1,947+ entries / ~9,000 vocabulary items was not attempted.** Instead, 12
page-spreads were sampled at roughly even intervals across the entire entry range (1-9, 10-19,
94-106, 157-168, 281-291, 389-399, 517-528, 792-804, 1083-1096, 1374-1391, 1614-1629, 1900-1947),
deliberately spanning all six proficiency levels (第1〜第6水準) plus the terminal 特殊 (special-use)
section, so that early/common kanji, mid-frequency kanji, and rare/specialized kanji are all
represented. Every headword kanji entry that fell on a sampled page-spread is captured here, each
with its on'yomi/kun'yomi readings and 2-4 representative compound vocabulary items (the "基本語例"
and following illustrative compounds); the remaining compound vocabulary on each sampled page
(typically 3-8 more items per entry) was not transcribed, consistent with sampling rather than
exhaustive transcription. No handwritten marginalia was found on any sampled page — all content
inspected was clean typeset print.

**Vision-reading note.** No text layer at all (confirmed via `pdftotext`, zero output); every sampled
page was read as a rendered image. Print quality is good (clean typeset, no scanner noise or
font-substitution cipher of the kind seen in other languages' sources); all readings below are
transcribed with high confidence directly from the printed `▶`(on'yomi)/`▷`(kun'yomi) markers and
printed compound-word blocks.

## Grammar points

This source is a kanji-and-vocabulary reference dictionary, not a grammar textbook — it contains no
explicit grammar-point explanations of the kind found in lesson-structured sources. The book's own
front matter (pp. 1-33 of the PDF, not individually extracted into the table above) instead explains:

### Kanji entry structure and the `▶`/`▷` reading-marker convention

Each entry presents a boxed kanji headword with its stroke count, followed by `▶` (on'yomi,
Chinese-derived reading, conventionally katakana) and `▷` (kun'yomi, native Japanese reading,
conventionally hiragana) markers, then a column of representative compound vocabulary with furigana
readings and English glosses. A small number entry cross-references another entry sharing a
semantic/phonetic component (printed as `cf.` plus the target kanji and its own entry number) — this
reflects the book's stated organizing principle (section 5 of the front matter, p.10): kanji are
grouped first by shared phonetic/semantic component ("字形要素"), and only secondarily by raw
frequency, with six increasingly specialized "水準" (proficiency levels) plus a final "特殊"
(special-use) section for kanji with narrow/limited modern usage (numeral-substitute characters used
on legal documents, now-obsolete traditional units of weight/area/capacity, etc.).

### Annotation marks (`◇`, `*`, `cf.`, `cv.`, `vt.`/`vi.`, antonym box)

The front matter's legend (pp. 27-29 of the PDF) defines several per-entry annotation marks seen
throughout the table above: `◇` marks a vocabulary item considered important but containing a
not-yet-studied kanji or reading; `*` marks a reading not recognized as a standard Jōyō-kanji
reading; `cv.` marks a compound noun usable as a verb via する; `vt.`/`vi.` disambiguates transitive
vs. intransitive sense where the English gloss alone would not; `cf.` marks a usage/related-vocabulary
note (including the component-component kanji cross-references used throughout this table's Notes
column); `abbr.` marks a vocabulary item that is itself an abbreviation of a longer compound; and a
boxed-antonym mark denotes an explicit antonym pairing (e.g. 主観的な vs. 客観的な; 日の出 vs. 日の入り).

### Workbook cross-reference numbering

Each reference-book entry carries a small circled lesson number (e.g. ③, ⑮, ㉗) cross-referencing the
corresponding lesson in the companion workbook volume (not part of this PDF) — visible in the Notes
column context above as the small circled numbers on sampled pages, not transcribed per-entry here
since the workbook itself was not supplied as a source.
