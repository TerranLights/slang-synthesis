## Grammar points

### 1. らしい — hearsay/inference, contrasted with ようだ
らしい attaches to the plain form and expresses an inference based on secondhand information or
circumstantial evidence — weaker/more detached than ようだ's direct-observation inference. The
lesson's dialogue grounds this contrast in a speaker's explicit dialect self-identification
("関西生まれの関西育ち," Kansai-born-and-raised), using やくざ as an example word while discussing
how regional background colors speech perception — a genuine explicit regional/dialectal
annotation. (p. 121)

### 2. ~すぎる
Verb-stem/adjective-stem + すぎる means "too much...": 食べすぎる「to overeat」. (p. 122)

### 3. Hearsay: そうです／って話です／って
Three near-equivalent hearsay markers are given on a politeness cline: そうです (neutral/formal),
って話です (slightly more conversational), and bare って (explicitly described in-text as "quite
common in informal speech"). (p. 122)

### 4. みたいです
Explicitly glossed in-text as "the colloquial form of ようだ" — the same inference-from-observation
meaning as Lesson 7 Note 2's ようです, but register-marked as casual: 好きみたい (= 好きなようだ). (p. 122)

### 5. Verb(plain, imperative-shortened)+な — rough imperative
A blunt imperative formed by adding な after a verb's plain form; the text notes this is "usually
used by men" — an explicit gendered-register tag on a whole construction, not just a lexical item.
(p. 122)

### 6. もの — spoken-language complaint/justification particle
Sentence-final もの, softening a complaint or justification, is explicitly noted as "usually used
by women" — the gendered counterpart to Note 5's rough-imperative な. (p. 123)

### 7. ~だけです
"Only/just...", limiting scope to the stated item. (p. 123)

### 8. ~かどうか
"Whether or not...", embedding a yes/no question as a clause: 彼が来るかどうかわかりません「I don't
know whether he'll come.」(p. 123)
