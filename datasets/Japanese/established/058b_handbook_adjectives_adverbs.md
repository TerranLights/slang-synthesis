## Grammar points

### Part II.1 — Noun Modifiers: Adj + N (pp. 35–39)
The simplest adjectival construction: an i- or na-adjective directly precedes the noun it modifies
(*akai hana*, "red flower"; *nigiyakana machi*, "bustling city"). Two or more adjectives may stack to
modify one noun, just as in English. *Ichiban* placed before an adjective forms its superlative
("the most ~"), e.g. *ichiban wakai hito*, "the youngest person."

### Part II.2 — Adjectival Predicates: full present/past/negative paradigm in two speech styles (pp. 38–39)
Both tenses (present, past) and their negatives are given for an i-adjective (*takai*) and a
na-adjective (*kireida*) in both plain and polite registers. Negative na-adjective predicates may use
either *de wa nai* or the more colloquial *ja nai* — the book explicitly flags *ja* as more colloquial
than *de wa*.

### Part II.2, sentence pattern 2.1 — N wa/ga + Adj pred: topic vs. subject marking (p. 39)
The noun a predicate adjective describes is marked by *wa* when functioning as the sentence topic, or
by *ga* when functioning as the grammatical subject; the topic is frequently also the subject.

### Part II.3 — Expressions Following Adjectival Predicates (pp. 45–71): a long catalog of boxed constructions
This section catalogs numbered sentence patterns (3.1 through at least 3.22) in which a conjugated
adjectival predicate combines with an auxiliary, particle, or conjunction. Patterns sampled directly:
- **3.1 Adj pred + darō/deshō** ("probably") — expresses the speaker's conjecture; *deshō* is the
  polite equivalent of *darō*, the presumptive form of the copula *da*.
- **3.7 Adj pred + dake da** ("only, just, that's all") — a restrictive-focus construction; *da*
  shifts to *na* before *dake* when attached to a na-adjective (*majimena dake da*).
- **3.12 Adj pred + sō da** ("I heard that ~") — reported-speech/hearsay, distinct in both form and
  attachment point from the appearance-conjecture *sō da* of pattern 4.5 (see below): this one
  attaches to the *full* adjectival predicate (present/past inflected form), not the bare stem.
- **3.17 Adj pred + uchi ni** ("while") — marks a period during which a state holds; the adjective
  before *uchi ni* is always in the present tense regardless of the main clause's own tense.
- **3.18 Adj pred + to** ("if") — a conditional particle specifically for conditions whose result is
  uncontrollable or unavoidable; again, the clause before *to* must stay present tense regardless of
  the main clause's tense.
- A separate sentence-final particle, **kashira** ("I wonder if"), likewise follows an adjectival
  predicate (p. 56) to express the speaker's uncertainty/musing — distinct from the darō/deshō
  conjecture pattern in that kashira marks a question posed to oneself rather than a probability
  claim.

### Part II.4 — Auxiliary Adjectives in use (pp. 71–77)
- **4.1 Adj pred + kamoshirenai** ("might") — attaches to the full adjectival predicate; *da* drops
  before it for na-adjectives.
- **4.5 Adj stem + sō da** ("look, look like") — the conjectural-from-appearance auxiliary (first
  introduced in Part I.3) attaches instead to the bare *stem* of an adjective, and its prenominal form
  *sōna* can modify a following noun directly (*taka sōna kuruma*, "an expensive-looking car"). The
  book notes an irregularity: *ii* (good) and negative *nai* become *yosa* and *nasa* respectively
  before *sō da* (e.g. *yosa sō da*, "looks good"), paralleling the irregular noun-form behavior of
  *ii/yoi* already seen in Part I.

### Part II.5 — The Conjunctive (ku) and Te forms as clause-linkers (pp. 86–87)
The *ku*-form of an i-adjective (5.1) and the *te*-form of an i- or na-adjective (5.2) both link
adjectives or clauses meaning "and." With the *te* form specifically, the state named by the first
adjective is often implicitly the *reason* for the state or action that follows (e.g. *Yasuko wa
sunao de, kawaikute, mina ni aisareta*, "Yasuko was obedient and cute, and [so] was loved by
everyone").

### Part II.6 — The Adverbial Form modifying verbs (p. 97)
An i-adjective's adverbial form is made by changing final *-i* to *-ku*; a na-adjective's is made by
adding *ni* to the stem. Either modifies a following verb, another adjective, or another adverb (e.g.
*hayaku okiru*, "get up early"; *shinchō ni okonau*, "act prudently").

### Part II.7 — The Conditional (ba/nara) and Tara forms, plus a comparative pattern (pp. 102–103)
For i-adjectives (including auxiliaries and negative *nai*), the conditional is formed with *-kereba*
on the stem; for na-adjectives, with *nara(ba)* or *de areba*. The more colloquial *tara*-form adds
*-kattara* (i-adjective) or *dattara* (na-adjective) to the stem. A further pattern, **7.2 Adj cond ~
hodo/dake ~** ("the more ~, the more ~"), pairs the conditional form of an adjective with its
plain/na-adjective form plus *hodo* or *dake* to build a proportional-comparison construction (e.g.
*kuwashikereba kuwashii hodo*, "the more detailed [it is], the more ~").

### Part II.8 — The Tari form: alternating/indefinite states (pp. 108–111)
**8.1 Adj tari + Adj tari suru** ("sometimes ~ and sometimes ~") repeats the *tari* form of an i- or
na-adjective (*-kattari* / *dattari*) followed by *suru*, expressing an unordered alternation between
two or more states (e.g. wrapping paper that is "sometimes red, sometimes white, sometimes blue"
across a list of three colors, not just two). **8.2 Adj tari + (Adj) nakattari suru** ("sometimes ~
and sometimes not ~") instead pairs an adjective's affirmative *tari* form with its own *negative*
*tari* form plus *suru*, marking a toggle between a state and its absence rather than between two
distinct named states.

### Part II.9 — Noun Forms: productive -sa and limited -mi suffixes (p. 111)
Any i- or na-adjective stem can take the suffix *-sa* to form an abstract noun ("-ness"), e.g.
*shirosa* ("whiteness"), *rippasa* ("fineness"). A second suffix, *-mi*, exists but is noted as usable
only with a limited, closed set of i-adjectives (not enumerated on the sampled page) — i.e. *-sa* is
the general/productive strategy and *-mi* a lexically restricted alternative.

### Part II.10 — Polite Forms via o-/go- prefixing (p. 115)
Prefixing *o-* or *go-* onto an adjective expresses politeness. As a rule of thumb, *o-* attaches to
Japanese-origin words (covering all i-adjectives and some na-adjectives) and *go-* to Chinese-origin
na-adjectives, though the book notes some Chinese-origin na-adjectives irregularly take *o-* instead
of the expected *go-* (exact irregular items not enumerated on the sampled page).
