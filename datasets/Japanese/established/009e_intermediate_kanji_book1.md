## Grammar points

### Not every kanji has both an on- and a kun-reading — Lesson 8 §①
The book states this as a foundational caution against assuming all kanji are fully bidirectional:
40 常用漢字 characters have no on-reading at all (貝,株,届ける, etc.), with 国字 (kanji invented in
Japan rather than borrowed from Chinese, e.g. 畑,峠,込む) being the main subclass — of these, only
働く(どう) is noted as an exception that DOES carry an on-reading. Conversely, 736 常用漢字 have no
kun-reading at all (医,演,械,感,希,職, etc.) — these must be learned purely by their on-reading
compounds since no native-word gloss exists for them.

### Kanji with multiple on-readings can carry distinct, unrelated meanings per reading — Lesson 8 §③a
楽 splits ガク("music": 音楽,楽器) from ラク("comfort/amusement": 楽な,楽園); 率 splits ソツ("to
lead": 引率する,率先する) from リツ("ratio": 進学率,百分率); 象 splits ショウ("image/symbol":
現象,対象) from ゾウ("elephant": 象,象牙); 画 splits ガ("picture/painting": 画家,画面,映画) from
カク("a drawn stroke/line": 計画する,企画する). The book frames multiple on-readings as a historical
layering effect — different on-readings for the same kanji were borrowed from Chinese at different
historical periods/regions (漢音,呉音,唐音), which is why they can diverge semantically rather than
being free variants of one meaning.

### Some on-readings are restricted to one or a few fixed words — Lesson 8 §③b
A second class of multi-on-reading kanji has one reading that is fully productive and another that
survives only in a small closed set of fixed compounds: 去 is usually キョ(去年,死去する,退去する)
but コ survives only in 過去; 平 is usually ヘイ(平均する,平和な,公平な) but ビョウ survives only in
平等な; 盛 is usually セイ(全盛,盛大な,盛況な) but ジョウ survives only in 繁盛する; 留 is usually
リュウ(留学する,残留する,留意する) but ル survives only in 留守; 神 is usually シン(精神,神経,神話)
but ジン survives in a shrine-related subset (神社,神宮,天神); 望 is usually ボウ(希望する,願望,有望
な) but モウ survives in 本望,所望する; 相 is usually ソウ(相談する,相当する,手相) but ショウ is
restricted to the specific "〜相 = cabinet minister" set (首相,外相,文相).

### Some single kanji function as whole words despite having only an on-reading — Lesson 8 §④
Normally a kanji used alone as a word takes its kun-reading (動く,車), but a residual class of
on-reading-only kanji are nonetheless used as standalone words or as the stem of a 〜する/〜じる
verb: 駅(station),肉(meat),役(role, read as kun エキ/ヤク per the book's own classification quirk)
are given as nominal examples, and 愛する,演じる,熟する,感じる,禁じる as verbal examples.

### On-reading phonology: Sino-Japanese syllables never exceed two morae, predicting kun vs. on at a glance — Lesson 8 §⑤
A single kanji's on-reading is always one or two morae (never three or more), and when two morae,
the second mora is restricted to one of exactly seven possibilities: イ(愛,英,会,係,再), ウ(王,求,
交,層,周), キ(駅,激,式,的,歴), ク(悪,育,作,服,役), チ(一,七,八,吉,日), ツ(決,雑,室,接,熱), or ン
(安,温,官,神,戦). This gives a reliable heuristic: if a single kanji's reading is three or more
morae (e.g. 頭=あたま), it MUST be a kun-reading, since on-readings are categorically barred from
reaching that length. Kun-readings, by contrast, are usually two or more morae, with genuinely
one-mora kun-readings being rare (子,手,火,尾,血,値,歯,野,家 are given as the rare exceptions).

### Pitch accent/reading choice can shift a word's register, not just its meaning — Lesson 8 §⑥
Beyond meaning-distinguishing reading pairs (生物=セイブツ"organism" vs なまもの"perishable food";
色紙=シキシ"autograph card" vs いろがみ"origami paper"; 初日=ショニチ"opening day" vs はつひ"New
Year's sunrise"; 風車=フウシャ"windmill" vs かざぐるま"pinwheel toy"; 上手=ジョウズ"skillful" vs
うわて"upper hand" vs かみて"stage right," explicitly contrasted with 下手=しもて"stage left"; 市場=
シジョウ"economic market" vs いちば"physical marketplace"), the book gives three pairs where the
reading choice shifts connotational *register/image* rather than denotation: 工場(コウジョウ, a
large modern machine-based factory) vs 工場(こうば, a small town workshop with 2-3 workers); 立食
(リッショク, a formal hotel buffet party) vs 立食(たちぐい, a casual station stand-up noodle stall);
and 明日, which has three competing readings (ミョウニチ,あした,あす) without the book assigning
them a further functional split beyond free variation.
