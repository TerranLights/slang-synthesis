## Grammar points

### Wasei eigo as a systematic word-formation category, not scattered individual slang
The chapter establishes wasei eigo (English made in Japan) as a productive, rule-governed lexical
category — most visibly the -kon suffix (clipping any English com-/con- word: mazakon, rorikon,
pasokon, eakon, zenekon) and the systematic verbing of English nouns into -ru verbs (daburu, toraburu,
ajiru, panikuru, saboru). These can cause real miscommunication, unlike purely decorative English
text elsewhere in Japanese signage/products. p.93-103.

### Dutch-, German-, French-, Russian-, Korean-, and Portuguese-origin "hidden" loanwords
Because Nagasaki's Dutch trading post was Japan's only official European contact point during the
Edo-period isolation policy (sakoku), several basic everyday words (biiru, koohii, koppu, garasu)
are Dutch, not English, despite resembling English cognates; other loanwords entered via German
(meruhen, gaaze, gerende, gebaruto), French (abekku, saboru), Russian (ikura), Korean (noppo), and
Portuguese (juban, tenpura) — many assumed by ordinary Japanese speakers to be native words. p.102-103.

### Japanese-origin words that entered English (reverse-direction loanwords)
Distinguished from the older, fully assimilated set (sake, kimono, jujitsu, sumo, geisha, ukiyo-e,
"more than a century" in English dictionaries) are newer business/pop-culture terms (keiretsu,
kaizen, zaibatsu, tankan, Gojira/Godzilla, anime, Pokemon) that entered English more recently,
sometimes (kaizen) as a re-import of an originally American concept. p.104-105.

### Ten Meiji-era coined/calqued abstract concept words
Shakai, kojin, kindai, bi, ren'ai, sonzai, shizen, kenri, jiyuu, kare/kanojo were all 19th-century
coinages translating European concepts with no prior Japanese or Chinese equivalent; shakai and kojin
are specifically credited to educator/translator Yukichi Fukuzawa, working partly from English-
Chinese missionary dictionaries. Some of these words took decades to settle into their modern sense
(shakai originally had to cover senses as different as "government" and "human intercourse"). p.108.
