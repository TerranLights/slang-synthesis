## Grammar points

### Lesson 22 (アルバイト — "Part-Time Job"), pp. 42–51
Goals: using potential form to describe ability/circumstance; stating whether one has done something before.
Opening sociopragmatic framing note (before 文型1): directly stating or asking one's own/others' ability ("私は日本語が話せます"/"あなたは英語が話せますか") is rare in natural Japanese and can read as impolite/presumptuous; the lesson instead builds potential-form practice around the natural context of job-hunting (reading want-ads, phoning to inquire) specifically to avoid that pragmatic trap.
1. **可能形 (potential form)** — covers ability (中国語が話せます) and situational possibility (図書館で新聞が読めます), with this lesson focusing on the ability sense. Particle shift: を→が (or を→は for contrast, 自転車には乗れません). Explicit ら抜き言葉 (ra-nuki) note: colloquial contracted potential forms for Group 2/3 verbs (食べれる, 起きれる, 来れる) are increasingly common in casual speech, but the textbook standardizes on the full canonical forms (食べられる, 起きられる, 来られる) for teaching purposes — a real, named sociolinguistic variation the book consciously declines to teach as the default. List of verbs that cannot take potential form because they inherently already carry a possibility/change meaning: わかる、知る、間に合う、治る、慣れる、増える、減る、上手になる.
2. **〜ことがあります (experience)** — "have done X before." Contrasted with simple past (朝ごはんを食べましたか vs. 食べたことがありますか). Negative-answer particle note: in direct answer to a question, negative form takes は (行ったことはありません), but が is used when not directly answering a yes/no question — textbook defaults to が as the more common form. Common learner error flagged: ×〜したことがありました (wrong tense) vs. correct 〜したことがあります.
3. **〜なら (topic-taking conditional)** — two illustrated uses: (1) responding to a counter-proposal by narrowing which alternative works (土曜日はちょっと…日曜日なら行けるんですが), (2) answering a question by specifying which part of it applies (日本語が読めますか → ひらがなとカタカナなら読めます). The "動詞現在形+なら" conditional sense is deferred to L31.
4. **〜ば (conditional)** — covers only general-truth/necessary-consequence conditionals (春が来れば花が咲く type); the "hypothetical assumption + judgment" sense is deferred to L35. Explicit learner-confusion warning: the ば-form of a verb can resemble its potential form (行けば vs. 行ける), leading some learners to mistakenly read in a potential meaning. と vs. ば is distinguished pedagogically: と states what naturally follows from a premise; ば asks what condition is needed to bring about a result — both usable for general-truth statements and largely interchangeable in that register, but pedagogically framed differently.
5. **頻度/割合 (how often — どのくらい)** — asking and answering frequency (1週間にどのくらい) and also quantity (どのくらい泳げますか → 1,000メートルぐらい泳げます／ぜんぜん泳げないんです), the latter answered with either a concrete number or an adverb like あまり/ぜんぜん.
6. **〜ことができる** — alternate "can do" construction from 可能形, textbook specifically assigns this form to describing possibility arising from rules/regulations (アルバイトの方も使うことができます) rather than personal ability; negative is 〜ことはできません.

### Lesson 23 (ワンさんへのプレゼント — "A Present for Wang"), pp. 52–59
Goals: describing an impression from outward appearance using そう; conveying a third party's wishes/desires; appropriate gift-giving/receiving expressions.
1. **い形容詞+そうです (appearance)** — e.g. 涼しそうです. Explicitly distinguished from the hearsay そうです taught at L17 (different connection form to the adjective stem). いい is flagged as irregularly inflecting (よさそうです, not いいそうです — implied by the "activation is irregular" warning, consistent with standard Japanese grammar). A directly-observable property (e.g. 黒い color judged just by looking) cannot take this そう — ×黒そうです is flagged as wrong, since そう specifically marks an *inferred* impression, not a directly-seen fact.
2. **な形容詞+そうです (appearance)** — e.g. 便利そうです／便利じゃなさそうです (or ではなさそうです). Same そう/伝聞-そう distinction warning as 文型1; same "(誤)きれいそうです" pitfall flagged (きれい being a な-adjective needing な inserted before そう).
3. **動詞+そうです (appearance, "looks like it's about to")** — e.g. 雨が降りそうです. Textbook covers only the affirmative form here (negative is rare and would awkwardly become 〜そうにありません, deferred without drilling). The related "そう" indicating self-assessed feasibility (間に合いそうです) is deferred to L35.
4. **こんな／そんな／あんな (+ noun)** — demonstrative-adjective forms, governed by the same near/far/addressee-proximity (こ・そ・あ) rule taught at L2 for これ/それ/あれ. Scope explicitly narrowed: only the plain descriptive contrast between これ/それ/あれ and こんな/そんな/あんな is taught; special-nuance uses (そんな簡単なことは… "something as simple as that," こんなにすばらしいものを… exclamatory) and discourse-anaphoric (文脈指示) uses of these forms are out of scope for this book.
5. **ほしい (first-person desire for an object)** — treated as synonymous with ほしいと思っている in this book. Tense distinction flagged: present-tense ほしいんです = not yet obtained; past-tense ほしかったんです/ほしいと思っていたんです = now obtained (e.g. describing a gift one has just received) — explicitly the natural form to use when thanking someone for a gift. Object restriction: ほしい only takes physically-obtainable objects — ×あの先生の授業がほしい and ×貿易関係の仕事がほしい are flagged as ungrammatical/unnatural. Pragmatic warning: directly asking another person 何がほしいですか／ワンピースがほしいですか can sound rude or childish; the book avoids teaching this as a go-to question form.
6. **何か〜物／どこか〜所／誰か〜人／いつか〜時 (indefinite-quantifier + noun combinations)** — builds on か-compound interrogatives from L7. Distinguishes 何か + noun (non-interrogative "some [noun]," functioning just like a plain noun — 何か冷たい物が飲みたいです ≈ ジュースが飲みたいです) from the interrogative 何が (asking specifically what).
7. **〜ほしがっていた (third-person desire, object)** — marks that a third party wants/wanted an object; explicitly restricted in this book to the past/continuing-past form ほしがっていた even though the present ほしがっている is also valid and near-equivalent (simplification to avoid overloading beginners). Alternative near-equivalent construction 〜がほしいと言っていた also introduced as a recognizable paraphrase. Core underlying rule (implicit throughout, not named as a single rule but demonstrated via contrast): first-person desire predicates (ほしい/〜たい) cannot directly describe a third party's desire in plain form — third-person desire requires the 〜がる morphological marker (ほしがる/〜たがる).
8. **〜たがっていた (third-person desire, action)** — parallel to 文型7 but for verbal desire (〜たい, taught L11, becomes 〜たがっていた for third person). Same restriction to past/continuing-past form only. Particle-shift note paired with examples: first-person コーヒーを/が飲みたい alternates freely between を and が, but third-person コーヒーを飲みたがっていた takes を obligatorily (が is not used with 〜たがる).
