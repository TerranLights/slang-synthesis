## Grammar points

### False-friend and trap word classes

Three specific traps are cataloged: (1) words ending in -ei (きれい, しつれい, ゆうめい) that are な
adjectives despite the い ending, easily mistaken for い adjectives; (2) おおきな/ちいさな,
attributive-only variant forms of おおきい/ちいさい that look like な adjectives (ending in な) but
cannot take full な-adjective conjugation — they only ever precede a noun directly; (3) さまざま/
いろいろ, な adjectives describing categories/types rather than qualities, which do not follow the
usual contrastive-joining pattern other な adjectives use when two qualities are set against each
other with が.

### The ~がる derivation: converting first-person emotion adjectives for third-person use

A closed set of い-adjectives (うれしい, かなしい, さびしい, いたい, こわい, おそろしい, なつかしい,
くるしい, ほしい) is explicitly restricted by the source to expressing the speaker's or writer's own
feelings — using one of these directly about a third party is markedly odd. Third-person emotion
is instead expressed either by adding a hearsay/appearance marker (そうだ, らしい, そう) or by
deriving a verb with the suffix ~がる (e.g. こわがる "to show fear," いやがる "to be
reluctant/loathe," from the な-adjective いや). ~がる-derived verbs conjugate as ごだん verbs and are
most often found in the ~て+いる form describing an observed current state (子供が犬を怖がっている).

### Polarity-sensitive adverbs: a systematic inventory

The chapter tabulates a closed, named set of adverbs whose core meaning systematically differs
depending on whether the predicate is positive or negative (あまり, いっさい/一切, ぜんぜん/全然,
どうも, とても, なかなか, べつに/別に, ほとんど, まだ, もう, しばらく) — distinct from the separate
lists of adverbs that can *only* occur with a negative predicate (かならずしも, さっぱり, ぜんぜん,
にどと, めったに, ろくに) or *only* with a positive one (かならず, ぎりぎり). Presenting all three
lists together as a single polarity-governed system, rather than as scattered individual-word usage
notes, is this book's own distinctive organizational contribution. ぜんぜん is separately flagged as
able to appear with a positive predicate specifically in colloquial speech (全然大丈夫だ, "it's
perfectly OK") — an explicit register-shift note, not the book's general-register rule for the word.

### Noun-formation morphology

Three distinct noun-formation mechanisms are cataloged: derivational suffixation of an adjective
stem with ~さ (broadly productive, abstract quality/degree: おおきさ, おもさ, ひろさ, かなしさ) or
the narrower ~み (more subjective/felt quality, a smaller set: あまみ, くるしみ); direct
nominalization of a verb's bare conjunctive (pre-ます) stem with no suffix at all (かえり, むき,
つづき, おわり, このみ, ちぢみ); and "plural by duplication," a closed set of nouns (ひとびと,
しまじま, ところどころ, たびたび) formed by repeating the singular noun (written with the 々 iteration
mark), which the source explicitly distinguishes from the productive ~たち/~ら plural suffixes
(~ら flagged as "impolite and best avoided except in かれら").
