## Grammar points

### Copula desu/da and the question particle ka (Units 1–2, pp. 5–12)

The polite copula *desu* ("is/am/are") attaches to nouns and na-adjectives, e.g. *Gakusei desu*
("[I] am a student"). Adding sentence-final *ka* turns a statement into a yes/no question without
any word-order change: *Gakusei desu ka* ("Are [you] a student?"). The plain/informal counterpart is
*da*, used in casual speech and before conjunctions like *kara* (p. 59: *Kyō wa doyōbi da kara...*).

### Particle *no* as possessive/attributive (Unit 1)

*X no Y* means "Y belonging to/of X" — e.g. *sensei no hon* ("the teacher's book"). The same particle
links a modifying noun to a head noun generally, not just for possession.

### Negation: *iie*, *chigaimasu*, and adjective/verb negatives (Units 1, 6–7)

*Iie* negates a yes/no question generally ("no"). *Chigaimasu* specifically negates an identity
statement ("that's wrong/different") rather than a simple fact. Polite present-tense verb negation
uses *-masen* (e.g. *arimasen* "there isn't/doesn't have"); *-i* adjectives negate with *-ku
arimasen* (e.g. *takaku arimasen* "isn't expensive"); *na*-adjectives/nouns negate with *ja arimasen*.

### Time expressions: *-ji* and *han* (Unit 2)

Clock time is expressed with the counter *-ji* ("o'clock") attached to a number, and *han* ("half")
added for the half-hour: *hachi-ji han* = "8:30." *Kara...made* express a time range ("from...until").

### Existence verbs *arimasu* vs. *imasu* (Unit 3)

Japanese has two distinct "to exist/have" verbs depending on animacy: *arimasu* for inanimate objects
(*Hon ga arimasu*, "There is a book"), *imasu* for animate beings (*Neko ga imasu*, "There is a cat").
Location is marked with *ni*: *Rokkā no naka ni neko ga imasu* ("There's a cat inside the locker").

### Requests with *-te kudasai* (Unit 10)

The *-te* form of a verb plus *kudasai* forms a polite request/instruction: *Migi ni magatte kudasai*
("Please turn right"). Giving directions in this workbook consistently uses this pattern (*massugu
itte kudasai*, "please go straight," *mado o shimete kudasai*, "please close the window").

### Progressive/stative *-te imasu* (Unit 11)

The *-te* form plus *imasu* expresses an ongoing action ("is doing") or, for certain verbs
(*kiru*, *kaburu*, *haku*, *motsu* — all "wear/carry" verbs), a resulting state ("is wearing/carrying"):
*Kuroi jaketto o kite imasu* ("[He] is wearing a black jacket"). The same construction describes
weather in progress: *Ame ga futte imasu* ("It's raining").

### Comparatives with *yori* and *no hō ga* (Unit 13)

Comparison between two items uses *X yori Y no hō ga [adjective]* — "Y is more [adjective] than X."
E.g. *Wada san no hō ga se ga takai desu* ("Wada-san is taller"). *Yori* alone attaches directly to
the noun being compared against (p. 99: *Wada san yori*, "than Wada-san").

### Potential/ability with *koto ga dekimasu* (Unit 15)

Ability or possibility is expressed periphrastically: *dictionary-form verb + koto ga dekimasu/
dekimasen*, e.g. *Kanji o yomu koto ga dekimasu* ("[I] can read kanji"), *Kanji o kaku koto ga
dekimasen* ("[I] can't write kanji").

### *n desu* (explanatory/softening copula) (Units 16–17)

Appending *n desu* (contracted from *no desu*; informal *n da*) to a clause frames a statement as an
explanation or elaboration of context already understood between speaker and listener, rather than a
bare fact — common in giving excuses or reasons (*Atama ga itai n desu*, "[the thing is,] my head
hurts"). Used pervasively in dialogue throughout Units 16–19.

### *-tai* desiderative suffix (Unit 16)

Attaching *-tai* to a verb's *-masu* stem expresses the speaker's own desire to do something:
*tabetai* ("want to eat"), *yametai* ("want to quit"). Distinct from *hoshii* (want a thing).

### Giving/receiving verbs: *agemasu*, *moraimasu*, *kuremasu* (Unit 22)

Japanese marks the direction of a gift/favor with three distinct verbs rather than one: *agemasu*
("give," speaker/in-group to someone else), *moraimasu* ("receive," someone gives to speaker/
in-group), *kuremasu* ("give," someone else gives to speaker/in-group — emphasizes the giver's
kindness toward the speaker). Direction cannot be freely substituted; e.g. *Chichi ga kashite
kuremashita* ("My father kindly lent [it to me]") vs. *Chichi ni kashite moraimashita* ("I had my
father lend [it to me]") carry different nuances of whose initiative/benefit is foregrounded.

### Conditional *-tara* (Unit 19)

The *-tara* suffix (attached to the past/*-ta* form stem) forms a conditional "if/when X, then Y":
*Samuku nattara, mado o akete mo ii desu* ("If it gets cold, you may open the window"). Also used for
sequential "after" readings depending on context.

### Obligation and prohibition: *-nakereba narimasen* / *-te wa ikemasen* (Unit 20)

*-Nakereba narimasen* (attached to the negative/*-nai* stem) expresses obligation: "must do X," e.g.
*Eigo o hanasanakereba narimasen* ("[You] must speak English"). *-Te wa ikemasen* expresses
prohibition: "must not do X."

### *-tari...-tari suru* (non-exhaustive listing of actions) (Unit 21)

Lists two or more representative (not exhaustive) actions using the *-tari* form of each verb
followed by *suru*: *Uchi de hon o yondari, terebi o mitari shimashita* ("At home, I did things like
reading and watching TV [among other things]").

### Hiragana and katakana syllabaries (Reading Corner, all units; summarized Grammar p.109)

Both 48-symbol syllabaries are introduced progressively across the Reading Corner sections (a-row in
Unit 1, ka-row in Unit 2, sa-row in Unit 3, ta-row in Unit 4 [implied], na-row in Unit 5, ha-row in
Unit 6, dakuten/handakuten voiced variants (ga/za/da/ba/pa rows) interleaved from Unit 2 onward, and
katakana introduced starting Unit 11 (first 10 characters) building toward full katakana plus
small-ya/yu/yo combinations and small-tsu gemination by Units 19–23). Kanji reading practice
(a handful of common characters: yama, ki, mura, hayashi, naka, ta/da, mori) appears from Unit 23.

### Day/week/date/adjective-conjugation reference tables (Grammar appendix, pp. 109–114)

The appendix consolidates: the full kana syllabary chart; a ~140-verb conjugation table (dictionary
→ -masu → -nai → -te forms); days of the week; the two parallel date-counting systems (ordinal dates
1st–31st, mostly irregular/suppletive for 1st–10th then regular -nichi suffixation); the full -i and
-na adjective conjugation paradigm (positive/negative/past-positive/past-negative, e.g. *atsui* →
*atsuku arimasen* → *atsukatta* → *atsuku arimasen deshita*); the Chinese-style cardinal number
series (ichi, ni, san...) versus the native Japanese 1–10 counting-objects series (hitotsu, futatsu,
mittsu...); counters for people, long/thin objects, minutes, and thin/flat objects; and the four-way
ko-so-a-do demonstrative paradigm (koko/soko/asoko/doko, kore/sore/are/dore, kono/sono/ano/dono,
konna/sonna/anna/donna, kochira/sochira/achira/dochira).
