## Grammar points

### Lesson 29 (お見舞い — "Hospital Visit"), pp. 103–110
Goals: understand hierarchical (上下) social relationships and use 待遇表現 (honorific-adjacent politeness expressions) appropriately; understand basic Japanese letter format.
Pedagogical note embedded in the lesson's opening scene-setting: the teacher-guide explicitly flags that the dialogue's teacher character (萩原) speaks in women's casual/informal register, and instructs the classroom teacher to substitute male-register forms when teaching male learners — an explicit acknowledgment that the book's own model dialogue is gender-marked and not meant as a gender-neutral default.
1. **くださる／いただく (honorific giving/receiving verbs, first exposure ahead of full keigo at L30)** — honorific-register counterparts of くれる (L25)/もらう (L24), used specifically when the giver is a social superior. 私/私は is usually dropped from these sentences. Scope explicitly narrowed to self↔superior or self↔third-party exchanges only — third-party-to-third-party honorific exchanges (課長は部長に映画の切符をいただいたそうです) are explicitly excluded from this lesson's drilling. Aside: さしあげる (humble あげる) introduced only as a recognition item, with the presumptuousness warning described in the Vocabulary table above.
2. **〜てくださる／〜ていただく (honorific benefactive actions)** — action-extension of 文型1, paralleling how L28's てくれる/てもらう extended L24/25's object-giving verbs. Unlike L28's てくれる drilling (which restricted the receiver to "私" to simplify particle practice), this lesson's examples are not restricted that way, since the giver is now fixed as a social superior regardless of receiver. Worked learner-error example: students addressing a superior directly must not combine a vocative name-plus-さん with the third-person-style くださってありがとうございました sentence frame in the same breath the way they might with a peer — flagged via a marked-incorrect model sentence (×萩原先生は、先日は辞書を貸してくださってありがとうございました, said to 萩原先生's own face) as unnatural self-contradictory register mixing. For direct superior-to-speaker favors, the book instead teaches humble-register self-action verbs (お送りします／お持ちします, full system at L30) as the appropriate substitute when the speaker — not the superior — is the one doing something.
3. **〜たところです (just now completed)** — marks that an action has JUST finished, explicitly NOT a locational use of ところ. Two illustrated discourse functions: (1) describing an unlucky narrow-miss timing mismatch ("I had literally just finished when—"), (2) describing a fortunate, well-timed completion. Cross-referenced against two other ところ-constructions taught later: 〜ているところ (mid-action, "in the process of") at L33, and 〜るところ (about to begin) at L35 — the three-way ところ system (about to / in the middle of / just finished) is being built up incrementally across lessons rather than taught all at once.
4. **〜ようにしている (habitual effort/intentional practice)** — marks a deliberately cultivated habit/ongoing effort (いろいろな人と日本語で話すようにしています). ために ("for the sake/purpose of," health-related example こ健康のために) appears here for the first time as an aside, with full treatment deferred to L33; only passive comprehension is expected at this point.
