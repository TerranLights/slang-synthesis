## Grammar points

### Register-paired counters recur as a structural pattern

This Part adds a third documented instance of the plain/polite counter pairing already noted for
kinship terms in Part 3: -niN (plain "people" counter) vs. -mesama (polite, used by hotel/service
staff addressing a guest, p.201). Together with age (-sai), days, and the kinship-term pairing,
this confirms register-paired lexical doubling is a pervasive, systematic feature of this book's
material rather than a one-off honorific quirk — directly useful as a cross-checked pattern for
later Phase 3 mechanics analysis of Japanese register systems.

### Dual native/Sino-Japanese vocabulary doubling beyond numerals

Breakfast/lunch/dinner (asa-gohaN/chōshoku etc., p.205) and receipt (ryōshūsho/reshīto, p.204)
both show a native-Japanese-term vs. Sino-Japanese-or-loanword-synonym doubling pattern distinct
from the plain/polite register pairing above — these are simply synonym pairs from different
lexical strata (native wago vs. Sino-Japanese kango vs. Western loanword gairaigo), not a
politeness distinction. Worth keeping the two doubling types (register-pair vs. lexical-stratum
synonym) terminologically separate in later mechanics analysis.
