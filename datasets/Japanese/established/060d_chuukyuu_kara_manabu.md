## Grammar points

### Lesson 14

#### 「〜ては〜」(repeated/cyclical action: "doing X, then Y, over and over")

Describes a repeated cycle of two actions, often implying habitual or ongoing behavior. Source's
own example: "彼はいろいろな所へ行っては写真を撮っています" ("He goes to various places and [each
time] takes photos") (p. 76, pattern A).

#### 「まさか〜はずがない」("surely it couldn't be the case that ~")

Expresses strong disbelief that something could be true, combining まさか ("surely not") with the
はずがない ("there's no way that...") construction. Source's own example: "あんな高いマンションがまさか
買えるはずがありませんよ" ("There's no way they could possibly afford such an expensive
condominium") (p. 76, pattern B).

#### 「〜たところ」("upon doing ~, [then]...")

Marks the point at which an action was carried out and what was discovered/happened as a result —
a simple past-tense discovery construction, distinct from the concessive "〜たところで" seen in
Lesson 16. Source's own example: "先生にうかがってみたところ、分からないという返事でした" ("When I asked
the teacher, the reply was that she didn't know") (p. 77, pattern C).

#### 「〜うちに」("while ~ [is still the case]")

Marks an action undertaken within a limited window before a state changes — urgency implied.
Source's own example: "何回も練習しているうちにだんだん分かってきました" ("As I practiced over and over,
I gradually came to understand") (p. 77, pattern D).

### Lesson 15

#### 「〜わけだ」/「〜というわけだ」("it means that ~" / "so that's ~")

Marks a logical conclusion or restatement of a situation's underlying reason/significance —
presents something as a natural, expected consequence rather than a surprising fact. Source's own
examples: "時間に追われたくないから、時計を持たなくなったわけです" ("It's because I don't want to be
chased by time that I stopped wearing a watch") and, for the というわけだ variant, "きのうはあまりいい
日ではなかったというわけですね" ("So yesterday wasn't a very good day, I take it") (p. 82, patterns
A-1/A-2).

#### 「〜ながら」(concessive "even though ~", contrary-to-expectation sense)

Distinct from ながら's more common simultaneous-action sense ("while doing"); here it marks a
contradiction between what was promised/known and what actually happened. Source's own example:
"来ると約束しておきながら、けい子さんは来ませんでした" ("Even though she'd promised to come, Keiko
didn't show up") (p. 83, pattern B).

#### 「〜しようと〜」("no matter what/who/how ~")

Volitional-form + と pattern expressing indifference to varying conditions — "regardless of ~."
Source's own example: "何年日本に住んでいようと分からないことがあります" ("No matter how many years
[someone] has lived in Japan, there are still things they don't understand") (p. 83, pattern C).

#### 「〜まい」("I will not ~" / "surely ~ will not")

A formal negative-volitional auxiliary, expressing the speaker's firm resolve not to do something,
or a strong guess that something won't happen. Source's own example: "もう二度とお酒は飲むまいと思った
のに、また飲んでしまいました" ("I resolved never to drink again, but I drank anyway") (p. 83, pattern
D).

### Lesson 16

#### 「〜ようでは」("if things are such that ~ [then a bad outcome follows]")

Marks a hypothetical-but-plausible negative state of affairs as grounds for a negative judgment or
consequence. Source's own example: "こんなことでいらいらするようでは、胃がいくつあっても足りない" ("If
you're going to get this irritated over things like this, you'll run out of stomachs [no matter
how many you had]") (p. 88, pattern A).

#### 「〜と、〜」(purposive "so that ~")

と following a volitional-form clause expresses the goal/intention behind an action. Source's own
example: "早く花がさくようにと、毎日水をやっている" ("[I] water it every day so that the flowers will
bloom soon") (p. 88, pattern B).

#### 「〜たところで」(concessive: "even if one were to ~, [it wouldn't matter]")

Distinct from Lesson 14's simple-discovery "〜たところ" — here the construction is concessive,
asserting futility regardless of the stated action. Source's own example: "どんなに頑張ったところで、
社長になれるわけではない" ("No matter how hard you try, it doesn't mean you'll become company
president") (p. 88, pattern C).

#### 「〜まい」(second occurrence, formal negative-volitional)

Reintroduced in this lesson with a new example distinct from Lesson 15's: "もう春だから、少し寒くなっ
ても、雪はふるまいと思う" ("Since it's already spring, even if it gets a bit colder, I doubt it'll
snow") (p. 89, pattern D) — illustrating the "strong negative guess" sense of まい specifically, as
opposed to Lesson 15's "firm resolve" sense.

### Lesson 17

#### 「〜通り〜」("just as ~", "in accordance with ~")

Marks that something matches a previously stated expectation, instruction, or pattern exactly.
Source's own example: "ええ、その通り、五千円です" ("Yes, exactly right, it's 5,000 yen") (p. 94,
pattern A).

#### 「〜た上で〜」("after doing ~ [then, on that basis]")

Marks a necessary prior step that establishes the basis for a subsequent action or judgment.
Source's own example: "よく付き合った上で、その人を判断するようにしている" ("I try to judge a person
only after getting to know them well") (p. 94, pattern B).

#### 「〜によって」(causal/agentive "by means of ~" / "depending on ~")

Covers both an agent/means-marking sense and a dependency sense ("varies according to ~"). Source's
own examples: "意見は人によって様々である" ("Opinions vary from person to person") and "車が増えること
によって、空気は今よりもっときたなくなるだろう" ("As cars increase, the air will likely get even dirtier
than now") (p. 95, patterns C-1/C-2).

### Lesson 18

#### 「〜べき」("should ~", "ought to ~")

A formal obligation/recommendation auxiliary attached to a verb's dictionary form, expressing what
is normatively correct to do. Source's own example: "勉強はもちろん、趣味をたくさん持つべきでしょうね"
("Of course studying matters, but [young people] should also have plenty of hobbies") (p. 100,
pattern A).

#### 「〜というのである」("the reason is that ~" / "it amounts to saying that ~")

Marks an explanatory restatement — presents the preceding clause as the underlying reasoning or
intention behind a described action. Source's own example (about buying a cookbook): "時々は自分でも
料理を作る/作ろう/作りたい/作るからというのである" (the stated reason is "because [one] sometimes wants
to/will/would like to cook for oneself") (p. 100, pattern B).

#### 「とてもV-(可能形)ない」("there's just no way one could possibly ~")

Combines とても with a verb's potential-negative form for emphatic impossibility. Source's own
example: "歯が痛くて、ごはんはとても食べられなかったので、コーヒーで済ませた" ("My teeth hurt so much
there was just no way I could eat, so I made do with coffee") (p. 101, pattern C).

#### 「〜にもかかわらず」("despite ~", "in spite of ~")

Marks a concessive contrast between a stated circumstance and an unexpected outcome. Source's own
example: "外の寒さにもかかわらず、こたつの暖かさは心を和やかにする" ("Despite the cold outside, the
warmth of the kotatsu puts the heart at ease") (p. 101, pattern D).

#### 「せめて」("at least")

Marks a minimal concession — the least one asks for, given that a fuller alternative isn't
possible. Source's own example: "自分は我慢しても、せめて子供たちには個室を与えようとしてきた" ("Even if
[the parents] had to endure it themselves, they tried at least to give their children private
rooms") (p. 101, pattern E).
