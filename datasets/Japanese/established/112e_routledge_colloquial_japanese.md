## Grammar points

### i-adjectives vs. na-adjectives
Japanese has two adjective classes. i-adjectives show an "i" sound immediately before desu (takai
desu); na-adjectives generally don't (shizuka desu, not *shizukai desu), with a handful of
exceptions that look like i-adjectives but behave as na-adjectives (yūmē, kirē, genki) — flagged
with asterisks in the source and spelled with a word-final -ei in hiragana (yuumei, kirei) even
though the romanized macron form looks like a long vowel. pp. 56-58.

### i-adjective inflection
i-adjectives inflect by altering the final -i: takai (is), takakunai (is not), takatta (was),
takakunakatta (was not) — "i" is dropped from the latter three forms before adding -kunai/-katta/
-kunakatta. The adjective ī "good" is fully irregular: ī desu/yokunai desu/yokatta desu/
yokunakatta desu. p. 57-58.

### na-adjective inflection
na-adjectives inflect via the copula instead of the adjective stem itself: shizuka desu (is quiet),
shizuka dewa arimasen (is not quiet), shizuka deshita (was quiet), shizuka dewa arimasendeshita
(was not quiet) — mirroring noun+desu inflection exactly. The book notes explicitly that **dewa**
contracts to **ja** in colloquial speech — a directly relevant formal/colloquial register-pair data
point for the slang-synthesis corpus. p. 58.

### Adjective sentence pattern and dō
Predicate-adjective sentences use X wa + adjective (+ desu for na-adjectives): Kono hon wa takai
desu "This book is expensive." The interrogative for "how (is it)?" is **dō** (Sono hon wa dō desu
ka). p. 58.

### na-adjective + noun
When a na-adjective modifies a following noun, desu is dropped and **na** is inserted between
adjective and noun (kirē na hon "a beautiful book"); tense/polarity is instead expressed at the
sentence's final copula. The interrogative for "what kind of [noun]" is **donna** (always followed
by a noun): Nihon wa donna kuni desu ka "What kind of country is Japan?" p. 62.

### Adverbs of degree
A five-point degree scale modifies adjectives: totemo (~100%, "very"), māmā (~50-70%, "quite/so-
so"), chotto (~30%, "a little"), amari (~10-20%, "not so/very," requires a negative predicate),
zenzen (0%, "not at all," requires a negative predicate). p. 60.

### Likes, dislikes, and ability: X wa Y ga + adjective
suki(na) "like", kirai(na) "dislike", jōzu(na) "good at", and heta(na) "poor at" are grammatically
adjectives (not verbs, unlike their English translations) and share one structure: X wa Y ga
[adjective] desu, where X is the experiencer and Y (marked by the subject particle **ga**, not the
direct-object o) is the target of the feeling or ability (Watashi wa Nihon no ongaku ga suki desu
"I like Japanese music"; Imai-san wa Ēgo ga totemo jōzu desu "Mrs Imai is very good at English").
p. 64.
