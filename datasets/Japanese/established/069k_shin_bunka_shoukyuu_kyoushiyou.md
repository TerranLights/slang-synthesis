## Grammar points

### Lesson 33 (工場見学 — "Factory Tour"), pp. 138–143
Goals: understand a simple spoken explanation of instant ramen's history and manufacturing process.
1. **受身形 (neutral/non-emotional passive, continuing from L32)** — this lesson completes the passive-voice system split first introduced at L32: whereas L32 taught the 感情を伴う受身 (emotion/adversity-bearing passive), this lesson teaches 感情を伴わない受身 (emotion-neutral passive), characterized by three co-occurring features: (1) the agent is unspecified/generic or unidentifiable, (2) no emotional coloring is present, (3) an inanimate object is the grammatical subject (インスタントラーメンは1958年に日本で初めて作られました). Of the three surface forms this passive can take (〜れます／〜れています／〜れました), the lesson deliberately restricts classroom drilling to only 〜れています (repeated/general processes) and 〜れました (a single past event), explicitly omitting 〜れます from active production practice even though it appears receptively in the dialogue (describing an in-progress factory process, あちらの機械でのばされます) — teacher told this is because setting up a believable "happening right now" scene is logistically hard in a classroom.
2. **〜よう／〜みたい as a simile-comparison device (extending L31)** — building on the inferential-judgment sense taught at L31, this lesson adds the construction's noun/adjective/verb-attaching simile form (紙のように薄い), plus a further extension: using an already-familiar object as a reference point to describe an unfamiliar one (テレビのようなもの…パソコンのディスプレイ). A specific teaching note flags that different languages conventionalize different comparison objects for the same concept (「小さい」can be compared to a matchbox, an ant, or a child depending on convention) and invites students to share their own language's idiomatic comparisons.
3. **〜ているところ (progressive-in-progress, first of a three-stage system)** — marks an action currently underway, functionally close to plain 〜ている but specifically drilled here in the "please wait, I'm in the middle of X" framing; explicitly flagged as the second node in a three-lesson aspectual-ところ paradigm that will be completed at L35 (〜たところ was already taught at L29; 〜るところ "about to start" is deferred to L35).
4. **〜は〜が〜 (object-as-topic construction)** — distinguished explicitly from the L15-taught "whole-part" 〜は〜が construction (この部屋は窓が大きいです, where が marks a sub-part of the は-marked topic): here instead the が-marked element is the AGENT of an action, and the は-marked element is itself the semantic OBJECT of that action, fronted as topic (検査は機械が自動的に行います — lit. "as for the inspection, the machine does it automatically"). Recommended drill scenario: a shared cooking task where different people are responsible for different sub-tasks (野菜は田中さんが買います。料理は私がします。).
5. **〜ために (purpose, formally contrasted against 〜ように)** — explicit systematic contrast given between the two purpose-marking constructions taught across different lessons: 〜ように (L27) requires a前件 outcome the subject CANNOT directly will into being by their own agency (道に迷わないように、あらかじめ場所を調べておきます — "not getting lost" isn't a directly controllable action); 〜ために requires a前件 that IS the subject's own willed action or intention (海外旅行をするために、貯金しています). Teachers are explicitly warned not to construct example sentences that could be misread as expressing the causal/原因 sense of ために (カップの安全性が問題になったために — "because of," not "in order to") when drilling the purpose sense.

### Lesson 34 (毎日家の手伝いをさせました. — "I had her help with housework every day"), pp. 144–150
Goals: understand a televised interview about a person's upbringing.
1. **〜ばかり (exclusive-preference/habitual-fixation)** — taught in its object-attached "preferentially/exclusively does X" sense (コーヒーばかり飲んでいる), explicitly distinguished from the superficially similar but differently-scoped 〜てばかりいる sense (used for verbs without a direct object, or to criticize a persistent, excessive habit) — see Vocabulary table for the full minimal-pair breakdown.
2. **使役形 (causative, "force/have someone do" sense only)** — this lesson restricts the causative to its coercive/imposed-obligation sense, explicitly deferring the permission-granting sense of the same morphology to L35. Two structural templates are taught: (1) AはBに［他動詞］〜せる/させる (transitive-verb causative, object marked を, causee marked に) and (2) AはBを［自動詞］〜せる/させる (intransitive-verb causative, causee marked を). A footnoted exception flags that some を-taking-location verbs shift the causee's particle from を to に when a location is independently specified in the sentence (選手に公園を走らせる vs. 選手を走らせる), though the textbook itself doesn't drill this exception. A second explicit error-avoidance note: for the自動詞/他動詞 pairs taught at L26, learners must not causativize the intransitive member when a transitive counterpart already exists for the same meaning (誤：お母さんは子供を起きさせる should instead be the plain transitive お母さんは子供を起こす). Classroom practice is explicitly steered toward asymmetric-power relationships (parent/child, teacher/student, coach/athlete) since the causative's natural pragmatic home is a clear authority differential.
3. **〜なくて／causal-reason て-form (extending L19's 〜て and L21's 〜ないで)** — teaches the causal/reason use of 〜て and 〜なくて for describing something difficult, worrying, or (per the book's own framing) even happy; existence/possession content obligatorily uses なくて rather than ないで (お金がなくて買えません), and a self-willed/changeable circumstance in the前件 is explicitly flagged as ungrammatical with this causal frame.
4. **〜たらどうですか (suggestion, confidence-graded against 〜てみたらどうですか)** — see Vocabulary table for the full confident-vs-tentative contrast and the explicit marked-wrong classroom examples the book supplies for both directions of the error. A closing register note: 〜たらどうですか is framed as a genuine suggestion (less presumptuous than 〜たほうがいいです, L21) but can still read as dismissive/careless depending on delivery, so teachers are told to watch tone in practice.

### Lesson 35 (お待たせしてすみませんでした. — "Sorry to keep you waiting"), pp. 150–157
Goals: apologize to someone while giving a reason; use the causative form to politely request permission.
1. **〜そうです／〜そうにありません (ability/likelihood judgment, four-way taxonomy)** — completes a cross-lesson system whose appearance-based cousin (い/な-adjective + そう, verb + そう for visual impressions) was taught at L23; here the morphologically identical construction instead attaches to a verb's potential form to judge REALIZABILITY rather than visual appearance (読めそうです vs 読めそうにありません), split across four conditions: own-ability-likely, situation-likely, own-ability-unlikely, situation-unlikely. A pragmatic politeness note: declining an offer with flat 〜られません is blunter than softening it with 〜られそうにありません, which the book explicitly recommends for refusals.
2. **〜ている (resultant-state, current vs. past reference point)** — extends the resultant-state sense first taught at L27 (where it specifically marked a problem/fault discovery, e.g. 汚れています) to a broader, affect-neutral resultant-state use (講演会はもう始まっていますか) and explicitly contrasts the present-reference reading against a past-reference reading of the identical morphology (私が来た時、安部さんはもう来ていました) — flagged as a genuine, recurring learner confusion point.
3. **〜ているところ／〜たところ／〜るところ (completing the three-stage aspectual-ところ system)** — this lesson supplies the final member (about-to-start) of a paradigm deliberately staged across three separate lessons (〜たところ at L29, 〜ているところ at L33, 〜るところ here) — see Vocabulary table. Practiced specifically in a conversational frame where the speaker uses their own just-about-to state to either accept an invitation enthusiastically or decline one gracefully.
4. **使役形＋ていただきたいんですが… (polite permission-request formula)** — unlike L34's causative, this lesson's causative sense is the permission-granting one, taught as a single memorized chunk rather than decomposed morphologically for beginners — see Vocabulary table for the explicit "don't over-analyze the grammar" instruction given to teachers.
5. **いくら／どんなに〜ても (futile-repeated-effort)** — see Vocabulary table; builds on the plain conditional てもtaught at L26.
6. **〜てしまう (completion, non-regret sense)** — a second, distinct sense from the L19 regret/mishap use; see Vocabulary table for the explicit before/after minimal pair (もう読みました vs もう読んでしまいました as parallel-acceptable answers to "did you already read it?" vs. the marked-wrong use of 〜てしまいました to flatly report a simple past fact with no completion nuance, 誤：昨日、本を読んでしまいました).
7. **〜でも (universal concessive affirmation: いつでも/どこでも/だれでも/なんでも)** — see Vocabulary table for the two explicit learner-error flags (polarity confusion with も; the evasive-sounding 何でもいいです non-answer pattern and the book's recommended fix of narrowing to a category).
8. **〜ば (仮定条件, judgment-leading sense, distinguished from L22's general-truth sense)** — see Vocabulary table; な-adjectives are not drilled in this ば-form here because the book favors たら for that word class in practice.

### Lesson 36 (先輩にいろいろなことをさせられました. — "I was made to do all sorts of things by my seniors"), pp. 158–163 (final numbered lesson)
Goals: use the causative-passive to describe an unpleasant personal experience.
1. **使役受身形 (causative-passive, "forced to do" construction)** — see Vocabulary table for the full breakdown, including the textbook's deliberate choice of the uncontracted グループ1 form (行かせられる) over the colloquially common contracted form (行かされる) for classroom consistency. Teacher guidance explicitly frames this construction's core semantic requirement as a THREE-PARTICIPANT situation: a person imposing the action, a person performing it under some degree of compulsion/inconvenience, and (usually) the action itself as an object — mirroring but inverting the L32 adversity-passive's two-participant minimum.
2. **〜うちに vs 〜間に (closed-interval vs. single-endpoint time-window contrast)** — see Vocabulary table for the full description of this lesson's uniquely elaborate timeline-diagram teaching aid, which visually contrasts a doubly-bounded interval (間に) against a singly-bounded, urgency-flavored interval (うちに) against a window with no symmetric opposite at all (雨が降らないうちに). A connection-formation table is also given specifying how うちに attaches to each part of speech in both polarities (い-adjective: 暗くならないうちに; な-adjective: 新鮮なうちに／新鮮にならないうちに; verb: plain non-past／〜ないうちに; noun: 〜のうちに／〜でないうちに) — teacher told not to drill every cell exhaustively, just the combinations learners will realistically produce.
3. **〜てある (preparation-completion sense, distinguished from L32's descriptive-state sense)** — see Vocabulary table; this is the second of two distinct senses the book assigns to the same 〜てある morphology, taught five lessons apart specifically so students don't conflate "an object visibly displays the result of someone's earlier action" (L32) with "an intentional preparatory action has been completed, readiness achieved" (L36), which can overlap in surface form but differ in communicative function. The lesson also notes a deliberate pedagogical simplification: although the particle before 〜てある can surface as が or を in natural speech (お皿が／を準備してあります), the textbook's own drilled pattern avoids both by using a topic-marked elliptical question-answer frame (お皿は？→準備してあります) specifically to sidestep teaching particle choice at this stage — teachers are warned not to improvise particle-focused drills that go beyond what the book itself practices.

### Appendix 資料 2: 関連文型一覧 (Cross-Reference to Follow-On Patterns in *Bunka Chuukyuu Nihongo I*), pp. 171–181
A genuinely valuable, non-redundant appendix: for every major grammar pattern taught across both Shokyuu volumes (●-marked entries, from *Shokyuu I/II*) the book supplies the corresponding, more advanced treatment of the SAME pattern as it reappears in the publisher's own intermediate-level follow-on textbook, *Bunka Chuukyuu Nihongo I* (○-marked entries), each with its own fresh example sentence — functioning as a deliberately-designed pedagogical bridge showing how a beginner-level grammar point is narrowed, broadened, or re-purposed at the next proficiency level. Selected illustrative pattern-evolutions, paraphrased (not exhaustively reproduced — over 20 patterns are cross-referenced in total, spanning あげる/もらう/くれる, 意志形, 受身形, 〜うちに, 形式名詞「の」, 〜ことがある, 使役受身形, 使役形, 〜すぎ, 〜そう, 〜たら, 〜つもり, 〜て(原因・理由), 〜てある, 〜ている, 〜てしまう, 〜ても, 〜と, 〜ところ, 〜なら, 〜になる, 〜はず, 〜ば, 名詞修飾, もう/まだ, 〜よう/みたい):
- **受身形 (passive):** Shokyuu's adversity-passive and neutral-passive (this file's L32/L33) extend at Chuukyuu I to an honorific-れる/られる use (先輩は大学院の試験を受けられるんですか — indistinguishable in surface form from the potential/passive, disambiguated by honorific context), to object-as-viewpoint-subject passives naming an invention/product itself as the grammatical subject (「ウォークマン」という名前は、普通の名詞のように使われている), and to a distinct "A(large thing) is fitted with B(small thing)" possessive-style passive (ウォークマンには、小さいヘッドホンが取り付けられている) — the last explicitly a NEW passive subtype not covered by either of this book's two-way Shokyuu split.
- **〜うちに:** the Shokyuu sense taught at this file's L36 (日本にいるうちにいろいろな経験をしてみたらどうですか) extends at Chuukyuu I to a "something changes while an ongoing action continues" sense (橋を渡っているうちに、江戸時代にタイムスリップしているような気分になる) — a genuinely distinct meaning (concurrent gradual change) from the Shokyuu "before the window closes" sense this file's L36 diagram carefully distinguished from 間に.
- **使役受身形:** the Shokyuu coercion sense (this file's L36, 新入生は先輩にいろいろなことをさせられます) extends at Chuukyuu I to cover an alternative causative-passive morphological pattern for Group 1 verbs not introduced at the Shokyuu level (日本で暮らし始めてからは、過剰包装に悩まされるようになった).
- **名詞修飾 (relative-clause/noun-modification):** revealed by this appendix to be a FIVE-STAGE cumulative system spanning both volumes, far more systematically staged than apparent from any single lesson in isolation — その1 (Shokyuu I L13, modifier-clause-as-predicate, restricted to "person"-type head nouns only), その2 (Shokyuu I L18, modifier-clause-as-subject, still person-restricted), その3 (Shokyuu II L21, both clause positions now permitted, head-noun restriction lifted to "any noun"), その4 and その5 (both at Chuukyuu I L6, adding modifier clauses built on an embedded further-modified complex NP, e.g. 1960年に東京都が発表した「新宿副都心建設計画」によってスタートしました) — the head-noun-restriction loosening (person-only → any noun) between その2 and その3 is the single biggest formal jump in the whole five-stage system and is worth flagging for the project's own grammar-constraint tracking.
- **〜ている:** shown by this appendix to be an even larger cumulative system than the three-lesson-staged version visible within this book alone — Chuukyuu I adds a position/geography-describing sense (新宿は東京都23区のほぼ中央に位置しています) and an explicit habitual-repetition sense for both present and past reference (姉は週3回英会話教室に通っています／甲州街道を荷馽車や牛車が走っていたんですよ) not present anywhere in the Shokyuu-level treatment.
