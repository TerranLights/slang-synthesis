## Grammar points

### 末(に) vs. 挙げ句(に): a valence-polarity contrast, not mere synonyms

The book's final entry in this range is a clean example of its organizing method: both patterns
front an "outcome after process" meaning, but only 末(に) is polarity-neutral (it can close a
desirable or undesirable result), while 挙げ句(に) is lexically committed to a negative/undesirable
outcome, often reinforced with さんざん or escalated to 挙げ句の果てに. This mirrors the book's
recurring pattern (seen already in Part 1's これまで／これっきり and どうかすると／ややもすると) of
presenting near-identical surface forms that diverge on a single hidden semantic axis — here, emotional
valence rather than literal meaning.

### Voice and perspective marked morphologically: 〜がる and its passive

The 〜がる entry's うるさがられている／うるさがっている minimal pair is a notable case of this
section: the same root (うるさい "annoying") combined with the same suffix (がる "seem/act [adj]")
produces opposite grammatical subjects depending on voice — がられている (passive: "is found annoying
by others") vs. がっている (active: "is [itself] finding something annoying"). This shows 〜がる is not
simply "seems X" but fully participates in the regular verbal voice system, a point the book makes
concrete with paired example sentences rather than abstract rule statement.

### Formal/elevated-register markers cluster together

Three entries in this chunk — 以(もっ)て, Noun+たる, and 〜かねる — are explicitly marked by the
author as belonging to a more formal, written, or official register than the surrounding
colloquial-idiom entries, each noting a colloquial substitute exists (で for もって; implicitly plain
negation for たる-pattern modifiers; direct refusal for かねる). This is a useful register-tier
cross-check: the book is overwhelmingly colloquial-idiom focused, so these three stand out as a small
coherent formal-register cluster worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase (formal vocabulary is
a plausible register-inversion source for later synthesized slang).

### Outcome/result and concession patterns dominate this range

A notable thematic cluster in this chunk: とは〜ないまでも, いうまでもない, AにもBない, and
挙げ句(に)／末(に) all encode different flavors of "despite X, still Y" or "short of the maximum, at
least the minimum" reasoning — concession, thwarted intention, and outcome-after-effort are the
organizing semantic territory of this particular page range, distinct from the demonstrative-pronoun
focus of Part 1 and the connective-discourse focus of the earlier idiomatic-expressions entries.
