## Grammar points

### to-ie-sa / to-ii, to-itta, to-itte, to-iwa-zu — "yuu"-derived combination particles
Paraphrased from pp.210–213. All four combination particles here derive from forms of "yuu" (to say). "To ii...to ii" lists two topics for a shared comment ("the steak, as for taste and price, is perfect"). "To-itta" lists representative examples of a category ("accessories such as pearls and cloisonne"). "To-itte" is a sentence-initial adversative ("however"). "To-iwa-zu" issues a negative imperative affecting a quotation, or, doubled, builds an all-inclusive "not just X, but Y" pattern.

### toka (とか) — example-listing/hearsay particle
Paraphrased from pp.213–214. Lists several representative items (lessons "such as piano, drawing, and calligraphy") or — in a casual-suggestion variant — offers alternatives without commitment. The second sense marks hearsay/uncertainty ("that house sold for something like one hundred million yen, I hear").

### tokoro (ところ), tokorode (ところで), tokoro-e (ところへ), tokoroga (ところが), tokoro-o (ところを) — the "tokoro" family of sequencing/concessive particles
Paraphrased from pp.215–218. "Tokoro" marks plain temporal succession ("when I came home, I found..."). "Tokorode" is pessimistically hypothetical ("even if you were to grieve, it won't come back"). "Tokoro-e" (colloquially also just a coincidence marker) catches an interrupting event ("just when I was about to start my homework, Murata visited"). "Tokoroga" flags an anticipated-but-unexpectedly-negative result ("I bought a lot, but many were rotten"). "Tokoro-o" (colloquially "toko-o") marks a departure from the usual pattern ("although he usually comes home by ten, he hasn't tonight").

### tomo (とも) — absolute-determination/limit particle
Paraphrased from pp.218–219. In the "don-na ni...tomo"/"doredake...tomo" patterns expresses unconditional determination regardless of difficulty ("no matter how hard, I'll give it my best"). After an adjective, sets an approximate limit ("at least/at most"). Sentence-finally, curtly asserts a decision or fact ("I saw it, all right!").
