## Grammar points

### 1. Requesting
Reviews request structures of varying politeness built on くれる, くださる, もらう, いただく, ranked
from most to least formal: 手伝っていただけませんか／手伝ってくださいませんか (most polite) down through
手伝ってもらえませんか／手伝ってくれませんか to the bare て-form by itself for familiar requests: 手伝って。
「Give me a hand.」(p. 58)

### 2. XXに Verb-て ほしい／もらいたい／いただきたい
Expresses a wish for XX to do something, also used to make indirect requests: 新聞を買ってきても
らいたいんですが…「I want you to go buy a newspaper, (but...)」(p. 59)

### 3. The Causative and the Causative-Passive
Causative: -う verbs -u→-aseru (-waseru after a two-vowel stem); -る verbs -ru→-saseru; irregulars
する→させる, 来る→来させる. Means "to make/let someone do...". Note the particle difference between
transitive and intransitive bases: XXが...ZZを Verb(transitive) → YYがXXにZZを Causative-verb (子供が
野菜を食べました。→ お母さんが子供に野菜を食べさせました。). Causative-passive (-se+rareru, often
contracted to -sareru unless that would produce two さ's in a row, e.g. 話させられる keeps its full
form rather than becoming 話さされる) expresses being forced to do something: 私は先生に立たせられました
「I was made to stand by the teacher.」(p. 59–60)

### 4. って (quotative contraction)
The quoting particle と often becomes って in conversation; という can likewise shorten to って. って
at a sentence's end functions like そうだ ("I hear that..."). The plain form + って is used by both
men and women, but the formal-form + って is usually used by women: compare 山田さん、婚約したって話よ。
(女) vs. …話だよ。(男) for "I hear Yamada is engaged." (p. 60–61)

### 5. Sentence + ほど
Acts adverbially, meaning "to the extent that...": 歩けないほど疲れました「I am so tired I can't
walk.」(p. 62)

### 6. Sentence (plain form) + じゃありませんか／じゃないか（男）／じゃない
Presents a statement emphatically, with falling intonation; じゃないか is specifically marked as
men's speech. これ、高いじゃない。「This is expensive, isn't it (so let's not buy it).」(p. 62)

### 7. Verb (non-past plain form) ように
Quotes a request or order: 私の研究室へ来てください。→ 私の研究室に来るように言ってください「Please
tell him to come to my office.」てくれる/てくださる may precede ように when quoting a polite personal
request. (p. 62–63)

### 8. The Volitional form
-う verbs: -u→-oo; -る verbs: -ru→-yoo; irregulars する→しよう, 来る→来よう. In familiar speech, the
volitional form at a sentence's end corresponds to the formal -ましょう. Followed by と思う, it
expresses the speaker's volition/plan, similar to Verb+つもりだ: たばこをやめようと思っています「I've
been thinking of quitting smoking.」(p. 63)
