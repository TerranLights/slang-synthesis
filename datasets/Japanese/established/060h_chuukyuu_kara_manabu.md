**Coverage note — workbook structure confirmed across all 5 lessons:** each lesson follows a fixed
four-part structure: **速読** (a short themed reading passage, usually 1 page) → **内容チェック**
(comprehension-check True/False + short-answer drill, skipped per coverage rule) → **聴解Ｉ**
(dictation-style listening-comprehension drill, fill-in-the-blank dialogue transcripts, skipped) →
**聴解II** (a second, longer listening-comprehension drill, skipped). Unlike the main textbook, this
workbook has **no formal "新しい言葉" (new-words) vocabulary box** — new vocabulary must be drawn
directly from each lesson's 速読 passage text. Per the coverage rule and this chunk's extraction
priority, only the 速読 passages' genuinely new vocabulary/idioms were extracted; the comprehension
and listening drill sections were skipped in full — they are pure fill-in exercises recombining
material from their own passage, introducing nothing new themselves. None of the five lessons'
workbook content was *entirely* drill-only — every lesson's 速読 passage contributed at least a
handful of genuinely new items, so no lesson's table entry is empty, though Lessons 3 and 5 are
thin (their passages lean on largely familiar core vocabulary; see per-lesson notes below).

**Flagship finding (Lesson 2):** the Lesson 2 速読 passage is explicitly about information
compression in Japanese — it analyzes a real newspaper real-estate classified ad and shows how an
extremely compressed, abbreviation-heavy register (unit counters, truncated compounds, no
particles) can pack a large amount of information into a tiny physical space. This is a genuinely
distinct register from anything captured in the main textbook's Lesson 1 and worth flagging for
later slang-mechanics analysis (telegraphic/classified-ad compression as its own productive
register, parallel to but structurally different from colloquial clipping).

## Grammar points

No new grammar patterns were introduced in this chunk beyond what the main textbook's own 使いましょう
sections already cover — the workbook's 速読 passages are prose readings, not grammar-pattern drill
pages, and none of the five lessons' passages exhibited a construction distinct enough from ordinary
descriptive/narrative prose to warrant its own grammar-point entry. (This is expected: per this
file's coverage note above, this workbook's structure doesn't include a 使いましょう-style grammar
section at all within the assigned Lesson 1–5 range — that teaching function lives entirely in the
main textbook, already covered by `060_chuukyuu_kara_manabu.md` and its planned sibling files.)
