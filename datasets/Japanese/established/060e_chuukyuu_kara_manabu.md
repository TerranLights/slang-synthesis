## Grammar points

### Lesson 19「おもいだす」

#### 「〜限り」("as long as ~ / unless ~")

Expresses that a state or action will hold true for the duration of a condition, or will not
change unless that condition is broken. Source's own example (p. 106, pattern A): "よほどのこと
がない限り、そうします" ("I'll do so unless something extraordinary comes up"), drawn from the
reading passage's description of the father's unwavering 5:30 homecoming habit.

#### 「〜ふう」("in the manner/style of ~")

Describes doing something in a particular way or manner. Source's own example (p. 106, pattern B):
"分かったというふうに、彼女はニッコリうなずいた" ("She nodded with a smile, as if to say she
understood") — drawn directly from the reading passage.

#### 「どちらにしても」("either way, in any case")

Used to state that a conclusion holds regardless of which of two stated alternatives is true.
Source's own example (p. 107, pattern C): "どちらにしても私は行けないと思います" ("Either way, I don't
think I can go").

#### 「もっとも〜といっても」("that said, even though you call it ~")

A hedging construction: concedes a prior statement while immediately qualifying/downgrading the
claim that follows. Source's own example (p. 107, pattern D), drawn from the reading passage:
"花火をしました。もっとも花火といっても線香花火ですが" ("We did fireworks — though when I say
'fireworks,' I just mean sparklers").

### Lesson 20「しらせる」

#### 「あたかも〜かのように」("as if ~, as though ~")

Describes an impression or feeling as though something were true, when it is not literally so.
Source's own example (p. 112, pattern A): "あたかも自分自身がその場所にいるかのように感じる" ("One
feels as though one were actually there oneself") — describing television's immersive effect.

#### 「〜を通して」("through ~, via ~")

Indicates a medium or intermediary through which something is accomplished or experienced.
Source's own example (p. 112, pattern B): "外国の友達を通して、他の世界を知った" ("Through a foreign
friend, I came to know another world").

#### 「〜ことに」("to one's [emotion], ~")

Placed before a clause to frame the following statement through the speaker's emotional reaction
(e.g. 残念なことに, うれしいことに). Source's own example (p. 112–113, pattern C): "面白いことに、その
事件に対する人々の反応は意外に冷静だったのである" ("Interestingly, people's reaction to the incident
was surprisingly calm").

#### 「〜とはいえ」("even though ~, that said")

A concessive conjunction: grants the truth of the preceding clause while asserting something that
qualifies or contrasts with it. Source's own example (p. 113, pattern D): "テレビには問題も多いとは
いえ、その使命は今後ますます重要になるだろう" ("Even though television has many problems, its mission
will likely become increasingly important going forward").

#### 「〜だけに」("precisely because ~, all the more so because ~")

Expresses that a stated fact intensifies or specially justifies the consequence that follows —
often because the situation involved extra effort, expectation, or stakes. Source's own example
(p. 113, pattern E): "テレビはますます多様化していくと思われるだけに、その内容を見守る責任がある" ("Precisely
because television is expected to keep diversifying, there is a responsibility to watch over its
content").

### Lesson 21「まもる」

#### 「〜する一方」("while/even as ~ continues unabated")

Describes a situation that is continuously progressing in one direction, often with a negative or
escalating connotation. Source's own example (p. 118, pattern A), from the reading passage:
"環境破壊は進む一方です" ("Environmental destruction just keeps advancing").

#### 「〜ことなく」("without ever ~ing")

A formal negative-manner construction: describes an action carried out without the stated thing
ever happening. Source's own example (p. 118, pattern B): "暑い日も寒い日も、父は休むことなく仕事を
した" ("Whether hot or cold, my father worked without ever resting").

#### 「比べものにならない」("incomparable, not in the same league")

A fixed expression asserting that one thing vastly exceeds another along some dimension, to the
point comparison is meaningless. Source's own example (p. 118–119, pattern C): "速さという点では
飛行機は船とは比べものにならない" ("In terms of speed, airplanes are incomparable to ships") — echoing
the reading passage's point that an individual's power is incomparably small next to the forces
that have damaged the global environment.

#### 「〜次第」("depending entirely on ~")

Expresses that an outcome is wholly contingent on the stated factor. Source's own example
(p. 119, pattern D): "上手にできるかどうかは、練習次第です" ("Whether you can do it well depends
entirely on practice") — echoing the reading passage's closing line that the earth's fate is
"私たち次第" ("up to us").

### Lesson 22「ふれあう」

#### 「さすがに〜」/ 「〜だけあって、さすがに〜」 ("even ~, as might be expected")

さすがに marks that something holds true to a degree that confirms an expectation, even where one
might think otherwise (e.g. even the normally-mild early morning air felt cold). The compound
だけあって、さすがに〜 explicitly ties the confirming outcome to a stated reason/qualification.
Source's own examples (p. 124): "早朝の空気は、さすがに冷たい" ("The early-morning air was, as one
would expect, cold") and "三年間勉強しただけあって、彼は、さすがに日本語が上手だ" ("Having studied for
three years, he is, unsurprisingly, good at Japanese").

#### 「〜がち」("tending toward ~, prone to ~")

Attaches to a noun or verb stem to indicate a recurring tendency, often an undesirable one.
Source's own example (p. 125, pattern B): "あしたはくもりがちで、ところによっては雨が降るでしょう"
("Tomorrow will tend to be cloudy, with rain in some areas") — also seen in-text describing the
visually-impaired student's classmate as 遠慮がち (hesitant/reserved) when asking a question.

#### 「それにしても」("even so, be that as it may")

Introduces a reaction that concedes the preceding statement but still expresses a degree of
surprise, reservation, or qualification. Source's own example (p. 125, pattern C): "ああ、やっぱり
たばこはおいしいですね。それにしても、一日に三十本は、多すぎますよ" ("Ah, cigarettes really are
satisfying. Even so, thirty a day is too many").

### Lesson 23「うたう」

#### 「〜なんて、およそ〜ない」("the very idea that ~ is scarcely ~")

Expresses incredulity or strong rejection: the stated scenario is framed as nearly unthinkable.
Source's own example (p. 130, pattern A): "そんな事故が起こるなんて、およそ考えられない" ("The very
idea that such an accident could happen is scarcely conceivable").

#### 「やむを得ず〜」("having no choice but to ~")

Describes an action undertaken reluctantly because no alternative was available — distinct from
the related adjectival やむを得ない ("unavoidable," listed in the vocabulary table above). Source's
own example (p. 130, pattern B): "やむを得ず彼らは子供を殺す" ("They had no choice but to kill the
child") — directly echoing the reading passage's description of historical 間引き.

#### 「〜以上」("given that ~, since ~ (as a matter of principle)")

Introduces a stated fact or status as a premise from which an obligation or conclusion
necessarily follows. Source's own example (p. 130, pattern C): "学生である以上、勉強をすべきだ" ("Given
that one is a student, one ought to study").

#### 「〜に応じて」("in accordance with ~, depending on ~")

Expresses that something varies or is adjusted to match a stated condition. Source's own example
(p. 131, pattern D): "きせつに応じて、着るものを変えます" ("I change what I wear according to the
season") — also used in the reading passage's own argument: "社会が豊かになるのに応じて、今はもう
『間引く』という言葉など死語になってしまったようです" ("As society has grown more affluent, the word
'thin out [infants]' seems to have become obsolete").

### Lesson 24「なおす」

#### 「〜をはじめ」("~, among others; starting with ~")

Introduces a representative example as the first/most prominent of a broader category. Source's
own example (p. 136, pattern A): "医者をはじめ、各界の専門家から様々な意見が出されている" ("Opinions have
come from specialists across many fields, starting with doctors").

#### 「〜をめぐって」("concerning ~, surrounding ~ (as a point of contention)")

Marks the topic around which debate, dispute, or various opinions are centered. Source's own
example (p. 136, pattern B): "脳死をめぐって、様々な意見が出された" ("Various opinions have been raised
concerning brain death") — directly drawn from the reading passage's discussion of organ
transplantation and the donor-card system.

#### 「〜ことから」("given that ~, for the reason that ~")

Introduces a fact or observation as the basis/origin for a following conclusion or name. Source's
own example (p. 136, pattern C): "この町は川が多いことから、『水の町』と呼ばれている" ("Because this town
has many rivers, it's called the 'water town'").

#### 「〜わけにはいかない」("cannot (in good conscience) ~, must not ~")

Expresses that, given social, moral, or practical constraints, a course of action is not a real
option even if one might want to take it. Source's own example (p. 137, pattern D): "人の心の問題
を無視するわけにはいかない" ("One cannot simply ignore the question of the human heart") — echoing the
reading passage's "『もしかして』という思いで、なかなか死を認めるわけにはいかない" ("With a lingering 'what
if,' one cannot easily accept the death").

### Lesson 25「のびる」

#### 「〜さえ〜ば」("if only ~, as long as just ~")

Isolates a single minimal condition as sufficient for a result, implying everything else is
secondary. Source's own example (p. 142, pattern A): "優秀な人材さえ確保できれば、後は、あまり問題ない
ですよ" ("As long as you can just secure excellent talent, the rest isn't much of a problem").

#### 「〜たびに」("every time ~, each time ~")

Indicates that an event or state recurs reliably alongside a trigger condition. Source's own
example (p. 142, pattern B), drawn from the reading passage: "日本経済はそのたびにこれを乗り切ってきた"
("Each time, the Japanese economy pulled through").

#### 「〜における / 〜においては」("in ~, with regard to ~ (formal locative)")

A formal, written-register way of specifying the domain or setting in which a statement applies.
Source's own example (p. 143, pattern C): "世界経済における日本の役割は重要になっている" ("Japan's role
within the world economy has become important").

#### 「〜の下で」("under ~, in the context of ~")

Describes an action or situation taking place under the influence, authority, or banner of the
stated thing. Source's own example (p. 143, pattern D), drawn from the reading passage:
"国際化のかけ声の下で私たちは頑張った" ("We worked hard under the rallying cry of internationalization").

#### 「〜つつある」("to be in the process of ~ing, to be gradually ~ing")

A formal progressive construction marking a change that is currently underway, typically over an
extended timescale — distinct from Lesson 19's simultaneous-action 〜つつ. Source's own example
(p. 143, pattern E), drawn from the reading passage: "経済発展にばかり目を向けることによって、価値ある
ものを見失いつつある" ("By focusing solely on economic development, we are gradually losing sight of
things of real value").
