## Grammar points

### Onomatopoeia (giongo/giseigo) as a cross-linguistically variable system, not a universal

The book makes an explicit point (p.339) that students are often surprised how differently
Japanese renders the same real-world sound compared to English — e.g. a rooster's cry structured
completely differently (kokekokkō vs. "cock-a-doodle-doo") despite referring to an identical
acoustic event. This is a clean, source-stated confirmation that onomatopoeic/mimetic vocabulary is
itself a conventionalized, language-specific symbolic system rather than a direct acoustic
transcription — directly relevant background for this project's broader interest in Japanese's
large mimetic-word inventory (see the pain-descriptor mimetic words already catalogued in `102g`:
chiku-chiku, zuki-zuki, hiri-hiri, gaNgaN).
