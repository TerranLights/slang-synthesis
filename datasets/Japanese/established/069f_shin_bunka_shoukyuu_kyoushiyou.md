## Grammar points

### Lesson 28 (送ってくれてありがとう。— "Thanks for Giving Me a Ride"), pp. 94–102
Goals: thank someone for a favor received; describe things done/received/given as favors using the benefactive giving-receiving system (てくれる/てもらう/てあげる).
1. **〜てくれる (benefactive, favor received from another)** — extends the object-giving くれる (L25) to actions. Same giver/receiver participant-structure constraint as object-くれる (receiver = speaker or speaker's in-group). Explicit three-way verb/particle taxonomy given to prevent learner confusion, since the particle marking the "receiver" argument depends on the specific verb's own valency: (1) 〜に〜てくれる for verbs like 教える/書く/貸す/ごちそうする (receiver takes に), (2) 〜を〜てくれる for verbs like 送る/連れて行く/迎えに来る where the receiver is a direct object (を), (3) 〜の(物・こと)を〜てくれる for verbs like みる/持つ/手伝う that take an object belonging to the receiver. Worked warning: even a single verb (送る) can pattern either as "(人)に(物)を送ってくれる" or as "(人)を送ってくれる" depending on whether a physical object or the person themselves is being conveyed — flagged explicitly as not a reliable 1:1 particle-to-verb mapping. Ellipsis rule: when the receiver is "私," the 〜に phrase is usually dropped; when the receiver is "my family" or "the person I'm talking to," it's more often kept.
2. **〜てもらう (benefactive, favor obtained)** — parallel action-extension of object-もらう (L24). Unlike てくれる, てもらう's giver-marking に has no participant-structure restriction (the receiver isn't limited to speaker/in-group) and carries an implied "I had someone do this for me (possibly by asking)" nuance. Common learner-error flagged: direction-sensitive verb pairs (貸す/借りる, 見せる/見る) get their direction reversed when ported into てもらう — e.g. a learner will say ×着物を借りてもらいました when they mean 着物を貸してもらいました ("I had [a friend] lend me a kimono") — explicitly recommended to re-confirm each verb's inherent directionality before drilling てもらう.
3. **〜時 (two distinct timepoints: 辞書形+時 vs. た形+時)** — building on the simple "limiting-time" use of 〜時 from L13. 行く時 marks a point BEFORE the named event completes (ディズニーランドへ行く時、京葉線に乗ります — on the way there); 行った時 marks a point AFTER/AT arrival (ディズニーランドへ行った時、写真を撮ります — once there). Explicit learner-confusion flag: students fixate on た形 as "past tense" and have trouble parsing sentences like 明日、デパートへ行った時、買うつもりです, where 行った時 describes a future-but-already-arrived timepoint, not a past event — the lesson clarifies this is about completion-relative-to-the-main-clause-event, not absolute tense. Drilling restricted to motion verbs (行く/来る/帰る/出る).
4. **〜てあげる (benefactive, favor given to another)** — action-extension of object-あげる (L24), carrying the same "can sound presumptuous toward the addressee" pragmatic risk already flagged for object-あげる. The lesson narrows its own example set to satisfy three joint conditions specifically to avoid that presumptuousness: (1) the favor's recipient isn't present when it's discussed, (2) the recipient is limited to a friend/family member, (3) the favor is something the recipient actually wants and isn't a burden to receive. When offering directly to someone's face, the book recommends switching to a volitional-offer phrasing (持ちましょうか) instead of 〜てあげましょうか.
5. **名詞+しか〜ません (restrictive, "only/nothing but")** — contrasted directly with だけ (L7): しか obligatorily pairs with a negative predicate, while だけ can appear with either polarity; しか is also used to emphasize that a quantity/degree is surprisingly low (マフラーしか編めません — "[all she can manage is] only a scarf"). Companion contrast with も: the identical objective state (a glass half-full) can be framed either via しか (implying insufficiency, used by someone who wanted more) or via も (implying surprising abundance, used by someone who wanted less) — the choice reflects the speaker's subjective evaluation, not the objective fact; も itself was previously introduced at L18/L24.
6. **文脈指示：そ系 vs. あ系 (discourse-anaphoric そ/あ reference)** — extends the こ/そ/あ system (last touched at L21) to referring back to something already mentioned in the conversation, explicitly NOT covering the こ-series here. そ (それ/その/そこ) is used when only one conversation participant actually knows/shares the referenced thing (その店はどこにあるんですか — the asker doesn't know the place); あ (あれ/あの/あそこ) is used when both participants already share knowledge of the referent (あの店はよかったですね — both know the place from shared experience).
Practice-section register note (not a numbered 文型): the plain-register てくれてありがとう used throughout this lesson's dialogues is explicitly flagged as peer/family register only; toward a social superior the book specifies the substitute 〜てくださってありがとうございました (deferred to full treatment at L29 文型2).
