## Grammar points

### 1. Repeating a single kanji

Repeating a single kanji produces a jukugo with the same core meaning as the individual character,
but adding emphasis or a notion of plurality. The repeated kanji is written once, followed by the
kanji-repetition symbol **々**. Euphonic voicing (rendaku) sometimes affects the first syllable of
the repeated half (e.g. 人々 = ひとびと, not *ひとひと*).

Examples: 人+人=人々 (people); 山+山=山々 (mountains); 木+木=木々 (trees); 色+色=色々 (various);
続く+続く=続々 (successively, one after another); 少ない+少ない=少々 (a little, a few);
年+年=年々 (year by year); 近い+近い=近々 (in the near future, read ちかぢか or キンキン);
種+種=種々 (various); 時+時=時々 (sometimes).

### 2. Joining two kanji with similar meanings

Two kanji with similar/overlapping individual meanings combine into a jukugo whose meaning is
broader than either kanji alone.

Examples: 増す(increase)+加える(add)=増加 ゾウカ (increase); 永い(long time)+久しい(long time)
=永久 エイキュウ (forever); 河(large river)+川(river)=河川 カセン (rivers); 存(exist)+在る
(stay, exist)=存在 ソンザイ (existence); 生(birth, life)+産まれる(produce)=生産 セイサン
(production); 会う(meet)+合う(gather)=会合 カイゴウ (meeting, gathering); 表(surface)+現わす
(express)=表現 ヒョウゲン (expression); 道(way, road)+路(route)=道路 ドウロ (street, road);
開く(open)+始める(begin)=開始 カイシ (opening, beginning); 進む(progress)+行く(go)=進行 シン
コウ (advance, progress).

### 3. Joining two kanji with opposite or contrasting meanings

Two kanji representing opposite/contrasting concepts combine into a jukugo denoting the pairing or
spectrum between them.

Examples: 左(left)+右(right)=左右 サユウ (left and right); 上(top)+下(bottom)=上下 ジョウゲ (up
and down); 大きい(big)+小さい(small)=大小 ダイショウ (of different sizes); 長い(long)+短かい
(short)=長短 チョウタン (good points and bad points); 公(public)+私(private)=公私 コウシ (public
and private); 多い(many)+少ない(few)=多少 タショウ (more or less, some); 前(front, before)+後
(back, behind)=前後 ゼンゴ (before and after, order/sequence); 男(men)+女(women)=男女 ダンジョ
(men and women); 売る(sell)+買う(buy)=売買 バイバイ (buying and selling, trading); 天(sky,
heaven)+地(earth)=天地 テンチ (heaven and earth, the universe).

### 4. Joining two kanji with related meanings

Two kanji whose individual meanings are related (but not synonymous and not opposed) combine into
a jukugo representing a new idea derived from the combination.

Examples: 見る(see)+聞く(hear)=見聞 ケンブン (information, experience); 山(mountain)+川(river)=
山川 サンセン (mountains and rivers); 草(grass)+木(tree)=草木 ソウモク (plants, vegetation); 風
(wind)+雨(rain)=風雨 フウウ (wind and rain, rainstorm); 飲む(drink)+食べる(eat)=飲食 インショ
ク (eating and drinking); 金(gold)+銀(silver)=金銀 キンギン (money, valuables); 妻(wife)+子
(children)=妻子 サイシ (one's wife and children, family); 父(father)+母(mother)=父母 フボ
(one's parents); 手(hands)+足(feet)=手足 てあし (hands and feet); 年(year)+月(month)=年月 とし
つき/ネンゲツ (years).

### 5. Joining two kanji in which the first modifies the second

The first kanji functions as a noun modifier (often an adjective or a noun used attributively) and
the second as the noun being modified, in standard modifier+noun order.

Examples: 電(electricity)+車(car, vehicle)=電車 デンシャ (electric train); 青い(blue)+空(sky)=
青空 あおぞら (blue sky); 古い(old)+本(book)=古本 ふるホン (used book); 新しい(new)+車(car)=
新車 シンシャ (new car); 外(outside)+国(country)=外国 ガイコク (foreign countries); 朝(morning)
+食(meal)=朝食 チョウショク (breakfast); 夕(evening)+日(sun)=夕日 ゆうひ (setting sun); 美しい
(beautiful)+人(person)=美人 ビジン (beautiful woman); 高い(high)+山(mountain)=高山 コウザン
(high mountain); 冷たい(cold)+水(water)=冷水 レイスイ (cold water).

### 6. Joining two kanji in which the first is a verb and the second is an object

A verb-stem kanji is followed by a kanji denoting its direct object, in verb+object order (the
reverse of the expected SOV word order found in full Japanese sentences — a jukugo-internal
convention, not a sentence-level one).

Examples: 読む(read)+書(books)=読書 ドクショ (reading); 作る(make)+文(sentences)=作文 サクブ
ン (composition, essay); 開く(open)+会(meeting)=開会 カイカイ (the opening of a meeting); 休む
(be absent, rest)+学(school)=休学 キュウガク (temporary absence from school); 決める
(determine)+心(mind)=決心 ケッシン (making up one's mind); 求める(look for, seek)+人(people)=
求人 キュウジン (help wanted); 失う(lose)+業(job, business)=失業 シツギョウ (losing a job,
unemployed); 禁じる(prohibit)+煙(smoke)=禁煙 キンエン (no smoking); 投げる(throw, cast)+球
(ball)=投球 トウキュウ (pitching/throwing a ball); 預ける(deposit, entrust)+金(money)=預金 ヨ
キン (bank deposit).

### 7. Adding a kanji as a negative prefix to a second kanji

The kanji 不, 無, 非, and 未 function as negative prefixes, playing a role comparable to English
"in-"/"un-"/"dis-"/"non-" — the source notes each has a characteristic semantic flavor (不 "not,"
無 "without/lacking," 非 "mis-/non-," 未 "not yet").

Examples: 不+正しい(correct)=不正 フセイ (injustice); 不+足りる(sufficient)=不足 フソク
(insufficiency); 不(lack of)+幸せ(happiness)=不幸 フコウ (unhappiness, misfortune); 不+便
(convenience)=不便 フベン (inconvenience); 無(not, lack of)+理(reason)=無理 ムリ
(unreasonable); 無+言う(speaking)=無言 ムゴン (silence); 無(without)+線(line)=無線 ムセン
(wireless, radio); 非+常(ordinary)=非常 ヒジョウ (emergency, exceptional); 非(mis-)+行い
(behavior, action)=非行 ヒコウ (misconduct); 未(not yet)+定める(fix, settle)=未定 ミテイ
(undecided, pending); 未(not yet)+婚(marriage)=未婚 ミコン (unmarried); 未(not yet)+来る
(coming)=未来 ミライ (future).

### 8. Abbreviating a longer jukugo to two kanji

A longer jukugo formed by joining two multi-kanji words (each itself following pattern 5, first
modifies second) is sometimes contracted down to just the first kanji of each half, producing a
shorter two-kanji abbreviated jukugo with the same meaning.

Examples: 国際(international)+連合(union, alliance)=国連 コクレン (United Nations); 原子(atom)+
爆弾(bomb)=原爆 ゲンバク (atomic bomb); 特別(special)+急行(express train)=特急 トッキュウ
(special express train); 日本(Japan)+銀行(bank)=日銀 ニチギン (Bank of Japan); 東京(Tōkyō)+
大学(university)=東大 トウダイ (Tōkyō University); 入学(entering school)+試験(examination)=
入試 ニュウシ (school entrance examination); 私営(privately operated)+鉄道(railroad)=私鉄 シ
テツ (private sector railroad); 日本(Japan)+航空(aviation)=日航 ニッコウ (Japan Air Lines);
連続(continuation)+休暇(holiday)=連休 レンキュウ (consecutive holidays).
