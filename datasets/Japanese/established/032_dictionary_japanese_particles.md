## Grammar points

### ba (ば) — conditional/topic particle
Paraphrased from pp.1–4. Core sense is the conditional "if" (*ima dekakereba maniai-masu yo* — "If you leave now, you'll make it in time"), extending to cause-and-effect "when" (*koorogi ga naki-dasereba aki mo ma-jikai* — "When crickets start to chirp, autumn is near"), to set topic-marking phrases built on "to ieba"/"...reba" ("speaking of...", "according to..."), to an evidential "considering X, it's clear that Y" sense, to a listing use in "mo...ba...mo" ("neither...nor"), to the obligation construction "-(a)nakereba naranai" ("must do"), to fixed idioms "iwaba"/"tatoeba" ("so to speak"/"for example"), and finally "naze nara ba" ("the reason being").

### bakari (ばかり) — limiting/approximating particle
Paraphrased from pp.5–10. Primary sense restricts an action/state to one thing ("always just sleeping" — *sono koinu wa itsumo nemutte bakari imasu*), with the "bakari ni" sub-pattern blaming a single cause for an unlucky result (*ano densha ni notta bakari ni, jiko ni atta* — "It was only because I got on that train that I was involved in an accident"). A second cluster of senses uses "bakari" after quantities for rough approximation ("about two meters tall") and after verbs for "just about to"/"just did" (*taberu bakari no tokoro ni* — "just about to eat when..."; *naratta bakari no nihon-go* — "Japanese I had just learned"). A third cluster, "bakari ka...mo" and "bakari de(wa) naku...mo", gives the "not only X but also Y" construction. The casual variant "bakashi" repeats the approximation and limiting senses in colloquial register.

### dake (だけ) — limiting particle
Paraphrased from pp.10–12. Basic sense "only/just" (*akai ringo dake mittsu kudasai* — "Please give me three of the red apples only"). After a potential-form verb, gives a "to the fullest extent possible" sense (*dekiru dake tasukete age-mashoo* — "I'll help as much as I can"). The "...ba...dake" pattern gives a proportional "the more X the more Y" (*yasukereba yasui dake ii desu* — "The cheaper, the better"). "Dake de(ja) naku...mo" gives "not only X but also Y" (*keeki dake de naku, aisukuriimu mo tabemashoo yo* — "Let's eat not only cake but also ice cream").

### dake-ni (だけに) — combination particle
Paraphrased from p.13. Combines "dake" + "ni" (also appears as "dake atte"). When the outcome matches expectation, gives "since/because (as expected)..." (*yoku benkyoo-shita dake ni, ii seiseki de gookaku-shita* — "Since I studied hard, I passed with good grades, as expected"). When the outcome defies expectation, gives a "since (contrary to what was assumed)..., surprisingly..." reading (*kare ni wa moo ae-nai daroo to akirameteita dake ni, denwa ga kakatte-kita toki ni wa, totemo ureshikatta* — happiness at an unexpected call after having given up hope of ever seeing the person again).

### dano (だの) — listing particle
Paraphrased from p.14. Lists things successively in a "...dano...dano" pattern ("things like X and Y"), generally carrying a complaining or exasperated undertone in the book's own examples (litter "dano...dano" scattered on the street; friends listing reasons moving to New York is dangerous).

### datte (だって) — "even"/"whether...or"/quotative particle
Paraphrased from pp.14–17. "Even (something) is/does..." (*kodomo datte sore kurai wakari-masu* — "Even children understand that much"). The doubled "...datte...datte" (or "tatte...tatte") pattern lists alternatives indifferently ("whether it's X or Y, [it doesn't matter]"). Following an interrogative pronoun, gives universal-affirmative ("anyone/anytime/anywhere", e.g. *itsu datte mirare-masu yo* — "You can see it anytime") or, in a negative clause, universal-negative ("no one/nothing", e.g. *dare datte binboo wa iya da* — "Everyone dislikes being poor"). Placed sentence-finally, "datte" is quotative, reporting someone else's statement, often with a note of surprise or mild disapproval (*boku wa okane nanka karite i-masen yo datte. Wasureta no kashira* — "He said, 'I haven't borrowed any money.' I wonder if he forgot?").
