## Grammar points

### Loanword-by-year test-frequency list as de facto attestation data (p. 14)

Unusually for a teaching grammar, this source lists specific katakana loanwords by the specific year they appeared on the actual JLPT N2 exam (1992 through 1998), rather than just grouping them by difficulty or topic. The spec's general caution against inferring frequency from "lesson ordering" does not apply here — this is the source's own citation of real historical exam-appearance data, not a pedagogical ordering choice, so `Attested Era` was filled in per-entry above rather than left as `—`. The source draws an explicit methodological point from this list: of ~14 loanwords cited across 7 years, only プログラム repeats (appearing in both 1992 and 1994), which the authors use to argue that rote-memorizing a fixed "likely to reappear" loanword list has low payoff — students are better served broadly expanding loanword vocabulary than drilling past exam words specifically.

### Homophone-but-different-kanji word pairs requiring context, not sound, to disambiguate (p. 11)

Building on the same general phenomenon the N1 book's 文字・語彙 section teaches (056's kanji-writing-traps grammar point), this N2 book gives its own worked example: 異動 (job/personnel transfer) and 移動 (physical relocation) are perfect homophones (both いどう) that cannot be told apart by pronunciation alone — a listener/reader must rely entirely on sentence context (軍を移動する "to move troops" vs. 人事異動があった "there was a personnel reshuffle") to know which is meant. The source explicitly frames this as the core challenge of the 問題II kanji-writing section: knowing many kanji is useless without also knowing which contexts call for which near-homophone.

### Reading-comprehension question taxonomy, expanded 4-way split (p. 18)

Where the N1 book (056) taught a 2-way split for "what does the author think" questions, this N2 book teaches a more granular 4-way taxonomy for the full range of reading-comprehension question types: (1) questions about a sentence fragment — filling a blank, or explaining a difficult word/phrase/pronoun's referent in context (the 捕食者/擬態/彼ら/その時の私の気持ち examples); (2) questions about what こ・そ・あ demonstratives refer to; (3) questions asking the reader to infer an unstated agent, reason, or cause behind something the passage doesn't spell out directly; (4) questions about a whole paragraph's point; and a separate, usually-final whole-passage question asking for the author's main point, a title, or the author's likely profession. The source notes types (1)–(2) require precise close-reading, while the final whole-passage type requires big-picture synthesis — and recommends practicing each skill separately rather than assuming one implies the other.

### Listening-section procedural details: answer-marking discipline and timing (pp. 14–15)

The 聴解 section explains JLPT N2's specific listening-test mechanics: each dialogue plays twice (once before the question, once after); both the correct and the (plural) incorrect answers must be actively marked on a special answer sheet (unlike text sections where only the correct answer is marked) — a procedural trap the source specifically warns test-takers not to get wrong out of habit; and 問題I allows roughly 10 seconds of thinking time between items while 問題II moves on too quickly to allow real reflection, so the source recommends extensive practice to build comfort with the pacing rather than relying on in-the-moment reasoning time.
