**Source:** Marc Bernabé, *Japanese in Mangaland, Vol. 2* (English edition, Japan Publications
Trading Co.). Vision-read from `source_reference/languages/Japanese/03.Audio Courses, Textbooks/
Japanese in Mangaland/Japanese in Mangaland vol 2.pdf` (210 PDF pages; no text layer — confirmed via

simulating real manga typesetting. Appendices: exercise answers, a 100-new-kanji compilation, and a

**Coverage note — representative sampling.** Sampled lesson theory pages at PDF pp. 20 (L.31, volitive
form/wanting), 50 (L.34, interrogatives & future-time suffixes), 90 (L.38, particles で/に as
cause/combination), 100 (L.39, transport vocabulary + colloquial contraction), 130 (L.42, shopping —
notably colloquial/distorted speech), and 160 (L.45, giving/receiving verb formality). Also sampled
three pages of the vocabulary index (pp. 189, 199, 200) for calibration. **Register finding: this

**No marginalia found.** All sampled pages were clean typeset print (this is a non-scanned born-digital

## Grammar points

### Volitive/desire forms and the politeness-vs-superiority gap (Lesson 31)
Direct wish forms (～たい, ～ほしい) are first-person-only; a third party's apparent wish must be
expressed with the derived ～たがる/～ほしがる forms instead, since Japanese treats direct claims about
someone else's internal state as epistemically overreaching. The lesson's worked example — a tax
official telling a taxpayer て + ほしい ("I want you to pay") — is explicitly analyzed by the book as
simultaneously grammatically formal *and* pragmatically superior/condescending in tone: a clear case
of formal register not being the same axis as "neutral" or "deferential" register, worth keeping in
mind for this project's own register-taxonomy work.

### Future/past time-distance suffixes ～後／～前 (Lesson 34)
A fully regular pair of suffixes attaching to any time-unit noun to mean "in X time" (～後) or "X time
ago" (～前): 一週間後 "in a week" / 一週間前 "a week ago," 三日後/三日前, 五年後/五年前, etc. The "months"
slot is irregular, requiring ヶ月 rather than a bare counter.

### Weak-causal で vs. stronger から／ので (Lesson 38)
で can mark a cause/reason, but the book stresses it is deliberately weak — closer to "and" linking
two clauses than a true logical "because." から and ので (introduced later, across Vol. 2/3) are
explicitly flagged as carrying much stronger causal force, setting up an explicit three-way causal-
connector strength hierarchy (で weakest, から middle, ので/explicit stronger) worth tracking for later
mechanics-analysis work on how a conlang might similarly grade causal-connective strength.

### Colloquial contraction: ～てしまう → ～ちゃう (Lesson 39)
A manga-dialogue example (入れちゃった) is explicitly decoded by the book back to its full textbook
form (入れてしまった), confirming the regular colloquial contraction rule ～てしまう → ～ちゃう (and past
～てしまった → ～ちゃった) that carries the "by mistake / to one's regret" nuance. This is a clean,
source-confirmed sound-contraction rule directly relevant to any conlang slang layer modeling
casual-speech contraction.

### Full-sentence colloquial distortion, decoded (Lesson 42)
The book's most valuable finding in this sampling pass: a full manga sentence
(いくらすると思ってんのよ) is explicitly reconstructed by the author into its "normal"/textbook
equivalent (いくらすると思っているのですか), modeling the full chain of casual-speech reductions a learner
needs to reverse-engineer: ている → てん, のですか → のよ (feminine/casual sentence-final). This is the
single clearest documented case in this sampling pass of the author treating colloquial manga speech
as a systematically derivable transform of standard Japanese rather than a separate unanalyzable
register — directly useful precedent for slang-synthesis mechanics work.

### Giving/receiving verb formality and the uchi/soto frame (Lesson 45)
もらう (as a bare verb and as a gerund-attached auxiliary) encodes "I or someone in my in-group (uchi)
receives something"; くれる/くださる encode "someone outside my in-group (soto) gives to me/my group,"
with くださる as the explicit formal-register upgrade of くれる — and the book notes ～てください
("please") is historically just a frozen/shortened form of ～てくださる. This in-group/out-group
(uchi/soto) frame is flagged by the book as a recurring theme it will return to "in book 3," i.e. a
structural concept bigger than any single grammar point.
