skipped.

## Grammar points

### 「せっかく」(wasted/invested effort — "went to the trouble of ~ [but]")

Marks that significant effort or a special opportunity was expended, often setting up a contrast
with a disappointing or wasted outcome. Source's own example: "せっかくしゅくだいをしたのに、忘れて
きてしまいました" ("I went to the trouble of doing my homework, but I forgot to bring it") (Lesson 9,
p. 46, pattern A).

### 「どうしても〜ようとしない」("simply won't/refuses to ~ no matter what")

Combines the emphatic adverb どうしても with the negative volitional-attempt form ~ようとしない to
express stubborn, absolute refusal. Source's own example, drawn from the reading passage: "母親が
買ってきた赤いシャツをどうしても着ようとしなかった" ("He absolutely refused to wear the red shirt his
mother had bought") (Lesson 9, p. 46, pattern B).

### 「〜たものだ」(nostalgic habitual past — "used to ~")

Expresses a habitual action or state from the past, often with a nostalgic or reflective tone.
Source's own example: "私が子供のころには、黄色いセーターを着たりすると「男のくせに」と言われたものだ"
("When I was a child, if I wore a yellow sweater I'd be told 'that's not for boys'") (Lesson 9,
p. 47, pattern C).

### 「〜ように思う」(tentative impression — "it seems to me that ~")

A softened, hedged way of stating an impression or recollection, less assertive than a direct
statement. Source's own example: "ええ、どこかで聞いたように思ったんですが" ("Yes, I had the impression
I'd heard it somewhere") (Lesson 9, p. 47, pattern D).

### 「〜まま」(unchanged state — "while remaining as ~")

Indicates that an action or state continues unchanged while another event occurs, or that something
is left in a given condition. Source's own example: "私は電気をつけたまま、いつの間にか眠ってしまいま
した" ("I fell asleep with the light left on, before I knew it") (Lesson 10, p. 52, pattern A).

### 「つまり〜ということだ」("in other words, that means ~")

Used to restate or draw out the implication of a prior statement in plainer terms — a confirming
paraphrase. Source's own example: "結婚式はあたたかくなってからしようと思っているんです。" / "つまり、
一月、二月ではないということですね" ("'I'm thinking of having the wedding once it warms up.' / 'In
other words, not January or February, right?'") (Lesson 10, p. 52, pattern B).

### 「〜から〜にかけて」("from ~ through/spanning ~")

Marks a span across time or space, without the sharper boundaries implied by から〜まで. Source's
own example: "六月から七月にかけて雨が多いです" ("It rains a lot from June through July") (Lesson 10,
p. 53, pattern C).

### 「〜やら〜やら」(exasperated listing — "what with ~ and ~")

Lists two (or more) things, often troublesome or chaotic, with a nuance of being overwhelmed by
their combination. Source's own example: "急にお客が来たので、急いでへやを片付けるやらお茶を出すやら
で大変でした" ("Since guests arrived suddenly, what with hurriedly tidying the room and serving tea,
it was a real hassle") (Lesson 10, p. 53, pattern D).

### 「〜ものの」("although ~, even though ~")

A concessive connector similar to が/けれども but with a stronger sense that the following clause
runs contrary to a reasonable expectation set up by the first clause. Source's own example: "日本に
は来たものの、言葉が分からず困っている" ("Although I've come to Japan, I don't know the language and
am at a loss") (Lesson 11, p. 58, pattern A).

### 「〜につれて」("as ~, in proportion to ~")

Expresses that one change proceeds in step with another, gradually. Source's own example: "時間が
経つにつれて、何だか気味が悪くなってきた" ("As time passed, it somehow grew eerier") (Lesson 11,
p. 58, pattern B).

### 「〜によって」("by means of ~" / "according to ~")

Marks the means, method, or agent by which something is accomplished. Source's own example: "電話
によって、いろいろな情報を集める" ("Various kinds of information are gathered via telephone") (Lesson
11, p. 58, pattern C).

### 「〜として」("in the capacity of ~, as ~")

Marks the role or capacity in which a person/thing acts, distinct from として's more literal
"as" usage for describing a static attribute. Source's own prompt frames this with 田中先生は: e.g.
"学校では＿＿として働いています" ("At school, he/she works as ___") (Lesson 11, p. 59, pattern D).

### 「〜ば〜ほど」("the more ~, the more ~")

A correlative construction pairing a conditional ば-form with ほど to express proportional
intensification. Source's own example: "情報通信技術の開発が進めば進むほど、情報の管理がだんだん難しく
なってくる" ("The more information-communication technology advances, the harder information
management gradually becomes") (Lesson 11, p. 59, pattern E).

### 「〜ものですから」(explanatory reason, often apologetic — "because ~, you see")

A softer, more conversational way of giving a reason than から/ので, often used to excuse or explain
an action, frequently in apology. Source's own example: "急いでいるものですから、お先に失礼します"
("Since I'm in a hurry, please excuse me for going ahead") (Lesson 12, p. 64, pattern A).

### 「きっかけ」("the trigger/occasion for ~")

Used as a noun to identify the catalyzing event or reason behind a decision or change, typically in
the pattern 〜がきっかけで/きっかけなんです. Source's own example: "彼女にやめろと言われたのがきっかけな
んです" ("Her telling me to quit was what triggered it") (Lesson 12, p. 64, pattern B).

### 「〜ところ」(aspectual marker: about to / in the middle of / just finished)

Three related sub-patterns attach to different verb forms to mark a precise point in an action's
timeline: るところ ("just about to"), ているところ ("in the middle of"), たところ ("just finished").
Source's own examples: "今、出かけるところです" ("I'm just about to leave now"); "主人はおふろに入って
いるところですから" ("My husband is in the middle of bathing, so..."); "今、食べたところなんです" ("I
just finished eating") (Lesson 12, pp. 65, pattern C-1/C-2/C-3).

### 「〜にとって」("for ~, from the standpoint of ~")

Marks the perspective or standpoint from which a following evaluative statement is made — distinct
from に対して (directed at) or として (in the role of). Source's own example, drawn from the reading
passage: "田川さんにとって、山口さんを訪ねる月曜と木曜の午後は、とても楽しい一時なのである" ("For Ms.
Tagawa, the Monday and Thursday afternoons spent visiting Mr. Yamaguchi are a very enjoyable time")
(Lesson 12, p. 65, pattern D).

### 「〜はずだ」("should be, is expected to be ~" — confident supposition)

Expresses a strong, logically-grounded expectation about a fact or outcome, based on known
circumstances — distinct from a guess (でしょう) in its assumed certainty. Source's own example:
"二時前に家を出たそうだから、もうすぐ来るはずですよ" ("I hear he left the house before two, so he should
be here any minute") (Lesson 13, p. 70, pattern A).

### 「〜からといって、〜わけではない」("just because ~ doesn't mean ~")

Rejects an inference that might seem to follow from a stated premise — denies a false logical
implication. Source's own example: "毎日学校へ来ているからといって、日本語が上手になるわけではない" ("Just
because someone comes to school every day doesn't mean their Japanese gets good") (Lesson 13, p. 71,
pattern B).

### 「〜どころか〜さえ〜ない」("far from ~, not even ~")

A compounding double-emphasis construction: どころか dismisses a milder premise as far too modest,
and さえ〜ない escalates to deny even a more basic baseline. Source's own example: "漢字はいくつぐらい
読めますか。" / "漢字どころか、ひらがなさえまだ読めないんです" ("'About how many kanji can you read?' /
'Far from kanji, I can't even read hiragana yet'") (Lesson 13, p. 71, pattern C).
