## Grammar points

### Rule 16 — There are many types of hands; they all have the same meaning (いろいろな手があります／その意味は同じです)

A review/consolidation rule: the component meaning "hand" recurs across many different outlines —
手 (a normal hand with five fingers, seen at the left of 抱/招 from Rule 2), ナ/又 (as in 友, Rule 11,
and in 反/坂/桑/騒), 寸 (as in 村/寺, Rule 1). This rule gathers further hand-outlines: ナ (as in
左/右, where the hand holds a ruler or an article), ヨ (hand from above, as in 事/当), ク (hand from
above, as in 負/争/危, Rule 19), 又 (hand from below, as in 受), and ノツ (hand from above, as in
受/愛) — all different shapes, all meaning "hand." (pp. 83–87)

### Rule 17 — A combination of pictures; look at the entire picture (絵を組み合わせます／全体の絵を見てください)

Distinct from the stem+sign model (Rule 2): some kanji aren't built from a semantic "basic meaning +
narrowing sign," but are instead a single complete scene/picture that has been divided into two or
three recognizable parts for ease of writing, then reassembled — each part functions as a drawing
unit, not a meaning-unit in its own right. 東 ("east") is presented as one continuous scenic picture
(the sun behind a tree) split into 木+日; 夜 ("night") as a scene (a person, the moon, a covering
darkness) split into 亠+イ+夕. The rule also introduces 続け書き ("stroke continuation/blending"): when
two units share a vertical stroke at their junction (e.g. 田's and 木's verticals in 巣), that stroke
is written once, continuously, rather than as two separate overlapping strokes — a note on actual
handwriting stroke order, not just visual composition. (pp. 88–92)

### Rule 18 — Combining special features; consider the whole meaning (特色を組み合わせます／全体の意味を考えましょう)

A further refinement: a thing (a village, a nose-and-mouth scene, a wife's household role) actually
has many possible identifying features, but a kanji's designer selected only two (occasionally
three) of those features as diagnostic, discarding the rest. 村 ("village") could have been built
from "house" or "god" instead of "tree"+"hand," but those weren't chosen. This rule's examples (言
"language" = heart+mouth; 息 "breath" = nose+heart; 恥/妻/婦/筆/針/縄/絹) each show a deliberately
narrow, selective feature-combination standing in for a richer whole concept. (pp. 93–97)

### Rule 19 — Combinations become verbs; combinations also become adjectives (組み合わせが動詞になります／組み合わせが形容詞にもなります)

Extends Rules 2/9/10's compound-kanji logic explicitly to verbs and adjectives built by combining
two feature-units (as opposed to Rule 9's single-picture verbs or Rule 10's stem+dedicated-verb-sign
pattern): 休 (person+tree = to rest), 射 (body+hand = to shoot), 取 (ear+hand, referencing an old
Chinese battlefield trophy-taking custom = to take), 弔 (bow+pole, a mourning display = to mourn) for
verbs; 臭 (nose+big = foul-smelling), 細 (thread+ball-of-thread = slender), 易 (sun+streamer = easy),
危 (hand-from-above+cliff+squatting-person = dangerous) for adjectives. (pp. 98–102)

### Rule 20 — The outline of units changes; the meaning is the same (単位の形が変わります／その意味は同じです)

Two separate size/shape adaptations are named here. First, because every kanji is fit into a
uniform square regardless of its real stroke count, a unit occupying a kanji's upper/lower position
tends to widen while the same unit in a left/right position tends to elongate (worked example: the
日 "sun" unit is wide in 早 "early" (upper position) but tall/narrow in 明 "bright" (left position) —
same unit, same meaning, different proportion). Second, and more significantly, the same meaning-unit
can take on entirely different graphical outlines depending on its position or adjacent strokes —
not just resized but re-shaped: 手 ("hand") alone has the many different outlines surveyed in Rule 16
(手/ナ/又/寸/ヨ/ク/ノツ); 水 ("water") becomes シ (narrowed, three strokes) when placed at a kanji's
left side (seen in 酒); 刀 ("sword") becomes a vertically elongated リ-like stroke at a kanji's right
side (seen in 刈); 心 ("heart") becomes 忄 (standing upright) at a kanji's left side (seen in 忙).
Readers must learn to recognize a single underlying unit across its full range of surface outlines.
(pp. 103–107)
