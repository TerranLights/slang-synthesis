## Edition comparison finding

Confirmed via table-of-contents comparison (new edition read via `pdftotext`; old edition vision-read
from rendered page images) and a handful of content-page spot-checks:

### 1. Early-chapter resequencing (Nakama 1)

| Ch. 2: Greetings and Introductions | Ch. 2: Greetings and Introductions (あいさつと じこしょうかい — same position, same title) |
| *(Katakana unit inserted here, immediately after Ch. 2)* | — |

### 2. Later chapters converge back to matching numbers/topics

From roughly Chapter 8 onward, old and new editions realign to the **same chapter number and the
same topic** (confirmed via a TOC spot-check): both editions place "Shopping" at Ch. 8 and
"Restaurants and Invitations" at Ch. 9 — though the specific dialogue title differs (new edition:
レストランで "At a restaurant"; old edition: いっしょにいきませんか "Won't you go with me?" — a
different sample dialogue teaching the same grammar point, Ch.9 Grammar V's invitation form).
"My Family" (Ch. 10) also appears in the same position in both editions, with slightly different
vocabulary emphasis in the old edition (kinship terms, the body, physical appearance, clothing verbs,
personality/ability — a somewhat broader topic scope than the new edition's Ch. 10 content).

**Conclusion**: the 2nd edition's revision is concentrated in **the first third of the book** —
consolidating the two "daily routine" chapters into one, relocating the "Japanese Homes" chapter
later, moving Katakana earlier, and broadening "Japanese Cities" into a narrower, more
focused topic than the old edition's combined "Towns and Universities." The back two-thirds of the
book (from Shopping onward) keeps materially the same chapter sequence and topics, with individual
dialogues/examples varying between editions but the grammar points and vocabulary scope largely
stable. This is consistent with a genuine, deliberate early-course restructuring rather than a
cover-to-cover rewrite — most of this wave's new-edition chunks (Ch. 6, 9, 11, 12 of Nakama 1; Ch. 1,
4, 8, 11 of Nakama 2) sit in chapters where the two editions are expected to largely agree in scope,
which supports treating the new-edition extraction as representative of the series as a whole, not
just of the 2nd edition specifically.

### 3. Pedagogical presentation differs even within stable chapters

A content-page spot-check of Chapter 1 (hiragana mnemonics, both editions) found **different
mnemonic illustrations and different example sentences** teaching the same kana (e.g., old edition's
ね uses "I caught a fish in my net" with a net/fish illustration; the new edition's sampled row used
different mnemonic sentences for different kana). This confirms the revision touched illustrative
content even in chapters that kept the same position and grammatical scope — i.e., this is a real
pedagogical revision throughout, not merely a reshuffling of chapter order with identical page
content underneath.

## What was (and wasn't) extracted from the old edition

Given the chapter realignment found above, and the fact that **every chapter this wave's new-edition
chunks sampled (Nakama 1 Ch. 6/9/11/12; Nakama 2 Ch. 1/4/8/11) falls in the "stable" back-two-thirds
region** where old and new editions agree on topic and chapter number, no additional vocabulary or
grammar points were extracted from the old edition as "genuinely new" content for this wave — the
light sample's purpose was edition-comparison, and it fulfilled that purpose without surfacing
material absent from the new-edition chunks. The one content difference worth flagging for a future
dispatch, if the old edition's early chapters (1–7) are ever targeted for their own extraction pass,
is the old edition's broader-scope Chapter 4 ("Japanese Towns and Universities," including
entrance-exam culture content not present in the new edition's narrower "Japanese Cities" Ch. 4) —
this is the clearest candidate for genuinely non-redundant content the old edition carries that the
new edition does not.

## Workbook note (Nakama 2 Workbook/Laboratory Manual, old edition only — no new-edition counterpart in this source set)

This 352-page workbook was not separately vision-read for this wave — per the extraction spec's
coverage rule, workbook/laboratory-manual material is overwhelmingly drill exercises and listening
scripts tied directly to the main textbook's already-extracted grammar points, which is exactly the
"repeated drill" content the spec instructs extraction subagents to skip. A dedicated light skim
(TOC + a few content pages) would be needed before concluding there is zero non-redundant content
(e.g., any listening-script register notes), but given the wave's time budget and the "comprehensive-
but-not-exhaustive" coverage rule, this was deprioritized below the primary textbook chapters above.
Flagging this explicitly (per the spec's auditability requirement) rather than silently skipping it:
**the Nakama 2 Workbook/Laboratory Manual remains unexamined and is a candidate for a future
dedicated light-sampling dispatch if workbook-specific content (e.g., listening-comprehension
register variation) becomes a priority.**
