**Morpheme breakdown.**

> **oyaji gyagu (親父ギャグ)** = oyaji (親父, "old man/geezer") + gyagu (ギャグ, loanword "gag/joke")
> — a compositional slang coinage meaning "geezer pun" / groan-worthy pun, reflecting the
> stereotype that corny puns are chiefly told by older men.

## Grammar points

No grammar points are presented in this chapter — it is a vocabulary/slang-dictionary chapter
structured entirely around headword + romaji + script + example-sentence entries, plus several
short descriptive sidebar essays on social "stock character" archetypes. One naming-register system
is covered as vocabulary above rather than as grammar: the chapter's "Titles" sub-section catalogs
the honorific name-suffix paradigm (sensei/san/sama/kun/chan/chin/tan/pon) ordered from most formal
to most slangy — captured as individual rows above since each suffix is itself a lexical item with
its own usage-register note, not a productive grammatical rule.
