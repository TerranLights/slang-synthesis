**Morpheme breakdown (select compounds).**

> **kuidaore** (食い倒れ) = *kui* (食い, "eating," stem of *kuu/kuru* "to eat") + *daore* (倒れ,
> "collapsing/falling down," stem of *taoreru*) — literally "eating oneself into collapse,"
> the Osaka-associated food-bender custom.

> **kuwazu-girai** (食わず嫌い) = *kuwazu* (食わず, negative form "without eating," from *kuu* "to
> eat") + *girai* (嫌い, "dislike," from *kirau*) — "disliking without (having) eaten," i.e.
> prejudging a food (or, by extension, anything) without trying it; repurposed here as the
> name of a restaurant guessing-game.

> **kuinige** (食い逃げ) = *kui* (食い, "eating") + *nige* (逃げ, "fleeing," from *nigeru*) —
> "eat and flee," the dine-and-dash act.

## Cultural note — "Picky Eaters" (printed p. 147)

The chapter's closing section is a vignette, not a vocabulary list. It describes
*kuwazu-girai*, a guessing game played at *izakaya* and tapas-style restaurants: each
participant orders three dishes — two they genuinely like and one they secretly dislike —
then eats all three in front of the group while the others try to guess, from the eater's
reactions, which dish was the hated one. The source frames it as a way to learn friends'
culinary tastes (and to watch people's acting fail) while sharing small plates, including an
aside about fermented soybeans (*nattō*, implied but not separately glossed on this page) as
a common "dare" dish.
