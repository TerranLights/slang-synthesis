## Grammar points

### I. Talking about time using noun/adjective + 時, duration + 前／後

時 ("when, at the time of ~") is a noun that takes noun-の-noun or adjective-direct modification
(子供の時 "when I was a child"; ひまな時 "when I have free time"; いそがしい時 "when I am busy");
the negative ない behaves as an い-adjective here too (ひまじゃない時 "when I don't have free time").
Separately, duration + 前／後 (+ time-particle に) mark "ago"/"from now" (三年前に "three years ago";
三年ご "three years from now"). (p. 488–489)

### II. Talking about past experiences using 〜たことがある; listing activities with 〜たり〜たりする

**〜たことがある** marks lived experience (as opposed to a simple past event with 〜ました): 私は
ミュージカルを見たことがあります "I have [had the experience of] seeing a musical," contrasted with
見ました "I saw [it, a specific past event]." The source notes this construction is typically used
for experience in the **not-so-recent past**, not events as recent as 昨日/一昨日. **〜たり〜たりする**
lists representative (non-exhaustive) activities, parallel to Ch. 7's noun-listing や: 食べたり
飲んだりしました "I did things like eating and drinking" — implies other unlisted activities occurred
too, unlike an exhaustive と-list. (p. 492–496)

### III. Expressing frequency using time-span に frequency/duration/amount

Time-frame + に + frequency/duration/amount expresses rate: 一週間に三回ぐらい読みます "I read about
three times a week." The time-frame slot accepts any duration expression (分, 時間, 日, 週間, か月,
年). (p. 497–499)

### IV. Expressing hearsay using the plain form + そうだ

This introduces a **second, distinct そうだ construction**, contrasted explicitly against Ch. 9's
verb/adjective-stem + そうだ (inferential, "looks like"). Hearsay そうだ attaches to the **plain
form** (not the stem) of verbs/adjectives, including the plain past, and means "I heard/I've heard":
おまつりはおもしろいそうだ "I've heard the festival is interesting" (hearsay) vs. Ch.9's
おまつりはおもしろそうだ "The festival looks interesting" (inference, stem-only). Often paired with
〜によると ("according to ~"): スミスさんによると、ハロウィンはアメリカのおまつりだそうだ.

**Explicit near-homophone register warning from the source**, directly relevant to slang-mechanics
work on morphologically-adjacent-but-semantically-distinct forms: with い-adjectives the two
constructions differ by only one syllable and "can sound very similar" — おいしそうですね ("it looks
delicious," inferential) vs. おいしいそうですね ("I heard it's delicious," hearsay) — the source
states explicitly that listeners must attend closely to pronunciation to distinguish them.
(p. 500–502)
