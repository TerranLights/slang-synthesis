## Grammar points

### §9.1 What are adverbs? (category-flexibility)

Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs, and answer when/where/how/to-what-extent (p. 184). The source flags upfront that — as in English — some Japanese words shift part-of-speech by context: `kyō` "today" functions as an adverb in `Kyō Tōkyō ni ikimasu` "Today I'm going to Tokyo" but as a noun-predicate-argument in `Kyō wa nichiyōbi desu` "Today is Sunday" (p. 184).

### §9.2 Adverbs of place (recycled demonstratives)

The `koko`/`soko`/`asoko`/`doko` row of the ko-so-a-do demonstrative system (§5.5) does double duty as the adverb-of-place set — no separate adverb-of-place vocabulary exists beyond this reused paradigm (p. 185).

### §9.3 Adverbs of time

A closed, high-frequency inventory of time adverbs is given (full list in the vocabulary table above), covering relative-day reference (`kinō`/`kyō`/`ashita`), habituality (`mainichi`/`maiasa`/`maiban`), and a scatter of immediacy/recency adverbs (`ima`, `saikin`, `yagate`, `sugu`, `mada`) (p. 186).

### §9.4 Adverbs of manner and degree

Covers two overlapping subtypes. **Manner adverbs derived from adjectives** simply reuse the adverbial-form derivation already given for each adjective class (-i adjectives: stem+`-ku`; -na adjectives: bare form+`ni`) — this section adds no new derivation rule, just cross-references §8.2-3/§8.3-3 (p. 187). A second set of manner adverbs is, like the place adverbs, recycled from the ko-so-a-do demonstrative system's own adverb row (`kō`/`sō`/`ā`/`dō`, p. 187–188) — confirming that this four-term deictic stem system (first fully tabulated in §5.5) is genuinely productive across at least three separate grammatical categories (pronoun, place-adverb, manner-adverb) in this book. Beyond these two derived subtypes, the chapter closes with a large closed-class inventory of non-derived degree/frequency/certainty adverbs (p. 188, full list in the vocabulary table above) — these cover frequency (`itsumo`, `tokidoki`, `tamani`, `tabitabi`, `mata`), degree/intensity (`totetemo`/`totemo`, `taihen`, `takusan`, `sukoshi`, `chotto`, `zenzen`, `nakanaka`), and epistemic modality/probability (`tabun`, `taitei`, `kitto`). Two items are worth flagging for later register/mechanics work: `zenzen` requires a negated verb for its core "(not) at all" meaning, structurally similar to (though semantically more straightforward than) the earlier `hodo`/`shika` negative-polarity-with-affirmative-sense pattern from §6.2; and `ikaga` is explicitly given as the polite-register counterpart of plain `dō` "how" (p. 188) — a small but genuine lexical register pair, comparable in kind to the verb-level honorific/humble suppletive pairs from §7.16, just at the adverb level.
