## Grammar points

### I. Using honorific expressions to show respect

**Core finding, directly relevant to this project's register-mechanics work**: the source explicitly
establishes that honorific/humble register operates on an **axis independent of the plain/polite
(だ／です) axis** already taught throughout the series. Two close friends speaking in fully casual,
plain-form Japanese to each other can *simultaneously* use an honorific verb when the sentence's
*topic* (not the listener) is a social superior: 木村：リーさん、山田先生、もうお帰りになった。
("Mr. Li, has Prof. Yamada gone home?") — plain-form もう...た sentence structure, but おかえりに
なった (honorific) for the professor's action. **Honorific choice tracks who the sentence is *about*,
while plain/polite choice tracks who the sentence is *addressed to* — two independent register
dials, not one combined formality scale.** Social superiority is explicitly glossed as determined by
age, social status, experience, **or the benefactor-recipient relationship** (a favor's recipient
uses honorifics toward the giver) — and honorific forms are also the default toward an unfamiliar
listener (phone operators, hotel/restaurant clerks).

**Productive formation rules**: regular verbs form honorifics as お + verb stem + に + なる
(待つ→お待ちになる); verbal-noun+する verbs form them as お／ご + verbal noun + なさる
(連絡する→ご連絡なさる). Many high-frequency verbs instead use **irregular suppletive honorific
forms** (listed in the comparison table above) rather than the productive pattern. When a verb
carries an auxiliary (〜ている, 〜てくれる), typically **only the auxiliary needs to become
honorific** (読んでいる→読んでいらっしゃる; 貸してくれる→貸して下さる), though several common
exceptions exist (行っている→おいでだ／いらっしゃっている; 寝ている→お休みだ).

**Honorific requests**: お + verb stem + 下さい (お待ち下さい "please wait"); verbal-noun+する verbs
use お／ご + verbal noun + 下さい (ご連絡下さい); irregular honorifics ending in 〜になる swap directly
to 下さい (おいでになる→おいで下さい); other irregular honorifics use their て-form + 下さい
(いらっしゃる→いらっしゃって下さい). 下さいませんか／いただけませんか make any of these politer
still — directly recalling the politeness-tier work already logged for 〜てもらう／いただく in
`080g`.

**Honorific adjectives**: お + adjective (お上手 "skilled, honorific"); some な-adjectives take ご
instead (立派→ご立派). The source notes explicitly that **some adjectives have no honorific form at
all** — おもしろい, 大きい, 有名な are given as examples — paralleling the lexical gaps already noted
in the suppletive-verb table above. (p. 490–495)

### II. Using humble expressions to show respect

Humble verbs lower the speaker's (or speaker's in-group's) own action when that action affects or
relates to a social superior — the mirror-image of honorific verbs, which raise the superior's own
action. (Formation details and further examples fall past this chunk's sampled page range; the
comparison table above already captures the paradigm's full shape as presented earlier in the
chapter's vocabulary section.) (p. 496+)
