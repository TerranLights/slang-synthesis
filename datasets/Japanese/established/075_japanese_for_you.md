## Grammar points

### 1. Conjoining adjectives
To string two or more adjectives together, the non-final adjective changes ending: -い adjectives
drop い and add -くて; -な adjectives add -で. Example: 明るくて、いい部屋です「It's a bright, nice
room.」(p. 4–5)

### 2. Sentences ending with でしょう
でしょう expresses the speaker's opinion/belief ("it must be...," "I think...") and often invites
the addressee's agreement. The plain form precedes でしょう, but だ itself drops: 静かでしょう「Don't
you think it's quiet?」(p. 5)

### 3. XXはYYが — adjectival description
YY is filled by a part or possession of XX, describing a property of the topic XX — the core
"part-whole" description pattern (この部屋は天井が低いです「This room has a low ceiling」). (p. 5)

### 4. ~やすい、~にくい
Verb stem (ます-form minus ます) + やすい/にくい forms a compound adjective meaning "easy to.../hard
to...": 読みやすい/読みにくい. (p. 6)

### 5. ~そうだ (appearance)
Verb/adjective stem + そうだ means "looks like.../appears to...": 倒れそうだ, 元気そうだ. Two
irregular exceptions: いい→よさそうだ, ~ない→~なさそうだ. (p. 6)

### 6. V-ている
Indicates an action in progress or a resulting state: 汚れています「is (in a state of being)
dirty」, 作っています「is making」. (p. 7)

### 7. Particle が (descriptive/scene-setting subject marker)
When describing a scene or state as observed, が marks the subject: ふすまがちょっと汚れています。(p. 7)

### 8. Adjective + する/なる
(-い adj.)-く + する/なる or (-な adj.)-に + する/なる mean "to make X" / "to become X" respectively:
部屋をきれいにしてください「Please make the room clean.」(p. 7)

### 9. Contraction: V-ている → V-てる
In conversational Japanese, いる commonly contracts, dropping い: 壊れている→壊れてる. Flagged as a
genuine colloquial-register compression mechanism. (p. 8)

### 10. V-ているところだ
Focuses on an action taking place right now: 待っているところです「We are in the middle of
waiting.」(p. 8)

### 11. に、で、を (particle review)
に marks the place of existence (with ある/いる); で marks the place an action/event occurs in; を
marks a path traversed (running, walking, passing through). (p. 8)

### 12. ~んだから・~んですから
Used when the speaker presents a reason emphatically, especially a fact already apparent to the
hearer, or (when telling a new fact about the self) with a nuance of "honestly," "it's really
true": 貧乏いたいけど、お金がないんだから、しかたがありません。(p. 8)
