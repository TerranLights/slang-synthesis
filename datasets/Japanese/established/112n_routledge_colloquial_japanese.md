**Coverage note:** Unit 14 is built entirely around further applications of the te-form introduced
in Unit 13: giving directions (a full movement/transit vocabulary set plus the -me ordinal-position
suffix), stating a sequence of actions in succession ([V-te], [V-te]... with tense carried only by
the final verb), [V-te]+kara for emphasizing the completion of one action before the next, and
three distinct uses of [V-te]+imasu — present-progressive action-in-progress, a durable
resultant-state reading (with a vocabulary table systematically contrasting the action/state verb
pairs: mochimasu/motte imasu, sumimasu/sunde imasu, shirimasu/shitte imasu, tachimasu/tatte imasu,
suwarimasu/suwatte imasu, iremasu/irete imasu, kekkon shimasu/kekkon shite imasu — with the
irregular negative shirimasen, not *shitte imasen, for "not know"), and habitual/ongoing action.

## Grammar points

### Giving directions: movement vocabulary and -me ordinals
A dedicated direction-giving vocabulary set (michi/kado/hashi/kōsaten/shingō/tsugi plus the verbs
norimasu/orimasu/norikaemasu/watarimasu/magarimasu) is given with picture-matched particle usage:
basu ni norimasu ("get on a bus", ni), basu o orimasu ("get off a bus", o), michi/hashi o
watarimasu ("cross a street/bridge", o), kado o (migi ni) magarimasu ("turn (right) at a corner").
The -me suffix marks ordinal position in a numbered sequence (hitotsu-me/futatsu-me/mittsu-me (no
kado) "the first/second/third (corner)"). p. 167.

### Actions in succession: [V-te], [V-te]...
Chaining multiple te-form verbs states a sequence of actions happening (or having happened) one
after another; only the final verb in the chain carries tense/polarity marking — okite, tabete,
ikimasu ("I wake up, eat and go"). This construction can end not just in a plain masu/mashita verb
but in any of the te-form-adjacent patterns already learned (V-tai desu, [V-te]+mo ī desu, [V-te]+
kudasai), letting a whole compound sentence end in a desire, permission request, or polite
request. p. 168.

### After doing...: [V-te] + kara
Attaching kara to a verb's te-form emphasizes that the first action is fully completed before the
second begins — stronger/more explicit than the bare action-succession chaining above ("Watashi wa
itsumo ha o migaite kara nemasu" — "I always brush my teeth and then go to bed"). This reuses the
same kara morpheme already seen marking reason-clauses in Unit 10, now in a purely temporal
(not causal) sense — a genuine particle-polysemy case worth flagging alongside the earlier de/ga/
kara overloads noted in prior units. pp. 169-170.

### Describing an action in progress: [V-te] + imasu
The now-familiar te-form + imasu construction states that an action is currently (or was, at a
specified past reference time) ongoing: "Watashi wa ima kōhī o nonde imasu" ("I am drinking a cup
of coffee now"). p. 172.

### Describing a state: [V-te] + imasu (second sense)
The identical surface construction also expresses a durable resultant state rather than an
ongoing action, for a specific closed set of verbs whose action/state pairing is given
systematically in the vocabulary table: mochimasu/motte imasu ("hold"/"own, possess"), sumimasu/
sunde imasu ("[move to] live"/"live, reside"), shirimasu/shitte imasu ("get to know"/"know"),
tachimasu/tatte imasu ("stand up"/"be standing"), suwarimasu/suwatte imasu ("sit down"/"be
seated"), iremasu/irete imasu ("put in"/"have in"), kekkon shimasu/kekkon shite imasu ("marry"/"be
married"). A genuinely irregular exception is flagged in-text: the negative of shitte imasu
("know") is shirimasen, built from the action-verb's own negative rather than the expected
*shitte imasen — worth flagging alongside Unit 9's mō/mada-ni-wa-mo asymmetries and other
irregular-negative patterns accumulating across the course. pp. 173-174.

### Describing habitual action: [V-te] + imasu (third sense)
The same surface pattern once more describes a regularly/habitually performed activity rather
than a single ongoing event or static state: "Watashi wa Nihon no kaisha de hataraite imasu" ("I
work for a Japanese company"). This gives [V-te]+imasu three distinct readings
(progressive/stative/habitual) disambiguated only by context and verb semantics — a striking
case of grammatical polysemy worth flagging prominently for the mechanics-analysis phase. p. 175.
