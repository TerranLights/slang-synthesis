## Grammar points

### Abstract (non-pictorial) kanji for spatial/positional concepts

Alongside pictorially-derived kanji, a small group represents abstract ideas using simple shapes/lines rather than depicting an object: 上 ("above/on top" — a baseline with a "t"-shape above), 下 ("below/under" — the mirrored form with the "t"-shape beneath), and 中 ("inside/middle" — a box bisected by a vertical line). The book offers an alternative visualization of 上/下 as a plant's visible growth above ground vs. its roots below (p.78).

### 雨 (rain) as a recurring weather radical

雨 functions as a component/radical marking weather-related complex kanji: 雲 (cloud, "two cloud shapes" beneath the rain radical), 曇 (cloudy weather, cloud + 日 sun blocked out), 雪 (snow, literally "frozen rain," rain + a mountain-on-its-side shape evoking snow-covered peaks), 雷 (thunder, rain + 田 rice field, where farmers are acutely aware of storms), and 電 (electric, a further development from 雷 depicting lightning flashing through a rice field) (p.79).

### Distinguishing visually similar kanji pairs via their mnemonic stories

The unit explicitly pairs up visually similar kanji and gives a differentiating mnemonic: 犬 ("dog," 大 + a short stroke for a hanging tongue) vs. 太 ("fat," 大 + a short stroke marking the widest part) (pp. 79–80).

### Two kunyomi readings for one kanji, continued

少 has two distinct kunyomi depending on sense: sukuna(i) for "few" and suko(shi) for "a little" — an explicit example (flagged by the source) that a single kanji's multiple kunyomi can carry genuinely different (if related) meanings, not just stylistic reading variants (pp. 79, 82).

### Adjective reading convention: bracket-marked okurigana

When citing an adjective's kunyomi, the book writes the portion that is spelled out in hiragana (okurigana) in brackets after the kanji-reading stem, e.g. taka(i) for 高い "tall/expensive" — the bracketed -i ending is the hiragana suffix actually written after the kanji in the real word (p.82).

### Kanji compounds built from this unit's new kanji plus earlier units' kanji

Beyond compounds using only Unit 6's new kanji, the unit explicitly demonstrates combining them with already-learned kanji from Units 1–5 (年, 学, 女, 車, 手, 人, 口, 目, 聞, 話, 中) to build further compounds, e.g. 新聞 ("newly heard" = newspaper, combining new 新 with the Unit-4 verb 聞 "to hear") and 電話 ("electric talk" = telephone, combining new 電 with the Unit-4 verb 話 "to talk") (p.81).

### Milestone summary (Units 1–6)

By the end of Unit 6 the learner has covered 97 single kanji, 100 kanji compounds (including 13 surnames), and the complete 46-symbol hiragana syllabary (p.85).
