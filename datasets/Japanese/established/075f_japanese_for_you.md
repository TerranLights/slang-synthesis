## Grammar points

### 1. Exaltation (尊敬語 / 謙譲語)
Exaltation shows deference to a person appearing in a sentence, via two distinct mechanisms:
**Subject Exalting** (raises the person in subject position: お+verb-stem+になる, e.g. 書く→お書き
になる) and **Non-subject Exalting** (the subject takes a humble stance, which has the effect of
exalting someone else in the sentence: お+verb-stem+する, e.g. 貸す→お貸しする). A closed set of
common verbs instead take irregular, memorized special forms rather than the productive patterns
above:

| Plain | Subject Exalting | Non-subject Exalting |
|---|---|---|
| いる／行く／来る | いらっしゃる、おいでになる | — |
| 見る | 御覧になる | 拝見する |
| 言う | おっしゃる | 申し上げる |
| する | なさる | — |
| 食べる | 召し上がる | いただく |
| 飲む | 召し上がる／お飲みになる | いただく |
| 上げる | — | 差し上げる |
| くれる | くださる | — |
| もらう | おもらいになる | いただく |
| 訪ねる・尋ねる (visit, ask) | お訪ねになる／お尋ねになる | 伺う／お訪ねする／お尋ねする／お伺いする |
| 会う | お会いになる | お目にかかる／お会いする |
| 寝る | お休みになる | — |
| 知っている | 御存じだ | 存じあげている |
| だ (copula) | でいらっしゃる | — |

This full-paradigm honorific/humble verb system is the lesson's core content and the single
richest register-stratification data point in the book so far. (pp. 92–95)

### 2. Stylization
Stylization — independent of Exaltation — shows deference or familiarity to the *listener*
(rather than to a third person referred to in the sentence), via a three-tier register system:

| Tier | Used when | Verb/adj. form | Example (いる) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Familiar | speaking to family/friends | plain form | いる |
| Formal | broadly used default | -ます form (Noun/Adj.+です) | います |
| Very formal | public announcements, formal phone calls, hotel staff, high-class restaurant waitstaff | a distinct special vocabulary set (not a productive suffix) | おります |

This explicit three-tier formality model — with its own vocabulary at the "very formal" tier —
is a directly reusable usage-tier reference distinct from, and orthogonal to, Exaltation (Note 1).
(p. 95)

### 3. お + verb-stem + です
A shorter alternative to the full Subject Exalting お+stem+になる pattern, used in everyday polite
speech (e.g. in telephone openers and service contexts). (p. 96 area, paraphrased from dialogue
usage)

### 4. お／ご — exalting prefixes
お generally attaches to native Japanese (和語) nouns and ご to Sino-Japanese (漢語) nouns to form
polite/exalting noun forms (お荷物, ご経験). Usage is explicitly noted as having a gendered skew in
casual speech — women use these prefixes more broadly and on more nouns than men do in informal
registers. (p. 96)

### 5. Verb (non-past plain form) ように
Reviewed again in this lesson's drills in the sense of expressing purpose/a goal state ("so
that..."), extending Lesson 4 Note 7's quoting use to a plain purpose-clause function. (p. 97)

### 6. ~ことにする
"To decide to...". Distinguished from the passive-feeling ~ことになっている (Lesson 5 Note 8, "it has
been decided that...") by its active, self-determined nuance — the subject is the one making the
decision. (p. 97)

### 7. ~ちゃう／~じゃう (contraction of ~てしまう／~でしまう)
A colloquial contraction expressing completion or an unintended/regrettable result, extremely
common in casual spoken Japanese: 食べてしまう→食べちゃう, 飲んでしまう→飲んじゃう. Flagged as a core
colloquial-compression mechanism parallel to Lesson 1's ~ている→~てる and Lesson 3's ~ては→~ちゃ.
(p. 97)
