**Morpheme breakdown (agglutinative compound forms).** Japanese verb/adjective morphology is
agglutinative; several forms in this tale stack multiple grammaticalized morphemes onto a single verb
stem, where an atomic gloss would hide real compositional structure:

> **たいせつにそだてましょう** (p.3, dialogue) = たいせつ (precious/important, na-adj. stem) + に
> (adverbializing particle) + そだて- (stem of そだてる "to raise") + -ましょう (polite volitional
> suffix, "let's ~") — "let's raise [her] carefully." Demonstrates na-adjective-to-adverb derivation
> stacked with the polite volitional.

> **かがやきはじめ** (p.7) = かがやき- (stem of かがやく "to shine") + はじめ- (stem of はじめる "to
> begin," here as a suffixed compound-verb element) — "began to shine." A productive Japanese
> compound-verb pattern (V-stem + はじめる) marking inchoative aspect, distinct from a separate
> clause "it began, and it shone."

> **いってしまいました** (p.8) = いっ- (stem of いく "to go," with the irregular て-form sound change
> いく→いって) + -て (connective) + しまい- (stem of しまう "to finish/put away," here grammaticalized)
> + -まし- (polite stem of ます) + -た (past tense) — "went away (completely/with finality)." The
> ~てしまう construction here marks completive aspect with an emotional nuance of irreversibility/
> regret — Kaguyahime is gone for good, which the grandparents cannot prevent (see the following
> sentence, どうすることもできませんでした).

## Grammar points

### むかしむかし — folk-tale opening formula

The tale opens (p.2) with むかしむかし ("long long ago"), the standard fixed formula that opens
nearly all Japanese folk tales (mukashi-banashi), directly parallel to English "once upon a time."
Functions as a single frozen adverbial unit, not a compositional "long ago, long ago."

### ~と as a narrative conditional/temporal connective

Used twice in near-identical frames: おじいさんがたけやぶへいくと、きらきらひかるたけがありました
("when the old man went to the bamboo grove, there was a sparkling bamboo," p.2) and
おじいさんがたけをきると、なかには...ねむっていました ("when the old man cut the bamboo, inside...
[she] was sleeping," p.3). This narrative use of と marks "when X happens, Y [is discovered to be
the case]" — a factual/sequential connective distinct from the hypothetical-conditional reading と
can also carry; here it signals a plot-advancing discovery.

### ~ていました — past progressive/continuous

ねむっていました ("was sleeping," p.3) uses the standard ~ている (progressive/stative) construction
in its polite past form ~ていました, describing an ongoing state discovered at a narrative moment.

### Quotation with と + いう/いいました

Direct speech throughout is marked with「...」brackets followed by と + a speech verb, e.g.
「どうかわたしとけっこんしてください。」といいました is implied by context (the explicit と is elided
in some lines, e.g. p.4's 「どうか...ください。」おじいさんとおばあさんはおおよろこびです. follows the
quote directly) — the quotation particle と is the standard marker binding reported/direct speech to
its governing verb in Japanese narrative prose.

### ~てください — polite request

「どうかわたしとけっこんしてください。」(p.4) — the standard polite request construction, V-stem
て-form + ください ("please do X"), softened further here by the sentence-initial どうか ("please,
I beg you").

### ~そうに — "appearing to be ~" (hearsay/appearance -sou)

かなしそうにいいました ("said [it], looking sad," p.5) attaches そう to the stem of the i-adjective
かなしい (かなし- + そう), producing an "appears to be / looks like" reading, then adverbializes with
に to modify いいました. Distinct from the hearsay そう that attaches to full plain-form predicates.

### ~けれど — concessive "but, although"

はなれるのはとてもつらいけれど、つぎのまんげつのよるに...くるのです ("although it's very painful to
be separated, on the night of the next full moon... [they] will come," p.5) — けれど links a
conceded clause to a following clause that proceeds despite it; a slightly softer/more literary
register than plain が in this function.

### はならぬ — classical/literary negative prohibition

「うつくしいひめをつきにかえしてはならぬ。」("The beautiful princess must not be returned to the
moon," p.6) — spoken as the emperor's order. ~てはならぬ is the classical/literary equivalent of
modern ~てはならない ("must not do X"); its use here register-marks this line as a formal command
from authority, distinct from the plain narrative register surrounding it (one of the tale's few
explicit register shifts).

### Compound verb formation (V-stem + auxiliary verb)

Several verbs in this tale stack a second verb onto a stem to add aspectual/directional meaning
(でてくる "come out," p.3; かがやきはじめ "began to shine," p.7; いってしまいました "went away [for
good]," p.8) — see the Morpheme breakdown section above for the two fully decomposed examples. This
V-stem+V compounding is a core, highly productive feature of Japanese verb morphology.

### ~ことができる/できません — potential "can/cannot do X"

どうすることもできませんでした ("there was nothing [they] could do," p.8) nominalizes する with こと
and combines it with できる's negative past, できませんでした, yielding the idiomatic "could do
nothing about it" — the tale's closing line.
