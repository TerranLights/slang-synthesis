## Grammar points

### Politeness Level (PL) tagging system (source's own scale)

Mangajin's own PL1-PL4 scale marks each example dialogue's politeness register explicitly (PL1
being the most casual/rude, PL4 the most humble/formal), independent of this project's Usage Tier
taxonomy. Treated here as the explicit register annotation the coverage rule calls out — recorded
per-entry above where a line is directly tagged. (p.13, referenced throughout.)

### -i adjective → adverb (-ku)

Adjectives ending in -i form adverbs by changing -i to -ku: 早い (hayai, "quick") → 早く (hayaku,
"quickly"). Same pattern produces yoroshii → yoroshiku. (p.14)

### o- honorific prefix

The prefix o- attaches to nouns as a general honorific/politeness marker (o-negai, o-cha). Not
every noun takes it, and it does not have one single fixed meaning beyond softening/elevating.
(p.14)

### -masu stem as noun

The stem of a -masu verb (minus -masu) can function as a noun: negau/negaimasu → (o-)negai "a
request." (p.14)

### itashimasu vs. shimasu (humble vs. plain-polite)

*Itashimasu* is the humble form of *suru*, usable only for the speaker's own actions (never for
someone else's) and marks PL4 rather than PL3 ordinary-polite *shimasu*. (p.15-16)

### sumu → sumimasen semantic bridge

*Sumimasen* is explained as the negative of a verb literally meaning "end/be settled," so its
core meaning is "this isn't over (yet)" — i.e., an unresolved debt, whether of apology (I did
something wrong) or of gratitude (you did something for me). This single root explains why the
same word covers "I'm sorry," "excuse me," and "thank you." (p.20)

### -nasai command softening

Commands formed with -nasai (orinasai, dokinasai) are derived from the honorific verb nasaru and
read as softer/more child-directed than the bare imperative (oriro, doke). (p.21)
