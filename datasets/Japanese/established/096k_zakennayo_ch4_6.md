## Grammar points

This source is a slang dictionary/phrasebook, not a grammar reference — no systematic grammar
points are presented in this page range. A small number of genuinely grammatical/pragmatic notes
embedded in vocabulary glosses are captured here for completeness:

### `jan` as reinforcing (not negating) particle

The source explicitly flags a pragmatics point worth preserving: `jan`, though historically a
contraction of `ja nai` (a negative construction, "is it not"), functions colloquially as a tag
that *reinforces* the preceding statement rather than negating it (p. 52) — e.g. "rakki jan"
("lucky, right?") does not mean "not lucky."

### `domo` as a maximally polysemous core term

The source singles out `domo` (p. 61) as "probably the single most versatile word in Japanese,"
able to mean "Hi!," "Thank you," "I'm taking leave," and more depending on context — worth noting
as a register-neutral core-vocabulary item that nonetheless carries heavy pragmatic/contextual
load.

### `gei`/"art" homophony

The source notes (p. 63) that `gei` ("gay") is a homophone of the standard word for "art," and
that Japanese speakers have widely observed/played on this overlap — e.g. *gei no kai*
("artistic circles") doubling as a euphemism for "homosexual circles." This is a genuine
wordplay/euphemism-formation mechanism worth flagging for the later slang-synthesis mechanics
analysis phase.
