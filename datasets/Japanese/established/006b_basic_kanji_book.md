## Grammar points

### Counting things vs. counting people/years — native (ひとつ/ふたつ…) vs. Sino-Japanese (いち/に…) number series

Lesson 3's detail tables make explicit, kanji by kanji, that Japanese numbers 1-10 carry two parallel
series: a native kun-yomi series used as a generic counter for objects (一つ/ひとつ, 二つ/ふたつ, 三つ/
みっつ … 十/とお), and the Sino-Japanese on-yomi series (いち、に、さん…じゅう) used for counting people,
years, dates, money, and compounding with other kanji (一人/ひとり and 二人/ふたり are kun-yomi-derived
irregular exceptions for "one/two people," after which 三人/さんにん onward reverts to the on-yomi
series). The irregular day-of-month readings 六日(むいか), 八日(ようか) are flagged in the source itself
with an asterisk, implicitly marking them as exceptions to the otherwise regular Sino-Japanese-plus-日
pattern (e.g. 三日/みっか, 四日/よっか, 五日/いつか, 九日/ここのか, 十日/とおか all instead use the native
kun-yomi series for dates 1-10, a pattern distinct from both the generic object-counter series and the
plain Sino-Japanese series — the date-reading series for days 1-10 of a month is its own irregular,
memorized set).

### Price/quantity question pattern: "(counter) いくらですか"

The greengrocer applied-vocabulary page models a recurring question frame: [item] は、一つ/一本
いくらですか。 ("How much is one [item]?"), varying the counter word by the shape class of the item
being counted — 一つ (generic/round items: apples, oranges, grapes, watermelon, tomatoes, cabbage) vs.
一本 (long cylindrical items: bananas, carrots, cucumbers). This is the first explicit appearance of
Japanese's noun-classifier/counter-word system in this source.
