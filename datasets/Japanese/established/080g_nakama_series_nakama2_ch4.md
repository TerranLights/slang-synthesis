## Grammar points

### I. Expressing/inquiring about factual knowledge using the clause か（どうか）

Embedded information-questions end plain-form + か (だ dropped for copula/な-adjectives, paralleling
でしょう／かもしれません formation); embedded yes-no questions use かどうか instead of bare か.
Common governing verbs: 知っている, 見る, 聞く, 分かる, かんがえる, 教える, 忘れる, きめる,
おぼえている. (p. 186–189)

### II. Expressing movement away from/toward the speaker using 〜ていく and 〜てくる

〜ていく marks movement away from the speaker's current position (持っていく "to take," lit.
"hold and go"); 〜てくる marks movement toward the speaker (持ってくる "to bring"). The source notes
Japanese いく／くる track the **speaker's deictic center**, not simply English "go/come" — when
called, an English speaker says "I'm coming" but Japanese says すぐ行くよ ("I'm going," matching the
addressee's frame, not the speaker's).

**Explicit colloquial contraction, directly relevant to slang-mechanics work**: in casual speech the
い of 〜ていく is dropped — 作っていきますよ → 作ってくよ; 持っていって下さい → 持ってって; この
パターンの縮約 again parallels the 〜ている→〜てる and 〜てはいけない→〜ちゃいけない contractions
already logged in `080f` and `080d` — a recurring family of vowel/syllable-drop reductions in casual
register that this project's slang-mechanics phase should treat as one connected phenomenon rather
than isolated cases. (p. 189–191)

### III. Expressing a desire for someone to do something: 〜てもらう／いただく and 〜てほしい

**〜てもらう／〜ていただく** (て-form + "to receive" + たい) expresses wanting someone to do
something; **いただく is explicitly the polite version of もらう**, "used toward a superior and/or
in a formal situation" — a direct source-marked politeness-tier pair. Adding と思います or んです
softens/politens further without changing the core request. The potential form (もらえませんか／
いただけませんか) turns either into an actual request.

**Explicit near-equivalence note, high-value for register mechanics**: the source states **the
politeness level of 〜てくれませんか and 〜てもらえませんか is "roughly the same,"** as is
〜てくださいませんか vs. 〜ていただけませんか — and that **the choice between these pairs "tends to
depend on personal preferences, regional differences, stylistic variations, and generation."** This
is a rare explicit acknowledgment in the textbook that near-synonymous grammaticalized forms can
vary by sociolect/generation/region even within "standard" polite Japanese — directly relevant
groundwork for how this project's synthesized slang might later layer onto existing register
variation rather than only onto single fixed forms.

**〜てほしい** (て-form + ほしい "want") expresses wanting someone else to act, with the actor marked
by に (often omitted); the source explicitly states **〜てほしい cannot be used to request action
from a social superior — use 〜ていただきたい instead** — a hard register ceiling on this
construction. Negating the desire: 〜てほしくない／〜てもらいたくない／〜ないでほしい (three
near-equivalent surface forms for "I don't want you to ~"). (p. 192–196)

### IV. Expressing willingness using 〜ましょう／ましょうか

Reuses Nakama 1 Ch.9's suggestion form to instead express the **speaker's** willingness to act for
someone (明日電話しましょうか "Shall I call you tomorrow?"). **Casual-register substitute, source-
documented**: plain volitional forms (行こう, 食べよう, 作ろう) replace ましょう in casual speech,
optionally emphasized with 私が／ぼくが. **Explicit benefactive-register restriction**: て-form +
あげましょう(か)／あげよう(か) explicitly marks the speaker as doing the listener a favor, and the
source states this form **"should not be used with a social superior, because offering a favor to a
superior sounds arrogant in Japanese"** — a benefactive-verb politeness ceiling structurally parallel
to Grammar III's てほしい restriction above. (p. 196–199)

### V. Expressing time limits using までに

までに marks a deadline by which the main-clause action must be completed (明日までに宿題を
しなければならない "I must do my homework by tomorrow"), taking a plain-present-form clause. The
source explicitly distinguishes までに from 前に: 前に is purely sequential (chronological "before"),
while までに specifically encodes a **deadline/must-complete-by** sense, which is why までに commonly
co-occurs with なければならない ("must"). (p. 199+)
