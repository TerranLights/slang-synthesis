## Grammar points

### sa (さ), sae (さえ), sura (すら) — emphasis and extreme-example particles
Paraphrased from pp.177–180, 185. "Sa" is a casual sentence-final emphasis marker, doubling as a brusque question-intensifier after an interrogative, and — in the "te sa"/"to sa" patterns — a hearsay quotative. "Sae" cites an extreme example to make a point about the norm ("even cats wouldn't look twice at this fish"), expresses a sufficient condition ("if only I had time..."), and marks additive emphasis ("not only is she not eating fish, she's not even drinking milk"). "Sura" functions almost identically to "sae" for extreme examples ("cannot even get three square meals a day").

### shi (し) — list/reason-connecting particle
Paraphrased from pp.180–182. Lists parallel situations ("he lost his job, and he split with his wife, and he's very depressed"), lists reasons culminating in a decision ("it's windy and it's started to snow, so let's not go out"), lists conflicting items ("I want new clothes, but I don't have money"), and — in the "aru mai shi" pattern — dismisses a premise as untrue ("it's not as if it's summer, aren't you cold?").

### shika (しか) and shimo (しも) — exclusive-negation and emphatic-universal particles
Paraphrased from pp.182–185. "Shika" always pairs with a following negative to mean "nothing but/only this" ("only a week left until finals") or "no choice but to..." ("no choice but to work on it all night"). "Shimo" appears in fixed emphatic patterns ("mada shimo", "ori shimo"), the universal "dare shimo" ("anyone/everyone"), and the partial-negation pattern "kanarazu shimo" + negative ("not necessarily").

### tara (たら) — casual topic/surprise particle
Paraphrased from p.186 (entry continues into part 12). Often contracted to "ttara". Casually marks a topic with an undertone of familiarity or mild criticism ("she's still in bed!"), and in the "ttara...nai" pattern expresses emphatic surprise at an unusual situation ("I've only had three hours sleep, I'm so sleepy!"). A third sense (impatience) begins on the next page and is covered in part 12.
