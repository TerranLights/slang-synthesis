**Source:** グループ・ジャマシイ (Group Jamashii; representative editor 砂川有里子/Sunakawa Yuriko),
*日本語文型辞典 — 教師と学習者のための* (*Nihongo Bunkei Ziten: A Dictionary of Japanese Grammar
with Sentence Patterns*), くろしお出版 (Kurosio Publishers), 1998. Front matter (pp. i–vii) read in
full. Main-entry body (printed pp. 2–~660, roughly PDF pages 7–332) sampled broadly across the full
50-on (gojuon) alphabetical range, since the book holds ~3000 entries across 355 scanned PDF pages —
true exhaustive extraction is out of scope for a single dispatch. Back matter (50-on index,
reverse-ending index, semantic/functional index, PDF pages ~333–355) was not extracted — it is a
pure page-number index with no independent grammatical content.

**Source has no text layer** (`pdfinfo` reports Producer: "Adobe Acrobat 6.02 Image Conversion
Plug-in"; `pdftotext` returns empty output on every page checked) — this entire file was vision-read
from rendered page images. **Format discovery:** each of the 355 PDF pages is a full two-physical-page
spread (confirmed on PDF page 1, the cover), not one printed page per PDF page — verified directly by
rendering rather than assumed from a fixed offset, per the extraction spec's page-range gotcha.

**Representative sampling plan actually used:** front matter in full (preface はじめに, the

page-spreads sampled at roughly even intervals through the main-entry body (PDF pages 7, 25, 50, 75,
100, 125, 150, 175, 200, 225, 250, 275, 300, 320, 330), giving headwords spanning あ→いつ→か→し→た
→て→と→な→に→は/ば→ま→よ→わ/を — i.e., genuine coverage of the full alphabetical range rather than a
single region.

**Non-redundant-supplement scoping against prior Japanese grammar references** (established/029–031
Makino/Tsutsui trilogy; 032/034/036 particle references; 037 Comprehensive Grammar): several sampled
headwords here cover territory already touched by those sources at the paradigm level (conditional
ば/と/たら/なら networks, benefactive てあげる/てもらう/てくれる, concessive としても/にしても). This
file does **not** re-paraphrase those prior sources' explanations. What is kept is this book's own
distinct contribution: (1) its own, independently authored example sentences (not reused from
Makino/Tsutsui or the particle dictionaries); (2) its explicit （誤）/（正）contrastive error-correction
pairs, a format largely absent from the paradigm-focused prior sources; (3) its systematic
cross-reference network (the → pointer system), which surfaces how many surface-distinct patterns the

pp. 486–487 collecting ってば/とおもえば/とすれば/とすると/ならば/にしてみれば and others as surface
variants); and (4) several headwords/senses not prominently covered in the prior sources at all

and まさか's quotative-disbelief and "worst case" noun-modifying senses). Nothing here was found to be
a near-total duplicate warranting an empty table.

## Grammar points

### Entry-structure legend (凡例, pp. ii–vii)

Every entry is built from up to three heading levels: 大見出し (the bracketed headword itself, e.g.
【うち】), 中見出し (a numbered sub-sense, 1/2/3), and 小見出し (a lettered sub-pattern within a
sub-sense, a/b/c). Grammatical restrictions that would be unwieldy to mark directly on a heading are
instead given in square brackets, e.g. [N のうちにはいらない]. Main headwords are ordered in strict
50-on order; numbered/lettered sub-headings are not independently alphabetized. A small number of
heavily-compounded forms (てはいけない, とする) are filed as their own headwords rather than requiring
the reader to find them under their base verb (いける, する) — a deliberate usability choice the editors
call out explicitly, since a standard kokugo dictionary would do the opposite.

### Notation system (記号一覧, pp. vi–vii)

This is the single most load-bearing piece of front matter for reading any entry in the book, so it is
recorded here in full rather than paraphrased loosely:

- **N** = noun phrase; **Na** = な-adjective stem; **A** = plain-form い-adjective, **A-** = its stem
  (used to build derived forms like A-そうだ); **V** = plain-form verb; **R-** = a verb's 連用形 (the
  ます-stem with ます removed).
- A systematic suffix notation builds every inflected form off these roots: V-る (dictionary form),
  V-た (past), V-て (te-form), V-ば (conditional), V-よう (volitional), V-れる/V-られる (potential/
  passive), V-させる (causative), V-ない (negative), V-たろう (past tentative), and the い-adjective
  equivalents A-い/A-く/A-くない/A-くて/A-かった/A-かろう/A-かったろう/A-ければ.
- Other symbols used throughout every entry: **／** = "or" (showing an either-or choice inside one
  bracketed form); **（ ）** = optional material; **〈 〉** = marks the semantic/functional label for a
  pattern (e.g. 〈推量〉"conjecture"); **[ ]** = the pattern's grammatical-attachment formula; **（例）**
  = an illustrative example embedded in the explanatory prose itself (distinct from the entry's own
  numbered example sentences); **（誤）**/**（正）** = an explicitly incorrect vs. corrected usage pair;
  **→** = "see also"; a subscript number on an identical bracketed headword (のに₁, のに₂) distinguishes
  two unrelated entries that happen to share a surface form.

### だけ₁'s connective flexibility (p. 189)

Worth noting as a genuinely distinct observation from this source: the book states explicitly that
だけ attaching after が/を can often drop that case particle entirely (Nだけが → Nだけ), while だけ
attaching after に or から instead has **two** valid orders — Nだけに and NにDけ — which are not always
freely interchangeable; the entry flags that a usage difference between the two orders can exist "in
some cases" without fully spelling out when, leaving it as an open nuance for the learner to notice
contextually rather than claiming a clean rule.

### と₁'s ban on volitional/imperative continuations (p. 288)

The general/natural-law conditional sense of と (sense 1, "X occurs, Y reliably follows") is
grammatically barred from taking a volitional (V-よう), imperative, request, or suggestion-type main
clause — the book gives an explicit （誤）/（正） pair (雨天だと明日の試合は中止しよう marked wrong;
雨天なら明日の試合は中止しよう marked correct) to show that なら must be substituted whenever the
speaker wants to issue a directive contingent on a hypothetical, precisely because と carries an
implication of near-certain, already-"in motion" realization that a volitional act cannot coherently
follow.

### てあげる's third-party-contact restriction (p. 239)

A genuinely fine-grained point this book captures with contrastive examples: V-てあげる cannot be used
when the benefactive action involves directly touching another person's body part or belongings as the
verb's grammatical object — おばあさんに手を引いてあげた (literally "pulled for grandmother's hand") is
marked （誤）, while おばあさんの手を引いてあげた (「hand」 reanalyzed as the possessed object of a
whole-part construction, with おばあさんに as the benefactive target) is marked （正）. The same
contrast is repeated for 荷物を持ってあげた→友達の荷物を持ってあげた and 手伝ってあげた→キムさんを
手伝ってあげた, establishing this as a systematic constraint rather than a one-off example.
