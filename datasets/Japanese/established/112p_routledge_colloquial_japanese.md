## Grammar points

### Verb dictionary form derivation
The third and final non-masu verb base taught in the course. **Group 1**: replace the final -imasu
with -u (kaerimasu→kaeru, kakimasu→kaku, nomimasu→nomu). **Group 2**: replace -masu with -ru
(tabemasu→taberu, misemasu→miseru, nemasu→neru). **Group 3**: fully irregular, memorized as a
pair — shimasu→suru, kimasu→kuru. pp. 191-192.

### Nominalizing a verb: [V-dic.] + koto
Parallel in function to Unit 13's [V-stem]+kata ("how to...") but structurally distinct: koto
attached to a verb's dictionary form (not its stem) turns the verb into a noun denoting the
action/activity itself, analogous to English "-ing" (kikimasu "listen" → kiku koto "listening";
benkyō shimasu "study" → benkyō suru koto "studying"). p. 192.

### Stating a hobby/dream: X wa [V-dic.+koto] desu
The nominalized verb fills Y in the already-familiar X-wa-Y-desu copula pattern: "Watashi no shumi
wa sakkā o suru koto desu" ("My hobby is playing football"); "Watashi no yume wa kashu ni naru
koto desu" ("My dream is becoming a singer"). p. 193.

### Liking/disliking/skill at an activity: X wa [V-dic.+koto] ga + adjective
The same nominalized-verb-as-Y pattern extends into the X-wa-Y-ga+adjective structure from Unit 12,
restricted to a closed adjective set: suki ("like"), kirai ("dislike"), jōzu ("good at"), heta
("poor at") — "Watashi wa Nihon no ongaku o kiku koto ga suki desu" ("I like listening to Japanese
music"). pp. 194-195.

### Before...: [V-dic.] + mae ni
Explicitly contrasted with Unit 15's ato de ("after..."): while ato de takes a verb's ta-form
([V-ta]+ato de), mae ni takes the dictionary form ([V-dic.]+mae ni) — "Gohan o taberu mae ni te o
aratte kudasai" ("Please wash your hands before eating the meal"). A further asymmetry is flagged
in-text: when mae ni attaches to a bare numeric time-counter (e.g. -nen "year", -jikan "hour")
rather than a full noun phrase, the connecting no is dropped — "Howaito-san wa san-nen mae ni
Nihon e kimashita" ("Miss White came to Japan three years ago"), reusing mae ni idiomatically to
mean "... ago" rather than literally "before". pp. 196-197.

### Describing ability or possibility: [V-dic.] + koto ga dekimasu
dekimasu ("can do") combines with a nominalized verb via the now-familiar X-wa-Y-ga pattern to
state ability: "Kimura-san wa gitā ga dekimasu" ("Mr Kimura can play the guitar") shows dekimasu
taking a bare noun object directly, while "Kimura-san wa piano o hiku koto ga dekimasen" ("Mr
Kimura cannot play the piano") shows the full nominalized-verb form; negates to dekimasen. The
identical construction also expresses whether an action is possible or permitted in a given
context, independent of personal skill: "Koko de oyogu koto ga dekimasu ka" ("Is swimming allowed
here?"). pp. 197-198.

behind-after/money-gold/week/day-of-the-week") — see the Vocabulary table above for kun-yomi/
on-yomi/meaning per character. This is the final kanji set taught in the main 16-unit course,
bringing the book's total taught kanji to roughly 60 as stated in the Unit 11 introduction

family) are paired in-text with their distinct someone-else's-family honorific equivalents
(onēsan/onīsan) via the back-matter glossary, continuing the family-term register distinction the
