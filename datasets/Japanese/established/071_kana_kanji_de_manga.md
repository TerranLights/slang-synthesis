# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Kana de Manga (Hiragana & Katakana)

**Source:** Glenn Kardy (creator) & Chihiro Hattori (art), *Kana de Manga: The Comic Book That
Teaches You Hiragana and Katakana!* (Manga University / Japanime Co. Ltd., Tokyo/San Francisco).
Full book, vision-read from `source_reference/languages/Japanese/03.Audio Courses, Textbooks/
Japanese in Mangaland/Kana de manga.djvu` (53 DjVu page-images). Companion volume to the
*Kanji de Manga* series by the same creator/artist team and publisher.

**Duplicate-check note (per dispatch instructions).** This dispatch also covered two "Kanji de
Manga" files under `.../Japanese in Mangaland/`: `Kanji de manga vol 1.pdf` (106 pages) and
`Kanji de manga vol 2.djvu` (52 pages). **`Kanji de manga vol 1.pdf` was checked and confirmed to
be the SAME book already fully extracted as `011_kanji_de_manga_1.md`** (Glenn Kardy/Chihiro
Hattori, *Kanji de Manga*, Vol. 1, ISBN 4-921205-02-7, 106 pages, ~80 JLPT-4/N5 kanji) under a
different source path (`02.Japanese Writing System/Kanji de Manga I.pdf`). Confirmation method:
identical title/author/publisher, identical 106-page count, no text layer in either copy, and a
direct content match — the "Page Guide" annotated example on PDF page 14 (printed p.12) of this
copy uses the 時 ("time") kanji exactly as described in `011`'s own extraction note ("This page's
layout is reused as the book's own 'Page Guide' annotated example on p.12"). **`Kanji de manga vol
1.pdf` was NOT re-extracted — see `011_kanji_de_manga_1.md` for its content.** `Kanji de manga vol
2.djvu` is a genuinely distinct second volume (different 80-kanji set, ISBN 4-921205-03-5, JLPT
Level 3/old-numbering) and is extracted separately in `071b_kanji_de_manga_vol2.md`. This file
covers only `Kana de Manga`, which teaches no kanji at all and has zero content overlap with either
Kanji de Manga volume.

**Coverage note — full extraction, no text layer.** This DjVu has no text layer (scanned/image-based
book, confirmed via `djvutxt` producing no usable output and `djvudump` showing JB2/IW4 image-only
page structure); every page was vision-read from rendered page images. **Spread-scan gotcha
confirmed**: each of the 53 page-images is a two-printed-page spread (verso+recto), not one
printed page per image — printed page numbers run to ~103 despite only 53 image files, consistent
with the same gotcha flagged elsewhere in this project's extraction spec. Structure: front matter
(title/copyright page, table of contents, foreword-style introduction covering romanization,
dakuten/handakuten, small-っ gemination, long vowels, and stroke order, pp.1-12), the main kana
study section (one hiragana+katakana pair per spread, each with its own illustrative example word,
pp.13-101 — covers all 46 base kana plus を/ヲ and ん/ン, 48 pairs total), and a blank
stroke-practice grid section at the very end of several spreads (skipped per the coverage rule — no
new vocabulary/grammar). **No handwritten marginalia of any kind was found** — every page is clean
typeset printed content; some page-images show faint scan/bleed-through discoloration (not
handwriting), which did not obscure any text.

Each kana spread follows a fixed template: the kana in large print; a romanized reading; one
illustrative vocabulary word (a different word for the hiragana side vs. the katakana side, since
katakana words are typically loanwords); and a short cultural/etymological caption. Per the
copyright rule, captions are paraphrased/summarized below, not quoted at length; only single short
words or phrases are quoted directly as the entries' own content. Bonus vocabulary items that
appear inside a page's caption prose (not the page's own headline example word) are folded into
that row's **Notes** column rather than given separate table rows, to keep the table navigable —
this mirrors the precedent set by `011_kanji_de_manga_1.md`'s handling of in-panel incidental
vocabulary.

**Vision Reading Confidence:** `verified` throughout — every kana and its reading were cross-checked
against the page's own repeated appearances (large display form, stroke-order practice cells, and
the romaji caption all agree), and against the hiragana/katakana reference charts printed on the
book's inside front/back cover pages, which independently list the same 46+dakuten/handakuten kana
in the same gojūon order.

## Grammar points

### Romanization system (Hepburn-style) and the two kana syllabaries

The book's introduction explains hiragana and katakana are two parallel syllabaries representing
the same 46 base sounds (plus dakuten/handakuten-voiced variants); hiragana is used for native
Japanese words and grammatical elements, katakana chiefly for loanwords, foreign names, onomatopoeia,
and emphasis. Pronunciation is given via Hepburn-style romanization throughout (pp.6-9).

### Dakuten (゛) and handakuten (゜)

A small diacritic mark added to certain kana converts an unvoiced consonant to its voiced
counterpart (か ka → が ga) via dakuten, or converts an h-row kana to its p-sound counterpart
(は ha → ぱ pa) via handakuten. The book's inside-cover reference chart lists the full dakuten/
handakuten kana set for both hiragana and katakana, though (per the coverage rule) these voiced
kana are not individually taught with their own manga-illustrated pages the way the 46 base kana
are — only the base 46 plus を/ん get dedicated spreads. p.10.

### Small っ/ッ (sokuon) — consonant gemination

A small-sized つ/ツ inserted before a consonant indicates a doubled/geminated consonant in
romanization (e.g., きっぷ kippu "ticket," not kipu). Explained in the front-matter phonology
section. p.11.

### Long vowels

A vowel sound held for two beats (long vowel) is marked in hiragana by adding the appropriate
vowel kana after the base kana (おかあさん, lit. "o-ka-a-san") and in katakana by a long dash (ー,
as in ケーキ "cake," seen repeatedly in the vocabulary above). p.11.

### Stroke order

A short illustrated section explains the general conventions for writing kana strokes (top-to-
bottom, left-to-right, etc.), matching the numbered stroke-order diagrams printed under every kana
entry throughout the book. p.12. (This chart's content is effectively identical to the "Kanji
Stroke-Order Rules" chart captured from Kanji de Manga Vol. 1 in `011_kanji_de_manga_1.md` and from
Vol. 2 in `071b_kanji_de_manga_vol2.md` — not re-transcribed a third time here.)

### を (particle, hiragana) and ヲ (katakana equivalent, rare)

を marks the direct object of a verb (ほんをよむ, "to read a book" — ほん "book" + を + よむ "to
read"). The book notes を is used *strictly* as a grammatical particle, never to spell an ordinary
word. The katakana equivalent ヲ is presented "for reference only" — the book explicitly states it
is almost never used in formal Japanese writing. p.102-103.

### ん/ン (moraic nasal, hiragana and katakana)

ん (and its katakana equivalent ン) is pronounced "n" or "m" and is the *only* consonant sound that
can end a Japanese word (e.g., ほん hon "book"). It can also appear mid-word but — unlike most other
kana — is never used to begin a word. The book notes the same rule applies identically to both the
hiragana and katakana forms. p.102-103.
