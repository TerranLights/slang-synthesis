## Grammar points

### Colloquial contraction cluster around 〜なければならない (obligation)

The teaching notes for Units 90-91 spell out, more explicitly than the student book's bare unit
titles do, that the formal obligation construction 〜なければならない has at least three live
colloquial contractions in natural speech (-nakucha, -nakya, -nakerya), that the latter half
("...ならない") is itself frequently replaced by なりません/いけません (polite) or なんない/いけない
(further-colloquial) or dropped outright, and that none of these variants differ in meaning — only
in register/formality. The notes also flag a specific phonological detail: in combinations like
送らなきゃ ("have to send"), the ら syllable can surface as ん (送んなきゃ) in casual speech, an r→n
reduction in rapid colloquial verb conjugation worth tracking alongside this project's other
documented Japanese sound-shift findings (cf. the baby-talk shi→chi shift documented in
`066c_mangajin_mastering_by_ear.md`). (Teacher's Book p.54)

### んじゃない as a genuine hedging/softening device, not a literal negative question

Units 86-87 (both the worksheet's unit titles and the Teacher's Book's confirming script,
p.156) establish that 〜んじゃない, despite its surface resemblance to a negative question, actually
functions as a softened-opinion tag ("I'd say...", "probably...") in casual conversation — distinct
from both a literal yes/no question and a flat assertion. 〜んじゃないかと思う stacks this same hedge
with と思う ("I think") for an even more tentative, doubly-hedged register. Both are genuinely
colloquial constructions worth noting for a synthesis phase interested in how Japanese softens
direct assertion in casual registers, distinct from (but related to) the honorific-politeness
system covered extensively elsewhere in this Wave.
