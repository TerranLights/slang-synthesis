## Grammar points

### to (と) — the core listing/comitative/quotative particle
Paraphrased from pp.200–209, one of the dictionary's largest and most structurally central entries. The basic listing function ("apples and grapes") comes with an explicit usage warning: unlike English "and", "to" cannot link two independent sentences or open a new sentence — "soshite" (p.202) must be used for that instead, and the book stresses this as a common learner error to avoid. Beyond listing, "to" marks a co-participant ("I went swimming with my brother"), an adversarial opponent ("he fought against his fears and finally conquered them"), the complement of certain intransitive verbs (becoming/naming/calling), achievement of a goal or new state ("the field day has finally come"), and — paired with a quantity and a negative — a "not even/never" comparison ("that kind of work wouldn't take more than three days"). The disjunctive "...yoo to...mai to" pattern yields "whether...or not, it doesn't matter". As a quotation particle, "to" introduces reported thought or speech after verbs like "omou" (think), "yuu" (say), and "kangaeru" (consider). It also adverbializes mimetic/onomatopoeic words ("sit still", "thunder rumbling"), marks immediate temporal sequence ("as soon as the teacher entered..."), forms a general conditional ("if it's too cheap, that worries me"), builds the universal "ari to arayuru" ("every possible thing"), and the ceaseless "...to naku...to naku" pattern ("without day or night").
