## Grammar points

### 1. Three types of Japanese verbs (as classified by this source)
- **-る verbs** (a.k.a. ichidan/"one-step" verbs in standard linguistic terminology, though the
  book itself doesn't use that label): the dictionary form ends in い+る or え+る. Examples given:
  いる, 見る, 借りる, 食べる, 寝る, やめる.
- **-う verbs** (godan/"five-step" verbs): the dictionary form ends in う, く, ぐ, す, つ, ぬ, ぶ, む,
  or る. Examples given: 会う, 泣く, 急ぐ, 話す, 待つ, 死ぬ, 遊ぶ, 読む, 取る. The book explicitly flags
  a genuine ambiguity risk: some -う verbs *also* end in いる or える on the surface (e.g. 帰る,
  切る, 入る, 知る, しゃべる, 走る) and must not be mistaken for -る verbs purely by their ending.
- **Irregular verbs**: 来る and する — the only two, each with its own unpredictable conjugation
  pattern not derivable from either regular class. (p. 208)

### 2. The conjugation paradigm itself (structure, not full reproduction)
The table conjugates each of the 16 example verbs across 11 forms: the -ます form, -て form, past
tense, negative present, potential, passive, causative, -ば form, -たら form, volitional, and
imperative. A few illustrative rows, one per verb type, to show how the paradigm generalizes (and
where the irregular verbs break the pattern):

- **-る verb example (食べる "to eat")**: ます-form 食べます; て-form 食べて; past 食べた;
  negative-present 食べない; potential 食べられる; passive 食べられる; causative 食べさせる; ば-form
  食べれば; たら-form 食べたら; volitional 食べよう; imperative 食べろ. (Note: for -る verbs, the
  potential and passive forms are identical in shape — られる — distinguished only by context.)
- **-う verb example (読む "to read")**: ます-form 読みます; て-form 読んで; past 読んだ;
  negative-present 読まない; potential 読める; passive 読まれる; causative 読ませる; ば-form 読めば;
  たら-form 読んだら; volitional 読もう; imperative 読め. (Note: for -う verbs, potential and passive
  are distinct shapes — unlike -る verbs.)
- **Irregular verb する**: ます-form します; て-form して; past した; negative-present しない; potential
  できる (fully suppletive — not derived from する at all); passive される; causative させる; ば-form
  すれば; たら-form したら; volitional しよう; imperative しろ.
- **Irregular verb 来る (くる)**: ます-form きます; て-form きて; past きた; negative-present こない
  (stem vowel shifts to こ-); potential こられる; passive こられる; causative こさせる; ば-form くれば;
  たら-form きたら; volitional こよう; imperative こい. The irregularity is confined to the reading of
  来, which shifts among く/き/こ depending on the form — the okurigana pattern otherwise tracks
  -る-verb-like endings once the irregular stem is accounted for.
- The source marks a small number of individual cells in the full table with an asterisk as
  "exceptional" within their row (e.g., 食べる's て-form is one of the asterisked cells, flagging
  that its て-form doesn't follow the straightforward pattern some neighboring -る verbs show in
  that same column) — the asterisk key itself is explicit in the table's own caption ("The forms
  with * are exceptional"), but the book does not gloss in prose which specific irregularity each
  asterisk refers to beyond that caption. (pp. 208–209)

This appendix functions as a condensed cross-reference/review of conjugation mechanics already
taught incrementally across the book's 12 lessons, rather than introducing new vocabulary — hence
no Vocabulary table accompanies this file.
