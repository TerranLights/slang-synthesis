## Grammar points

### 〜なり's five senses radiate from a single etymological root

The author explicitly roots every sense of 〜なり in the verb 成る ("to become"), tracing an original
"shape or condition" meaning that splits into five distinct modern uses: two nominal/adjectival senses
(N／Adj + なり(に), "in keeping with X's character") and three verb-governed senses (なり〜なり
alternatives; Verb-なり "almost simultaneous actions"; 〜たなり "unchanged resulting condition," pp.
84–86). This is a clear case of the book's broader method — showing that a single surface particle/suffix
is not one meaning but a small family of related-but-distinct grammaticalized senses, each requiring its
own gloss.

### A cluster of near-synonym constructions around "unchanged/ongoing state" and "immediate succession"

Pages 86–89 present a tight cluster of constructions that a bilingual dictionary would likely flatten
into one gloss each but that the author treats as a connected contrast set: 〜たなり ≈ 〜たきり (colloquial
variant) both mark "condition X created persists, nothing further happened"; 〜(た)まま／〜(た)きり／〜っぱなし
add a third axis (static vs. discontinued vs. actively-ongoing-and-blameworthy); and 〜たとたんに is
distinguished from 〜なり and が早いか specifically by a same-subject/different-subject constraint between
the two clauses. Taken together these five+ forms show that "immediate temporal succession" and "ongoing
unchanged state" are grammaticalized far more granularly in Japanese than a single English gloss like
"as soon as" or "left as is" would suggest.

### Suffix stacking around "resembling/characteristic of X": 〜っぽい, 〜らしい, 〜じみた, くせに

Four suffixes attach to nouns/adjectives to mark some form of "being like X," and the author is careful
to separate their evaluative valence: 〜らしい marks a trait as typical/ideal/expected by society (neutral
to positive); 〜っぽい marks a trait or color-tinge as a salient characteristic, often with a mildly
negative shading (quick to lose interest, quick to anger); 〜じみた is explicitly negative, derived from
"to stain/dye"; and くせに marks behavior that *violates* a らしい-type social expectation (used to scold
or criticize, e.g. a boy crying "not like a boy at all"). This four-way cluster (pp. 93–97) is one of the
clearest illustrations in this section of the book's central thesis that near-synonymous "X-like" markers
carry systematically different evaluative weight, not just stylistic variation.

### Paired adverbs of annoyance/effort/degree built on the same contrastive method

Several further pairs follow the book's signature method of isolating the one dimension that actually
differs between apparent synonyms: 途中／中途 (neutral "in the middle of" vs. negatively-toned "left
unfinished"); うるさい／やかましい (general annoyance-source vs. specifically noise/voice); せっかく／わざわざ
(goodwill-focused vs. effort/time-focused); だらけ／まみれ (countable-or-uncountable "scattered over" vs.
uncountable-only "smeared, filthy"); よほど／よっぽど (register: written-neutral vs. spoken-emphatic). The
recurring technique is to name the one semantic or pragmatic axis (countability, politeness register,
visibility to others, moral blame) that a bilingual dictionary entry would otherwise erase.
