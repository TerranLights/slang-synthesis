## Grammar points

### 1. Interrogative nouns in conditional clauses
Interrogative nouns like なに、どこ、だれ appear commonly inside a conditional clause (どこへ行けば…
／何時に伺えば…) to politely ask for advice/information — ば is favored over たら in this construction
in the formal style. (p. 155–156)

### 2. XXって 何ですか／だれですか
Asks for the identification of XX before って: 夏目漱石ってだれですか。「Who is Sōseki Natsume?」The
lesson's own illustrative example of this pattern directly engages taboo vocabulary as subject
matter: A: "Four letter wordsって何ですか。" B: "Four letter wordsって(いう)のは品が悪いことばのことで
す。" — the book itself glosses English profanity via the Japanese euphemism 品が悪いことば ("vulgar
words") rather than naming or translating specific taboo terms, a deliberate copyright/politeness-
conscious paraphrase strategy worth noting as a methodological data point. (p. 156)

### 3. ~んじゃない
Used to negate an assumption the addressee holds: A: いいテレビを買いましたね。"You bought a nice TV,
didn't you?" B: いいえ、買ったんじゃないんです。もらったんです。"No, I didn't buy it — it was given to
me." (p. 156)

### 4. ~なんて
Quotes an episode followed by the speaker's emotional comment on it (shame, disgust, or
admiration): こんな難しいことを子供にやらせるなんて無理ですよ。「It's too much to ask a child to do such
a difficult thing.」毎日15時間も勉強しているなんてすごいですね。「Isn't it amazing you study 15 hours a
day!」(p. 156)

### 5. Verb-ばよかった（と思う／んだけど…）— "I wish I had done...(but I didn't)"
Expresses spontaneous regret; と思う or んだけど… is added when the regret is reflected upon rather
than blurted spontaneously: カメラを持ってくればよかった。「I wish I'd brought a camera.」(p. 157)

### 6. ~ことは~ — "It is true that..., but..."
こと, being a noun, is modified by the preceding predicate in its noun-modifying form; sets up a
concessive "yes, but" structure. (p. 157)
