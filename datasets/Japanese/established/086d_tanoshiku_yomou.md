## Grammar points

### 受身形 (Passive form) — Units 30–31

Named by the teacher's manual as the shared grammar focus of both units: Unit 30 uses the passive
chiefly in comparison/description of the astronaut's spaceflight experience (what effects are
"caused" on the body by zero gravity); Unit 31 uses it chiefly for produced/imported-where phrasing
("小麦は〜から輸入されている," "wheat is imported from ~"). No new paradigm content beyond what's
already captured in this project's passive-form grammar-reference extractions — logged here only to
record which already-covered pattern these two reading-workbook units reinforce, per the
"non-redundant supplement" dispatch convention.

### Self-marked transitive/intransitive labeling convention (Unit 29, cross-referencing Unit 26/086c)

Unit 29's New-Words box is the only place in either volume where the source itself appends
"（他動詞）"/"（自動詞）" directly onto vocabulary-box entries (溶かす／溶ける), rather than leaving the
distinction to be inferred from an English gloss or a narrative illustration (as in Unit 26's
cautionary-tale treatment, `086c`). Flagged together as the series' two distinct pedagogical
approaches to the same grammar point — one narrative/applied, one directly labeled — both already
anchored to existing self/other-verb paradigm coverage elsewhere in this project rather than
duplicating it here.

## Series-wide closing notes

**Genre classification (teacher's manual, Book II).** Each unit is explicitly classified by genre in
the manual's per-unit headers: 体験談 (personal account/testimonial — Units 18, 34), エッセイ (essay —
Units 19, 24, 30), 説明文 (expository — Units 20, 22, 27, 28, 29, 31), アンケート (questionnaire — Unit
21), マンガ (manga — Unit 23), 昔話 (folktale — Unit 25). Book I's units are not labeled with this same
genre-tag system in its own (much sparser) answer-key booklet, but the pattern is consistent with
Book I's stated three-way split of realia/scanning texts, intensive-reading expository texts, and
for-pleasure texts (folktales, manga, puzzles) described in its own front matter (`086`).

**Register/dialect finding, summarized across the whole series.** This source carries **no
explicit dialectal or regional annotation anywhere** (it is a standard-Japanese reading-skill
workbook, not a dialect-comparison source) — the one genuinely explicit register marking in the
entire two-volume set is Unit 23's "友達言葉" (friend-speech/casual register) framing for its manga
excerpt (`086c`), plus a handful of individually-flagged colloquial items (いや, おい, くれ, いんちき,
ばかだなあ, めんどうくさい) that stand out register-wise against the otherwise uniformly `core`,
textbook-standard vocabulary of the rest of the series.

**Overall coverage summary (both volumes, all four files 086–086d).** 34 units read across 189 PDF
pages (77 + 90 main-text pages, 11 + 11 answer-key/teacher's-manual pages); every unit's New-Words
vocabulary box was read in full; representative sampling (not frame-by-frame reading) was used for
repeated-drill/comprehension-question pages per the coverage rule. No handwritten marginalia was
found anywhere in either volume. No new core-textbook grammar is introduced by this source — its
real contribution is (1) realia-genre vocabulary (menus, receipts, TV listings, postcards, letters,
municipal infrastructure terms) not necessarily captured by other established/ sources, (2) two named
folktales with literary-register vocabulary, (3) one manga excerpt with an explicit casual-register
label, and (4) a handful of explicit lexical-equivalence and transitive/intransitive-pair
self-annotations that are genuinely useful cross-reference data points for the mechanics-analysis
phase.
