full source-identification note. This file covers the standalone **numbered kanji-entry section**
(each entry: kanji character + stroke-order diagram + radical-decomposition codes + on/kun readings
+ English meaning + 4–5 compound words with readings, glosses, and cross-reference page numbers to
other entries using the same kanji).

**Scale finding — read before treating this as complete:** confirmed entries run at least from
**#131 to #758** across roughly 200 physical pages (2 entries/page for the first ~100 pages sampled,
transitioning to 4 entries/page by around entry #475). At ~4–5 compound words per entry, this
section alone represents a potential 2,500–3,500-row vocabulary table — far larger than one
extraction chunk should carry. **Only 16 entries were vision-read this pass**, specifically chosen
to span the section's full page range (early/middle/late) and confirm the numbering/format is
internally consistent throughout, not to exhaustively cover it. The rest — the other ~600+ entries
— is a known, explicit gap requiring its own dedicated wave of follow-up dispatches (see
continuation note).

**Vision-reading guard:** no handwritten marginalia found. All four sampled pages (133–134 context,
143–144, 475–478, 755–758) show faint gray "ghost" kanji silhouettes bleeding through from the
facing page of the physical scan into blank practice-writing cells — this is print show-through,
not a reader's pencil/pen marks, and was excluded from extraction as it carries no legible content
of its own (see `004`'s header for the same note).

**Copyright discipline:** compound-word glosses below are short, factual dictionary-style English

## Grammar points

### Entry format and cross-reference system

Each numbered entry presents, left to right/top to bottom: the kanji in large stroke-order-numbered
form; a 2–3-element radical/component breakdown with lookup codes (e.g. "4c, 3b, 1a2"); the on-
reading(s) in full capitals and kun-reading(s) in lowercase italics (okurigana in parentheses); an
English meaning gloss; 4–5 compound words each with their own reading, English gloss, and a
right-hand-column cross-reference number (or comma-separated list of numbers) pointing to the
*other* entries in this same book that use the shared kanji in that compound. This cross-reference
system is internally self-consistent across the full sampled range (entry #133's 車 is correctly
cross-referenced from entry #478's 歯車 compound more than 600 entries later; entry #131's 書 is
correctly cross-referenced from entry #755's 書架 compound), which is what let this extraction
positively confirm the kana-teaching pages in `004`/`004b` belong to the *same* book despite the
large apparent gap in page count between them.

### Register pairs visible in the sample (*kango* vs. *wago* doublets)

Two entries in this small sample independently show the same structural pattern: a Sino-Japanese
compound reading (*on*-reading based, more formal/technical) given alongside a native-Japanese
compound (*kun*-reading based, more everyday) for the same core meaning — 知人 *chijin* vs. 知り合い
*shiriai* (both "an acquaintance"), and 歯科医 *shikai* vs. 歯医者 *haisha* (both "dentist"). This
register-doublet pattern (technical/formal Sino-Japanese term alongside an everyday native
alternative) recurring independently at entries #214 and #478 — nearly 300 pages apart — suggests
it is a systematic feature of how Japanese vocabulary stratifies by register, worth tracking
further once more of this section is extracted.

## Continuation note

#131–#758; the book's own back cover claims "over 750" characters, so the true ceiling may run
slightly higher before the combined 1,945-character index section begins). A dedicated follow-up
extraction plan is needed:

- Recommended chunking: ~50 entries (~25 pages at the early 2-entries/page rate, ~12–13 pages at
  the later 4-entries/page rate) per subagent dispatch, numbered `004d`, `004e`, ... sequentially.
- Before dispatching, locate the exact page where entry #131 begins (this sample did not determine
  that boundary precisely — entries #1–#130 were not located in this pass and may require checking
  whether they appear in pages between the kana-teaching section and entry #131, or are missing
  from this scanned copy entirely) and the exact page where the entry sequence ends and the
  combined index begins (the index was independently confirmed starting by PDF p. ~318, showing
  entries numbered into the 1,900s — i.e. referencing *Book 2*'s entries too, confirming the index
  is shared/combined across both books, not specific to Book 1).
- Given the 20-concurrent-subagent platform ceiling (see `00_Reference_Extraction_Spec.md`), a
  ~600-entry, ~200-page section like this should be planned as its own multi-wave effort, not
  bundled into a single additional dispatch.
