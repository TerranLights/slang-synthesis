## Grammar points

### Body parts
A full body-part vocabulary set is given with a labeled diagram: kao (face), me (eye), mimi
(ear), kuchi (mouth), te (hand/arm), yubi (finger), ashi (leg/foot), onaka (stomach), karada
(body), se (height/stature) on the full-body figure, plus atama (head), hana (nose), kami (hair),
nodo (throat), kubi (neck) on a separate head/face diagram. p. 143.

### Describing attributes: X wa Y ga + adjective
To describe an attribute of a whole by naming one of its parts/aspects, X (the whole, e.g. a
person or place) is marked wa as the sentence topic, and Y (the specific part/aspect being
described) is marked ga as the subject of the following adjective: "Nihon wa sakana ga oishī
desu" ("Talking about Japan, its fish is tasty"), "Kimura-san wa me ga ōkī desu" ("Mrs Kimura has
big eyes"). This builds directly on the possession/existence arimasu-ga pattern from Units 8/10,
now generalized to any descriptive adjective. p. 143.

### Superlative: ichi-ban + adjective
ichi-ban ("the best/most") placed directly before an adjective forms a superlative: "Watashi wa
tenisu ga ichi-ban suki desu" ("I like tennis the most"). The comparison set is specified with
...no naka de ("among..."), either by listing every compared item (X to Y to Z no naka de) or by
naming the category they belong to ([Category] no naka de). Questions substitute an interrogative
(dore/doko/dare depending on what's asked) in Y's slot, followed by ka. When the underlying
sentence is itself an X-wa-Y-ga+adjective structure (e.g. se ga takai "tall"), ga appears twice in
the resulting superlative question/answer: "Kazoku no naka de, dare ga ichi-ban se ga takai desu
ka" ("Who is the tallest of the family?"). pp. 144-145.

### Colors
Most color terms are i-adjectives, and dropping the final i converts the i-adjective into the
corresponding color noun: akai→aka (red), aoi→ao (blue), kuroi→kuro (black), shiroi→shiro
(white), kīroi→kīro (yellow), chairoi→chairo (brown). Two colors — midori (green) and pinku
(pink, a loanword) — exist only as nouns, with no corresponding i-adjective form, an irregular gap
in an otherwise systematic derivational pattern worth flagging for mechanics-analysis. p. 147-148.

### Comparative: X wa Y yori + adjective
To compare two items directly, X (the more [adjective] item) is marked wa, and Y (the lesser item)
takes yori ("than"): "Nihon wa Igirisu yori ōkī desu" ("Japan is bigger than the UK"). The
interrogative is dochira (no hō) ("which one of the two"), with the full pattern "X to Y to
dochira (no hō) ga + adjective ka" for questions (no hō may be dropped here) and "X no hō ga +
adjective" for answers (no hō may NOT be dropped in the answer — an asymmetric omission rule worth
flagging). "Both" is answered with dochira mo, which — unusually — takes no ga after it. pp.
148-149.
