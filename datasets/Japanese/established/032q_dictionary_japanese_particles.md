## Grammar points

### wa (は) conclusion — further topic/contrastive/concessive uses
Paraphrased from pp.237–239, concluding the entry begun in part 16. "Wa" follows particles/adverbs/verbs/adjectives/te-forms to mark them as the topic, marks an explicit contrast between two topics ("days are hot, nights are cold"), and — in the "...(de)wa...(da)ga" pattern — concedes one point while adding another ("I did telephone, to be sure, but I'll also send a letter").

### wa (わ) — the women's-language sentence-final interjection
Paraphrased from pp.239–240. Distinct from topic-marking は despite sharing the pronunciation "wa" (the book's own orthographic rule, noted in part 16, distinguishes them by position). The source explicitly marks this whole entry as women's language. Softens a statement ("I'm certain I put the keys here, but..."), voices casual surprise/exclamation ("I'm surprised! I hadn't thought dinosaurs were that big"), and — often doubled — builds an exclamatory list ("I had a fever and a sore throat; this cold was pretty severe").

### ya (や) — the "vague/category" listing particle, with register-marked sentence-final uses
Paraphrased from pp.241–246. The book draws a deliberate contrast with "to" (p.200): where "to" lists specific items, "ya" implies a looser category including unlisted similar things ("candies and drinks, among other things"). Beyond listing, "ya" marks immediate sequence (a contraction of "ya ina ya", p.247: "as soon as she saw me, she went into a fit of anger"). Two sentence-final uses carry explicit register tags: a coaxing/inviting "shall we?" use is marked as men's language, while a name-following softening use ("Yoshiko, my dear, will you...") is marked as mainly used by elderly speakers. An untranslatable resignation/helplessness use and an intensifying role in the fixed phrases "mata mo ya"/"mashite ya" round out the entry.

### yai (やい) — a scornful/familiar vocative particle
Paraphrased from p.246. Calls out to someone with a tone of familiarity or outright scorn, e.g. taunting someone as a coward.
