## Grammar points

### Lesson 4 「あきれる」

#### 「〜（さ）せる」(causative: "to make/let someone do ~")

Standard causative construction for making or allowing someone to perform an action. Source's own
example: 子供がマッチで遊んでいたらどうしますか。⇒すぐやめさせます。("What would you do if a child
were playing with matches? ⇒ I'd make them stop right away.") (p. 20, pattern A-1).

#### 「〜たばかり」("just finished ~, no sooner than ~")

Marks an action as having just been completed. Source's own example: 日本に来たばかりなので、何も分
かりません。("Since I just arrived in Japan, I don't understand anything yet.") (p. 21, pattern B).

#### 「〜て（で）たまらない」("unbearably ~, can't help but ~")

Expresses an emotion or sensation so intense it's unbearable. Source's own example illustrating
the construction from the lesson's own passage: どうして我慢できるのだろうかと不思議でたまりませんで
した。("I found it utterly bewildering how [they] could stand it.") (p. 21, pattern C).

#### 「〜と聞いていた」("had heard that ~")

Reports a prior expectation based on hearsay, often set up to be contradicted. Source's own
example: 日本人は礼儀正しいと聞いていたのに、そうでもありませんでした。("I had heard Japanese people
were polite, but that wasn't really the case.") (p. 21, pattern D).

### Lesson 5 「たべる」

#### 「〜ものだ」(general tendency/expected social norm)

States what is generally expected or considered the norm, in either the affirmative or negative
(〜ないものだ). Source's own example: 食事のときは、みんなで楽しく話をするものです。("At mealtimes,
people generally talk and enjoy themselves together.") (p. 25, pattern A-1).

#### 「当たり前」("to be expected, obviously so")

Marks a conclusion as self-evidently natural given the stated premise. Source's own example:
世界は広いのだから、料理も食べ方もいろいろあるのは当たり前だ。("Since the world is big, it's only
natural that there are many different foods and ways of eating.") (p. 26, pattern B).

#### 「〜てみると、思っていたより〜」("when I tried it, it turned out more ~ than expected")

Describes a gap between prior expectation and the outcome of actually trying something. Source's
own example: 食べてみると、思っていたよりおいしかったということもよくある。("Often when you actually
try eating it, it turns out to be tastier than you expected.") (p. 26, pattern C).

#### 「どうしたら〜か」("what should one do to ~")

A question-and-answer frame for eliciting a method or means. Source's own example: どうしたらその
土地の人と友達になれるのかと聞いてみると、「そこの人たちの食べるものを一緒に食べることだ」という答えが
返ってきた。("When I asked how one could become friends with the locals, the answer that came back
was 'eat what the locals eat, together with them.'") (p. 26, pattern D).

### Lesson 6 「あそぶ」

#### 「〜てきた」("has been ~ing up to now, has come to be ~")

Describes a state or practice that has continued up to the present. Source's own example:
「○○道」は、長く伝えられてきた形や決まりを大切にしてきました。("'○○-dō' arts have, for a long time,
valued the forms and rules handed down to them.") (p. 30, pattern A).

#### 「〜まで」("even to the extent of ~, to the point of ~")

Extends a scope of concern or effect further than might be expected. Source's own example: その
技術を使う人の心の持ち方までも問題にしているのです。("It treats even the mindset of the person using
that skill as a matter of concern.") (p. 30, pattern B).

#### 「〜といっても」("even though one says ~, ~ as it may be")

Qualifies a prior statement by noting it doesn't mean what it might seem to imply. Source's own
example: 腕ひとつといっても、技術的に上手か下手かだけではなく、気持ちの持ち方までも問題にしているので
す。("Even though we say it's all down to skill, that's not just about technical competence — it's
also a matter of one's mindset.") (p. 30–31, pattern C).

#### 「もしかすると〜のかもしれません」("perhaps it might be that ~")

Hedges a speculative guess about a cause or situation. Source's own example: 彼はなかなか来ません
ね。もう三十分も過ぎたのに。⇒もしかすると時間を間違えたのかもしれません。("He's not coming, is he.
It's already been thirty minutes. ⇒ Maybe he got the time wrong.") (p. 31, pattern D).

### Lesson 7 「いう」

#### 「〜つもり」("intend to ~, with the intention of ~")

Marks a stated or implied intention behind an action. Source's own example from the passage:
特別料金は五百円だから、コーヒーを一杯飲んだつもりで、特急電車に乗った。("Since the surcharge was 500
yen, I told myself it was like buying a cup of coffee, and boarded the limited express.") (p. 35,
pattern A-1).

#### 「〜させられる」(causative-passive: "to be made to ~, to be forced to ~")

Expresses being compelled into an action or emotional state by circumstance, often without a
clear external agent. Source's own example: あまり飲めないのに、飲まされました（飲ませられました）。
("Even though I can't drink much, I was made to drink.") (p. 35, pattern B-1); and, closer to the
passage's own usage, アナウンスの言葉を聞いて、言葉の使い方を考えさせられた。("Hearing the announcer's
words made me reflect on how language is used.") (p. 36, pattern B-2).

### Lesson 8 「かざる」

#### 「ところで〜といえば」("by the way, speaking of ~")

A two-part discourse marker: ところで shifts topic, then 〜といえば pivots to a related new subject.
Source's own example: 駅の近くに、新しい病院ができましたね。⇒大きな病院ですね。ところで、病院といえば、
山田さんが入院したそうですよ。("A new hospital opened near the station, didn't it. ⇒ It's a big
one. By the way, speaking of hospitals, I heard Yamada-san was admitted to one.") (p. 40, pattern
A).

#### 「〜ばそれだけで〜」("just by ~ alone, that's enough to ~")

Asserts that a single minimal condition is sufficient to produce an effect. Source's own example:
有名な会社名の入った名刺をもらえば、それだけで安心する。("Just receiving a business card with a famous
company's name on it is enough, by itself, to put someone at ease.") (p. 40, pattern B).

#### 「いくら〜ても、〜ば〜」("no matter how much ~, if ~ then ~")

A concessive-conditional pairing: however far the first clause goes, the outcome still depends on
the second condition. Source's own example: いくら外側を飾っても、中身がなければ何にもならない。("No
matter how much you decorate the outside, it's meaningless if there's nothing inside.") (p. 41,
pattern C).

#### 「〜と同時に」("at the same time as ~, simultaneously with ~")

Links two things that hold true or occur concurrently. Source's own example: 自分の意見も言うと同時
にほかの人の考えもよく聞くことが大切だ。("It's important to state one's own opinion while, at the
same time, also listening carefully to others' views.") (p. 41, pattern D).
