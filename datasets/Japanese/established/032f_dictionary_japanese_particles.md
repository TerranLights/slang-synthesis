## Grammar points

### mono-de (もので) / monoka (ものか) / mononara (ものなら) — reason, emphatic negation, and conditional-dare combination particles built on "mono"
Paraphrased from pp.99–102. "Mono-de" (colloquially "mon de") gives a causal reason, close to "mono" itself. "Monoka" (colloquially "monka"/"mon desu ka") is an untranslatable exclamatory emphatic negation ("How could you ever understand how I feel!"). "Mononara" (colloquially "mon-nara") covers three related but distinct uses: warning of an undesirable consequence ("should there be a fire..."), a resentful hypothetical concession ("if it's fine with you no matter how poor you become, I won't say anything"), and a daring challenge to the listener ("if you think you can fly, go ahead and try").

### monono (ものの) and mono-o (ものを) — concessive/regretful particles
Paraphrased from pp.102–104. "Monono" sets up a situation and then introduces a conflicting one ("although I left early, the bus was late and I was late [to my destination]"). "Mono-o" voices dissatisfaction that a better alternative existed ("I got sick because I pushed myself, although I would have been fine resting"), and sentence-finally becomes a pure regret exclamation ("if only you had checked the forecast!").

### na / naa (な/なあ) — the richly register-marked exclamatory/command particle
Paraphrased from pp.105–107, with three of its eight senses explicitly tied to a marked register by the source itself: sense 1 (emotional exclamation, "you've grown so much!") is men's language; sense 5 (a polite favor-request softener following "kudasai"/"nasai") is women's language; and sense 6 (a lecturing tone, "listen, this is a secret") is men's language. The remaining senses are presented as register-neutral: casual emphasis on an opinion, eliciting confirmation ("...right?"), casual wishful thinking ("I wish I could relax a little"), a strong negative imperative ("don't give up until the end!"), and a colloquial direct command ("walk quickly!").
