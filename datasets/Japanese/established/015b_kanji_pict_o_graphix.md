# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Kanji Pict-O-Graphix — Kanji Sample Set 1 (entries #1–496, Chapters 1–5: World/Food/Animals/People/Body)

**Source:** Michael Rowley, *Kanji Pict-O-Graphix: Over 1,000 Japanese Kanji and Kana Mnemonics*
(Stone Bridge Press, Berkeley, California; bibliographic year 1992 per publisher record, not
independently confirmed from this scan's own front matter — see `015_kanji_pict_o_graphix.md` for
the full source note and scan-quality details).

**Coverage note:** this is a **representative sample**, not an exhaustive extraction, of a
1,225-entry mnemonic kanji dictionary — standing practice for oversized dictionary-style references
in this project. Entries below are drawn from 7 complete 2-page mnemonic spreads chosen to spread
across this half of the book's entry-number range and across 5 of its 11 thematic chapters:
entries 1–7 ("The Sun", Ch. 1 World), 36–43 ("Time", Ch. 1), 105–110 ("Metal", Ch. 1), 201–206
("Field & Plant", Ch. 2 Food), 311–317 ("Snakes & Birds", Ch. 3 Animals), 400–404 ("People's
Burdens", Ch. 4 People), and 491–496 ("Tooth, Fang, & Claw", Ch. 5 Body). See `015c` for the
second-half sample (entries 598–1225, Chapters 5–11). No handwritten marginalia was found on any
sampled page — all content is clean typeset print.

Each entry's mnemonic sentence is paraphrased/summarized rather than quoted in full per copyright
discipline, and the picture mnemonics themselves are not reproduced, only described when relevant.
Entries that are bound/combining-form **elements** rather than stand-alone readable kanji are
marked as such in Part of Speech; the book gives no independent on/kun reading for these (they
exist only as mnemonic-schematic building blocks for other entries).

## Grammar points

### Entry-number cross-reference system (applies throughout the dictionary)

As noted in `015`, each entry's schematic boxes cite other entries by their sequential number (not
page number), letting the mnemonic chain build later entries out of earlier ones within the same
thematic cluster — e.g. 蛇 "snake" (#312) reuses 虫 "snake" (#311) as its left-hand element, and
the whole "Metal" spread (#105–110) reuses 金 "metal" (#105) as a shared right-hand radical across
five following entries. This sampled range shows the pattern holds both within a single 2-page
spread and across spreads several pages apart (e.g. #317 "crane" cross-references #331 "heron",
two spreads ahead in the book's own progression).

### Radical/element-only entries (no stand-alone on/kun reading)

A recurring entry type in this sample (#402 "burdens", #493 "nails") is a kanji component the
author presents purely as a combining-form building block, with no independent reading given on
that page — distinct from ordinary stand-alone kanji entries that do carry on/kun readings. See
`00_Word_Concept_and_Morphological_Typology_Guide.md`'s general treatment of sub-word composed
units; here the "morphemes" are visual/graphic components of a logographic character rather than
phonological morphemes, so no morpheme-breakdown block is used for these rows — the Notes column
captures the composition instead, consistent with how the book's own schematic captures it.
