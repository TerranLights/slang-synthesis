**Morpheme breakdown.** Japanese Sino-Japanese (kango) compounding is productive and semantically
transparent often enough to merit breakdown for the chapter's core teaching examples — showing how
the same root recombines with different adjectival/nominal formants:

> **論理的** (ronri-teki) = 論 (ron, "discourse") + 理 (ri, "principle") + 的 (-teki, adjectival
> formant) — "logical." Contrast **理論的** (riron-teki) = 理 (ri) + 論 (ron) + 的 (-teki) —
> "theoretical." Same two morphemes, reversed order, genuinely distinct meaning — the chapter's own
> central illustration of why *ron*/*ri*/*setsu* compounds must be learned as combinations, not
> derived compositionally from the parts alone (p.19).

> **合理化** (gōri-ka) = 合 (gō, "fitting/combining") + 理 (ri, "reason") + 化 (-ka, "-ization") —
> "rationalization" (p.20).

> **国連安全保障理事国** (kokuren-anzen-hoshō-rijikoku) = 国連 (kokuren, "UN") + 安全保障
> (anzen-hoshō, "security") + 理事国 (rijikoku, "member/director nation" ← 理事 riji "director" +
> 国 koku "nation") — "member nation of the UN Security Council" (p.21).

## Grammar points

### *-ron*/*-ri*/*-setsu* as productive bound compound-formants, not free words in the modern register

De Wolf frames the chapter's organizing idea: 論, 理, and 説 each function as bound Sino-Japanese
morphemes that attach as either the initial or final element of a compound, with the resulting
compound's meaning not always transparent from the parts (e.g., 理学 *rigaku* "physical sciences,"
理髪 *rihatsu* "barbering," 小説 *shōsetsu* "novel"). Each root can also combine with the adjectival
formant 的 (*-teki*) to derive an adjective (論理的/理論的), and several can verbalize with する
(*suru*) directly as a noun-verb (議論する, 討論する) (pp. 15, 19–21).

### Semantic field distinctions between near-synonymous roots

*Ron*, *ri*, and *setsu* overlap in English gloss (all can translate as "theory") but the author
distinguishes their core senses: *ron* centers on argument/advocacy/ideology (hence -ism
compounds); *ri* centers on principle/underlying reason, closer to logos-as-order-of-nature; *setsu*
centers on a stated view/account (lit. "speak"), hence its use in 小説 "novel" and 伝説 "legend"
where no argumentative stance is implied. The author gives 定説 vs. 学説 vs. *teiron* (定論, a less
common but possible variant of *teisetsu*) as evidence the two roots partially substitute for each
other in some compounds but not others (pp. 19, 22–23).

### Verbalization patterns

Compounds in -ron/-ri/-setsu verbalize in at least three ways documented in this chapter: (1) plain
+ する (議論する, 論争する); (2) the bound verb form -じる (-jiru) directly suffixed to the root,
as in 論じる (*ronjiru*) "to discuss" (p.18); (3) some nominal compounds take a different light
verb entirely, e.g. 結論 pairs with を出す (*o dasu*, "to draw/reach [a conclusion]") rather than
する (p.18).
