# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: New Approach Japanese — Intermediate 1 (representative sample across Lessons 1–20)

**Source:** Trinh Van Viet (compiler/scanner credit "Takahata," Japanese Study Network Forums /
TiengNhatClub.Com), *New Approach Japanese — Intermediate I*, scanned PDF, 192 pp. No publisher/
year metadata is printed on the scanned pages themselves; PDF metadata records a 2008 distiller
date, so "contemporary (source scanned/distilled 2008)" is used for Attested Era where the source
otherwise supports a value.

**Source type note:** this is a structured intermediate-level reading/grammar textbook (20 numbered
lessons, each built around a single reading passage + comprehension questions + a formal new-vocabulary
list + grammar-pattern ("文型・表現") explanations + fill-in-the-blank drills + a composition exercise),
followed by a "会話文型・表現" (conversational-expression) appendix and a "長文読解練習" (long-reading-practice)
appendix. `grammar_reference` throughout.

**No text layer — vision-read in full.** `pdftotext` on this PDF returns only a repeated forum
watermark/footer ("JAPANESE STUDY NETWORK FORUMS / Created by Takahata / WwW.TiengNhatClub.Com /
takahata" plus page numbers) on every page; the entire 192-page body — Japanese text, English
instructions, everything — is a raster scan with zero real text layer. Confirmed by checking
`pdftotext -f 20 -l 30` output (3 non-boilerplate "words" across 11 full pages) before committing to
vision-reading.

**Coverage note — representative sampling, not exhaustive (explicitly authorized for this dispatch
given the source's size).** The book has 20 lessons across ~150 pages plus two appendix sections
(~45 pages); exhaustively vision-reading all ~192 pages was out of scope for this pass. A lesson-boundary
map was built first (rendering + reading the running header strip of pages at regular intervals across
the whole book) to identify each lesson's title and approximate page range, then a representative subset
of lessons was vision-read in full detail, spread across the book's progression (grammatical topics
repeat in a 1/2 pattern — e.g. 比較(1) Lesson 1 / 比較(2) Lesson 11; 対比・逆接(1) Lesson 4 / (2) Lesson 14 —
so early + late sampling of a repeated topic captures both halves of its pedagogical arc). Lessons not
vision-read in full are listed by title/topic only, from the header-map pass, so a future targeted
dispatch can resume at an exact page. **No handwritten marginalia was found on any page read for this
extraction** — all content read was clearly typeset/printed; this scanned copy shows no annotation.

## Grammar points

### Lesson 1 — "比較" (Comparison), pattern set 1 (pp. 3–5)

The lesson opens a two-part comparison-grammar arc (Part 2 is Lesson 11). Patterns, from least to
most elaborate:

1. **AはBより…** — basic comparative ("A is more ~ than B"). Example: この教室はとなりの教室より広いです
   ("this classroom is wider than the next one").
2. **AとBとどちら/どっちが…** — "which of A and B is more ~"; どっち flagged as the casual/spoken variant
   of どちら (no explicit register tag in the source beyond the variant pairing itself).
3. **(Aより)Bのほうが…** — "B (rather than A) is more ~."
4. **AはBほど…ない** — "A is not as ~ as B" (negative-scope comparison). The source cross-references
   forward to three later occurrences of the same ほど pattern family: L.3 「〜ば〜ほど」("the more ~, the
   more ~"), L.11 「〜ほど〜はない」("nothing is as ~ as ~"), and L.13 「<動詞>+ほどだ」("to the extent of
   doing ~").
5. **X(の中)で何/だれ/どこ/いつ/が一番…** — superlative within a named category.
6. **X(の中)で〜が一番…** — superlative with a stated reference set (e.g. a list of countries visited).
7. **AとBとCの中でどれ/だれ/どこ/いつ/が一番…** — three-way superlative choice.
8. **『比べる』 1) AはBに/と比べて(Xが)…** — the verb 比べる ("to compare") used as a connective-style
   structure, distinguished from 2) **AはBに比べると(Xが)…**, a near-synonymous variant using the
   conditional-form -と rather than -て.

Additional "その他の文型・表現" (other patterns) from the same lesson, p. 4:
- **<い形容詞>く+感じる** — "to feel ~ly" (adverbial form of an i-adjective + 感じる), e.g. 話がつまらないと、
  時間が長く感じます ("when the talk is boring, time feels long").
- **なんとなく** — "somehow, for no clear reason"; explicit source gloss: used when the reason/cause is
  not clearly identifiable.
- **<副詞>+の+<名詞>** — adverb-as-noun-modifier pattern, e.g. ほとんどの人 "most people," だいたいのこと "the
  general gist," かなりの人 "quite a lot of people."
- **(Xは)〜によって違う/いろいろだ** — "(X) varies/differs depending on ~." Cross-referenced to the Lesson 2
  reading passage's own use of a related 〜によって〜たり〜たりする construction.
- **〜のではないでしょうか** — a hedged-opinion pattern ("isn't it the case that ~?"), explicitly
  cross-referenced to 会話文型・表現<1>「考えを言う」(Conversational Pattern 1, "stating an opinion") in the
  appendix — confirming the appendix is a structured cross-reference target for in-lesson patterns, not
  a standalone unit.

### Lesson 4 — "対比・逆接" (Contrast/Concession), pattern set 1 (pp. 23–25)

Part 1 of a two-part arc (Part 2 is Lesson 14). The lesson's centerpiece is an explicit **register
ladder for contrastive connectives** — exactly the kind of source-marked register annotation this
project's coverage rule prioritizes. Paraphrased from pp. 24–25 (connective → source's own register
gloss):

1. **〜が** — neutral, sentence can be plain or polite style. ("(※文は普通体も丁寧体もある)")
2. **〜けど** — spoken language ("※話し言葉"). Casual/conversational.
3. **〜けれど(も)** — a step more neutral/general than けど; usable in both plain and polite sentences.
4. **〜。けれども、〜。** — "hard/stiff expression" ("※硬い表現"), used as a full-stop sentence-initial
   connective rather than a clause-linker.
5. **〜。だが、〜。** — written-language register ("※書き言葉"), sentence itself in plain (普通体) form.
   Cross-referenced forward to its own appearance in the Lesson 6 reading passage.
6. **〜。ですけれども、〜。** — polite spoken language ("※丁寧な話し言葉"), sentence in polite (丁寧体) form —
   the politeness-matched counterpart to だが.
7. **〜。でも、〜。** — no register tag given; presented as usable with either plain or polite sentences,
   i.e. the unmarked default.
8. **〜。しかし、〜。** — hard/stiff expression ("※硬い表現"), usable with either plain or polite sentences.
9. **〜。ところが、〜。** — explicitly glossed as carrying "an unexpected feeling" ("※意外な気持ち"), stronger
   than plain でも/しかし.
10. **〜のに、〜。** (preceding clause in plain form) — the source explicitly states that using のに
    makes the feeling of surprise/discontent *stronger* than ところが — i.e. のに > ところが > でも/しかし on an
    explicit surprise/dissatisfaction intensity scale, a genuinely useful register-and-pragmatics
    annotation rarely spelled out this plainly in a textbook.
11. **(Aは)…。それなのに、…。** — "even so, despite that" — concessive, stronger emotional coloring than
    plain のに; example given: 一生懸命お願いしました。それなのに、友達は何もしてくれませんでした。("I asked
    earnestly, but even so my friend did nothing.")

Also on the same spread: **『違う』『違い』** three sub-patterns — 1) AはBと違って… ("unlike B, A ~"),
2) AとB(と)の違いは…(という)ことです ("the difference between A and B is that ~," cross-referenced to the
appendix's 長文読解<1>), 3) AがBと違うのは…(という)点です ("where A differs from B is the point that ~," with
an explicit usage note that 点 here means ところ/物事 "a point/matter," cross-referenced to L.2's 〜ところ
pattern, 長文読解<1>, and the L.20 reading passage).

**その他の文型・表現 (other patterns), p. 25:**
- **〜たびに** — "every time ~" (※〜(スル)といつも、〜の時はいつも). Four graded examples, including one
  with そうです (hearsay) layered on top: あの女性はパーティーのたびに、新しいドレスを買うそうです.
- **使役受け身文 (causative-passive)** — full paradigm given:
  - Group II verbs: 〜させられる (e.g. 食べさせられる "was made to eat").
  - Group I verbs, long form: 〜せられる (e.g. 歌わせられる "was made to sing").
  - Group I verbs, short/contracted form: 〜される (e.g. 歌わされる, same meaning as 歌わせられる — the
    source explicitly notes 話す is long-form-only: 話させられる, no contracted variant).
  - Irregular verbs: 来る → 来させられる; する → させられる.
  - Explicit contrastive note: the causative-passive (〜せられる/〜される, "was made to do X," no
    say in the matter) is paired against 使役て形+もらう (〜せてもらう, "got permission/was let to do X," a
    position of agency) for three parallel example pairs — 行かせられる vs 行かせてもらう, 歌を歌わせられる vs
    歌を歌わせてもらう, 仕事をさせられる vs 仕事をさせてもらう — a clean minimal-pair illustration of a
    grammatical-voice distinction with real pragmatic stakes (coercion vs. granted permission).

### Lesson 7 — "様子・推測" (Appearance/Conjecture), pattern set 1 (pp. 46, 48)

Part 1 of a two-part arc (Part 2 is Lesson 17).

- **〜そうだ (visual/inferential conjecture)** — "looks like ~, seems ~ (based on what I can see right
  now)." Full paradigm given across i-adjective, na-adjective, stative-verb, and potential-form
  examples (e.g. 一人で持てそうです "it looks like I could carry it alone," marked ※可能形 "potential
  form"). Two explicit grammar notes: (1) nouns have no 〜そうだ form at all — ~のようだ/みたいだ must be
  used instead (あの人は日本人のようだ／日本人みたいだ); (2) 〜そうだ conjugates exactly like a na-adjective
  (cross-referenced forward to L.17's 〜そうに…).
- **その他の文型・表現, p. 48:**
  - **以上/以下/以内/以外** — four boundary-quantifier suffixes: 以上 "~ or more/above" (inclusive),
    以下 "~ or less/below" (inclusive), 以内 "within ~" (e.g. 30分以内に "within 30 minutes"), 以外 "other
    than ~, except ~." All four marked ★ (basic/elementary-level patterns revisited here).
  - **それでは** — explicit source gloss: "(※今までしていたことを終えて次に進む)" — used to close out a prior
    activity and move to the next one (e.g. transitioning a class to the next exercise); its casual
    contraction それじゃ is given a separate register note: "(※話が終わって別れる時のあいさつ)" — used
    specifically as a parting greeting when a conversation ends (それじゃ、またあした "well then, see you
    tomorrow"). Cross-referenced back to Lesson 3's related それでは(なぜ)〜 pattern.
  - **〜としたら/すると/すれば** — hypothetical-conditional set, with an explicit source nuance note:
    "(※仮定の話：実際にはそうではない／そうしないかもしれないというニュアンスがある)" — marks the statement as
    genuinely hypothetical, carrying the nuance that reality is not (or may not end up) that way. Example:
    沖縄に旅行に行くとしたら何月が一番いいでしょうか "if we were to suppose a trip to Okinawa, which month
    would be best?"

### Lesson 9 — "原因・理由" (Cause/Reason), pattern set 1 (p. 62): a second explicit register ladder

Like Lesson 4's contrast-connective ladder, Lesson 9 lays out a graded set of causal connectives with
explicit register/usage notes — directly complementary content for a register-focused extraction:

- **〜の/<動詞た形>ために…** — "because of ~." Explicit source note: "(※原因と結果を客観的に述べる文なので、
  硬い文章によく使われる)" — used for objectively stating a cause-and-result relationship, and therefore
  common in *hard/stiff written prose* (硬い文章). A further note allows the more general
  <動詞辞書形/ない形>ために for ordinary (non-formal) causal statements.
- **〜によって… / 〜による〜** — "due to ~ / ~ caused by ~." Demonstrated as a productive pair: a
  によって-clause (ストレスによっていろいろな病気になる "various illnesses are caused by stress") and its
  nominalized による-attributive counterpart used to build a noun phrase (ストレスによる病気(が怖い) "stress-
  caused illness (is frightening)") — shown across 5 parallel example pairs. Cross-referenced to its own
  appearance in the causative-passive construction ("〜によって" 受け身文, i.e. the agent-marking によって
  used with passive verbs) and forward to L.12's によって used for "means/method" instead of cause.
- **<順接の接続詞>(sequential/causal connectives) to express cause/reason:**
  1. **〜から、… / ですから、…** — explicit register note: "(※文末に意志表現、主観的な表現がよく使われる)" —
     the clause-final verb commonly carries a volitional/subjective expression (intentions, plans,
     invitations) rather than a purely objective statement.
  2. **だから、…** — explicitly glossed as the plain-style (普通体) equivalent of ですから, same usage
     otherwise.
- **『おかげ』(a word for "thanks to," used when a good outcome follows)** — two sub-patterns:
  1) **〜おかげで** — "thanks to ~." The source gives both the standard grateful use (ジョンさんのおかげで
     本当に助かりました) and an explicit note on its *ironic/sarcastic* use: "(※皮肉で使うこともある)" — can be
     used sarcastically, illustrated with あいつのおかげでひどい目にあった "thanks to that jerk, I had a
     terrible time" — a genuinely useful source-marked register/pragmatics annotation (politeness-marked
     vocabulary repurposed for sarcasm) of exactly the kind this project's coverage rule flags as
     high-value.
  2) **…のは〜おかげだ** — same meaning, topicalized/cleft-sentence variant.
  Also notes the fixed greeting おかげさまで、元気です ("thanks to you/everyone, I'm doing well" — a set
  phrase, not compositional).

### Lesson 12 — 様子・類似(2) 格言・名言 (Proverbs and Famous Sayings), p. 86

Lesson topic is itself linguistically notable for a slang-synthesis project: an intermediate-level
reading lesson built entirely around Japanese proverbs/famous sayings (格言・名言). Grammar points:

- **いわゆる vs. つまり** — both roughly "that is to say, so-called," but presented as a forced-choice
  discrimination drill (fill in one or the other across 5 sentences) rather than simple synonyms —
  implying a genuine usage distinction the source expects a learner to work out contextually (e.g.
  いわゆる precedes a noun being specifically labeled/named — "来年、定年で退職するので、(いわゆる)第二の人生を
  どのように送ろうか" "since I'm retiring next year, I'm thinking about how to spend my so-called second
  life" — while つまり restates/summarizes a preceding clause — "山田さんは80年卒業で、私は85年卒業です。
  (つまり)私の先輩に当たります" "Yamada graduated in '80 and I in '85 — that is to say, he's my senior").
- **Xを〜と呼ぶ** — "to call X ~" (naming/labeling construction), e.g. 彼女の名前は「○○」だから、みんなから
  「○○ちゃん」と呼ばれている — directly relevant to this project's interest in nicknaming/naming
  conventions.
- A 作文練習 (composition exercise) topic list for this lesson includes "あなたの好きな言葉（格言、名言、
  慣用表現など）を紹介してください" — asking the learner to introduce a favorite proverb/saying/idiom from
  their own language, confirming the lesson is explicitly designed as a cross-cultural proverb-comparison
  exercise.

### Lesson 16 — "時" (Time), pattern set 2 (p. 114): weather/disaster 関連語 cluster

### Lesson 18 — 予想・期待(2) 行列のできる店 (Expectation 2), p. 128

Part 2 of the arc begun at Lesson 8. Confirmed grammar points (drill page, full example sentences
elided per the drill-skip rule, but the patterns themselves are substantive):
- **〜どおり** — "just as expected/planned" (期待どおり "as hoped," 予想どおり "as predicted").
- **〜に反して** — "contrary to ~" (期待に反して "contrary to expectations").
- **案の定、案外、まさか** — a three-way contrastive set of expectation-outcome adverbs: 案の定 "just as
  expected" (typically of a *foreseen negative* outcome), 案外 "unexpectedly" (outcome differs from
  expectation, often pleasantly/neutrally), まさか "surely not!" (strong disbelief, often followed by
  はずがない "there's no way that ~"). Explicitly cross-referenced forward to 長文読解<4> in the appendix.

### Lesson 20 — 説明・結論 車のコミュニケーション (Explanation/Conclusion), p. 141

- **〜わけだ** — "it follows that ~, no wonder ~" (stating a natural/obvious conclusion). Explicit source
