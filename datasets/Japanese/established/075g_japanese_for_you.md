## Grammar points

### 1. ~に決まっています (~に決まっている) — "It's bound to be the case that..."
Expresses strong, confident expectation ("obviously," "it is sure to be..."). The plain form
precedes に決まっている, but Noun+だ or -な adjective+だ drop だ: うそだ→うそに決まっている「It's
obviously a lie.」A drill version of this pattern is given with explicit gendered answer-tags:
西高に決まってるよ。(男) vs. 西高に決まってるわよ。(女) — confirming the men's よ / women's わよ
sentence-final pattern already seen in earlier lessons generalizes to this construction too.
(pp. 109, 113)

### 2. ~ようです (~ようだ) — "It seems that..."
よう is a noun, so the preceding predicate takes its noun-modifying form: Noun+の (アメリカ人のようです)、
-な adjective+な (親切なようです)、-い adjective and verbs unchanged (忙しいようです、行くようです).
Expresses an inference based on what the speaker has observed. (p. 110)

### 3. ~そうです (~そうだ) — hearsay, "they say.../I hear..."
The plain form precedes そうです (distinct from the appearance-そうだ of Lesson 1 Note 5, which
attaches to a stem): 山田さんはスピード違反でつかまったそうです。「I hear Mr. Yamada was caught
speeding.」(p. 110)

### 4. ~でしょう（~だろう）"probably" / ~かもしれません（~かもしれない）"might be..."
Both take the plain form, with Noun+だ／-な adjective+だ losing だ before either: 好きだ→彼女は好きで
しょう／きらいかもしれません。でしょう signals a stronger probability judgment than the more tentative
かもしれません. (p. 111)

### 5. ~はずです（~はずだ）and its negative ~はずがない
はず, like よう, is a noun, so the preceding predicate takes its noun-modifying form. Its core
meaning is "expectation based on reliable information/knowledge" — stronger and more
evidence-grounded than でしょう: 先生は大学のそばに住んでいらっしゃるはずです。「I'm sure the professor
lives near the university (I have good reason to believe this).」~はずがない expresses a strong
*negative* expectation, "there's no way that...": 夫人に子供の気持ちがわかるはずがない。「Adults surely
can't understand how children feel.」(pp. 112–113)
