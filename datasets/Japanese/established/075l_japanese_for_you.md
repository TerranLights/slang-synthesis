## Grammar points

### 1. ~とばかり思っていた
"Always thought that... (but it was wrong)"; "felt sure that..." — used when a belief turns out
to be mistaken: 日本の女の人はみんなやさしくて、おとなしいとばかり思っていました「I always thought all
Japanese women were gentle and reserved」. (p. 190)

### 2. って
A contraction of と, という, or というのは. Can also be used clause-finally like そうだ ("I hear...")
— see Lesson 8 Note 3: 彼はびっくりしたって言ってました「He said that he was surprised」. (p. 191)

### 3. ~って（という）感じだ
"To give an impression of..." — みそはアメリカのピーナツバターって感じですね「*Miso* reminds me of
American peanut butter」. (p. 191)

### 4. Verb (past)/stative verb/-い adjective/-な adjective な/Noun の + つもりだ
"To think... (erroneously)"; "under the impression of..." — used when the speaker holds a belief
that others disagree with: 彼は日本語が上手なつもりです「He thinks that his Japanese is good」. (p.
191)

### 5. ばかり
"Do nothing but..."; "do... all the time" — replaces the direct-object marker を: 彼は朝からコーヒー
ばかり飲んでいます「He has done nothing but drink coffee since morning」. (p. 192–193)

### 6. ため
"For...; in order to..." — Verb (plain, non-past) + ため modifies a following noun, while ために
modifies a predicate adverbially: 車を買うために、お金をためています「I'm saving in order to buy a
car」. (p. 193)

### 7. ~たり、~たりする
Expresses variable/alternating conditions ("some are..., others are..."; "sometimes..., sometimes
..."); see also Lesson 5 Note 5. Usually followed by する, though する can be deleted in some cases:
雨が降ったり、やんだりしています「It is raining on and off」. (p. 193)

### 8. まさか…じゃないでしょうね
"Don't tell me that..." — expresses the worst possibility the speaker fears: テイラーさんがいません
ね。まさかまだ寝ているんじゃないでしょうね「I don't see Taylor here. Don't tell me he is still
asleep」. (p. 193)

### 9. Question word…ても、~
"No matter what/where/who, etc."; "whenever, whatever, whoever": どこへ行っても、じろじろ見られます
「Wherever I go, I am looked up and down」. (p. 193–194)

### 10. ~に（は）+ ぴったりだ／派手だ／地味だ etc.
The particle に indicates suitability or fitness with predicates such as ぴったりだ, 似合う, いい,
悪い, 派手だ, 地味だ: この色はあなたには似合いませんよ「This color doesn't suit you」. (p. 194)

### 11. Verb (plain form) + 気がしない
"Not feel like..." — 寒くて、布団から出る気がしません「It's so cold that I don't feel like getting
out of bed」. (p. 194)

### 12. ~っぽい
A suffix adding the meaning "...ish" to nouns and (a limited set of) verbs; derived adjectives are
colloquially used: 日本の大学生には子供っぽい人が多いです「Many Japanese university students are
childish」; 年を取ると、忘れっぽくなります「As we get older, we become forgetful」. (p. 194)

### 13. Adjective stem + さ
Nouns are derived from adjectives by adding the suffix さ to the stem; the derived forms indicate
a degree of the quality (not the quality itself): 大きい→大きさ "size"; 長い→長さ "length"; 高い→高さ
"height"; 稀い→稀さ "fearfulness" (as glossed by the source). (p. 195)
