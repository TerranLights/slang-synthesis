## Grammar points

### 1. XXこそ
Emphatic focus marker meaning "precisely, indeed" — spotlights XX, often in a rebuttal: 君こそ
ひどいじゃないか「No way, you are the one who is lousy」. (p. 171)

### 2. ~せいだ ／ ~おかげだ
Both express cause. せいだ is chosen when the result is bad ("as a result of...; to be blamed
on..."); おかげだ when the result is good ("thanks to...; owing to..."). Both せい and おかげ are
nouns and take the preceding predicate's noun-modifying form: 熱のせいで考えられない「I can't think
because I have a fever」; 友達に教えてもらったおかげで、試験にパスしました「Thanks to my friend
having taught me, I passed the exam」. (p. 171)

### 3. ~っけ
Used when the speaker is trying to recall a fact he/she should already know; added to the past
tense or copula だ (which itself takes だっけ): 財布はどこに置いたっけ「Now let's see if I can recall
where I put my wallet」. (p. 172)

### 4. 〈Sentence 1〉たら、〈Sentence 2 (past)〉
"When Sentence 1 happened, Sentence 2 happened" — describes events occurring in sequence; this
たら cannot be substituted by ば. Crucially, if Sentence 2's action is something the speaker does
intentionally after Sentence 1 completes, たら cannot be used at all (the book flags this as a
genuine usage limitation with its own corrected-vs-inadequate example pair). (p. 172–173)

### 5. ~ふりをする
"To pretend..." — ふり is a noun, so the preceding predicate takes its noun-modifying form: 私は
フランス人のふりをしました「I pretended to be French」; irregularly, 死んだふりをする "to pretend to
be dead." (p. 173)

### 6. ~じゃありませんか (with ~じゃない for men or women, ~じゃないか for men only)
Used to present an opinion, suggestion, or reason emphatically: 仕事がいやなら、やめればいいじゃあり
ませんか「If you don't like the work, why don't you quit?」(p. 173–174)

### 7. ~わけがない
"There is no reason (to think) that...; I can't believe that..." — わけ is a noun, so the
preceding predicate occurs in its noun-modifying form; usually interchangeable with ~はずがない:
彼はさっきテニスをしてたから、病気のわけがありません「He can't be sick because he was playing tennis
a while ago」. (p. 174)

### 8. ~てしかたがない (しょうがない)
"It's terribly..." — used with adjectives/verbs expressing emotion (loneliness, sadness, joy),
desire ("want to..."), or physical state (hunger, thirst): うちへ帰りたくてしょうがないんです「I
really want to go home」. (p. 174)

### 9. Sentenceのは XXだ
"It is XX that..." — focuses on an element XX (noun, phrase, or clause) of an ordinary sentence;
the rest of the sentence precedes のは: 文句を言いたいのはぼくだよ「It's me who wants to complain」。
(p. 175)

### 10. Verb-stem っぱなし
Indicates the uninterrupted continuation of an action/state for quite a long period: 窓が開けっぱな
しです「The window is kept open (although it's raining)」. (p. 175)

### 11. ~ことがある
"There are times when..." — preceded by the predicate's non-past form; こと is a noun, so the
preceding predicate occurs in its noun-modifying form: たいてい暇ですが、忙しいこともあります「We are
usually free, but there are times when we are busy」. (p. 175–176)
