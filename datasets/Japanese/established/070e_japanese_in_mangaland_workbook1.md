## Grammar points

### Block structure deliberately isolates drill content from narrative content
The book's own introduction (pp. 5–6) explains each block's fixed structure: Rakujō manga chapter →
reading comprehension → vocabulary drill → grammar drill → kanji practice. This confirms, from the
source's own design, that the narrative (Rakujō) pages are where any register-bearing natural
dialogue lives, while the drill pages that make up most of the book's bulk are deliberately
decontextualized practice items (matching exercises, stroke-order grids, romaji transcription) with
no register content of their own — justifying this chunk's light-touch extraction approach.

### Two command registers in a single dialogue exchange (Chapter 6 climax)
The sampled climax page (p.68) juxtaposes だまってくれ ("shut up," curt ～てくれ request form used by an
angry male character against a subordinate who keeps talking) and 待ちなさい ("wait!", the firmer but
non-vulgar ～なさい imperative used by an angry mother figure toward her own son). Both are commands,
both are emotionally charged, but they sit at different register/vulgarity levels — a clean,
source-internal example of how Japanese command-form choice encodes social relationship and
emotional register simultaneously, reinforcing (with a second independent data point) the point Vol.
1's Lesson 30 makes explicitly about commands being a manga-speech hallmark largely avoided in real
polite conversation.
