## Grammar points

### Lesson 2

#### 「〜ことにした」("decided to ~")

Marks a deliberate decision by the speaker/subject to do something, framed as the outcome of a
choice rather than a simple future-tense statement. Source's own example, drawn directly from the
invitation letter: 私たちは今度結婚することにしました。("We've decided to get married.") (p. 9,
pattern A).

#### 「〜よう（だ）」(conjecture/appearance marker, combining two clauses)

Used to report that something appears/seems to be the case based on observable evidence, and to
chain that observation into a following clause via 〜ようで. Source's own example: 昔からの友だちから
招待状が来ると、家族のだれかが結婚するようで、うれしいです。("When an invitation arrives from an old
friend, it seems like someone in the family is getting married, and I'm happy about it.") (p. 10,
pattern B).

### Lesson 3

#### 「〜がる」(expressing a third party's visible emotion/sensation)

Attaches to the stem of an emotion/sensation adjective (e.g. 寂しい → 寂しがる) to describe an
outwardly observable state in someone other than the speaker — contrasted against a stated claim
that may be insincere. Source's own example from the reading passage: 母は父がいないと楽だと言って
いました。もちろんこれは冗談で、本当は寂しがっているんです。父が電話をかけてくると喜ぶので分かります。("Mother
says it's easier without Father around. Of course that's a joke — she's really lonely for him.
You can tell because she's delighted when he calls.") (p. 15, pattern A).

#### 「できるだけ〜ようにする」("try to ~ as much as possible")

Expresses a habitual effort toward a goal, rather than a guaranteed outcome. Source's own example:
できるだけ手紙を書くようにしています。("I try to write letters as much as I can.") (p. 15, pattern B).

#### 「〜なんて」(dismissive/minimizing "things like ~")

Marks the preceding noun or clause as something the speaker considers unworthy of concern or not
worth taking seriously — frequently paired with a negating follow-up. Source's own example: A: 辞書
がなければこれは読めないでしょう。B: 辞書なんてなくてもいいです。("A: You probably can't read this without
a dictionary. B: Who needs a dictionary — it's fine without one.") (p. 16, pattern C).

#### 「〜とか〜とか」(listing illustrative examples, "~ or ~ or something")

Strings together two (or more) example items/clauses without claiming the list is exhaustive —
softer and more casual than a full enumeration. Source's own example, drawn from the reading
passage: ごはんのときいつもうちに帰っているかなとか、この番組を見てるかなとか言っています。("At mealtimes
we're always saying things like 'I wonder if he's home now' or 'I wonder if he's watching this
show.'") (p. 16, pattern D).
