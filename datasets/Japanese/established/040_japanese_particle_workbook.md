# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Particle Workbook (Full Volume, Usages 1-188)

**Source:** Taeko Kamiya, *Japanese Particle Workbook* (Kodansha USA, 2006 — Image Conversion Plug-in
scan dated 2006), pp. 13-157 of the 192-page scan (the "Japanese Particle Workbook" lesson section
proper, usages 1-188 of 188).

**Coverage note / non-redundant-supplement scope decision:** This project already has three other
particle references extracted: `032_dictionary_japanese_particles.md` (Kawashima), `034_all_about_particles.md`
(Chino), and `036_tell_difference_particles.md` (Chino). Per the non-redundant-supplement pattern,
this workbook is Kamiya's own independently-authored presentation of the same ~60 particles' ~188
usages — every lesson restates a known particle rule, so **grammar rule prose is kept only where this
book's own phrasing/framing differs usefully or adds a nuance/exception not already on file; where a
rule is a straightforward restatement, it is noted tersely with a cross-reference rather than
re-paraphrased at length.** What this book contributes that is genuinely new regardless of rule
overlap: (1) its own full set of distinct example sentences/vocabulary — a fresh, independently
authored corpus of ~350+ sentences across 188 usages, useful raw material for later mechanics
analysis; (2) a few usages/sub-distinctions (e.g. `dake ni`, `ni shite wa`, `ni totte`, `ni tsuite`,
`mono no`, `tokoro de`, `dokoro ka`, `koto`, `mono ka`, `kashira`, `kana`, compound forms like `to ka`,
`te mo`, `te wa`, `nado`/`yara`/`dano`/`toka` list-particles, and sentence-final particles `ne`, `nee`,
`na`, `nā`, `yo`, `wa` (female), `sa`, `ze`, `zo`) not previously tabulated as their own entries in
032/034/036. The **Vocabulary** table below captures every distinct content word (nouns, verbs,
adjectives, proper names) appearing across the example sentences — not a sample, every distinct item
— while skipping vocabulary that is a pure repeat of a word already logged in this same file.

**Scope excluded as redundant, by design:** (a) the book's own 56 "Exercise" fill-in-the-blank drill
sets (per the book's own introduction, exercises reuse only vocabulary already introduced in the
example sentences — "those already learned are used again for reinforcement" — so they introduce no
new vocabulary or grammar); (b) the "Answers to Exercises" section (pp. ~186-192 of the scan — pure
particle-choice answer keys, no explanatory content); (c) the "Alphabetical Finding List" section
(pp. ~159-185 of the scan) — confirmed by inspection to be a full A-Z reorganization of the *same*
188 usages already covered in the main lesson sequence (pp. 13-157), each usage restated with one
abbreviated example pair instead of the main lesson's fuller 2-4 examples. Spot-checked against the
main-lesson entries for the same usage numbers (e.g. `dake` 1-5, `bakari` 5-6, `dake ni`) and confirmed
to be the identical rule/example content resequenced alphabetically by particle name for quick lookup,
not new material — skipped in full per the coverage rule's "repeated... duplicate example sentences
that don't introduce new vocabulary or grammar" clause.

**Vision-reading note:** This source has no text layer at all (`pdftotext`/`pdfimages` confirm pure
CCITT-encoded scanned raster images, Adobe Acrobat 7.05 "Image Conversion Plug-in," no ToUnicode
mapping of any kind) — every usage below was read directly from rendered page images. No handwritten
marginalia was found anywhere in the sampled/read pages; this appears to be a clean, unmarked copy.
Print was consistently sharp and legible throughout; no low-confidence reads were required for the
romanized/English portions. A handful of kanji in small body text were read with ordinary (not
"low_confidence") care given the print's generally crisp quality — flagged per-row only where genuine
doubt existed.

## Grammar points

### wa (1) — topic marker (usage 1, p.13)
Marks the topic of a sentence ("as for..."). The topic may or may not coincide with the grammatical
subject (e.g. *kaidan wa chūi shite kudasai*, "as for the stairs, be careful" — "stairs" is not the
subject of "be careful"). Already covered in 032/034/036; kept here only for this distinction note.

### ka (1) — question marker (usage 2, p.13)
Sentence-final particle marking a yes/no or content question. Standard; no new nuance over prior
sources.

### ga (1) — subject marker for new/emphasized information (usage 3, p.14)
Marks a grammatical subject when it is newly introduced information or being emphasized (contrasted
with topic-marking *wa* for already-known information), and marks an interrogative pronoun used as a
subject (*dare ga*, *nani ga*) as well as the subject of the answering sentence. Kamiya's own example
pair ("hana ga saite imasu. Hana wa kirei desu" — new-information *ga* then known-topic *wa* for the
same referent in the next clause) is a clean minimal-pair illustration of the wa/ga distinction, worth
noting as a teaching example distinct from 032/034/036's own illustrations.

### ni (1) — location/existence marker (usage 4, p.16)
Marks the location of an animate or inanimate thing with *aru*/*iru*, including as the complement of
a wa-topicalized location ("sono hon'ya wa Kanda ni arimasu").

### no (1) — possessive/modifying-phrase particle (usage 5, pp.16-17)
Links two nouns indicating possession ("watashi no jisho"), location ("tsukue no ue ni"), or an
attributive-noun modifier ("Nihongo no gakusei," "eiga no kippu," "Ginza no resutoran"). Notes that the
second noun may be omitted when context is clear (*Tanaka-san no desu*).

### wa (2) — contrastive topic marker (usage 6, p.17)
Marks contrast between two items, where the second contrasted item may be left implicit (and the
particle is then pronounced with stress). Distinct nuance from usage (1)'s plain topic-marking.

### ni (2) — time and frequency marker (usage 7, p.20)
Marks a specific point in time (*ichiji ni*, *getsuyōbi ni*) — optional with the four seasons — and,
in the pattern noun(duration) + *ni* + noun(frequency), marks a rate ("isshūkan ni nido," "twice a
week"); *oki ni* marks an interval ("jippun oki ni," "every ten minutes"). Explicitly lists time words
that never take *ni* because they don't pinpoint a specific time: *asa, kyō, kinō, asu/ashita, senshū,
raigetsu, kyonen, ichijikan, itsu* — a tidy closed-class list worth having on file verbatim since it's
a common learner-error point not spelled out as a list in 032/034/036.

### e (1) — direction marker (usage 8, p.21)
Marks direction/goal of motion ("Tōkyō e itta"). Can combine with *no* (*buchō e no tegami*, "a letter
to the department head") or *wa* (*ginkō e wa itta. Yūbinkyoku e wa ikanakatta* — contrastive).

### o (1) — direct object marker (usage 9, p.21)
Marks the direct object of a transitive verb. When an object is topicalized/contrasted, *wa* replaces
*o* entirely rather than combining with it (*wain wa nomu. Sake wa nomanai* — contrast drawn without
*o* ever appearing).

### to (1) — exhaustive-list conjunction (usage 10, p.24)
Joins two or more nouns as a complete list ("and," with the implication nothing else is included).

### ya (1) — partial-list conjunction (usage 11, p.24)
Joins two or more nouns as a non-exhaustive, representative list ("and ... among other things").
Contrasts directly with *to* (1)'s exhaustiveness.

### nado (1) — partial-list tag, often paired with ya (usage 12, p.25)
Added after a *ya*-list (or after a single noun) to mark it as non-exhaustive ("and so forth," "things
like"); the case particle that would otherwise follow (*wa, ga, o*) may be dropped when *nado* is
present. Can combine with *e* (*nado e*, "to [places like] X and so forth").

### mo (1) — "too / also / either" (usage 13, p.27)
Replaces *wa*, *ga*, or *o* on the noun it marks, meaning "too/also" in an affirmative context or
"either" in a negative one. Can double up with *ni* or *e* (*ni mo*, *e mo*).

### mo (2) — "both...and.../neither...nor..." (usage 14, pp.27-28)
Repeated as noun + *mo* + noun + *mo*: with a positive predicate, "both X and Y"; with a negative
predicate, "neither X nor Y." Works the same way doubled on adjectival stems (*hiroku mo semaku mo
nai*, "neither spacious nor cramped") and on double-marked location/direction phrases (*natsu ni mo
fuyu ni mo*, *Ginza e mo Ueno e mo*).

### ni (3) — indirect object marker (usage 15, p.28)
Marks the indirect object/recipient of a verb ("ane ni tegami o kaita," "imōto wa Amerikajin ni
Nihongo o oshiete iru"). Can double with *mo* (*Sara ni mo*) or *wa* (*Tomu ni wa hanashita. Jimu ni wa
hanasanakatta* — contrastive, paralleling *e wa*'s pattern above).

### ni (4) — surface marker for an action's location (usage 16, p.31)
Marks the surface on which an action takes place ("kokuban ni kanji o kaite imasu," writing kanji ON
the blackboard; "kami ni... kaite kudasai," write on paper; "hangā ni kakete kudasai," hang on a
hanger).

### de (1) — location-of-action marker (usage 17, p.31)
Marks where an action takes place, contrasted directly against *ni*'s existence-marking: the book's
own side-by-side note states that when either *de* or *ni* is grammatically possible, *de* puts
emphasis on the action while *ni* emphasizes existence ("kono koppu wa supā de/ni utte imasu" — sold
AT the supermarket, either reading works). Also covers *de* + *aru* for an event occurring at a
location ("kōdō de eiga ga arimasu," there will be a movie in the auditorium) — a usage-pattern
distinct from the plain action-location pattern, worth flagging since it can look like the existence
construction normally reserved for *ni*.

### to (2) — accompaniment marker ("with") (usage 18, p.32)
Marks a person with whom an action is performed, often paired with *issho ni* ("together"). The book
notes *to*/*to wa* can be replaced by *ni*/*ni wa* in this function — a cross-reference worth keeping
since it's an explicit interchangeability rule not spelled out as tersely in 032/034/036.

### no (2) — apposition marker (usage 19, p.33)
Links a noun (role/title) in apposition with a following name: "shachō no Miki-san" (Mr. Miki, the
president), "bengoshi no Hara-san" (Mr. Hara, a lawyer) — distinct from usage (1)'s possession/
location/modifier senses.

### kara (1) — starting point in time or place (usage 20, p.33)
Marks the temporal or spatial starting point ("kuji kara," from nine o'clock; "gaikoku kara," from a
foreign country). Can double with *no* or *mo*.

### yori (1) — formal/written equivalent of kara (1) (usage 21, p.33)
Same starting-point meaning as *kara* (1) but more formal and typically written rather than spoken
(e.g. train-platform announcements, formal event notices).

### made (1) — endpoint/limit marker (usage 22, p.34)
Marks a limit in time, space, or quantity ("jūji made," until ten o'clock; "ie kara gakkō made," from
home to school; "gonin made noreru," can seat up to five). Can double with *no* (a ticket's
destination) or *ni* (a deadline, "yūshoku made ni kaerimasu" — a usage-nuance distinction between
*made* "up to/through" and *made ni* "by [a point]" worth flagging explicitly, as it's a common
learner confusion).

### kurai/gurai (1) — approximate amount, colloquial register (usage 23, p.37)
Marks an approximate number/amount after a number or interrogative ("dono kurai," "ikura gurai").
Explicitly flagged by the book as the colloquial member of a three-way near-synonym set with *hodo*
and *bakari* (next two entries) — useful to have the register distinction on file as a trio rather
than as isolated entries.

### hodo (1) — approximate amount, neutral register (usage 24, p.38)
Interchangeable with *kurai/gurai* for approximate amounts, but without the colloquial coloring.

### bakari (1) — approximate amount (usage 25, p.38)
A third near-synonym for approximate amount, interchangeable with *kurai/gurai* or *hodo* in this
sense (its later usages, covered further on, diverge into "only"/"just" and causal senses not
synonymous with the other two).

### de (2) — means/instrument/material marker (usage 26, p.39)
Marks the means, instrument, or material by which an action is done ("fune de," by boat; "hashi de
tabemasu," eat with chopsticks; "kami de ningyō o tsukurimasu," make dolls out of paper) — distinct
from usage (1)'s location-of-action sense. Also covers language-of-speaking ("Eigo de hanashite
kudasai").

### ka (2) — alternative/choice marker (usage 27, p.40)
Marks a choice between two or more nouns ("sētā ka jaketto," a sweater or a jacket) or between two
full sentences ("anata ga ikimasu ka. Yamada-san ga ikimasu ka" — "are you going, or is Miss Yamada
going?"), distinct from usage (1)'s plain question-marking function.

### o (2) — point-of-departure marker (usage 28, p.40)
Marks the point of departure/separation with verbs like *deru* (leave), *oriru* (get off),
*sotsugyō suru* (graduate), *intai suru* (retire) — "ie o demasu," "densha o orimashita," "daigaku o
sotsugyō suru."

### o (3) — route-of-motion marker (usage 29, p.41)
Marks the path or route traversed with motion verbs ("hiroi michi o arukimashita," "hikōki ga sora o
tonde iru," "kado o hidari e magatte kudasai") — distinct from the direct-object (1) and
point-of-departure (2) senses already covered.

### ni (5) — point-of-arrival marker (usage 30, p.44)
Marks the destination/arrival point with verbs like *tsuku* (arrive) — "densha ga eki ni
tsukimashita," "fune wa itsu minato ni tsukimasu ka."

### ni (6) — entering-motion marker (usage 31, p.45)
Marks the target of an entering motion — boarding (*noru*), entering a bath (*hairu*), joining a club
(*hairu*) — "densha ni norimashita," "o-furo ni hairimasu," "tenisu kurabu ni haitte iru."

### ni (7) — purpose marker with motion verbs (usage 32, p.45)
Marks purpose when paired with a motion verb expressing movement from one place to another: either
noun(activity) + *ni* + motion verb ("kaimono ni ikimasu," go shopping) or V(-masu stem) + *ni* +
motion verb ("ringo o kai ni mise ni haitta," went into the store to buy apples; "eiga o mi ni itta,"
went to see a movie).

### to (3) — quotation marker (usage 33, p.46)
Marks a direct or indirect quotation, or what someone thinks/hears, before verbs like *iu* (say),
*omou* (think), *kiku* (hear) — "Jimu ga 'Ohayō' to iimashita," "Tsuri ni ikō to omoimasu," "Biru ga
nyūin shita to kiita."

### to (4) — naming marker in the form "to iu" (usage 34, p.47)
In the fixed pattern noun + *to iu* + noun, names or identifies something ("Miki to iu hito," a person
called Miki; "Yamato to iu ryokan," an inn called Yamato).

### to ka (1) — "something like" / hedged quotation (usage 35, p.47)
Combination of *to* + *ka* expressing an approximate or hedged quotation/naming ("something like,"
"something to the effect that") — distinct from the exhaustive-naming *to iu* (usage 34) and from
*toka*'s later, unrelated list-marking sense (usage 93, a separate orthographic/usage entry the book
itself distinguishes by spacing: "to ka" here vs. "toka" later).

### de (3) — limit/scope marker for a superlative (usage 36, p.49)
Marks the scope/domain within which a superlative holds ("kurasu de ichiban ii gakusei," the best
student IN the class; "Nihon de ichiban nagai hashi," the longest bridge IN Japan), including as
noun + *no naka* + *de* ("sensei no naka de," among the teachers).

### to (5) — comparison marker, paired form (usage 37, p.49)
In the pattern noun + *to* + noun + *to*, sets up an explicit two-item comparison typically answered
with *dochira* ("kono tokei to sono tokei to, dochira ga takai desu ka").

### yori (2) — comparative marker ("more than") (usage 38, p.50)
Marks the standard of comparison ("kore wa sore yori karui desu," this is lighter than that). Also
covers the V-u + *yori* ... *hō ga* idiom structure for preference ("hikōki de iku hō ga fune de iku
yori anzen desu," going by plane is safer than going by boat) and the *yori shikata ga arimasen*
idiom ("there's no choice but to...").

### yori (3) — superlative marker with an interrogative (usage 39, p.51)
Interrogative + *yori (mo)* produces a superlative sense ("doko yori (mo) shizuka desu," quieter than
anywhere else; "dare yori (mo)," more than anyone; "nani yori (mo)," more than anything).

### kurai/gurai (2) — comparison in a positive sentence ("as...as") (usage 40, p.52)
Noun + *kurai/gurai* + positive predicate expresses equivalence ("gorufujō kurai ōkii," as big as a
golf course) — *gurai* again flagged as the more colloquial variant.

### hodo (2) — comparison in a negative sentence ("not as...as") (usage 41, p.52)
Noun + *hodo* + negative predicate expresses a negative comparison ("Tomu hodo ii gakusei wa
imasen," no student as good as Tom; "neko wa inu hodo hayaku hashirenai"). Together, *kurai* (2)/
*hodo* (2) form a polarity-conditioned pair — positive-sentence vs. negative-sentence equivalence —
worth keeping as a pair rather than two unrelated entries.

### ka (3) — indefinite-pronoun marker ("something/someone/somewhere/someday") (usage 42, p.54)
Interrogative + *ka* forms an indefinite pronoun ("nani ka," something; "dare ka," someone; "itsu
ka," someday; "doko ka e," to somewhere). Distinct from ka(1) question-marking and ka(2)
choice-marking.

### mo (3) — negative-indefinite marker ("nothing/nobody/nowhere") (usage 43, p.55)
Interrogative + *mo* + negative predicate forms a negative indefinite ("nani mo arimasen," there is
nothing; "dare mo shiranai," nobody knows), paralleling ka(3)'s positive indefinite but requiring a
negative verb and allowing further doubling (*e mo*, *to mo*).

### ni (8) — marker of change/choice with naru, suru (usage 44, p.55)
Marks the result of a change (noun + *ni* + *naru*, "pairotto ni narimasu," will become a pilot; "byōki
ni narimashita," became ill) or a choice/decision (noun + *ni* + *suru*, "sushi ni shimasu," I'll have
sushi; "kayōbi ni shimashō," let's make it Tuesday).

### o (4) — occupation/position marker with suru (usage 45, p.58)
Noun + *o* + *suru* names an occupation or position someone holds/held ("isha o shite imasu," is
working as a doctor; "gakuchō o shite ita," was serving as university president).

### demo (1) — emphatic "even" (usage 46, p.58)
Marks emphasis, "even X [can/does Y]" — "kodomo demo dekimasu," even a child can do it; "sensei demo
shiranai," even a teacher doesn't know; can double with *made* (*made demo*) or *to* (*to demo*).

### demo (2) — positive-emphasis indefinite ("anything/anyone/any time/anywhere") (usage 47, p.59)
Interrogative + *demo* (with a positive predicate) forms a universal/free-choice indefinite ("nan
demo tabemasu," eats anything; "dare demo tsukaeru," anyone can use it; "itsu demo," any time) —
contrasts with mo(3)'s negative-predicate indefinite and complements ka(3)'s single-instance
indefinite. Can double with *kara* (*kara demo*, "from anywhere").

### de (4) — cause/reason marker (usage 48, p.60)
Marks the cause of a resulting state ("byōki de shigoto ni ikemasen," can't go to work due to
illness; "kaji de ie ga yakemashita," the house burned down due to a fire).

### kara (2) — source marker (giver, cause, or material) (usage 49, p.60)
Marks the source of something received or a cause ("Oda-san kara... kiita," heard from Mr. Oda;
"seifu kara hojokin o moratta," received a grant from the government; "tsukare kara byōki ni natta,"
became ill from fatigue), and marks a raw material that undergoes a transformation ("wain wa budō
kara tsukuru," wine is made from grapes). The book draws an explicit, pedagogically useful
distinction here between *kara* and *de* for materials: ***de*** is used when the material retains
its original physical state in the result (e.g. "kami de ningyō o tsukutta," a doll made of/with
paper — the paper is still recognizably paper), while ***kara*** is used when a chemical/
transformative change occurs in the process (grapes → wine is no longer recognizably grapes). This
material-*de*-vs-*kara* distinction is a genuinely new, precisely-stated nuance not found this
explicitly in 032/034/036.

### ni (9) — giver/service-provider marker (usage 50, p.61)
Marks a person who gives something (noun(person) + *ni* + noun(thing) + *o* + *morau*/*narau*,
"chichi ni kamera o moraimashita," received a camera from father) or who performs a service for the
speaker (noun(person) + *ni* + V-te + *morau*, "Hara-san ni... itte moratta," had Miss Hara go for
me). The book flags that *ni* is replaceable by *kara* for the giver sense, but **not** for the
service-performing sense, and that an impersonal/institutional giver requires *kara* rather than *ni*
("seifu kara hojokin o moratta," not *ni*) — a precise distributional constraint worth keeping
verbatim since it's a common error point not spelled out this specifically in 032/034/036.

### ga (2) — double-ga marker for sensation/ability/necessity/emotion predicates (usage 51, pp.61-62)
In the construction noun(topic) + *wa* + noun(subject/object) + *ga*, marks the grammatical subject of
a body-part/attribute predicate ("zō wa hana ga nagai desu," elephants have long trunks — lit. "as for
elephants, the trunk is long") or the logical object of a verb/adjective of sensation ("ongaku ga
kikoemasu," can hear music), ability/necessity (*hanasemasu*, *dekimasu*, *irimasu*), adjectival-noun
quality (*jōzu*, *suki*), or emotion/desire (*ureshii*, *kanashii*, *hoshii*). A single broad
construction covering many sub-senses; the book's own examples are a good set of minimal pairs for
each predicate class.

### ga (3) — conjunction connecting two clauses, with or without contrast (usage 52, p.62)
Joins two sentences as "but" when there is a genuine contrast ("Tomu wa kimasu ga Biru wa kimasen"),
but is also used as a softening transition word with no real adversative meaning, especially before
a request or additional comment ("konsāto ni ikimasu ga issho ni kimasen ka," I'm going to the
concert — would you like to come along?). This two-use split (true contrast vs. mere transition) is
worth keeping distinct since the second use is easy to mistranslate as "but" when it isn't adversative
at all.

### o (5) — cause-of-emotion marker with emotive verbs (usage 53, p.63)
Marks the cause/target of an emotive verb ("kare no seikō o yorokonde imasu," pleased about his
success; "kodomo no shi o kanashinda," grieved over the child's death; "inu o osoremasu," afraid of
dogs) — a distinct sense from ga(2)'s emotion-adjective construction, since here the predicate is a
verb rather than an adjective.

### de (5) — condition marker (usage 54, p.67)
Marks the condition/manner under which an action occurs ("hadashi de hashitte iru," running
barefoot; "nama de tabemasu," eat it raw) or a rate ("jikan de haraimasu," pay by the hour; "ichi
rittoru kan de uru," sell in one-liter cans).

### de (6) — numerical limit marker (usage 55, p.67)
Marks a bounding number/quantity — a price, an age, a duration within which something is completed
("hitori de," alone, by just one person; "nisen'en de," for 2,000 yen; "hachijūgosai de
shinimashita," died at 85; "ninen de... sumasemashita," finished in two years).

### dake (1) — limit on things/amounts ("only") (usage 56, p.68)
Marks a limit on a thing or amount, "only" ("otoko no ko dake," only boys; "ichido dake," only
once"). Can double with *no*, *to*.

### dake (2) — limit on an action/state ("only, just") (usage 57, p.70)
Marks a limit on an action or state rather than a thing ("jūsu o ippai nomu dake da," I just drink one
glass of juice; "amai dake desu," is just sweet, that's all; "takai dake de yokunai desu," just
expensive and no good").

### de (7) — completion/expiration-time marker, replaceable by ni (usage 58, p.72)
Marks the time by which something is completed or expires ("sangatsu de kansei shimasu," will be
completed as of March; "gogatsu mikka de kireru," expires as of May third"). The book draws an
explicit nuance distinction here: *de* hints that something lasts up to the stated time, whereas
*ni* simply states the time plainly, without that "up to/through" implication — a subtle but genuinely
useful distinction not found this explicitly elsewhere on file.

### demo (3) — "or something" alternative marker, following a noun (usage 59, p.72)
Suggests an unspecified alternative after a single noun ("o-cha demo ikaga desu ka," how about tea or
something?), distinct from demo(1)'s "even" and demo(2)'s universal-indefinite senses.

### mo (4) — emphatic marker in both positive and negative sentences (usage 60, p.73)
Number/counter + *mo* + positive verb emphasizes a surprisingly large amount ("gosatsu mo
kaimashita," bought as many as five; "nando mo," many times); "one" + counter + *mo* + negative verb
emphasizes "not even one" ("ippiki mo imasen," not even one fish; "hitotsu mo kawanakatta," didn't
buy even one).

### demo (4) — "and so on" marker for two-or-more listed items (usage 61, p.73)
Noun + *demo* + noun + *demo* lists two or more items as representative examples of a larger set
("Nihongo demo Chūgokugo demo hanasemasu," can speak Japanese, Chinese, and so on; "gorufu demo
tenisu demo yarimasu," plays golf, tennis, and what not) — a *demo*-doubled counterpart to the
*ya*/*nado* partial-list pattern covered earlier (usages 11-12), applied here to full predicates
rather than bare nouns.

### bakari (2) — "nothing but" (usage 62, p.74)
Noun/V-te + *bakari* marks exclusivity of an ongoing action or an adjectival state ("sake bakari
nonde imasu," drinking nothing but sake; "terebi o mite bakari iru," doing nothing but watching TV;
"yasui bakari desu," is just cheap [and nothing else]). Doubles with *de* or *ni* (*de bakari*,
*bakari de*, *ni bakari*, *bakari ni*) — distinct from dake(1)/(2)'s plain "only," since *bakari*
here carries a mildly critical "nothing but X" nuance the book's examples consistently show (lazy/
overindulgent habitual behavior), replaceable by *dake* only in the last (adjectival) usage pattern.

### bakari (3) — "just did" / action just completed (usage 63, p.76)
V-ta + *bakari* marks an action completed only a moment ago ("okita bakari desu," just got up;
"kūkō ni tsuita bakari desu," just arrived at the airport) — the past-tense counterpart to tokoro(1)'s
V-u-stem "about to."

### tokoro (1) — "about to / in the midst of / just did" (usage 64, p.76)
A three-way aspectual marker depending on the preceding verb form: V-u + *tokoro* = about to do
something ("ima dekakeru tokoro desu," about to go out now); V-te iru/ita + *tokoro* = in the midst
of doing it ("shukudai o shite iru tokoro desu"); V-ta + *tokoro* = just finished doing it ("kare wa
dekaketa tokoro desu," he has just left) — the last of these is replaceable by *bakari* (3). A clean
single-particle illustration of how the preceding verb's aspect/tense alone switches the meaning
between "about to," "mid-action," and "just finished."

### ga (4) — subject marker in a subordinate clause with a different subject from the main clause (usage 65, p.77)
Marks the subject of a subordinate clause (*toki*, *made*, *ato de*, *mae ni*) specifically when that
subject differs from the main clause's own subject/topic ("kare ga kita toki, watashi wa shokuji o
shite imashita," when HE came, I was having a meal). Notes the main clause's topic/subject may
precede the subordinate clause ("watashi wa kare ga kuru mae ni, heya o sōji shita").

### kara (3) — conjunction "after" (usage 66, p.78)
V-te + *kara* marks a sequential "after doing X" ("shawā o abite kara nemasu," go to bed after
showering), including as a durational "it has been [n] since" construction ("kekkon shite kara gonen
ni naru," it's been five years since we married) — distinct from kara(1)/(2)'s starting-point/source
senses.

### nagara (1) — simultaneous actions by one subject ("while") (usage 67, p.78)
V(-masu stem) + *nagara* marks two actions performed simultaneously by the same subject ("aruki-
nagara hanashimashō," let's talk while walking; "rajio o kiki-nagara benkyō shimasu," study while
listening to the radio).

### ga (5) — subject marker of a relative clause (usage 68, p.79)
Marks the subject of a relative (noun-modifying) clause ("haha ga yoku kau mise," the store where my
mother often shops; "Hara-san ga tsukatte iru tsukue," the desk Miss Hara is using).

### no (3) — subject marker of a relative clause, replaceable by ga (usage 69, p.80)
The same relative-clause-subject function as ga(5), but using *no* instead — explicitly
interchangeable with it ("kare no kaku hon," the books he writes = "kare ga kaku hon"). The book
treats this *ga*/*no* interchangeability in a relative/modifying clause as a distinct, nameable rule
rather than a stray footnote, which is useful to keep on file as a single cross-reference between
usages 68 and 69.

### kara (4) — conjunction "because" (usage 70, p.81)
Sentence + *kara* gives a reason for the following clause ("ashita shiken ga aru kara, konban benkyō
shimasu," because I have an exam tomorrow, I'll study tonight") — distinct from kara(1)/(2)/(3)'s
starting-point, source, and "after" senses; this is the plain causal conjunction.

### node (1) — "because," more concrete/objective than kara (usage 71, p.81)
A second causal conjunction, presented as expressing a more concrete/objective reason than *kara*,
with an explicit restriction: the book states *node* **cannot** be followed by a main clause
expressing the speaker's volition, a command, or an invitation (unlike *kara*, which can: contrast
usage 70's own "isogashii kara, ato de kite kudasai," a request, with *node* never licensing an
equivalent construction) — a sharp, testable grammaticality distinction between the two causal
conjunctions worth having precisely on file, since it is exactly the kind of rule a learner needs to
avoid an ungrammatical sentence rather than just an awkward one. Also notes the copula *da* becomes
*na* before *node* ("shizuka na node").

### no (4) — pronoun-substitute marker ("the ... one") (usage 72, p.82)
Noun/adjective/adjectival-noun + *no*, or V + *no*, stands in for an omitted noun ("chairo no o
kudasai," give me a brown one; "kowareta no wa sutete kudasai," throw away the broken ones) —
distinct from no(3)'s relative-clause-subject sense, even though both can follow a verb.

### no (5) — nominalizer for verbs and sentences (usage 73, p.83)
V-u/sentence + *no* nominalizes an action or event so it can function as a sentence's subject or
object ("narau no wa oshieru no yori yasashii," learning is easier than teaching; "Tomu ga dansu
suru no o mita," saw Tom dance; "Hara-san ga yameru no o shiranakatta," didn't know that Mr. Hara is
quitting). Distinct from no(4): here the nominalized element is an entire action/event rather than a
stand-in for an omitted noun.

### nara (1) — conditional conjunction, supposition/condition (usage 74, p.83)
Noun/adjectival-noun/(adjective +) (no) + *nara*, or verb + (no) + *nara*, sets up a hypothetical or
conditional ("watashi nara, sonna koto wa shinai," if I were you, I wouldn't do that; "sono hon ga
hitsuyō nara, kainasai," if you need that book, buy it").

### nara (2) — topic marker, replaceable by wa (usage 75, p.85)
Noun + *nara* marks a topic, interchangeable with *wa* ("yōsho nara, sangai ni arimasu," as for
foreign books, they're on the third floor) — a conditional-flavored topic marker distinct from
nara(1)'s hypothetical-supposition sense.

### to (6) — conditional marker for an uncontrollable/automatic result (usage 76, p.85)
V-u/-nai, adj-i, or noun/adjectival-noun + *da* + *to* sets up a condition whose result is automatic
or uncontrollable ("haru ga kuru to, atatakaku narimasu," when spring comes, it gets warm). The book
states an explicit grammaticality restriction: the main clause **cannot** be a command, a request, or
volitional — mirroring node(1)'s restriction but for a different reason (here because the whole
construction describes a natural/automatic consequence, not a reasoned cause), so *to*-conditionals
read more like "whenever X, Y happens" than "if X, then please/let's Y."

### ba (1) — conditional conjunction "if...then" (usage 77, p.86)
V-e/-re or adj-kere + *ba* sets up a hypothetical/conditional with a probable or inevitable result
("chikatetsu de ikeba, nijuppun kakaru," if you go by subway, it takes 20 minutes; "yasukereba,
kaimasu," if it's cheap, I'll buy it").

### ba (2) — "ba ii" idiom, "you need only / all you have to do is" (usage 78, p.87)
V-e/-re + *ba ii* forms a fixed idiom meaning the minimum sufficient action ("mainichi sukoshi undō
sureba ii," you need only exercise a little every day; "shiritakereba, dare ka ni kikeba ii," if you
want to know, all you have to do is ask someone") — a named, fixed extension of ba(1) worth keeping
separate since the idiomatic "that's all you need to do" reading doesn't fall out automatically from
the plain conditional sense.

### ba (3) — enumerating two-or-more actions/states ("both...and" / "neither...nor") (usage 79, p.87)
Noun + *mo* + V-ba/-nakereba ... noun + *mo* + positive/negative verb (or adj-ku + *mo* +
*nakereba* ... adj-ku + *mo* + *nai*) enumerates two coordinated actions or states ("Jimu wa sashimi
mo tabereba sushi mo taberu," Jim eats both sashimi and sushi; "sono niwa wa hiroku mo nakereba
semaku mo nai," the garden is neither large nor small") — a doubled-*ba* construction distinct from
ba(1)'s single conditional, structurally parallel to the earlier mo(2) "both...and/neither...nor"
pattern (usage 14) but built on the conditional form rather than plain nouns/adjectives.

### tara (1) — conditional conjunction "if/when" (usage 80, p.88)
V-ta/-nakatta, adj-katta, or noun/adjectival-noun + *datta* + *ra* sets up a conditional similar to
*ba*/*to* ("yuki ga futtara, jitensha ni noremasen," if it snows, I can't ride a bike; "Tomu ga
kitara, issho ni sushi o tabe ni ikimasu," if/when Tom comes, I'll go eat sushi with him").

### tara (2) — conjunction, action preceding the main clause's action ("when/after") (usage 81, p.89)
V-ta + *ra* marks an action that must complete before the main clause's action occurs ("kono shigoto
o sumasetara, uchi e kaerimasu," when I finish this work, I'll go home; "hon o yondara, kaeshite
kudasai," please return the book when/after you finish reading it) — a sequential-"after" nuance of
*tara* distinct from tara(1)'s plain hypothetical "if/when."

### tokoro (2) — conjunction, a condition that brings about a discovery (usage 82, p.89)
V-ta + *tokoro* marks an action that leads to an unexpected discovery ("mō ichido shirabeta tokoro,
kono dēta wa machigatte ita," when I checked again, this data turned out to be wrong; "kare no ofisu
e itta tokoro, dokoka e iten shite ita," when I went to his office, it had moved somewhere") —
distinct from tokoro(1)'s aspectual "about to/mid-action/just finished" sense.

### te mo (1) — conjunction "even if" (usage 83, p.90)
V-te/-nakute or adj-kute + *mo* marks a concession ("isoide mo ma ni aimasen," even if you hurry,
you won't make it; "kusuri o nomanakute mo ii desu," you don't have to take the medicine," lit. "even
if you don't take it, it's fine").

### te mo (2) — paired contrasting verbs/adjectives, "whether...or not" (usage 84, p.91)
A doubled V-te mo ... V-nakute mo (or adj-kute mo ... adj-kunakute mo) construction meaning the
outcome holds regardless ("itte mo ikanakute mo watashi wa ikimasu," I'll go whether [he does] or
not; "takakute mo yasukute mo kaimasen," whether it's expensive or inexpensive, I won't buy it").

### te mo (3) — "no matter what/when/where/who," with an interrogative (usage 85, p.92)
Interrogative (+ particle) + V-te + *mo* generalizes over all values ("itsu kite mo, kare wa rusu
desu," no matter when I come, he's out; "dare ni kiite mo, shirimasen," no matter whom I ask, nobody
knows). Also covers *donna ni* + adj-kute + *mo* ("donna ni isogashikute mo," no matter how busy").

### te wa (1) — condition leading to a negative conclusion (usage 86, p.92)
V-te or adj-kute + *wa* sets up a condition whose natural conclusion is negative/undesirable ("koko
de tabete wa ikemasen," you shouldn't eat here; "konna ni furukute wa dame desu," if it's this old,
it's no good) — functions as a softened prohibition, "if you do X, it leads to [bad outcome] Y."

### nomi (1) — limit marker "only," formal/written register (usage 87, p.93)
Replaceable by *dake*, but more formal/literary ("kachō nomi ga enkai ni shusseki shita," only the
section chief attended the banquet; "ōkii nomi desu," is only big [and nothing else]). Can double
with *ni* or *de*.

### made (2) — emphatic "even" (usage 88, p.93)
Noun/V-te + *made* adds emphasis in the sense of "even" ("sensei made kōgi ni shusseki shita," even
the teachers attended the lecture; "gakkō o yasunde made tetsudatte kureta," [he] even skipped
school to help me") — distinct from made(1)'s plain "up to/until" limit sense.

### sae (1) — emphatic "even" (usage 89, p.94)
Noun/gerund + *sae* adds emphasis, "even" ("sonna koto wa saru (de) sae dekiru," even a monkey can
do that; "tatsu koto sae dekinakatta," couldn't even stand up) — a fourth member of the "even"-marker
family alongside demo(1), made(2), and (the next entry, sae itself continuing) — doubles with *kara*
or *ni*.

### sae (2) — "if only / as long as," conditions required for a result (usage 90, p.95)
Noun + *sae* + V-ba, or V(-masu) + *sae* + *sureba*/V-te + *sae* + *ireba*, or adj-ku + *sae* +
*areba*, marks the minimum necessary condition for a result ("benkyō sae sureba, ii daigaku ni
hairemasu," if you just study, you can get into a good college; "jikan sae areba," if only I had
time). A conditional-emphasis combination distinct from sae(1)'s plain "even."

### noni (1) — conjunction "although / in spite of the fact that" (usage 91, p.96)
Verb, adjective, or noun/adjectival-noun + *na* + *noni* marks an unexpected or contrary-to-
expectation contrast ("kanojo wa netsu ga aru noni, kusuri o nomanai," although she has a fever, she
doesn't take medicine; "kare wa saijitsu na noni, shigoto ni itta," although it's a holiday, he went
to work").

### nagara (2) — conjunction "although / though / but" (usage 92, p.96)
V(-masu)/adj-i/noun + *nagara* marks a concessive contrast ("o-kane ga arinagara haraimasen,"
although he has money, he doesn't pay; "mazushii nagara daigaku ni itta," although I was poor, I
went to college) — a second, concessive function of *nagara* distinct from nagara(1)'s
simultaneous-action "while" sense (usage 67); includes the fixed phrase *zannen nagara* ("I'm sorry,
but...").

### toka (1) — non-exhaustive list marker (usage 93, p.97)
Noun + *toka* + noun + *toka* (or V-u + *toka* + V-u + *toka*) lists representative, non-exhaustive
items or actions ("depāto de kutsu toka sētā toka (o) kaimashita," bought shoes, sweaters, and other
things at the department store) — orthographically and functionally the single-word counterpart to
usage 35's two-word "to ka" ("something like"), which the book itself treats as a separate entry;
functionally close to usage 12's *ya*...*nado* pattern but applicable to full predicates as well as
bare nouns.

### tari (1) — lists two-or-more actions in no particular sequence (usage 94, p.97)
V-tari + V-tari + *suru* lists representative actions without implying strict order ("nondari
tabetari odottari shita," we did things like drinking, eating, and dancing) — a single action can
also stand as representative of a broader unspecified set ("fuyu ni wa onsen ni ittari shimasu," in
winter I do things like going to hot springs").

### tari (2) — alternating actions or states ("sometimes X, sometimes Y") (usage 95, p.97)
V-tari + V-tari/-nakattari + *suru* (also adj-kattari + adj-kattari/-kunakattari + *suru*, or
noun/adjectival-noun + *dattari* + *dattari* + *suru*) marks actions or states that alternate
("hito ga ittari kitari shite iru," people are coming and going; "tenki wa yokattari yokunakattari
shita," the weather was sometimes good and sometimes not; "kare wa bengoshi dattari sakka dattari
suru," sometimes he's a lawyer, sometimes a writer") — distinct from tari(1)'s "things like X, Y, Z"
reading, here emphasizing back-and-forth alternation of exactly the listed states/actions.

### noni (2) — purpose/function marker (usage 96, p.98)
V-u + *noni* marks the purpose a thing/action serves ("sakubun o kaku noni wāpuro o tsukaimasu," I
use a word processor [in order] to write compositions; "shoppingu mōru wa kaimono o suru noni benri
na tokoro desu," a shopping mall is a convenient place for shopping) — a distinct, unrelated function
from noni(1)'s concessive "although" (usage 91); the two share spelling/pronunciation but not
meaning, which the book itself flags with separate usage numbers.

### no (6) — explanation-seeking/giving marker, "no desu" (usage 97, p.98)
Sentence + *no desu* (colloquially contracted to *n desu*) asks for or gives an explanation/reason
("naze ikanai no desu ka," why aren't you going? / "atama ga itai no desu," [the reason is] I have a
headache). The book explicitly flags *n desu* as the more colloquial contraction of *no desu* — a
register distinction worth keeping since it tells a learner which form fits a casual register.

### kiri (1) — limit on an amount, replaceable by dake (usage 98, p.98)
Number + counter + *kiri* marks an exact/exclusive limit ("kuruma ga nidai kiri tomatte iru," only
two cars are parked there; "hon ga sansatsu kiri oite aru," only three books have been placed here").

### kiri (2) — "the last time," marking that an event hasn't recurred since (usage 99, p.99)
Pronoun/V-ta + *kiri* marks a single occurrence with no repeat since ("kare ni au no wa kore kiri
desu," this is the last time I'll see him; "Oda-san wa Kanada e itta kiri kaette kimasen," Mr. Oda
went to Canada and hasn't come back since") — distinct from kiri(1)'s plain numeric limit.

### tomo (1) — "both / all," with numbers and counters (usage 100, p.99)
Number + counter + *tomo* means the whole set, "both" or "all" ("kare no kuruma wa nidai tomo
ōkii," both of his cars are big; "watashi-tachi wa sannin tomo bengoshi desu," all three of us are
lawyers).

### nagara (3) — "both / all," with numbers and counters, replaceable by tomo (usage 101, p.100)
The same "both/all" function as tomo(1), interchangeable with it ("kono ie wa niken nagara furui
desu," both of these houses are old; "musuko wa sannin nagara isha da," all three of my sons are
doctors") — a third, unrelated function of *nagara* alongside nagara(1)'s "while" (usage 67) and
nagara(2)'s "although" (usage 92); again the book distinguishes these as separate numbered usages
despite identical spelling.

### shika (1) — "only," obligatorily paired with a negative verb (usage 102, p.101)
Noun/number+counter + *shika* + negative verb restricts to an exact amount ("Miki-san shika tabako o
suimasen," only Mr. Miki smokes; "tōsuto o ichimai shika tabenakatta," ate only one piece of toast") —
unlike *dake*, *shika* grammatically requires a negative predicate even though the English
translation reads as positive ("only X smokes" = "shika... -masen," literally "no one BUT X
smokes").

### shika (2) — "shika nai," "have no choice but to" (usage 103, p.101)
V-u + *shika nai* is a fixed idiom meaning there's no option besides the stated action ("ashita iku
shika arimasen," I have no choice but to go tomorrow; "sensei to sōdan suru shika nai," have no
choice but to discuss it with the teacher") — a named idiomatic extension of shika(1)'s negative-only
restriction.

### shi (1) — conjunction listing two or more actions/states (usage 104, p.102)
Verb/adjective/noun + *shi* lists coordinated facts, often implying they jointly support a
conclusion ("kare wa e mo kaku shi, shashin mo torimasu," he paints as well as takes photographs;
"eiga mo mita shi, kaimono mo shita," I saw a movie and went shopping").

### shi (1) continued — adjective/adjectival-noun forms (p.103)
Extends shi(1) (usage 104) to adjective + *shi* ("yawarakai shi, iro mo kirei desu," soft, and the
color is nice too) and adjectival-noun + *da* + *shi* ("shizuka da shi, keshiki mo utsukushii," quiet,
and what's more the scenery is beautiful) — the *shi*-list reading regularly carries a "what's
more"/cumulative-supporting-reasons nuance in the book's own examples.

### shi (2) — conjunction indicating a reason (usage 105, p.104)
Functionally close to shi(1)'s listing function, but the book frames this usage specifically as
giving supporting reasons for a conclusion ("ashita shiken ga aru shi, konban asobi ni ikemasen," I
have an exam tomorrow, so [among other reasons] I can't go out tonight"; "ame ga futta shi, kinō no
pikunikku wa tanoshikunakatta," it rained, so yesterday's picnic wasn't fun) — presented as a
separate numbered usage from shi(1) despite the close family resemblance; kept distinct per the
source's own numbering rather than merged.

### tomo (2) — inclusion marker ("including") (usage 106, p.106)
Noun + *tomo* (*de*) marks an amount that includes a named component ("gopāsento no tesūryō tomo
(de) hyakudoru haratta," paid 100 dollars including the five-percent commission; "zeikin to
sābisuryō tomo (de) ichiman gosen'en ni narimasu," comes to 15,000 yen including tax and service
charge") — distinct from tomo(1)'s "both/all" sense (usage 100).

### ni (10) — actor/acted-upon marker in passive, causative, and causative-passive sentences (usage 107, p.106)
Three related but distinct patterns: noun(actor) + *ni* + passive verb marks the agent of a passive
action ("watashi wa sensei ni shikarareta," I was scolded by my teacher; "kare wa dorobō ni jitensha
o nusumareta," he had his bike stolen by a thief — the Japanese "adversity passive" where the
grammatical subject is harmed by the action); noun(acted upon) + *ni* + causative verb marks who is
made/allowed to do something ("sensei wa gakusei ni hon o yomaseru," the teacher makes the students
read books); noun(actor) + *ni* + causative-passive verb marks who was made to do something against
their will ("kare wa tomodachi ni sake o nomaserareta," he was made to drink sake by his friends). A
genuinely useful three-way minimal-pair set for a grammatical relation (who does what to whom) that's
easy to confuse across passive/causative/causative-passive morphology.

### ka (4) — marks uncertainty about a state or reason ("I wonder if") (usage 108, p.107)
Verb/adjective + *deshō*/*darō* + *ka*, or verb/adjective + *no* + *ka*, expresses the speaker's own
uncertainty/wondering ("Sumisu-san wa kuru deshō ka," I wonder if Mr. Smith is coming; "shiken ga aru
no ka—Jimu wa yoku benkyō shite imasu," I wonder if there's an exam — Jim is studying hard").

### ka (5) — marks a question embedded within a larger sentence (usage 109, p.108)
Interrogative (+ particle) + verb + *ka* embeds an indirect question as a clause ("kaigi wa itsu aru
ka oboete imasen," I don't remember when the meeting is). Also covers the fixed pattern verb/
adjective + *ka* + *dō ka*, "whether or not" ("kare ga kuru ka dō ka shirimasen," I don't know
whether he'll come or not).

### dano (1) — non-exhaustive list marker, replaceable by toka (usage 110, p.108)
Noun + *dano* + noun + *dano* (or V-u + *dano* + V-u + *dano*) lists representative items/actions
("kanojo wa inu dano neko dano (o) katte iru," she keeps a dog, a cat, and other animals; "nomu dano
taberu dano odoru dano shita," did things like drinking, eating, dancing") — explicitly
interchangeable with toka(1) (usage 93), joining *ya*/*nado*, *toka*, and *dano* as a family of
near-synonymous non-exhaustive-list markers the book introduces one at a time.

### dano (2) — a pair of opposite actions or states (usage 111, p.109)
V/adj + *dano* + V/adj(negative or opposite) + *dano* contrasts two opposite options being alternately
asserted ("kanojo wa iku dano ikanai dano (to) itte iru," she says she's going and then that she's
not; "kare wa samui dano atsui dano (to) itte soto e demasen," saying it's [too] cold, then [too]
hot, he won't go outside") — narrower than dano(1)'s general list, specifically pairing opposites.

### nado (2) — "something to the effect that," replaceable by to ka (usage 112, p.109)
Sentence + *nado* (+ *to*) softens/hedges a reported statement ("kare wa gakkō o yameru nado to
itte iru," he's saying something to the effect that he's quitting school) — distinct from nado(1)'s
plain partial-list sense (usage 12) and interchangeable with to ka (usage 35).

### yara (1) — two-or-more items of a longer list, replaceable by toka or dano (usage 113, p.109)
Noun + *yara* + noun + *yara* lists representative items of an open-ended set ("depāto de kutsu yara
sētā yara takusan katta," bought a lot of things — shoes, sweaters, and so on — at the department
store; "haha wa kaimono yara sōji yara de isogashii," what with shopping, cleaning, and the like, my
mother is busy") — joins toka(1)/dano(1) as a third near-synonymous non-exhaustive-list marker.

### yara (2) — marks uncertainty, replaceable by ka (usage 114, pp.110-111)
Interrogative + *yara* forms an uncertain indefinite ("nani yara arimasu," there is something [or
other]; "dare yara kimashita," someone [or other] came) — and, doubled as noun/verb/adj + *yara* +
noun/verb/adj(negative/opposite) + *yara*, marks genuine uncertainty between two possibilities ("ame
yara kiri yara wakarimasen," I can't tell if it's rain or fog; "sono tsutsumi wa omoi yara karui yara
wakaranai," I can't tell if the package is heavy or light") — this doubled form functions much like
a "whether...or" construction, parallel to ka(5)'s *ka dō ka*.

### te mo (4) — approximate limit, "at the most" (usage 115, p.111)
Adj-kute + *mo* sets an upper bound ("kōgi wa nagakute mo nijikan gurai darō," the lecture would last
two hours at the most; "kamera wa takakute mo sanman'en hodo deshō," the camera would cost about
30,000 yen at the most) — distinct from te mo(1)'s "even if" concession (usage 83); here the
concessive form is pressed into service specifically for an upper-bound estimate.

### tomo (3) — approximate maximum or minimum (usage 116, p.111)
Adj-ku + *tomo* sets an approximate bound in either direction depending on the adjective: "sukunaku
tomo" (at least) vs. "ōku tomo" (at most) — "kore wa sukunaku tomo gosen'en ijō deshō," this would
cost at least 5,000 yen; "gaikoku no baiyā wa ōku tomo rokujūnin gurai datta darō," the foreign
buyers numbered about sixty at most.

### wa (3) — marks a number as the highest or lowest limit (usage 117, p.112)
Number + *wa* emphasizes that a quantity is at least (in an affirmative sentence) or at most (in a
negative sentence) the stated amount ("kono jisho wa ichiman'en wa suru," this dictionary costs at
least 10,000 yen; "kono kabin wa gosen'en wa shinai," this vase wouldn't cost more than 5,000 yen") —
a numeral-bounding sense distinct from wa(1)'s topic-marking and wa(2)'s contrast.

### to (7) — emphasizes a number in a negative sentence (usage 118, p.113)
Number + *to* + negative verb stresses that not even the stated small amount holds ("eki made aruite
gofun to kakaranai," it won't even take five minutes on foot to the station; "ano mise de wa nido to
kaitakunai," I never want to shop at that store again [lit. "not even a second time"]).

### nado (3) — marks examples, simple/plain register (usage 119, p.113)
Noun + *nado* gives an example without the hedging-quotation flavor of nado(2) ("watashi wa bara
nado suki desu," I like things like roses; "Furansu ryōri nado dō desu ka," how about French cuisine
or something like that?) — the plainest of the three *nado* usages (12, 112, 119).

### kurai/gurai (3) — extent of an action or condition (usage 120, p.114)
Noun or verb/adjective + *kurai* marks the degree to which an action/condition holds, "to the point
that/as much as" ("hiragana kurai yomemasu," I can read as much as hiragana [i.e. that much, at
least]; "nodo ga itaku naru kurai utatta," sang so much that my throat hurt") — again *gurai* flagged
as the more colloquial variant, extending the kurai/gurai family (1)/(2)/(3) across plain amount,
positive-sentence equivalence, and now degree-of-action.

### hodo (3) — extent of an action or condition, replaceable by kurai/gurai (usage 121, p.115)
Verb/adjective + *hodo* marks degree, "so much that..." ("Biru wa shinbun ga yomeru hodo kanji o
shitte iru," Bill knows kanji well enough to read a newspaper; "okirarenai hodo tsukarete imasu," I'm
so tired I can't get up") — parallel to kurai(3) and explicitly interchangeable with it.

### hodo (4) — "the more...the more..." relationship (usage 122, p.115)
V-ba + V-u + *hodo*, or adj-kereba + adj-i + *hodo*, builds a proportional relationship ("renshū
sureba suru hodo jōzu ni naru," the more you practice, the better you get; "hayakereba hayai hodo ii
desu," the sooner, the better").

### dake (3) — extent "as...as" (usage 123, p.116)
V-u, or V-tai/hoshii/suki na + *dake*, marks doing something to the full extent desired/possible
("arukeru dake aruita," walked as much as I could; "nomitai dake nonda," drank as much as I wanted;
"suki na dake terebi o mita," watched TV as much as I liked").

### dake (4) — "the more...the more..." relationship, replaceable by hodo (usage 124, p.117)
V-ba + V-u + *dake*, or adj-kerebe + adj-i + *dake*, is interchangeable with hodo(4)'s proportional
construction ("tabereba taberu dake futorimasu," the more you eat, the fatter you get; "yasukereba
yasui dake yoku ureru," the cheaper it is, the better it sells").

### to (8) — "about to do" / "trying (often unsuccessfully) to do" (usage 125, p.117)
V-ō/-yō + *to* + *suru* marks an attempted or imminent action, frequently one that fails ("tatō to
shita ga tatenakatta," tried to stand up but couldn't; "neko ga sakana o torō to shite iru," a cat is
trying to get the fish").

### to (9) — "even if" / "whether...or not," listing conditions against which the main clause holds (usage 126, p.118)
V-ō/-yō + *to* ... V-ō/-yō + *to* (or with the negative volitional V-mai, or adj-karō + *to* + adj-
karō + *to*) lists contrasting/exhaustive conditions under which the main clause remains true
regardless ("arashi ga koyō to kōzui ga koyō to, kono ie wa anzen da," even if a storm comes or a
flood comes, this house is safe; "ame ga furō to furumai to, ikimasu," whether it rains or not, I'll
go; "yokarō to warukarō to kaimasen," whether it's good or bad, I won't buy it") — a volitional-form
cousin of te mo(2)'s "whether...or not" pairing (usage 84), built on V-ō/-yō instead of V-te.

### nari (1) — "either...or / whether...or," a choice (usage 127, p.119)
Noun + *nari* + noun + *nari* (or V-u + *nari* + V-u + *nari*) presents an open choice between
options ("Oda-san nari watashi nari ga kaigi ni shusseki shimasu," either Mr. Oda or I will attend
the meeting; "iku nari yameru nari hayaku kimemashō," let's decide quickly whether we're going or
not").

### nari (2) — "anything/anyone/any time," with an interrogative (usage 128, p.119)
Interrogative + *nari* + *to* forms a free-choice indefinite ("nan nari to itte kudasai," please say
anything; "dare nari to kikinasai," ask anyone) — functions like demo(2)/te mo(3)'s "no matter
who/what" but built on *nari* + *to* instead.

### koso (1) — emphasizes the preceding word ("indeed," "for sure," "the very") (usage 129, p.120)
Noun (or noun + double-marked *de koso*/*to koso*) + *koso* gives strong emphasis to exactly that
item ("rainen koso Igirisu e ikitai desu," next year for sure I want to go to England; "Sumisu-san
koso gorufu ga jōzu desu," Mr. Smith is a good golfer indeed; "Hara-san to koso tenisu o shitai," Miss
Hara is the very person I want to play tennis with").

### koso (2) — emphasizes a reason or cause (usage 130, p.120)
V-ba/adj-kereba + *koso* (often paired with a following *no desu*) stresses that the stated reason is
the real/precise one ("o-kane ga ireba koso, yoru mo hataraite iru no desu," it's precisely because I
need the money that I'm working nights too; "wakakereba koso, anna kiken na koto ga dekiru no da,"
it's only because he's young that he can do such a dangerous thing"). Also covers sentence + *kara*
+ *koso* ("sono mise wa sābisu ga ii kara koso, o-kyaku ga ōi n desu," it's precisely because the
service is good that the store has so many customers).

### te wa (2) — marks repetition of an action (usage 131, p.121)
V-te + *wa* (optionally doubled, V-te wa ... V-te wa + *suru*) marks a habitually repeated action,
often with a negative/wasteful connotation ("kare wa keiba ni itte wa kane o nakushite iru," every
time he goes to the races, he loses money; "gakusei ga kokuban ni kanji o kaite wa keshi, kaite wa
keshi shite iru," the student keeps writing kanji on the board and erasing them, over and over") —
distinct from te wa(1)'s negative-conclusion conditional sense (usage 86).

### ni (11) — joins two or more nouns as a list, replaceable by to (usage 132, p.122)
Noun + *ni* + noun lists items much like *to* ("shussekisha wa Wada-san ni Hara-san deshita," those
present were Mr. Wada and Miss Hara; "enkai de wain ni uisukii ni sake ga deta," wine, whisky, and
sake were served at the banquet").

### ni (12) — connects two items as a matching or contrasting pair (usage 133, p.122)
Noun + *ni* + noun juxtaposes two items that conventionally pair or contrast, often in fixed/
idiomatic combinations ("Romeo ni Jurietto," Romeo and Juliet; "Fuji-san ni sakura," Mt. Fuji and
cherry blossoms; "kuro no sūtsu ni kuro no nekutai," a black suit and a black tie) — distinct from
ni(11)'s plain listing in that these pairings carry a conventional/iconic association, not just
coincidental co-occurrence.

### ni shite wa (1) — "for a..., considering that..." (usage 134, p.123)
Noun/verb + *ni shite wa* measures something against a generally agreed-upon standard implied by the
noun/verb ("kare wa Amerika-jin ni shite wa se ga hikui," he's short for an American; "yoku nomu ni
shite wa yowanai," considering that he drinks a lot, he doesn't get drunk").

### ni totte (1) — marks an effect on or value to a person/thing (usage 135, p.124)
Noun + *ni totte* frames a judgment from a particular standpoint ("yasai wa karada ni totte ii
desu," vegetables are good for the body; "sono eiga wa kodomo ni totte yokunai to omoimasu," I think
that movie is not good for children"). Doubles with *wa* or *mo*.

### ni tsuite (1) — "about, concerning" (usage 136, p.124)
Noun + *ni tsuite* marks the topic being discussed ("Nihon no keizai ni tsuite hanashite kudasai,"
please talk about the Japanese economy; "kyō no kōgi ni tsuite nani ka shitsumon ga arimasu ka," are
there any questions about today's lecture?). Doubles with *no* (an attributive "about X" phrase) or
*wa* (topicalized).

### tomo (4) — "can't say whether...or," paired with a negative form of iu (usage 137, p.125)
Noun + *tomo* + noun + *tomo* (or verb/adjective + *tomo* + verb/adjective + *tomo*), always paired
with a negative *ienai*/*iemasen* ("can't say"), expresses genuine inability to judge between two
options ("kono sakuhin wa kessaku tomo dasaku tomo iemasen," I can't say whether this work is a
masterpiece or trash; "sono geki wa ii tomo warui tomo ienai," I can't say whether the play is good
or bad") — a fourth, idiomatically narrower function of *tomo* alongside (1) both/all, (2) including,
and (3) approximate bound.

### ga (6) — "even if / whether...or not," listing conditions, replaceable by to (usage 138, p.126)
V-ō/-yō + *ga* ... V-ō/-yō + *ga* (including with negative volitional V-mai, or adj-karō + *ga* +
adj-karō + *ga*) lists conditions against which the main clause holds regardless ("ame ga furō ga
yuki ga furō ga, watashi wa ikimasu," even if it rains or snows, I'll go; "heya ga hirokarō ga
semakarō ga kamaimasen," it doesn't matter whether the room is big or small") — functionally
identical to to(9) (usage 126), explicitly cross-referenced as interchangeable.

### wa (4) — emphasizes contrasting elements (usage 139, p.126)
V(-masu) + *wa* + *suru* (implying "did do X, but...") or V-te + *wa* + *iru* (implying "do have/am
doing X, but...") emphasizes that one thing is true while implying a contrasting qualification ("bā
de nomi wa shita ga, shokuji ni wa ikanakatta," we did drink at the bar, but didn't go out for
dinner; "kamera o motte wa iru ga, mada tsukatte (wa) inai," I do have a camera, but haven't used it
yet") — a fourth *wa* function alongside topic (1), contrast (2), and numeral-bounding (3).

### toshite (1) — "as," marking status, capacity, or function (usage 140, p.127)
Noun + *toshite* marks the role/capacity in which something acts or is treated ("kare wa tsūyaku
toshite yatowareta," he was hired as an interpreter; "kono heya wa shosai toshite tsukatte iru," I'm
using this room as a study").

### toshite (2) — emphasis in a negative sentence, replaceable by mo (usage 141, p.128)
One + counter + *toshite* + negative verb emphasizes an absolute zero ("kono mise ni wa hitotsu
toshite yasui mono wa nai," there isn't a single item in this store that's inexpensive; "kare wa
ichido toshite gaikoku e itta koto wa nai," he's never even once been to a foreign country") —
distinct from toshite(1)'s plain "as" role-marking (usage 140).

### bakari (4) — "not only...but also," in the form "bakari de naku...mo" (usage 142, p.128)
Noun/verb/adjective + *bakari de naku* ... *mo* builds a "not only X but also Y" construction ("kare
wa Eigo bakari de naku Doitsu-go mo hanasu," he speaks not only English but also German; "kono heya
wa hiroi bakari de naku tenjō mo takai," this room is not only spacious but the ceiling is high,
too").

### dake (5) — "not only...but also," in the form "dake de naku...mo," replaceable by bakari (usage 143, p.129)
The same "not only X but also Y" construction as bakari(4), built on *dake* instead and explicitly
interchangeable with it ("niku dake de naku sakana mo katta," bought not only beef but also fish;
"sono ryōri wa oishii dake de naku nedan mo yasui," the food is not only tasty but the price is
reasonable, too").

### nomi (2) — "not only...but also," in the form "nomi narazu...mo," replaceable by bakari/dake de naku (usage 144, p.129)
A third, more formal/literary variant of the same "not only X but also Y" pattern ("kaiga nomi
narazu chōkoku mo mita," saw not only paintings but also sculptures; "dansei nomi narazu josei mo kono
kurabu ni haitte iru," not only men but also women are members of this club") — completes a
three-register family (bakari/dake de naku, plain; nomi narazu, formal) for the same construction.

### nari (3) — "as soon as" (usage 145, p.130)
V-u + *nari* marks immediate succession ("asagohan o taberu nari, ie o deta," as soon as I ate
breakfast, I left the house; "mise o deru nari, takushii ni notta," as soon as I left the store, I
got in a taxi") — a third, unrelated function of *nari* alongside (1) "either...or" and (2)
"anything/anyone" (usages 127-128).

### ga (7) — "as soon as," in the form "...ga hayai ka" (usage 146, p.130)
V-u + *ga hayai ka* marks immediate succession, near-synonymous with nari(3) ("heya ni hairu ga
hayai ka, dentō o tsuketa," as soon as I entered the room, I turned on the light; "ame ga yamu ga
hayai ka, shiai ga hajimatta," as soon as the rain stopped, the game began").

### ya (2) — "as soon as," in the form "...ya ina ya" (usage 147, p.130)
V-u + *ya ina ya* marks immediate succession ("okiru ya ina ya, mado o aketa," as soon as I got up, I
opened the window; "kare wa keikan o miru ya ina ya, nigeta," as soon as he saw a policeman, he ran
away") — a third member of the "as soon as" family alongside nari(3) and ga(7), distinct from ya(1)'s
partial-list conjunction (usage 11).

### ka (6) — "no sooner had...than," in the form "...ka...nai uchi ni" (usage 148, p.131)
V-u + *ka* + V-nai + *uchi ni* marks that the main action occurred before the first was even
complete ("ofisu ni hairu ka hairanai uchi ni denwa ga natta," no sooner had I entered the office
than the phone rang; "jugyō ga owaru ka owaranai uchi ni gakusei wa tachiagatta," no sooner had class
ended than the students stood up") — completes a four-way family of "as soon as / no sooner than"
constructions (nari(3), ga(7), ya(2), ka(6)) the book introduces back-to-back, each built on a
different particle but expressing essentially the same temporal immediacy.

### bakari (5) — "the only action left to do" (usage 149, p.133)
V-u + *bakari* (+ *desu*) marks that everything is ready except for one final action ("mō hikkoshi o
suru bakari desu," the only thing left for us to do is move) — distinct from bakari(1)-(3)'s
"nothing but"/"just did" senses.

### bakari (6) — "bakari ni," emphasizes a reason/cause, "just because" (usage 150, p.133)
Verb or adj/adjectival-noun + *bakari ni* stresses that a (often minor) cause led to a
disproportionate consequence ("kaze o hiita bakari ni, gaishutsu dekinai," just because I caught a
cold, I can't go out; "michi ga semai bakari ni, basu wa tōrenai," because the street is narrow,
buses can't get through").

### sura (1) — emphatic "even," replaceable by sae (usage 151, p.133)
Noun/gerund + *sura* is interchangeable with sae(1) ("akanbō (de) sura oyogemasu," even a baby can
swim; "Tomu wa muzukashii kanji sura yomeru," Tom can read even difficult kanji; "taberu koto sura
dekinai," can't even eat").

### nado (4) — deprecatory/humble marker (usage 152, p.135)
Noun + *nado* expresses self-deprecation or dismissiveness toward the marked item ("watashi nado,
totemo kore wa yakusemasen," someone like me could never translate this; "sonna takai mono nado
totemo kaenai," I could never buy such an expensive thing") — a fourth, attitudinal *nado* function
distinct from its list-marking senses (12, 112, 119).

### tomo (5) — emphatic "no matter what / even if" (usage 153, p.136)
V-ō/-yō + *tomo* lists a condition against which the main clause holds regardless ("nani ga okorō
tomo, saigo made ganbarō," no matter what happens, let's do our best to the end; "kare ga yameyō
tomo, kono kikaku wa kawaranai," even if he should resign, this plan won't change") — functions like
ga(6)/to(9) but built on *tomo*.

### tomo (6) — "tomo arō hito," someone acting contrary to expectation of their role (usage 154, p.136)
Noun(person) + *tomo arō hito* marks someone whose action contradicts what their position/role
would lead one to expect ("keikan tomo arō hito ga nusumi o shita," although he is supposed to be a
policeman, he stole something).

### dokoro ka (1) — "far from / not to mention" (usage 155, p.137)
Noun/verb/adjective + *dokoro ka* (optionally + *mo*) flatly rejects a stated possibility in favor of
something further from it ("kare wa ie dokoro ka kuruma mo nai," he doesn't even have a car, not to
mention a house; "heya wa hiroi dokoro ka semasugiru," far from being large, the room is too small;
"yoku hataraku dokoro ka asonde bakari iru," far from working hard, he does nothing but play").

### dake ni (1) — marks a cause or reason (usage 156, p.137)
Noun/adjective/verb + *dake ni* emphasizes that a result follows naturally/proportionately from a
stated cause ("ichiryū no gaka dake ni, kanojo no e wa subarashii," as you'd expect of a first-rate
painter, her paintings are wonderful; "kore wa chiisai dake ni, tsukaiyasui," because this is small,
it's easy to use; "yoku doryoku shita dake ni, seiseki ga agatta," since he tried hard, his grades
improved").

### made (3) — "made mo nai," negative emphasis "there's no need to" (usage 157, p.138)
V-u + *made mo nai* is a fixed idiom meaning an action is unnecessary ("kono repōto wa yomu made mo
nai," there's no need to read this report) — a third *made* function alongside (1) plain limit and
(2) emphatic "even."

### mono no (1) — conjunction "but / although," replaceable by ga (usage 158, p.138)
V + *mono no* concedes a point before contradicting its expected consequence ("sekkyō suru mono no,
jikkō wa muzukashii," although you may preach, practicing it is difficult; "kore o katta mono no,
tsukaikata ga yoku wakaranai," I bought this, but I don't know how to use it very well").

### tokoro de (1) — conjunction "even if" (usage 159, p.139)
V-ta + *tokoro de* concedes a hypothetical action won't change the outcome ("shōtai shita tokoro de,
kare wa konai darō," even if we invited him, he probably wouldn't come; "isoida tokoro de, ma ni
aimasen," even if we hurry, we won't make it") — note this is a distinct, V-ta-based conjunctive
usage from ni totte/ni tsuite-style compound particles; cross-reference tokoro(1)/(2)'s aspectual
and discovery senses (usages 64, 82), which this "even if" sense does not share.

### keredomo (1) — conjunction "but / although," replaceable by ga (usage 160, p.139)
Sentence + *keredomo* connects two contrasting clauses ("Tomu wa kuru keredomo Jimu wa konai," Tom
is coming but Jim isn't; "tenki wa ii keredomo kaze ga aru," the weather is good but it's windy").

### keredomo (2) — marks a preliminary remark, replaceable by ga (usage 161, p.140)
Sentence + *keredomo* softens an opening statement before getting to the point, much like English
"well, ..." ("Oda desu keredomo, Hara-san wa irasshaimasu ka," I'm Oda — is Miss Hara in?; "ano ken
desu keredomo, dōshimashō ka," speaking of that matter, what shall we do?").

### ga (8) — softens a statement or refusal (usage 162, p.141)
Sentence + *ga*, left trailing without a following clause, softens an assertion or an implicit
refusal ("daijōbu da to omoimasu ga," I think it'll be all right [...]; "kachō wa ima kaigichū desu
ga," the section chief is in a meeting right now [so he's not available]") — a conversational
hedging function distinct from ga(3)'s explicit conjunction (usage 52).

### keredomo (3) — indicates a desire, replaceable by ga (usage 163, p.141)
Sentence + *keredomo*, again left trailing, softens an expressed wish ("mō sukoshi chiisai no ga
hoshii no desu keredomo," I'd like something a little smaller [if possible]; "Pari e ikitai n da
keredomo," I wish I could go to Paris [but can't]").

### ne (1) — sentence-final particle, confirmation (usage 164, p.146)
Sentence + *ne* seeks the listener's agreement/confirmation ("samui desu ne," it's cold, isn't it?).

### ne (2) — sentence-final particle, softening a request/suggestion (usage 165, p.146)
Sentence + *ne* softens a request or suggestion ("zehi o-negai shimasu ne," please be sure to do it;
"mō kaerimashō ne," let's go home, O.K.?).

### ne (3) — sentence-final particle, reason or cause (usage 166, p.146)
Sentence + *kara* + *ne*, or V-te + *ne*, marks a reason offered conversationally, "you know" ("kaigi
ga aru kara ne," I have a meeting, you know; "arukisugite ne," I walked too much, you know).

### nē (1) — sentence-final particle, emotion (usage 167, p.147)
Sentence + *nē* (the lengthened variant of *ne*) expresses emotional reaction/exclamation
("omoshiroi hanashi deshita nē," what an interesting talk it was!; "suteki na doresu (desu) nē,"
what a gorgeous dress!).

### yo (1) — sentence-final particle, strong conviction (usage 168, p.147)
Sentence + *yo* asserts something with confidence, often correcting or informing the listener ("iie,
kyō wa itsuka desu yo," no, today is the fifth, you know; "Hara-san wa ashita kimasu yo," Miss Hara
will come tomorrow for sure").

### yo (2) — sentence-final particle, request or suggestion (usage 169, p.147)
Sentence + *yo* adds emphasis to a request or suggestion ("hachiji ni kite kudasai yo," come at eight
o'clock [okay?]; "chotto yasumimashō yo," let's take a break").

### kashira (1) — sentence-final particle, uncertainty/request/self-question, used mainly by women (usage 170, p.148)
Sentence + *kashira* expresses wondering, a hesitant request, or a question to oneself, gendered as
primarily feminine speech ("kare wa kuru kashira," I wonder if he's coming; "kore tetsudatte moraeru
kashira," I wonder if you could help me with this").

### kana (1) — sentence-final particle, uncertainty/request/self-question, used mainly by men (usage 171, p.148)
The male-register counterpart to kashira(1), same functions ("ano mise wa nanji ni aku kana," I
wonder what time that store opens; "kono isu o hakonde moraeru kana," I wonder if you could carry
these chairs for me") — the book presents kashira(1)/kana(1) as an explicit gendered pair for the
same pragmatic functions, worth keeping as a cross-referenced pair.

### na (1) — sentence-final particle, confirmation, used by men (usage 172, p.149)
Sentence + *na* seeks agreement/confirmation in masculine speech, functionally close to ne(1) but
gendered ("ano bā wa takai na," that bar is expensive, isn't it?; "kare wa yoku yatta na," he did
well, didn't he?").

### na (2) — sentence-final particle, prohibition, used by men (usage 173, p.150)
V-u + *na* forms a blunt masculine prohibition, "don't..." ("ima, iku na," don't go now; "koko de
taberu na," don't eat here") — distinct from na(1)'s confirmation-seeking sense (usage 172), both
gendered masculine but functionally unrelated.

### nā (1) — sentence-final particle, emotion, used mostly by men (usage 174, p.150)
Sentence + *nā* (the masculine-register lengthened *na*) expresses emotional exclamation ("yoku ame
ga furu nā," how hard it's raining!; "yūbe wa samukatta nā," how cold it was last night!) — the
masculine counterpart to nē(1) (usage 167).

### nā (2) — sentence-final particle, indicates a desire (usage 175, p.150)
V-u + *to ii* + *nā*, or V-ba + *yokatta* + *nā*, expresses a wish, either forward-looking or
regretful ("kyō kanojo ga kuru to ii nā," I wish she would come today; "motto benkyō sureba yokatta
nā," I wish I'd studied more").

### no (7) — sentence-final particle, question or softened command, used usually by women (usage 176, p.151)
Sentence + *no* forms a gentle question ("doko e iku no," where are you going?) or a soft, feminine-
register command/prohibition ("koko de asobanai no," don't play here) — a conversational,
gender-marked extension distinct from no(1)-(6)'s connective/nominalizing functions.

### wa (5) — sentence-final particle, softens a statement, used by women (usage 177, p.152)
Sentence + *wa* (distinct in pronunciation/pitch from the topic-marking *wa*) softens an assertion in
feminine speech ("Wada-san wa konai to omou wa," I think Miss Wada won't come; "shiai wa
tsumaranakatta wa," the game was boring) — the fifth and final function of *wa* in this book,
clearly set apart from (1)-(4)'s topic/contrast/bounding functions by its sentence-final position and
gendering.

### sa (1) — sentence-final particle, slight emphasis, used mostly by men (usage 178, p.152)
Noun/adjective/adjectival-noun/verb + *sa* adds casual, mostly-masculine emphasis ("kore ga shashin
sa," this is the photo [I told you about]; "shukudai wa ato de suru sa," I'll do my homework later
[no big deal]").

### koto (1) — sentence-final particle, emotion, used by women (usage 179, p.153)
Sentence + *koto* expresses an exclamation in feminine register ("mā, hana ga utsukushii koto," oh,
how beautiful the flowers are!; "yoku ganbatta koto," how hard you tried!").

### koto (2) — sentence-final particle, suggestion or invitation, used by women (usage 180, p.153)
V-nai + *koto* poses a gentle suggestion or invitation in feminine register ("kaimono ni ikanai
koto," shall we go shopping?; "uchi e asobi ni konai koto," won't you come over to my place?") —
structurally a negative question softened into an invitation, distinct from koto(1)'s exclamatory
sense.

### mono (1) — sentence-final particle, reason or excuse (usage 181, p.154)
Sentence + *mono* offers a reason as a (often petulant or defensive) excuse ("naze ikanai no."
"isogashii mono," why aren't you going? — "I'm busy, you know"; "mō nai no. Watashi ga minna
tsukatta mono," all gone — I used them all").

### tomo (7) — sentence-final particle, assertion ("certainly," "of course") (usage 182, p.154)
Sentence + *tomo* affirms something emphatically ("mō kaette mo ii desu ka." "ii (desu) tomo," may I
go home now? — "certainly"; "motto benkyō suru tomo," I will definitely study harder") — a final,
sentence-ending function of *tomo* distinct from its earlier connective/bounding senses (1)-(6).

### mono ka (1) — sentence-final particle, negative determination or rhetorical question, mainly men; "mono desu ka" usable by women (usage 183, p.155)
V-u + *mono ka*/*mono desu ka* emphatically rejects a possibility, "as if I would..." ("anna tokoro e
nido to iku mono ka," I'll never go to such a place again; "mō ano mise de kau mono desu ka," I'll
never shop at that store again") — the book explicitly notes the register split: *mono ka* skews
masculine, *mono desu ka* is available to women.

### ya (3) — sentence-final particle, softens a statement/request/suggestion, used by men (usage 184, p.155)
Sentence + *ya* softens an assertion in masculine register ("kore wa umai ya," this is tasty; "mō
yameyō ya," let's quit now) — a sentence-final function distinct from ya(1)'s partial-list
conjunction (usage 11) and ya(2)'s "as soon as" (usage 147).

### tara (3) — sentence-final particle, suggestion or proposal ("why not...") (usage 185, p.156)
V-ta + *ra*, left as a sentence-final fragment, proposes an action conversationally ("kono hon o
yondara," why not read this book?; "kochira no akai no o kattara," why not buy this red one?") — a
sentence-final use distinct from tara(1)/(2)'s conjunctive conditional/sequential senses.

### yara (3) — sentence-final particle, a rhetorical question with negative implication (usage 186, p.157)
Sentence (+ *no*) + *yara* expresses genuine, slightly plaintive uncertainty ("kono ame wa itsu
yamu (no) yara," I wonder when this rain will stop; "kare no shōrai wa dō naru yara," I wonder what
his future will be like") — distinct from yara(1)'s list-marking and yara(2)'s plain-uncertainty
senses (usages 113-114).

### ze (1) — sentence-final particle, adds force, used by men (usage 187, p.157)
Sentence + *ze* adds blunt masculine force/assertiveness ("saki ni iku ze," I'm going on ahead;
"tanonda ze," I'm counting on you).

### zo (1) — sentence-final particle, adds force (stronger than ze) or marks a question to oneself, used by men (usage 188, p.157)
Sentence + *zo* is the most forceful of the masculine sentence-final particles covered, and can also
mark a self-directed observation ("kore wa muzukashii zo," this is difficult; "jikan da zo. Sorosoro
dekareru zo," it's time, I'm leaving now; "doa ga aite iru. Hen da zo," the door is open — strange!).
This is the book's final (188th) numbered usage, closing out a run of ten sentence-final particles
(164-188: ne, nē, yo, kashira, kana, na, nā, no, wa, sa, koto, mono, tomo, mono ka, ya, tara, yara,
ze, zo) presented as a connected pragmatic/sociolinguistic system — several explicitly gender-marked
(kashira=feminine/kana=masculine; koto/no/wa=feminine; na/nā/sa/ya/ze/zo=masculine) — which is a
