# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Shin Nihongo no Kiso — Series Overview + Level I (本冊)

**Source (primary):** 海外技術者研修協会 (AOTS — The Association for Overseas Technical
Scholarship), *新日本語の基礎 I* (*Shin Nihongo no Kiso I* / "New Basic Japanese I"), 1989 revised
edition. Kanji-kana-majiri (kanji/kana mixed-script) main volume, 255 DjVu pages, no text layer
(image-only scan — `djvutxt` returns empty output on every page checked). Vision-read throughout.
Companion: *Shin Nihongo no Kiso (Kanji Workbook), English edition* ("Japanese Kanji Workbook I"),
same DjVu-no-text-layer status, 203 pages.

**Series scope note.** *Shin Nihongo no Kiso* is a two-level (I + II), 50-lesson beginner course
originally designed for AOTS's 100-hour technical-trainee program (研修生 "technical trainees" from
partner countries sent to Japanese companies) and revised as a unified I/II curriculum starting
1985. This file (`084_shin_nihongo_no_kiso.md`) covers the series overview and Level I (Lessons
1–25). See `084b_shin_nihongo_no_kiso_ii.md` for Level II (Lessons 26–50, the *Textbook*/*Kanji
Workbook*/*English Explanations* trio).

**Scope exclusions (per `00_Book_Triage_Catalog.md`):** Level I's two Russian-medium companion
volumes (`Shin Nihongo no Kiso(грамматический комментарий)` — grammar commentary, and `Shin
Nihongo no Kiso(перевод)` — translation booklet) are **excluded** as wrong-metalanguage material,
not reference English/target-language content for this project. Level II's Spanish-language-edition
kanji workbook is likewise excluded (confirmed Spanish-medium despite sitting next to the
English-medium companion files) — see `084b` for the in-scope Level II kanji workbook note.

**Representative sampling, not exhaustive extraction.** Given the combined two-level, 50-lesson,
~650-page scale, this file samples across Level I's lesson progression rather than extracting every
page: the full table-of-contents sentence-pattern headline for **all 25 lessons** (giving a complete
grammar-point skeleton for the level), plus full vocabulary/example-sentence extraction from five
representative lessons spanning the beginning, early-middle, middle, and end of the level (Lessons
1, 6, 11, 19, 25), the pre-Lesson-1 front matter (pronunciation/classroom language/greetings/
numbers), the book's own particle-usage reference appendix (助詞), and a sample from the companion
kanji workbook (compound-ideograph pedagogy + a slice of its kanji-reading index).

**Vision-reading guard — marginalia found and excluded.** Lesson 25's vision-read page (djvu p.221)
carries two previous-owner's marks: a faint handwritten annotation beside example sentence 1 (an
illegible pencil/pen note, not matching the printed typeface) and a diagonal pencil line near
example sentence 4. Both are clearly non-print and were **excluded entirely** from extraction —
only the printed textbook content from that page is captured below.

## Morphological note

Japanese is agglutinative; per this language's established convention (see
`../00_Extraction_Checklist.md`), vocabulary entries record the writing-system form used
(kanji/hiragana/katakana) alongside romanization-free gloss notes, since script choice itself
carries register information. *Shin Nihongo no Kiso*'s own vocabulary is presented kanji-kana-majiri
with furigana readings; this table preserves the kanji/kana form as `Term`, folding the reading into
`Notes` where it isn't independently recoverable. A few entries show explicit morpheme composition
(e.g. 研修生 = 研修 "training" + 生 "-student/trainee" agentive suffix; おいくつ = お honorific prefix
+ いくつ "how many") — flagged with morpheme breakdowns below.

## Grammar points

### Copula です／ではありません (Lesson 1)

The basic predicate-nominal copula. Affirmative です, negative ではありません (contracting
colloquially to じゃありません, a contraction the textbook itself parenthetically flags right in
the Lesson 1 sentence-pattern list — an explicit register note rather than something left implicit).
Question form adds か; topic-ellipsis is standard in answers (あなたはラオさんですか。…はい、
[わたしは] ラオです).

### 〜も "also/too" and its negative-polarity use (Lesson 1; systematized in the 助詞 appendix)

も replaces は/が to mean "also." Separately, when attached to an interrogative pronoun (どこ, 何,
だれ) and followed by a negative predicate, も produces total negation ("nowhere," "nothing,"
"nobody") — a distinct, systematic pattern the book's own particle appendix groups as its own
subcategory rather than treating as incidental.

### Counters (助数詞) — Lesson 11

Japanese obligatorily marks count nouns with a counter suffix keyed to the noun's semantic class:
generic small objects (〜つ, with an irregular native-Japanese series ひとつ/ふたつ/みっつ...),
machines/vehicles (〜台), people (〜人, irregular ひとり/ふたり for 1–2 then regular 〜人), flat
objects (〜枚), duration units (〜年/〜週間/〜時間/〜回). The lesson explicitly drills いくつ vs.
何人 vs. 何回 as counter-specific question words, not a single generic "how many."

### 〜たことがある — experiential aspect (Lesson 19)

Past-tense plain-form verb + ことがある marks "have had the experience of doing X at some point,"
distinct from simple past tense. Negated as 一度もない "not even once." Example: 富士山を見たこと
がありますか。…いいえ、一度もありません。ぜひ見たいです。

### 〜たり〜たりする — non-exhaustive listing of actions (Lesson 19)

Lists representative (not exhaustive) actions from among several performed, each verb taking the
past-tense plain form + り, closed by a conjugated する: 買い物したり、映画を見たりします
"I do things like shopping, watching movies, [etc.]."

### 〜たら conditional and 〜ても concessive (Lesson 25, the level's final new grammar point)

〜たら (past-tense-form + ら) covers both hypothetical "if" and temporal-sequential "when X then Y."
〜ても (て-form + も) marks "even if X, [still] Y" — concessive conditional, contrasted directly
against 〜たら in the lesson's own paired pattern sentences (雨が降ったら、行きません。 vs. 雨が
降っても、行きます。).

### 助詞 appendix's の — four grouped subcategories

The book's own back-matter particle reference splits の into: (A) possessive/attributive
noun-linking ("of"), (B) clause-level attributive linking a verb phrase to a following noun phrase,
(C) temporal/locative attributive (きのうの晩, 机の上に), and (D) a nominalizing pro-form use
standing in for an elided noun (小さいのを見せてください "show me the small one"). Treating these as
one grouped family — rather than four unrelated entries — is the appendix's own organizing choice
and is preserved here as a single grammar point per the book's presentation.

### Kanji-workbook compound-ideograph pedagogy (会意文字 kaii moji)

The companion English-edition kanji workbook explicitly teaches a subset of kanji as transparent
compounds of two simpler elements whose combined meaning motivates the compound's meaning — e.g.
日 "sun" + 月 "moon" = 明 "bright"; 木 "tree" + 人 "person" = 休 "rest" (a person resting against a
tree); 木 "tree" + 日 "sun" = 東 "east" (sun rising behind a tree). This is distinct from the
phonetic-ideographic (形声文字) category the same workbook section introduces alongside it (not
sampled in this pass — see `084b`'s companion volume note for how kanji pedagogy continues into
Level II).
