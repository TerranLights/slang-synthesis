## Grammar points

### 「〜すぎる」("too much ~, excessively ~")

Attaches to a verb stem or adjective stem to indicate an excessive degree of an action or quality.
Attested twice in this chunk's readings, in both nominalized and predicative form: Lesson 14's
narrator is warned 「すいすぎに気をつけて」("be careful not to smoke too much," p. 76, nominalized
as 吸い過ぎ), and Lesson 15's narrator wonders whether スミスさんの考えは厳しすぎるのではないか ("whether
スミスさん's view might be too strict," p. 82, predicative 厳しすぎる). Productive pattern, not
confined to either individual lesson's specific vocabulary.

### 「〜(ら)れる」as adversative/"suffering" passive

A passive form applied to an intransitive verb (here, 死ぬ, "to die") to express that the speaker
or subject was negatively affected by an event they didn't cause or control, rather than describing
a literal grammatical passive of being acted upon. Source's own example, from the tobacconist
シノさんの own words in Lesson 14's reading passage: 「主人や息子には、早く死なれてしまったけど、今は、
おおぜいの家族と生活してるようなもんだよ」 ("I had my husband and son die on me early, but now it's as
if I'm living with a large family," p. 76) — 死なれて (adversative passive of 死ぬ) frames her
husband's and son's deaths as misfortunes that happened *to* her, a construction with no direct
single-word English equivalent.

### 「〜ことに慣れている」("to be accustomed to ~ing")

A nominalizing こと clause plus 慣れている expresses habituation to an ongoing state or repeated
action. Source's own example, from Lesson 15's reading passage, paraphrasing スミスさんの explanation
of Japanese social discipline: 日本人はいつも自分をころすことに慣れている ("Japanese people are always
accustomed to suppressing themselves," p. 82) — the lesson's central idiomatic claim, built on this
pattern.
