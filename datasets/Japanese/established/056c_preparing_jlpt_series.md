**What kind of source this is, and why it earned extraction.** Unlike the N1/N2 books (056/056b), this is not a test-strategy guide organized by exam section — it is a pure **listening-drill workbook**: 96 short themed dialogue scenarios (Part 1, "with pictures," 48 scenarios organized under category headings like 順番/どこ/地図/物の形/スポーツ/仕事/天気予報/数字/いくら/体に関することば/グラフ; Part 2, "without pictures," 48 more scenarios under 食べる/選ぶ/時間/家族/会社/動物/なに/いつ/どこ/いくら/どうして/どう・どんな/どうする/数), each with a multiple-choice comprehension question. The front matter (English-language preface, p.5) describes it candidly as themed-vocabulary repetition drilling, which would normally fail the coverage rule's "pure drill" test. However, the **scripts-and-answers section** at the back of the book (printed pp. 97 onward, i.e., roughly PDF images 50+) pairs every single dialogue script with a short "学習のポイント" (study point) box that genuinely explains a grammar pattern or vocabulary distinction used in that dialogue — numbered points with glosses, antonym pairs (対語), and illustrative example sentences distinct from the dialogue itself. This is real explanatory content per the coverage rule, just delivered as 96 small per-item notes rather than a handful of large grammar-point essays — so extraction here samples representatively across study points rather than attempting all 96.

**Coverage note:** representative sampling of study points spread across the book's thematic range — sampled scenario 52 "事故" (グラフ/Part 1 topic), scenario 12 "きょうだい" (家族/Part 2 topic), and scenario 37 "駐車場の料金" (いくら/Part 2 topic), plus the front-matter guide and table of contents to confirm overall structure. The great majority of the book's 96 scenario scripts + study points were not individually read — this is intentional sampling across a confirmed-uniform format (every scenario follows the identical script→question→study-point→answer-key structure), not an attempt at exhaustive coverage. ## Grammar points

### Antonym-pair (対語) teaching convention

This source's 学習のポイント boxes consistently flag genuine lexical antonym pairs with an explicit 対語 label (as seen with 以上/以下 above) rather than just defining each word independently — a convention worth tracking across this project's Japanese corpus, since explicit antonym-pairing is exactly the kind of "explicit annotation the source itself marks" the extraction spec prioritizes capturing. The 上/下 (relative-age sense) pair in scenario 12 is the same pattern, though not labeled with the 対語 tag there.

### Numeric-threshold vocabulary (以上/以下) tied to inclusive-boundary illustration

Rather than giving an abstract definition, the source teaches 以上/以下 by showing a literal age-range chart with ×/○ marks straddling a cutoff value — making explicit that both terms are **inclusive** of the boundary number itself (18 and up marked ○ starting exactly at 18, not 19). This is a clearer pedagogical treatment of the inclusive/exclusive distinction than a bare dictionary gloss would give, and is worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase as a concrete numeric-boundary convention in standard Japanese.

### "(〜く／に) 見える" adjectival-appearance construction

The pattern [adjective stem]+く/に+見える ("looks/appears [adjective]") attaches differently depending on whether the preceding adjective is an i-adjective (〜く見える, e.g. わかく見える "looks young") or a na-adjective/noun (〜に見える, e.g. 下に見える "looks younger/lower-ranked" in the sibling-order scenario). The source's study-point box generalizes across both attachment forms in a single entry, which is a compact and genuinely useful paraphrase of a pattern that's often taught separately for i- vs. na-adjectives in other grammars.

### Loanword-to-kango equivalence teaching convention

The パーキング/駐車場 pairing illustrates a recurring teaching move in this source: introducing a katakana loanword's native-Japanese synonym directly alongside it, rather than assuming the loanword alone suffices — useful corroborating evidence (alongside the N2 book's by-year loanword-attestation list in 056b) that loanword/native-word doublets are treated as a live register distinction worth explicit teaching attention in JLPT-prep materials generally, not just an incidental feature of any one book.
