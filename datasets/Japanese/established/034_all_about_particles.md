## Grammar points

### 1. Wa は (p.10–12)
Basic topic marker: sets off a topic from the rest of the sentence (does not itself indicate
grammatical case). Four sub-uses: (1) presents info about something already known/identified; (2)
introduces a topic that is then identified/explained; (3) in the N1 *wa* N2 *ga* construction,
marks the overall topic while *ga* marks the specific quality/aspect being described (e.g. "elephants
— trunk is long"); (4) shows contrast between two items, each separately marked by *wa*. A further
note: *wa* almost always follows other particles (*ni wa*, *de wa*) in its contrastive function,
with two exceptions — it can directly replace *ga* and *o*. Cross-referenced throughout the *ga*
entry as the particle that substitutes for *ga* under contrastive emphasis.

### 2. Ga が (p.13–26)
The book's largest and most structurally complex entry, split into two major parts.
**I. Subject/object marker** (14 sub-senses): marks existence (with *aru/iru/irassharu*-type
verbs); marks the grammatical subject of the predicate; marks the subject of an intransitive verb;
marks the subject of a sentence presenting new/unknown information (vs. *wa* for known topics,
e.g. introducing "this is Mr. X"); used with interrogatives in questions (both general questions and
either/or choice questions — *wa* can replace *ga* when a topic, e.g. the interrogative's referent,
is already established); marks the subject of a relative clause; marks the subject of a clause
ending in *ka*; marks the subject of a subordinate or conditional clause when that subject differs
from the main clause's subject; marks the object of verbs of ability (*dekiru*, *wakaru*, potential
forms); marks the object of verbs of sensation (*mieru*, *kikoeru*) and certain uses of *suru*; marks
the object of necessity verbs/adjectives (*hitsuyō da*, *iru*); marks the object of desire adjectives
(*hoshii*, *-tai* forms — contrast with *o* for the *-tai*/*-garu* forms of verbs); marks the object
of emotion verbs/adjectives (*suki da*, *kirai da*, *ureshii*, *kanashii*, *kowai*, *shinpai suru*);
marks the object of ability adjectives (*jōzu na*, *heta na*, *tokui na*, *kiyō na*). In nearly every
one of these, *wa* can substitute for *ga* to create contrastive emphasis — this wa/ga interplay is
flagged by the author's preface as one of the book's recurring comparative threads.
**II. Clause-connector** (8 sub-senses, "but" family): connects two opposed clauses ("but,
although"); connects clauses with two different qualities of one subject; connects clauses with no
adversative sense at all ("and" — distinguished from sense II-1 by the absence of contrast); marks a
preliminary remark, often left dangling at a true sentence end so the listener can pick up the
implied continuation (a social-softening device, e.g. when giving one's name before asking a
favor); the dangling-*ga* pattern recurs [advanced] implying an unstated contrasting idea ("well,
yes, but…"), softening a refusal, and — when preceded by *to ii* — expressing an unrealizable or
uncertain wish ("it would be nice if…"); [advanced] used idiomatically after the *-ō* volitional form
of contrastive verbs/adjectives, or after *-ō...-mai* pairs of the same verb, to mean "whether or
not" (dismissing the relevance of either outcome); and the idiom *V-ga hayai ka* ("no sooner had…
than").

### 3. Mo も (p.26–29)
Seven senses: equates two things ("also, too"); shows similar nouns in parallel ("both X and Y");
indicates addition ("as well as"); emphasizes absence of doubt about a quantity/frequency question
("any number of times, continually" — with interrogatives like *nando* "how many times" or *itsu*
"when"); indicates total negation with a negative verb ("nothing, no one" — with interrogatives like
*nani*, *dare*); emphasizes the extent of a number ("as much/many as," e.g. knowing all 5,000
*kanji*); indicates an approximate upper limit ("around, up to" — "if you have as much as an hour…").

### 4. -Te mo (-de mo) ても（でも） (p.29–30)
Verb -*te*/-*de* form (or adjective -*kute* form) + *mo*. Two senses: "even if, even though"
(concessive); and, after verbs with an interrogative, "no matter where/who/what" (emphatic
indifference to any value of the interrogative).

### 5. De mo でも (p.30–33)
Distinct from #4 (de mo attaches to nouns, not verb/adjective conjunctive forms); informally
replaceable by *datte* (not covered in this book) in every sense except no. 6. Four core senses plus
a final idiomatic one: after nouns, emphasizes a hypothetical ("even, even if" — "even a boring
meeting is still work"); after nouns for simple emphasis ("even" — "even an animal can understand");
after an interrogative for positive emphasis ("any-," e.g. *nan de mo* "anything," *itsu de mo*
"anytime"); in the form *donna...de mo* ("whatever kind of"); with two or more nouns as
representative examples of a larger set ("either X or Y [or similar]"); and finally indicates one
tentative possibility among others ("...or something," e.g. suggesting a movie "or something").

### 6. To と (p.33–39)
Three major functions. **I. Follows nouns** — joins nouns ("and," usually two or three, contrast
with *ya* #8 which implies a non-exhaustive list); indicates comparison/contrast or, in a choice
question, "or" (in this usage *to* must follow each noun); "together, with"; [advanced] indicates a
change/result, commonly in the phrase *to naru* (*ni*, #13 sense I-8, can replace it in the same
pattern, but *to* is more formal and typically written); [advanced] after a quantity expression,
reinforces negation ("not as much as," "never again" — *nido to* "never twice [again]").
**II. After onomatopoeic adverbs or clauses** — precedes verbs like *iu*, *kiku*, *omou* to report what
someone said, asked, or thought (quotative *to*); also follows onomatopoeic adverbs directly (e.g.
*sarasara to*, *kirakira to*) describing manner.
**III. Follows verbs/adjectives to form a conditional** ("if, unless, whether or not") — indicates an
immediate second action following the first ("as soon as," often with *sugu*; note *-tara* #35 and
*nari* #46 share this meaning); indicates the inevitability of a following action ("when, as" — a
general/habitual conditional, e.g. "when spring comes, cherry trees bloom"); indicates a hypothetical
condition ("if, unless" — compare *-ba* #34 and *-tara* #35, which share this meaning closely);
indicates something learned as a result of an action ("when, after, as a result of" — *-tara* #35 can
share this meaning); and the *-yō/-ō...-mai* double-verb pattern expressing indifference to which of
two outcomes occurs ("whether...or [not]").

### 7. To wa とは (p.40)
Two senses: introduces a word/phrase being defined, or asked to be defined ("X refers to..."); and
[advanced], between two opposed clauses, usually in the form *to wa ie*, concedes the first clause
while asserting the second anyway ("though, even though").

### 8. Ya や (p.41–42)
Joins nouns to indicate a non-exhaustive, example-only list ("such things as... and..."); contrasts
with *to* (#6, I-1), which implies an exhaustive list of exactly the items named. Often combined with
*nado* ("and such") to reinforce the non-exhaustive sense. [Advanced] second sense: the idiom *ya ina
ya*, following a verb root, means "as soon as, no sooner had."

### 9. To ka とか (p.42–43)
Joins nouns, verb clauses, or adjectives to list several representative items from a larger set
("among other things, such things as"); with nouns this overlaps with *ya* (#8, I-1) but is more
informal (see also *ka* #11, II-4). [Advanced] second sense: used after pairs of antonymous words to
indicate uncertainty or inconsistency (e.g. reporting someone who alternately said they would and
would not do something).

### 10. Nado など (p.43–45)
*Nanka* is an informal equivalent; *nazo* and *nanzo* are more formal equivalents. Senses: marks a
list as non-exhaustive ("etc., and so forth," often paired with *ya* #8); indicates a tentative
suggestion ("or something, somewhere"); [advanced] expresses humbleness about the speaker ("someone
like me, the likes of me"); [advanced] emphasizes the negative/belittling aspect of a situation
("anyone like, the likes of" — dismissive of a third party's chances, e.g. "someone like Tanaka could
never become company president"); [advanced] emphasizes the impossibility/unacceptability of a
situation ("such, anything like" — typically with *nazo*, a more literary/formal register marker
within this same particle family); after verbs/adjectives, "something to the effect that" (a vague
quotative hedge, distinct from plain *to* + verb of saying).

### 11. Ka か (p.45–49)
Two major functions. **I. Sentence-final question marker**: simple question; an inquiry about
feelings/intentions or a suggestion ("how about..."); a rhetorical question; indicates anger or
censure ("So you...?"); [advanced] indicates the speaker talking to themselves (self-directed
musing, e.g. "Monday, is it..."). **II. Mid-sentence, indicates choice/doubt/uncertainty**: indicates
a choice ("or, whether or not," including the *ka dō ka* "whether or not" pattern); indicates
uncertainty about a state or reason ("I wonder if..."); [advanced] the idiom *ka...-nai uchi ni*
("hardly had...than, no sooner had..."); with an interrogative word, "something, anything; someone,
anyone" (e.g. *dare ka* "someone," *nani ka* "something"); following other particles, indicates
uncertainty or doubt (cross-referenced with *to ka* #9).

### 12. De で (p.49–52)
Eight senses: location of an action ("at, in" — contrast with *ni* #13, no. 2, used for permanent/
static location rather than one-time action location); means or implement ("by, with"); materials
used ("of, from, with" — *kara* may replace *de* here except when the raw material is unmistakably
evident, e.g. paper/wood/glass/cloth/string/leather, in which case *de* is required; compare *kara*
#15, I-6); indicates the greatest/extreme of a category ("the highest mountain in the world"); amount
and scope ("within the space/time of, in, for" — "readable in an hour"); the mode/condition of an
agent (not to be confused with the grammatical subject, e.g. "living alone," "as a whole family");
time or age ("when, at the age of" — compare *ni* #13, no. 3); and the reason for something
("because of").

### 13. Ni に (p.52–59)
The book's second-largest entry (15 sub-senses). Marks where a person/thing is — concrete place (1a)
and abstract place (1b, e.g. "in conference"); marks the location of an action for "non-action"/
permanent-state verbs (2a, e.g. *sunde iru* "lives") and for verbs whose resulting condition is
static (2b) — contrasted explicitly with *de* (#12, no. 1), which marks one-time/short-term action
location; marks time (3a specific time; 3b a recurring interval, "every, per"); marks movement from a
larger to a smaller physical (4a) or abstract (4b) place ("in, into" — contrast *o* #18, no. 6);
marks movement toward a place ("to" — *e* #14, no. 1 can also be used); marks the object of an
action ("to," used after a noun implying an action, e.g. *kaimono ni iku* "go shopping," or after a
-*masu* verb base); marks the recipient of an action/indirect object ("to, from" — *e* may replace
*ni* for "to," *kara* may replace it for "from"); marks the result of a change or impending change
("into, become"); marks a condition already in existence (usually with *natte iru*, "to be"); marks
the agent of a passive verb ("by"); marks the person made to do something in a causative sentence;
marks the agent of a causative-passive verb ("by"); [advanced] joins nouns, usually three or more
("and" — equivalent to *to* #6 I-1 but more common in writing); [advanced] indicates a pair of
people/things commonly mentioned together ("and," e.g. fixed pairings like "Romeo and Juliet"); and
[advanced] indicates the basis or means by which an action takes place (typically with *motozuku* "to
be based on" or *yoru* "owing to").

### 14. E へ (p.60–61)
Two senses, with *ni* able to replace *e* in both except when *e* is followed by *no*: indicates a
direction/goal/destination ("to"); marks the recipient of an action/indirect object ("to").

### 15. Kara から (p.61–65)
**I. Follows nouns and the -*te* form of verbs** ("from"): after nouns, the time something begins
("from, at"); after nouns, the place something begins ("from, at"); [advanced] idiomatic figurative
place references (e.g. *sumi kara sumi made*, "from corner to corner" = cover to cover; "from a
woman's viewpoint"); after -*te* form verbs, an action beginning right after the previous one ends
("after"); after -*te* form verbs, passage of time ("since, for"); materials used ("from" — compare
*de* #12, no. 3: *kara* suits materials that are the result of a complex transformative process,
e.g. wine from grapes or tofu from soybeans, while *de* suits materials that retain their original
visible form); the agent of a passive verb ("by" — can replace *ni* when the preceding noun reads
more as a source than an agent, or to avoid repeating *ni*, with verbs like *ai suru*, *kiku*,
*meirei suru*, *shikaru*, *shiraberu*).
**II. Follows verbs/adjectives** to indicate cause/reason ("since, because" — replaceable by *no de*
#26; *kara* is the more subjective of the two, *no de* more objective/polite); [advanced] used
trailingly at a sentence's end to imply censure or warning ("so you'd better..." — the clause is left
dangling, with the consequence merely implied).

### 16. Made まで (p.65–68)
Indicates a time limitation (often paired with *kara*, "until, till, to"); indicates the place to
which an action extends (often paired with *kara*, "to"); indicates the degree of a condition by
citing an extreme example ("even, so...that"); [advanced] indicates an extreme/hypothetical condition
(e.g. contemplating suicide, or being willing to sacrifice everything); [advanced] at a sentence's
end, indicates a limitation or extent ("that is all," e.g. closing a report with "for your
reference"); [advanced] in the idiom *made mo nai* (following a verb root), emphasizes that an action
is unnecessary ("there is no need to," including the fixed phrase *iu made mo nai*, "needless to
say").

### 17. No の (p.68–71)
**I. Between two nouns**, indicating possession or modification, or replacing *ga* for a clause's
subject: possession ("'s" — second noun omittable when understood from context); position/location
(used repeatedly in a string, e.g. "on the desk, under the chair, in front of the school, behind this
building"); indicates the first noun modifies the second by kind/category (e.g. "a teacher of
English"); indicates two nouns in apposition (e.g. "Mr. Imai, the president of K University"); and
replaces *ga* to mark the subject of a clause modifying a noun (e.g. "the painting [that] Sakamoto
painted").
**II. Nominalizes verbs and adjectives**: a simple nominalizer ("-ing, what," e.g. turning "go for a
drive" into "going for a drive is..."); as a nominalizer before verbs of perception (*mieru*,
*kikoeru*, e.g. "[the fact of] the cars running can be clearly seen").
**III. Sentence-final particle** (colloquial/spoken register, explicitly register-flagged by the
source): indicates a question in colloquial speech; [register note] imparts a softer tone to a
statement, and the source explicitly states this use is "usually used by women"; [advanced] indicates
a mild command (e.g. "don't say such things").

### 18. O を (p.72–75)
Eight senses: marks the direct object of an action; marks the direct object of a passive verb (e.g.
"had money stolen," "had a hand bitten"); marks the person/thing made to do something in a causative
sentence; marks a specific occupation/position (usually with *suru*, e.g. "works as a physician");
used with -*tai*/-*tagaru* desire forms of verbs (contrast *ga* #2, I-12, used with *hoshii* and
adjectival desire forms); marks movement from a smaller to a larger place, both physical (6a) and
abstract (6b) (contrast *ni* #13, no. 4; note *kara* sounds plausible from an English standpoint but
should not substitute for *o* here); with verbs of motion, marks the place being traversed/moved
along/through (e.g. crossing a bridge, walking along the left side of a road, passing in front of a
store); marks the starting point of an action (e.g. a train leaving a station).

### 19. Kurai (gurai) くらい（ぐらい） (p.75–76)
Two senses: an approximate amount or extent, with no particular connotation about upper/lower bound
(contrast *hodo* #20 and *bakari* #21, which may replace it here but carry their own nuances);
indicates the extent of an action/condition via a specific illustrative example ("so...that, to the
extent that" — *hodo* #20, no. 3 may replace it here); and a comparison ("as...as" — *hodo* #20, no.
2 may replace it here).

### 20. Hodo ほど (p.77–78)
Three senses, "somewhat more formal" than *kurai*: an approximate amount or maximum extent,
stressing the upper limit (vs. *kurai*'s neutral approximation and *bakari*'s tendency toward
smallness); a comparison used only in negative sentences ("as...as," e.g. "not as cold as last
year" — *kurai* may substitute here, but *hodo* is more common); the extent of an action/condition via
a specific example (similar to *kurai* #19, no. 2); and the correlative pattern *V-ba + V hodo* ("the
more..., the more...," e.g. "the further north you go, the colder it gets").

### 21. Bakari ばかり (p.78–81)
Informal variants: *bakkari*, *bakashi*, *bakkashi*. Senses: an approximate amount/extent, tending
(but only tending) toward smallness (contrast *kurai*/*hodo*, which may substitute); "not only...but
also" (*dake* #22, no. 4 may replace this, but *bakari* is slightly more emphatic); emphasizes the
singularity of the immediately preceding word ("only, nothing but" — position-sensitive: in loose
usage *bakari* can semantically "shift" from the word it's meant to modify, with meaning recovered
from context; carries a nuance of disapproval that *dake* lacks); after the -*ta* form of verbs,
"just" (*tokoro* #38, no. 1 is similar but less emphatic); [advanced] in the phrase *bakari ni*,
emphasizes a reason/cause ("just, merely because, for the simple reason that" — frequently with a
disproportionate or regrettable outcome).

### 22. Dake だけ (p.81–83)
Senses: an absolute quantitative limit connoting a small amount ("only, just"); an extent or amount
("as...as," e.g. "drink as much as you like," "go as quickly as you can"); [advanced] in the phrases
*dake ni*, *dake atte*, and *dake no koto wa aru*, indicates the cause/precondition behind a result or
state of affairs, in three flavors: (a) when the result meets expectations and justifies the effort
("...was worthwhile"); (b) when the result falls short of expectations, making it discouraging
("given the fact that..."); (c) when the outcome is simply the natural/expected result of the
foregoing condition ("as you might expect").

### 23. Shika しか (p.84–85)
Used only with negative verbs; may combine with (follow) *dake*, *nomi*, and *kiri* for further
emphasis. Two senses: after nouns, indicates nothing more than the stated quantity, with a
connotation that the amount is small/unsatisfactory ("only, nothing but, merely"); [advanced] after
verbs, indicates a limit to the action stipulated by the verb ("there is no choice but, all one can
do is," in the fixed pattern V + *shika nai*).

### 24. Nomi のみ (p.86–87)
A more literary/written register equivalent of *dake* (#22, no. 1) — explicitly flagged as "used
more in writing than in the spoken language." [Advanced] senses: "only" (combinable with following
*shika* for emphasis); and the correlative pattern *A nomi narazu B mo* ("not only A but also B" —
essentially equivalent to *bakari* #21 no. 2 and *dake* #22 no. 4, but more literary).

### 25. Kiri きり (p.87–88)
A more colloquial register equivalent of *dake* (#22, no. 1); alternative pronunciation *kkiri*.
Senses: after nouns/verbs, a "cutting-off" limit ("only," e.g. "lives alone," "just sat there
without saying anything"); with a small number/amount, emphasizes a negative implication ("only" —
*dake* may replace *kiri* here); indicates the most recent occurrence of something ("the last," e.g.
"the last I heard from him was...").

### 26. No de ので (p.88)
Indicates a cause or reason ("in that, since, because"). Contrasted explicitly with *kara* (#15,
II-1): *no de* is the more objective of the two (*kara* the more subjective), and *no de* is the
more polite/softer of the two; *kara* tends to appear in prohibitions, commands, and questions where
*no de* would not. Note: *da* changes to *na* before *no de* (e.g. *byōki na no de*).

### 27. Mono de もので (p.89)
Indicates a cause or reason ("in that, inasmuch as, given that"); explicitly marked as more polite
and formal than both *no de* (#26) and *kara* (#15, II-1).

### 28. Keredomo けれども (p.90–91)
Informal/colloquial variants: *keredo*, *kedo*, *kedomo* — all softer in tone than *ga*, and the full
form *keredomo* tends to be preceded by *desu*/-*masu* forms (register-graded family, like *ga* II
but gentler). In every usage below, *ga* (#2, II-1, 2, 4, 5, 6) can substitute for *keredomo* and its
informal variants. Senses: between two opposed clauses ("but, although"); at a sentence's end,
implying an unstated qualification to what was actually said ("well, yes, but..." — the second clause
left unstated); indicates a preliminary remark (e.g. giving one's name before asking for someone on
the phone); [advanced] at a sentence's end, emphasizes the speaker's hope that an event comes out as
stated ("it would be nice if, I hope").

### 29. Tokoro de ところで (p.92–93)
Always follows the -*ta* form of a verb; the verb's own tense is governed by the main clause's tense,
not fixed to past. [Advanced] senses: between contrasting clauses, emphasizes that an extent/number/
amount is less than expected ("even if, even though" — similar to -*te mo* #4 no. 1, but with
stronger emphasis); indicates that the outcome of the first clause's content will not be favorable no
matter what ("even if, no matter how much").

### 30. No ni のに (p.93–94)
Between two clauses, indicates they are opposed in meaning ("although, even though, despite the fact
that") — explicitly noted as indicating *stronger* opposition than either *ga* (#2, II-1) or
*keredomo* (#28, no. 1); [advanced] at a sentence's end, indicates dissatisfaction with the second
clause left unstated ("despite the fact that, even though" — parallel structure to *keredomo* no. 2);
"to, in order to" (purpose/goal, e.g. "how long does it take [in order] to get from here to the
lake").

### 31. Kuse ni くせに (p.94–95)
"Despite the fact that, though" — similar to *no ni* (#30, no. 1) but explicitly carries an added
feeling of censure or contempt toward the subject (e.g. "children shouldn't interrupt adults'
conversation, [despite being merely] children"). [Advanced] sense 2: used at a sentence's end to
express censure or contempt directly (second clause left unstated, e.g. "as if you could do it
yourself").

### 32. Mono no もの (p.95)
[Advanced] "but, although" — explicitly noted as essentially equivalent to *ga* (#2, II) and
*keredomo* (#28) in meaning, but a more formal usage found more often in writing than speech.

### 33. Tokoro ga ところが (p.96–97)
Always follows the -*ta* form of a verb (tense governed by the main clause, not fixed to past).
Between two opposed clauses, with the result often unexpected: (a) an unexpected bad result (e.g.
arriving at the bank only to find it already closed); (b) an unexpected good result (e.g. receiving
an acceptance notice despite having expected to fail an entrance exam).

### 34. -Ba ば (p.97–99)
Six senses: a supposition and its result ("if...then" — similar to -*tara* #35 no. 1); the clause
after -*ba* as a result of the clause before it (*to* #6, III-3, can substitute); [advanced] a
plain hypothetical introduction without cause-effect force ("if," e.g. "if you would like..."); four
idiomatic patterns built on -*ba*: *...ieba* ("...stated," a framing hedge, e.g. "simply stated...");
*dekireba* ("if possible," lit. "if it can be done"); [advanced] connecting two similar events/states
("and," typically in negative-adjective pairs, e.g. "the weather is nice and there's no wind"); and
the pattern V-*ba ii* ("one ought to, one need only do," e.g. "you need only write your name here").

### 35. -Tara たら (p.100–101)
A supposition and result ("if...then" — similar to but gentler than -*ba* #34 no.1; -*tara* favored
when the result clause is an intention, request/command, or question); [register note] used at a
sentence's end to indicate a proposal ("how about, why not" — explicitly marked as "used often by
women"); [advanced] used at a sentence's end (in the variant form -*ttara*, added to the -*te*/
-*nasai* forms rather than the plain stem) to indicate irritation/impatience ("I'm telling you,
c'mon" — a distinctly colloquial, emphatic register); an action followed by something being learned
as a result ("when" — *to* #6, III-4, is similar); one action following immediately upon another
("when, as soon as").

### 36. Nara なら (p.102–103)
Often follows nouns directly (unlike -*ba*/-*tara*, which attach to conditional verb/adjective
forms); similar to -*ba* (#34, no. 1), -*tara* (#35, no. 1), and *to* (#6, III-3), but distinguished
from -*tara* by emphasizing that the result clause *would definitely* occur given the supposed
event, whereas -*tara*'s result clause merely emphasizes what would "happen to" occur. Senses: a
supposition and result ("if...then"); indicates a topic being brought up ("as for," e.g. "as for
that problem, it's already been settled" — functions almost like a conditional-flavored topic
marker, distinct from *wa*).

### 37. Mono nara ものなら (p.103–104)
Cross-referenced under *nara* (#36). [Advanced], all three senses carry an added nuance of censure,
threat, or genuine difficulty beyond plain *nara*: after a potential verb or -*ō*/-*yō* volitional
form, a condition-and-result with added censure/threat ("if...then," e.g. "if you think you can do
it, go ahead and try" — often implying the speaker doubts it's really possible or is daring the
listener); a condition leading to a bad result (e.g. "if the section chief handles it that way, his
subordinates will lose all faith in him"); a condition and bad result emphasizing near-impossibility
or extremity (e.g. "if it were possible to fly, I'd like to try it" — a wistful/unreal hypothetical).

### 38. Tokoro ところ (p.104–106)
A noun-like particle marking a point in an action's progress. Senses: on the verge of doing
something, preceded by the plain/dictionary root form of the verb ("just about to"); an action
presently in progress, preceded by V-*te iru* ("in the midst of, right now"); an action that has just
concluded, preceded by the -*ta* form ("just now finished"); the conclusion of an action at a
clause's end ("when, upon," e.g. "when I inquired at the department store, [I found] the item was
sold out").

### 39. Mono o ものを (p.106–107)
[Advanced] Usually sentence-final, indicating resentment or complaint about a counterfactual — "if
only..." (the actual, regretted outcome left for the listener to infer), e.g. "if you had told me, I
would have come to visit you at the hospital [but you didn't tell me, so I didn't]."

### 40. -Nagara ながら (p.107–108)
Requires the same subject in both clauses attached to the verb stem (-*i*/-*masu* base) + *nagara*;
in English translation the main and subordinate clauses are usually reversed from their Japanese
order. Senses: two actions happening simultaneously ("as, while," e.g. "watches TV while eating
breakfast," rendered in English as "eats breakfast while watching TV"); between two clauses,
emphasizes that they are opposed in meaning ("although, while," e.g. "although Takagi says he has
poor health, he often drinks until late at night").

### 41. -Tari たり (p.108–110)
Senses: lists several actions in no particular sequence, representative of a larger set ("such
things as," e.g. "on Sundays I do such things as play tennis with friends or go see movies" — note:
with only a single verb, -*tari* still marks that verb's action as merely representative of a larger
set of unstated activities); several like actions performed in series (e.g. "I read and write
*kanji*"); repetition of contrasting/opposite actions (e.g. "stop turning the TV on and off"); the
same verb in both positive and negative form ("sometimes I do and sometimes I don't").

### 42. Shi し (p.110–111)
Senses: indicates (and often emphasizes) the co-occurrence of two or more actions/states ("and, as
well as," e.g. "the climate is good and the flowers are beautiful"); indicates one or more reasons
leading to a following conclusion ("and..., so," e.g. listing a person's unpopularity and
untrustworthiness as reasons they can't lead a group).

### 43. Tomo とも (p.111–114)
Senses: after numbers/counters, "both, all" (e.g. "all three people," "both sweaters"); after the
-*ku* form of adjectives, an approximate maximum or minimum ("at the least, at the most"); indicates
inclusion ("including," e.g. a total that includes a service charge); [advanced] after a verb's -*ō*
volitional form, adds emphasis to a supposition ("even if, no matter," e.g. "no matter what happens,
he'll stick it out"); [advanced] in the fixed pattern *tomo arō* (noun) *ga*, "of all people/things"
(e.g. "for the prime minister, of all people, to do such a thing is unbelievable" — a register of
incredulous criticism); after two antonymous words followed by *ienai*, "can't say (yes) or (no)"
(e.g. "can't say whether it's good or bad"); at a sentence's end, adds decisiveness to a positive
statement ("indeed, certainly, of course," e.g. a one-word emphatic affirmative reply *Ii tomo* "Of
course [you may]").

### 44. Yara やら (p.114–116)
Senses: joins nouns for a non-exhaustive list ("such things as...; and what with" — explicitly noted
as essentially the same usage as *ya*, *to ka*, and *dano*, though those three are more common in
speech); used with antonymous or positive/negative word pairs to show uncertainty ("whether, whether
or not" — *ka* can substitute but *yara* is softer in tone); with interrogative words, expresses
uncertainty, with an implicit "I don't know" even when not stated (slightly softer than *ka* #11,
II-3); [advanced] at a sentence's end, a rhetorical "I wonder," typically with negative/uncertain
implications (e.g. "I wonder what next year will bring").

### 45. Dano だの (p.116–117)
Explicitly flagged as "a colloquial usage," similar to *yara* (#44) and *to ka* (#9). Senses: a
non-exhaustive list ("such things as," e.g. gifts received at Christmas); a pair of opposite
actions/states, or positive and negative forms of the same word (e.g. "stop saying you'll go and
then you won't, make up your mind").

### 46. Nari なり (p.117–119)
Senses: indicates a choice ("or, whether or not" — similar to the more common *ka* #11, II-1, but
*nari* lends slightly more emphasis); a suggestion ("or something," e.g. "let's have tea at a coffee
shop or something"); [advanced] one action following immediately upon another ("as soon as" —
similar to *to* #6, III-1 and -*tara* #35, no. 5); [advanced] the fixed expression *nan nari*
("whatever, anything," e.g. "please say whatever it is [that's unsatisfactory]"); [advanced] the
fixed expression *dai nari shō nari* ("whether big or small").

### 47. -Te wa (-de wa) ては（では） (p.119–120)
Covers *wa* in combination with the -*te*/-*de* conjunctive verb form (cross-referenced to *wa* #1,
no. 5, and *to wa* #7). Senses: the repetition of two contrasting, alternating actions (e.g.
"writes the kanji and erases them, writes and erases them again"; "the waves advance and recede,
recede and advance"); in the form *ni shite wa* (or *to shite wa*), "for [a given standard]" (e.g.
"for a Sunday, the department stores aren't very crowded"; "for a Japanese, [this non-native
speaker's] English is pretty good"); a conditional pattern whose result clause is always negative or
has a negative implication, in four sub-forms: (a) an undesirable possibility leading to a negative
result (e.g. the proverb "you can't fight on an empty stomach"); (b) the fixed form *-te wa komaru*
("there will be problems if, you shouldn't," e.g. "you shouldn't park here"); (c) the fixed forms
*-te wa ikenai* / *-te wa naranai* ("you mustn't, you shouldn't," e.g. "you must not smoke here"); (d)
the fixed form *-te wa irarenai* ("be unable to," e.g. "I cannot remain silent about this").

### 48. Dokoro どころ (p.120–122)
Senses: emphasizes a negative action/state against the impossibility of a contrasting positive one
("it is out of the question, unthinkable, far from," e.g. "I've been so busy lately that taking a
trip is unthinkable"; also "it is not merely a matter of being brilliant — he's an absolute genius");
in the form *dokoro ka*, negates the first of two parallel nouns/clauses to emphasize the second
("far from, not merely," e.g. "far from taking one hour [as usual], it took three"; "not merely
English, he's good at French too").

### 49. Toshite として (p.122)
Contrast with *o* (#18, no. 6). Senses: indicates status or position ("in the capacity of, as," e.g.
"Chopin is famous as a Polish composer"; "Ms. White, who came to Japan as an exchange student");
[advanced] used with "one" and counters incorporating "one" ("without exception," always in a
negative construction, e.g. "there isn't one good thing you can say about the work they do"; "the
section chief has never once won at Mahjong").

### 50. Yori より (p.123–125)
Sometimes displaced by the nonstandard variants *yori mo* and *yori ka*. Senses: a comparison
("than," e.g. "Yoshida is taller than Koide"); in the form *to iu yori*, "rather" (e.g. "he's a
dictator rather than a leader"); [advanced] with interrogative words, indicates the previously
mentioned thing is superlative (e.g. "I think this restaurant's French food is better than anywhere
else"); [advanced] the time/place at which an action begins ("from, at" — explicitly noted as
equivalent to *kara* #15, I-1/I-2, but *yori* is more formal, more common in writing and elevated
speech, e.g. "a cabinet meeting will be held from three o'clock"); [advanced] a place/position an
action occurs from ("from," e.g. "please stand behind the white line"; "my house is on this side of
the station"); [advanced] a cause, reason, or motivation ("as a result of," e.g. "as a result of
last month's market research..."; "infectious diseases broke out as a result of polluted rivers");
[advanced] the fixed form *yori hoka nai* ("there is nothing else but," e.g. "there's nothing to do
but finish it"; "nothing to do but wait until the electricity comes back on").

### 51. Sae さえ (p.126)
Senses: "even" — similar to *de mo* (#5, no. 2) but more emphatic (e.g. "even the teacher couldn't
answer that question"; "even a child knows something that simple"); in the pattern *sae...-tara* or
*sae...-ba*, implies that one additional thing being done (or having been done) would produce (would
have produced) a positive outcome ("if only," e.g. "if only we'd had five more minutes, we'd have
made the flight"; "if Ken just studies, he'll get into a good university").

### 52. Sura すら (p.127)
[Advanced] "Even" — a more formal equivalent of *sae* (#51, no. 1), seen most often in negative
sentences (e.g. "although Yamada is an English teacher, she can't even carry on an ordinary
conversation"; "the people rescued in the mountains were so exhausted they couldn't even move").

### 53. Koso こそ (p.127–128)
[Advanced] Adds emphasis to the word immediately preceding it ("precisely, for certain, indeed," e.g.
"next year for certain, I'm hoping to travel to Europe"; "let's give it our best this time, for
sure"); the fixed idiomatic expression *kochira koso* ("not at all, the pleasure is all mine" — a
set polite reply to thanks).

### Sentence-ending particles (p.128–)
The source's own introductory note: Japanese has many sentence-ending particles; used commonly in
spoken language, they convey emotional nuance (often via tone of voice) without changing a sentence's
explicit content — e.g. *onegai shimasu*, *onegai shimasu ne*, and *onegai shimasu yo* are all
requests, merely softened or emphasized differently by the appended particle. Some sentence-ending
particles are used predominantly by men, others predominantly by women (an explicit register
annotation preserved per the coverage rule). Several particles covered earlier in this book have
both a mid-sentence and sentence-ending usage: *ga*, *ka*, *kara*, *made*, *no*, *keredomo*, *no ni*,
*kuse ni*, -*tara*, *mono o*, *tomo*, *yara*.

### 54. Ne ね (p.128–130)
Variant pronunciation *nē*. Senses: indicates emotion or admiration (e.g. "what a pretty flower!" —
source explicitly notes that when this is combined with sentence-final *wa*, as in *...datta wa ne*,
the *wa* itself marks the speaker as a woman — register note); indicates agreement with the other
speaker ("yes, that's quite true"); softens a request (e.g. "if possible, please be sure to do it");
indicates a request for confirmation ("..., didn't you? I suppose..."); indicates a mild assertion of,
or variance in, opinion ("is that so? I wonder"); indicates a mild assertion generally (e.g. "I can't
understand what they're thinking").

### 55. Yo よ (p.130–131)
Senses: urges a course of action (e.g. "let's take a break, guys"; "come on, let's go to that
exhibition"); indicates a request, explicitly noted as somewhat stronger in force than *ne* (#54, no.
3) (e.g. "I really want you to take this on"; "please come to my house too"); indicates a statement
of certainty (e.g. "she's already thirteen, you know"; "today is Friday" stated to correct someone);
[register note] indicates scolding or contempt (e.g. "he just can't do the work!") — the source
explicitly notes that the combination *wa yo* here marks the speaker as a woman (cross-referenced to
#56).

### 56. Wa わ (sentence-final) (p.132–133)
Register note: written with the kana わ (or ワ), distinct from the topic-marker は used for particle
#1; explicitly noted as "used mainly by women." Senses: indicates emotion or admiration (e.g. "the
opera tonight was absolutely fabulous!"; "this flower arrangement is simply splendid!"); softens the
tone of a statement (e.g. "I think it would be better to buy it at another store"; "it was all my
fault, I'm sorry").

### 57. Ka na かな (p.133–134)
Register note: explicitly "used principally by men"; the women's equivalent is *kashira* (#58).
Senses: indicates uncertainty ("I wonder," e.g. "I wonder what time the section chief is coming");
indicates a question directed at oneself ("I wonder," e.g. "hmm, what day is it today?"); indicates a
hope or muted request ("I wonder," e.g. "I wonder if I could ask you to take care of this job" — a
softened way of making a request).

### 58. Kashira かしら (p.134–135)
The usages of *kashira* are explicitly noted as essentially the same as *ka na* (#57), except mostly
employed by women. Senses: uncertainty ("I wonder," e.g. "I wonder what time the president is coming
to the office today"); a question to oneself ("I wonder," e.g. "I wonder if it's all right to leave
now"; "oh, what a lovely present, I don't deserve it!"); a hope or muted request ("I wonder," e.g. "I
wonder if you could help me with my homework tonight").

### 59. Na な (p.135–137)
Variant lengthened pronunciation *nā*. Senses (explicitly flagged per-sense register where the source
marks it): indicates emotion, [register note] "mostly used by men" (e.g. "she's really something!";
"what a beautiful star!"); [register note] asks for another person's agreement, "used by men" (e.g.
"that's a new car, right?"; "it's cold there, isn't it"); softens the effect of an assertion, no
register restriction given (e.g. "I really think he's a fine person"; "this movie just wasn't very
good"); softens a command or request — the source gives a contrasting pair of examples here, one a
woman speaking (with the *kudasai na* pattern, e.g. "Narita Airport, please") and one a man speaking
(a direct imperative + *na*, e.g. "be sure to come tomorrow"); [register note] indicates a
prohibition, "used by men" (e.g. "stay away from that guy"; "don't ever go to that bar again").

### 60. Sa さ (p.137)
Senses: [register note] softens an assertion, "used mostly by men" (e.g. "of course I'm going to
Takahashi's party tomorrow"; "this sweater's bigger, I'd say"); indicates a critical response to
something, no register restriction given explicitly for this sense (e.g. "it's because they hire
incompetent people that the company doesn't grow"; "that's just like him").

### 61. Koto こと (sentence-final) (p.138)
Register note: explicitly "used mainly by women" as a sentence-final particle. Senses: indicates
emotion (e.g. "what an exquisite color this flower has!"; "what marvelously delicious food!");
indicates a suggestion or invitation (e.g. "shall we go flower-viewing somewhere?"; "shall we go
aboard the Queen Elizabeth sometime?").

### 62. -Kke っけ (p.139)
Register note: follows V-*ta* and Adj-*ta* forms; in feminine speech the preceding verb forms tend to
be *desu*, *deshita*, and V-*mashita*. Senses: a muted question when the speaker is trying to recall
information already shared with the listener (e.g. "what time was the wedding supposed to start
tomorrow, again?"; "now, where was your house again?"); indicates the speaker is remembering
something from the distant past (e.g. "didn't there used to be a school around here?"; "I remember
going drinking with him a lot back in the day").

### 63. -Tteba ってば (p.140)
Senses: indicates annoyance with another person ("I'm telling you," e.g. "I'm telling you, there'll
be trouble if it's not done by tomorrow"; "next year will be too late, I'm telling you"); indicates
an indirect command or prohibition (e.g. "I'm telling you it's no good if you do that"; "you won't
get anywhere without using the computer, I'd say").

### 64. -I い (p.140–141)
Register note: explicitly "used mostly by men." Following *da* or *ka*, indicates an informal
question (e.g. "why'd you go all the way to Shinjuku?"; "where'd you go drinking yesterday?"; "off to
the US again, huh? Hang in there"; "how was she?").

### 65. Mono もの (sentence-final) (p.141–142)
The source's own framing note: the primary meaning of sentence-final *mono* is "because, the reason
is," and this sense is vaguely felt beneath each of the individual usages below even when not
explicit. [Register note] All of sense 1's sub-cases are explicitly marked "used by woman": (a) a
reason or excuse (e.g. "that movie's simply too boring, that's why I didn't go"); (b)
dissatisfaction (e.g. "I don't want to work for the section chief, he's so cold to his
subordinates"); (c) a desire to be indulged or pampered (e.g. "I want it so badly! It's all right if
I buy it, isn't it?").

### 66. Ze ぜ (p.143)
Register note: "used mostly by men." Adds force to a sentence; when it overlaps in function with *zo*
(#67) it is somewhat less emphatic. Sense: used to make a declaration to someone or underscore a
point (e.g. "I'm going on ahead, see you there"; "that job's up to you now"; "I'm hanging tough").

### 67. Zo ぞ (p.143–144)
Register note: "used mostly by men"; adds force to a sentence more emphatically than *ze* (#66).
Senses: indicates a command or threat (e.g. "let's get the meeting under way"; "if you do that again
you're going to pay for it"; "you'd better get cracking on that job"); adds force to words of
self-encouragement or self-urging (e.g. "I can do it!"; "I'm coming up a winner this time").

### 68. Mono ka ものか (p.144–145)
Register note: men tend to use the forms *mono ka* and *mon ka*; women tend to use *mono desu ka* and
*mon desu ka*. Sense: emphasizes a determination not to do something by means of a rhetorical
question ("I certainly will not," e.g. "I wouldn't be caught dead going there again"; "I wouldn't
work with her again if my life depended on it").

### 69. Ni に (sentence-final) (p.145)
A distinct final entry from the case-particle *ni* covered at #13 — this sentence-final usage
commonly follows *darō*, *deshō*, and the V-*tarō* form, and is noted as essentially the same meaning
as *no ni* (#30, no. 2), though *no ni* is more common. Sense: expresses regret that something is
over and can't be regained ("despite the fact that, if only," e.g. "it could have been finished much
sooner if only someone else had done it [but they didn't]"; "the rain might have stopped if only
we'd waited a little longer [but we didn't]").
