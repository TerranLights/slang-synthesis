**Morpheme breakdown (agglutinative compounds worth unpacking):**

> **にほんじん** (Japanese person, used in L1 Word Power as にほんじん) = にほん ("Japan") + じん
> (suffix "-ese/-ian, person from") — the ～じん suffix productively attaches to any country name
> captured in the same vocabulary set (ちゅうごくじん "Chinese person," ドイツじん "German person,"
> アメリカじん "American," etc.), making this a fully generative nationality-formation pattern rather
> than a list of unrelated lexical items.

> **ひるごはん** (lunch) = ひる ("daytime/noon") + ごはん ("cooked rice; meal") — part of a 3-way
> paradigm with あさごはん (あさ "morning" + ごはん) and ばんごはん (ばん "evening" + ごはん); ごはん
> itself literally means cooked rice but has been semantically extended to mean "meal" generally,
> the way the pattern recurs across all three times-of-day compounds.

> **にほんりょうり** (Japanese cuisine) = にほん ("Japan") + りょうり ("cooking, cuisine") —
> a simple N+N compound, but worth flagging because it mirrors the も国 country-name-prefixing
> pattern seen with ～じん above (cf. a hypothetical ちゅうごくりょうり "Chinese cuisine," not itself
> attested in the sampled pages but predictable from the pattern).

## Grammar points

### Topic marker は + noun identification (noun1 は noun2 です)

Introduced in Unit 1 Grammar (p.2). は marks noun1 as the sentence's topic; noun2 is then identified,
and the sentence closes with です. The topic is often, but not necessarily, the same as the logical
subject. Example: グレイさんは べんごしです。("Mr. Grey is an attorney.")

### Yes/no questions with か, and はい/いいえ

The question particle か attaches to the end of a sentence with no word-order change, even for
content questions ("who," "what," "when," etc.) — か itself is the question marker, not interrogative
word order. はい ≈ "yes," いいえ ≈ "no." (p.2)

### Omission of the topic (noun1)

When the topic is contextually obvious, noun1 + は is dropped entirely — e.g. a lone (わたしは)
グレイです。("[As for me,] I'm Grey.") is natural; topic omission is especially common in answers to
questions. (p.2)

### Negative copula: ではありません／じゃありません

The negative of です. じゃ is explicitly marked as more informal than では; otherwise interchangeable.
Full paradigm table given: です (aff. present) / ではありません (neg. present) / でした (aff. past) /
ではありませんでした (neg. past). (p.2)

### の as possession/affiliation particle

の attaches to a noun and the noun+の combination modifies the following word, expressing belonging
or affiliation — e.g. のぞみデパートの たかはしです ("[I'm] Takahashi, of Nozomi Department Store")
shows company affiliation, which the book notes is customary to state when introducing oneself in a
Japanese business context. (p.3, Note 4)

### よろしく おねがいします as an introduction/favor-request formula

A fixed phrase combined with はじめまして when being introduced; also reused when taking leave after
asking someone a favor. よろしく itself glossed as "well," functioning as a request for the other
person's favorable future consideration. (p.3, Note 5)

### Counters and the two Japanese numeral systems (ひとつ system vs. いち system)

Introduced via まい (counter for flat objects, e.g. shirts, paper). Japanese has two parallel numeral
systems: a native ひとつ・ふたつ・みっつ... series usable independently (goes only up to とお, "10"),
and a Sino-Japanese いち・に・さん... series that combines with counters like まい or ほん(ぼん/ぽん,
for long slender objects). Beyond 10, only the いち-system + counter combination is used (じゅういち,
じゅうに...). Word order: thing + を + number(+counter) + ください. (p.37–38)

### で as the particle of location-of-action

で marks the place where an action occurs (as opposed to existence, which takes に) — e.g. ぎんざで
すずきさんと てんぷらを たべます ("[I'll] eat tempura with Mr. Suzuki in Ginza"). (p.89, Note 1)

### Polite vocabulary substitution for the listener's possessions: おたく vs. うち

When speaking politely about another person's house, おたく is substituted for the plain/neutral うち
— an instance of lexical (not just morphological) politeness substitution tied to whose referent is
being discussed. (p.131, Note 1)

### -て form of verbs (introduced as a Word Power drill paradigm)

The lesson presents the -ます/-て pairing across four conjugation-class examples without naming the
classes explicitly: いきます→いって, あいます→あって, かえります→かえって (u-stem geminating class);
かきます→かいて, ききます→きいて (k-stem); よみます→よんで, のみます→のんで (m/b/n-stem, voiced -nde);
たべます→たべて, みます→みて, きます→きて, します→して (ichidan/irregular class). (p.162)

### Prohibitive request: verb -ない + で + ください ("please don't...")

Formed as verb-ない stem + でください. Example: フラッシュを つかわないでください。("Please don't use
a flash.") The book notes this is often used by managers/officials, and can be softened by appending
a reason-clause with から. (p.195, Note 1)

### -ない form (plain negative) as a Word Power paradigm

Parallel conjugation-class drill to the -て form above, this time for the plain negative: すいます→
すわない, つかいます→つかわない, たちます→たたない, のみます→のまない, とります→とらない (u-stem);
あけます→あけない, しめます→しめない, とめます→とめない (ichidan); きます→こない, します→しない
(irregular). (p.196)
