# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *Mimi kara Oboeru JLPT Vocabulary Training N3* (representative sample)

**Source:** Ando Eriko (安藤栄里子), Megaya Yoko (恵谷容子), Iijima Michiko (飯嶋美知子), *耳から覚える
日本語能力試験　語彙トレーニング N3* ("Mimi kara Oboeru JLPT Vocabulary Training N3"), ALC Press,
PDF scan dated 2011 (Adobe Acrobat Image Conversion Plug-in — no text layer). Two source files:

- *…Vocabulary.pdf* — 206 pages, the main thematic vocabulary book (vision-read).
- *…Vocabulary Index + Answers.pdf* — 30 pages, confirmed to be **only** a 50-on alphabetical
  index (checkboxes + entry/page pointers) and practice-problem answer keys — **no new vocabulary
  or grammar content**, so no entries were drawn from it; noted here so a future pass doesn't
  re-open it expecting coverage.

**Coverage note — representative sampling, not exhaustive.** The vocabulary book has 880 numbered
entries (Units 01–12: 名詞A/B/C "Nouns A/B/C", 動詞A/B/C "Verbs A/B/C", 形容詞A/B "Adjectives A/B",
カタカナA/B "Katakana A/B", 副詞A/B "Adverbs A/B", 連体詞・接続詞 "Prenominal adjectives/
conjunctions") plus 22 thematic コラム ("column") sidebars and a cooking-verb まとめ ("summary")
box, spread across 206 pages — far too much for exhaustive single-pass extraction. Per dispatch
instructions, this file **samples across the book's full thematic/grammatical range** rather than
extracting all 880 entries:

- At least one page-group sampled from every one of the 12 units (both the people/society-nouns
  end and the appliance/tech-katakana end, both intransitive/transitive verb pairs, both i- and
  na-adjectives, adverbs, the conjunctions/prenominal-adjectives tail).
- 13 of the 22 コラム sidebars read directly (units of measurement, the 中/じゅう vs. ちゅう
  grammar point, station/train vocabulary, money-suffix morphology, computer parts, public
  facilities, time-expression register pairs, entertainment genres, mail, spatial/position words,
  and all three 衣食住 "clothing/food/housing" boxes) — these are the book's most genuinely
  thematic (non-drill) content and were prioritized.
- The cooking-verb summary box (836–845) and several explicit register-marked adverb/conjunction
  entries near the end of the book (863–880).

Repeated 練習問題 (practice-problem) pages were skipped per the coverage rule — one was opened
(PDF p.204/printed p.203) purely to confirm it was pure drill content with no new vocabulary,
then excluded.

**PDF-page-offset gotcha:** this scan's PDF page number is consistently **printed page number + 1**
(e.g. PDF page 39 shows printed "38") — confirmed across the whole sampled range, a single fixed
offset, not a spread-scan issue. Page citations below use the book's own printed page numbers.

**Vision-reading guard:** all sampled pages were clean, consistently-typeset print scans — **no
handwritten marginalia, underlining, or pencil annotation was found on any sampled page.**

**Notation key (from the book's own front matter, p.3, paraphrased):** 連 = collocation (a particle
+ word commonly paired with the headword), 合 = compound word formed with the headword, 類 =
synonym, 対 = antonym, 関 = related word, 慣 = idiomatic expression; (名)(動)(イ形)(ナ形)(副) tag
part of speech (noun/verb/i-adjective/na-adjective/adverb); 自 = intransitive verb, 他 =
transitive verb; underlines in the source mark the particle a verb takes. This system is
reproduced only as paraphrased structure below, never as bulk-copied source text.

## Grammar points

### 自動詞・他動詞 pairing (intransitive/transitive verb pairs), with explicit particle marking

The book systematically presents Japanese verbs in intransitive (自動詞, marked 〈自〉, takes ガ)/
transitive (他動詞, marked 〈他〉, takes ヲ) pairs sharing a stem, and underlines the particle each
member takes — e.g. つかまる〈自〉"to be caught" / つかまえる〈他〉"to catch" (p.36); 詰まる〈自〉/
詰める〈他〉"be clogged / pack" (p.96); the cooking-verb summary box devotes an entire two-page
spread (836–845, p.192) to this pattern purely within cooking vocabulary (炊ける/炊く, 煮える/煮る,
焼ける/焼く). A few verbs (開く, 閉じる) are presented as ambitransitive — usable with either ガ or
ヲ depending on whether the event or the agent is the topic. This is the single most pervasive
structural feature of the book's verb units and would be essential background for any later
mechanics-analysis pass over Japanese verb morphology.

### ~中 suffix: じゅう vs. ちゅう readings (コラム11, p.97)

The suffix 中 attached to a time-period noun has two distinct readings with different meanings,
paraphrased from the source's own four-way breakdown:

1. **じゅう** — "throughout the whole of ~" (duration sense): 夏休み中 "all through summer
   vacation," 一年中 "all year round."
2. **ちゅう (also read じゅう に)** — "by the end of ~": 今月中に "by the end of this month."
3. **ちゅう** — "currently in the midst of ~ / in the process of ~ing": 食事中 "in the middle of a
   meal," 準備中 "in preparation," 出張中 "away on a business trip."
4. **じゅう** — "all of, the entirety of" (totality sense, different nuance from #1): 世界中 "the
   whole world," 学校中 "the whole school."

The reading is not predictable from the base noun alone — it has to be learned per-compound,
making this a genuine (if narrow) piece of Japanese derivational morphology worth flagging for the
mechanics-analysis phase.

### ~的な: deriving na-adjectives from nouns (Unit07, p.127)

的 (-teki) attaches to a (frequently Sino-Japanese) noun to derive a na-adjective meaning
"-istic/-related to/-like": 基本 "basics" → 基本的な "basic," 国際 "international (relations)" →
国際的な "international." The book treats both the base noun and the derived adjective as separate
numbered vocabulary entries, which is itself informative about how productive and lexicalized this
pattern is treated as being by N3 level.

### ~代／~料／~費／~賃: a family of cost/fee-naming suffixes (コラム13, p.107)

A single themed sidebar contrasts four suffixes that all translate loosely as "cost/fee/expense"
but carry distinct nuances, paraphrased from the source's own four-row table:

- **~代** — the cost/fee paid for a consumable good or utility used up (洗濯代 "laundry cost,"
  電気代 "electricity bill," バス代 "bus fare").
- **~料** — the fee paid for the *use* of equipment, facilities, or a service (電気料金 "electricity
  usage charge," 授業料 "tuition," 送料 "shipping fee").
- **~費** — an expense incurred for a particular purpose, viewed as a budget category (生活費
  "living expenses," 交通費 "travel expenses," 医療費 "medical expenses").
- **~賃** — a fare or rent paid specifically for labor, transport, or facility rental (家賃 "house
  rent," 電車賃 "train fare," 賃貸 "rental").

The same base noun (e.g. 電気, 交通) often takes more than one of these suffixes with a subtly
different meaning per suffix, which the source cross-references explicitly (e.g. it distinguishes
電気代 "the electricity bill you personally pay" from 電気料金 "the utility's posted usage rate").

### Garment-specific "to wear" verbs (コラム20 衣食住「衣」, p.177)

Japanese has no single general verb for "to wear" — the correct verb is selected by what kind of
garment or accessory is being worn, illustrated by this column grouping items under their
collocating verb: 着る (upper-body garments: shirts, jackets, dresses, coats), はく (lower-body
garments and footwear: pants, skirts, socks, shoes), 巻く／する (things wrapped around the neck:
scarves), しめる／する (things fastened: neckties, belts), はめる／する (things put over fingers/
hands: gloves, rings), つける／する (accessories generally), かぶる／する (headwear). The source
notes 下着 "underwear" can take 着る, つける, or はく depending on the specific item. This is a
genuine grammatical-collocation system, not an arbitrary vocabulary list, and is directly relevant
to any future analysis of how Japanese categorizes body-part-linked action verbs.

### Explicit colloquial-register annotation on specific adverbs/conjunctions (Unit12, pp.197–199)

Per the extraction spec's instruction to never skip explicit register annotation: the source
directly labels three entries as conversational/colloquial in its own prose, rather than leaving
register to be inferred — **ちっとも** ("会話的な表現", a colloquial variant of 少しも/全く),
**とっても** ("『とても』の会話的な言葉", the colloquial form of とても), and the second sense of
**それで** ("会話的な言葉", used to prompt a conversational partner to continue). This is comparable
in kind to the dialectal `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]`-style tags flagged as high-value in other languages'
source material — a formal/colloquial register axis explicitly marked by the book itself, distinct
from every other entry's unmarked "core" default.
