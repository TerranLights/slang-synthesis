## Grammar points

### 1. V-てもいい
V-てもいいですか (also V-てもかまいませんか、V-てもよろしいですか) asks permission to do something:
行ってもいいですか「May I go?」(p. 24)

### 2. XXなんかどうですか — "How about XX?"
Suggests XX indirectly; XX is usually a noun, なんて follows a verb: ホテルのバーなんかどうですか。(p. 24)

### 3. Sentence1 し Sentence2
し connects two or more sentences with the sense "and also," "moreover" — sometimes the second
clause is left implicit, for the hearer to infer: 天井が低いし、窓が小さいです「The ceiling is low
and the windows are small (and so on).」(p. 24)

### 4. Women's speech and men's speech
The book gives a direct side-by-side comparison of informal-style sentence endings, an explicit
register table worth preserving in full structure (not just paraphrase), since it is the clearest
gendered-register data point in the lesson:

| Context | Women | Men |
|---|---|---|
| "Today is payday, isn't it" | 今日は給料日**ね**。 | 今日は給料日**だね**。 |
| "It's quiet, isn't it" | 静か**ね**。 | 静か**だね**。 |
| "That's nice" | いい**わね**。 | いい**ね**。 |
| "You may go" | 行ってもいい**わよ**。 | 行ってもいい**よ**。 |
| "Dutch treat" | 割り勘**よ**。 | 割り勘**だよ**。 |
| "Let's splurge" | ぜいたくしましょうよ。 | ぜいたくしようよ。 (identical) |

The pattern: women's informal speech tends to drop the copula だ before sentence-final particles
and favors わ/わよ/ね, while men's speech retains だ and favors よ/だね. (p. 25)

### 5. Contraction: ~ておく → ~とく
やめとく is a contracted form of やめておく. V-ておく indicates an action done for future use/with
future consequences in mind: 書いておく→書いとく、読んでおく→読んどく。(p. 25)

### 6. V-て あげる／くれる／もらう
These verbs express giving/receiving a favor. あげる or くれる is chosen when the subject is the
one doing the favor; もらう when the subject is the one receiving it. (p. 25)
