## Grammar points

### *...koto-ni suru* vs. *...koto-ni naru*: individual will vs. impersonal/collective decision
*...koto-ni suru* marks an intentional decision attributed to the individual speaker; *...koto-ni naru*
marks the same kind of decision framed as impersonal, made by circumstance, authority, or collective
will rather than by a single person alone. This isn't just a grammatical nuance — shopkeepers and
newly-engaged couples both conventionally choose the *...naru* framing in public-facing announcements
specifically to avoid sounding unilateral/self-asserting (pp.80-81).

### The past tense as a memory/confirmation marker, not just "past time"
Japanese frequently uses the past tense (-ta/-mashita form, sometimes intensified with suffix *-kke*)
to confirm or double-check information already acquired, regardless of whether the referenced fact is
itself temporally past — confirming someone's current age, a currently-true physical trait, or even
today's day of the week can all take the past form (pp.82-83).

### Honorific *o-* is multifunctional, not just "politeness"
The prefix *o-* serves at least three distinct functions — showing respect to the referent, marking
something as belonging to the addressee/a third party, and generally softening/elevating tone even
for referents that need no respect — and is subject to real restrictions: never on a negatively-viewed
referent, and (for pragmatic/assimilation reasons, not disrespect) not usually on recent loanwords
(pp.84-85).

### Mimetic (manner-describing) words are vivid but minimal-pair-fragile
Mimetic/mimesis words directly depict manner or state and are highly effective when pronounced
correctly, but because many near-minimal-pairs exist that differ only in syllable clarity or a single
vowel, mispronouncing the 2nd/4th syllable can flip the meaning entirely (pp.86-87).

### Quoted/embedded clauses stay in plain form even inside polite sentences
Natural Japanese keeps an embedded quoted clause (what someone said, or what the speaker thinks) in
plain form, with politeness marking carried only by the outer/matrix verb — matching the quote's own
register to the sentence's overall politeness level (e.g. *Okada-san-ga kimasu-to iimashita*) is
grammatically valid but reads as a sign of inadequate fluency rather than correct formal style
(pp.100-101).
