# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Tanoshiku Yomou II (楽しく読もう II), Units 18–26

**Source:** 文化外国語専門学校編 (Bunka Institute of Language, ed.), *楽しく読もう II — 文化初級日本語読解教材*
(mid-1990s companion volume to *Tanoshiku Yomou I*; see `086_tanoshiku_yomou.md` for full series
bibliographic detail, pedagogical framing, and vision-reading method — both volumes are the same
pure-image scan with no text layer, and the same representative-sampling approach was used). This
volume is keyed to *文化初級日本語* chapters 19–36 and contains 17 units (numbered 18–34 to continue
Book I's numbering), pp. 1–84. This file covers Units 18–26; `086d_tanoshiku_yomou.md` covers Units
27–34 and the series-wide grammar/register notes drawn from the companion teacher's manual.

**New in this volume — a teacher's manual with named grammar points and one explicit register tag.**
Unlike Book I's answer-key booklet (bare answers only), Book II's companion *解答・教師用マニュアル*
(Answer Key + Teacher's Manual) includes, per unit, a stated pedagogical goal (ねらい/目標), the main
skill practiced, and usage notes — and for several units it names the **specific grammar pattern**
the unit is built to reinforce (volitional form, 〜たほうがいい/〜なくてもいい, potential form,
giving/receiving verbs, transitive/intransitive pairs, passive form) and, once, an **explicit
register label**: Unit 23 (the manga unit)'s stated aim is "マンガに触れ、友達言葉に慣れる" — "to be
exposed to manga and get used to **友達言葉** (friend-speech / casual speech used among friends)."
That phrase is itself a source-marked colloquial/informal-register tag and is captured below. These
teacher's-manual notes are genuine content (not bare answers), consistent with this project's prior
finding that answer-key booklets sometimes carry real pedagogical explanation — paraphrased here, not
quoted at length. Each unit's genre label (as classified by the manual itself) is also noted.

## Grammar points

### 自動詞・他動詞 (Intransitive/transitive verb pairs) — Unit 26

The passage frames this as a cautionary tale rather than a paradigm table: a foreign student
(ヤーナさん) picks up a valuable plate at her guarantor's house "just to look at it" and drops it,
saying "お皿を落ちた" (using the intransitive 落ちる, "to fall," where the transitive 落とす, "to
drop," was called for) — the narrative's point being that the wrong self/other-verb choice makes the
apology sound like the plate fell on its own rather than that she dropped it. Three parts of this
unit (per the teacher's manual) sequentially: (1) read a cautionary narrative illustrating the
real-world stakes of the distinction, (2) infer self/other-verb status of unfamiliar verbs from
context and dictionary form alone, (3) spot an error in dialogue where the wrong member of a pair was
used. This is a more applied, narrative-embedded treatment of the self/other-verb distinction than a
typical grammar-reference's paradigm table, and is worth cross-referencing against that project's
existing self/other-verb paradigm coverage (e.g. in the Bunka Nihongo or Minna no Nihongo-family
extractions) as a real-world usage illustration rather than a duplicate rule statement.

### 〜たほうがいい / 〜なくてもいい (Unit 20) and 可能形 (Unit 21) and やりもらい (Units 24–25)

Named explicitly by the teacher's manual as each unit's grammar focus but not given worked paradigm
tables in the manual itself (both already have standard reference coverage elsewhere in this
project's established/ files) — logged here only to record *which* already-covered grammar point this
reading-workbook unit was designed to reinforce, per the "non-redundant supplement" dispatch pattern:
no new grammar content to add beyond what Bunka Nihongo/other core-textbook extractions already
capture, so these are named but not re-tabulated.
