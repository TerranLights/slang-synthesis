## Grammar points

### Idiomatic expressions as departures from compositional/literal grammar

The source's own framing for Part 2 (its introduction, p.60) is that these expressions "cannot be
understood literally or in a grammatically orthodox manner" — they must be learned as fixed units
whose surface grammar under-determines their actual meaning. This section bears that out
repeatedly: constructions built from ordinary particles/verb forms (ては, が早いか, ～なり, の in its
various senses) carry meanings a compositional reading would not predict, which is why the author
devotes most of each entry to pinning down functional nuance rather than literal translation.

### A recurring organizing method: near-synonym minimal pairs

As in Part 1, the great majority of entries here are explicitly presented as contrastive pairs or
small families rather than isolated idioms: ～てvs～ては (single vs. repeated occurrence); ますます
vs どんどん (quality-trajectory vs. manner-of-action); かねない vs おそれがある
(colloquial/neutral vs. formal); Xに入って／Xになって vs Xにあって (change-implying vs.
no-change-implying); ～と思ったら vs かと思った (perception-focused vs. outcome-focused); and
～次第 vs ～たら、すぐ (formal/written vs. casual-conversational register for the same "immediately
upon" meaning). This is the book's consistent methodological throughline across both parts.

### Register/formality annotations explicitly marked by the source

Several entries in this range carry an explicit register flag rather than being presented as
register-neutral: なまじっか is marked more colloquial than なまじ (p.61); 少なくても more
colloquial than 少なくとも (p.67); いくらなんだって/なんぼなんだって usable in conversation but not
serious writing (p.68–69); ～次第 formal/written vs. ～たら、すぐ casual (p.83); and ～きり more
colloquial than ～たなり in its "condition persists unchanged" sense (p.86). おそれがある is
explicitly flagged as more formal than its near-synonym かねない (p.76–77), and Xにあって reads more
literary/written than the otherwise-similar Xに入って／Xになって (p.80–81).

### Multi-sense entries requiring sub-sense tables

Three headwords in this range (が／は, の, ～なり) are substantial enough that the author organizes
them into internally numbered sub-senses (2, 5, and 5 respectively) rather than treating them as
single short idioms — a structural difference from most of Part 1's entries, reflecting how much
functional load these very common particles/suffixes carry. The spec's single-row-per-headword
format is preserved here by summarizing each numbered sub-sense within the Notes/Gloss fields rather
than splitting into separate rows, since the sub-senses share one headword and one surrounding
discussion in the source.
