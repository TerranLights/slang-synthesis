## Grammar points

### made (まで) — "until/to" limit particle
Paraphrased from pp.83–88. The book stresses distinguishing "made" from "made-ni". Covers temporal limits ("I work from nine to five"), spatial limits ("commute to school by bicycle"), quantity/range limits ("keep the budget within one million yen"), the extent a situation has reached ("eat even the last grain of rice"), an extreme-example-applies sense ("if even your friends desert you, that's the end"), and a by-chance/no-special-reason sense ("I got up early merely because I happened to be woken by birdsong").

### made-mo (までも) and made-ni (までに) — combination particles built on "made"
Paraphrased from pp.88–90. "Made-mo" marks something as unnecessary/obvious given a later negation ("it's not necessary to say it at this point"), or concedes a lesser necessity when an extreme case doesn't hold ("even though you might not remember clearly, can't you recall just a little?"). "Made-ni" has a self-humbling nuance when describing one's own modest action ("we're here just to introduce ourselves"), marks the extent something has reached ("her fame reached the point where her name was known worldwide"), and sets a deadline ("before the plane leaves").

### mo (も) — the core additive/inclusive particle
Paraphrased from pp.91–98, one of the dictionary's largest entries. Replaces "wa"/"ga" and precedes "o" wherever it applies. Covers same-kind additive marking ("I'm a student, too"), exhaustive listing ("both wine and beer"), the "doesn't matter" concessive pattern ("it doesn't matter whether you write in Japanese or English"), addition to an existing set, emphasis of quantity/distance/frequency ("I haven't been back to Japan for as long as ten years"), rough approximation ("around three million yen"), total negation after an interrogative or "once" ("I've never skied, not even once"), an unexpected-additional-condition "even" sense, and a colloquial intensifying use in "koo mo/soo mo/aa mo" ("I'm at a loss if you cry that much").

### mono (もの) — colloquial reason-giving sentence-final/conjunctive particle
Paraphrased from p.98 (entry continues into part 6). Sentence-finally gives a reason for a preceding statement, often paired with "ne"/"na" for a light exclamatory effect ("it's because your wife is a pianist too"), or — more colloquially — voices dissatisfaction or desire after stating a reason ("because I really want it..."). As a conjunctive particle it turns a clause into the reason for what follows ("since I'm now of age, it's all right for me to drink a little, isn't it?").
