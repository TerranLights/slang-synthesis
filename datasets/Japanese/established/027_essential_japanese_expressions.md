  JLPT grammar-function-word list for Levels 2 and 1 (N2/N1), with every pattern individually

  largely N4–N3-level material (potential forms, から/ので, はずです, ようです, etc.).

Both books are therefore extracted, as genuinely distinct sources, rather than treating one as a
duplicate of the other.

**Coverage note — representative sampling, not exhaustive, given scale.** At 238 vision-only pages

## Grammar points

### Progressive/recent-past aspect pair: 〜ているところです vs. 〜たばかりです (p.20)

The book treats these as a minimal pair distinguishing two "just happened" readings. 〜ているところです
marks being in the middle of an ongoing, volitional action at a specific point in time (the book's
own numbered diagram shows points ①② as "just before," ③④ as "during," ⑤⑥ as "just after" an
action) and is explicitly *not* used for stative situations or unintentional acts — the book flags
×今日本に住んでいるところです and ×彼は今せきをしているところです as ungrammatical for exactly this reason.
〜たばかりです instead emphasizes that little time has passed since an action *completed*, with (per
the book's own comparison note) a wider time-margin than たところです, and commonly appears in
causal/concessive frames: Vたばかりなので…／Vたばかりなのに….

### Potential vs. spontaneous-perception verb pairs (p.60, chapter "可能・難易")

This chapter is built around minimal pairs that look similar in English but diverge in Japanese:
ability-potential verbs (見られる, 聞ける) describe what an agent *can* do, while spontaneous-
perception verbs (見える, 聞こえる) describe what simply *is or isn't* perceivable regardless of
agent effort (e.g. an obstructed view makes something 見えない, not 見られない in the sense of an
inability). The chapter also drills a transitive/intransitive-potential distinction (入る → 入れる
potential vs. 入れる as the plain transitive "put in" verb) and the native potential-suffix pattern
〜やすい／〜にくい built directly onto a verb's ren'yōkei stem.

### Reason-marking register: から vs. ので (p.40, chapter "I 原因・理由")

The book pairs the four reason-markers から・て/で/なくて・ので・し・ため(に) (its own chapter-opening
box lists them in two columns) and flags a specific pragmatic trap: から reads as too blunt/forceful
to open a request or a refusal, where ので is preferred — illustrated with a starred bad example
(△辞書を忘れたから、ちょっと見せてくださいませんか) next to the book's preferred correction using んです
が instead of から.

### Epistemic/evidential modal pair: 〜はずです vs. 〜ようです (p.150)

はずです marks a confident inference the speaker treats as logically following from known facts
("of course it's expensive, so of course it tastes good"), while ようです marks an inference drawn
from direct sensory or situational evidence in the moment (a smell, someone's visible demeanor) and
can bleach into a near-formulaic closing use ("it looks like that's our time," i.e. "we're out of
time"). The book's own example set for ようです deliberately spans this range from concrete sensory
evidence to the bleached formulaic closing use, rather than presenting a single register.

### Causative passive V-させられます (p.190 — partially unreadable)

Chapter 190's running header identifies this as the causative-passive conjugation ("Be made to
(verb)"), with what appears to be a Group I/II/III(irregular) conjugation table, but the page body
itself was a mirrored/garbled scan on this specific page and could not be confidently transcribed —
see the vision-reading guard note above. Future extraction of this book's causative-passive chapter
