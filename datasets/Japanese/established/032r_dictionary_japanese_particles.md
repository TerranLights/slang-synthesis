# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: A Dictionary of Japanese Particles (particle dictionary, part 18: YA-INA-YA–ZO)

**Source:** Sue A. Kawashima, *A Dictionary of Japanese Particles* (Kodansha International, 1999),
dictionary entries YA-INA-YA, YARA, YO, YORI, ZE, ZO, pp. 247–258 (printed pagination).

**Coverage note / vision-reading note:** see part 1 for methodology. No marginalia found in this
range. **Notable finds:** "ZE (ぜ)" is explicitly described as "found only in casual conversation
among men, used only among colleagues or with those whose supposed social status is below that of
the speaker" — one of the richest social-register annotations in the whole dictionary. "ZO (ぞ)" is
likewise explicitly marked "found in men's language."

## Grammar points

### ya-ina-ya (やいなや) and yara (やら) — immediate-sequence and uncertainty particles
Paraphrased from pp.247–250. "Ya-ina-ya" is the full form behind the "ya" contraction covered in part 17, marking strict immediate sequence ("as soon as he got on the horse, he galloped away"). "Yara" clusters around uncertainty: emphasizing doubt after an interrogative, vaguely referencing something/someone ("someone named Takami or something"), expressing indecision between negated alternatives, non-exhaustively listing items, and — sentence-finally — voicing a casual, musing "I wonder...".

### yo (よ) — the core sentence-final exclamatory particle
Paraphrased from pp.251–253. Transforms statements into exclamations, adds criticism after an interrogative ("what's with your attire?"), emphasizes commands and suggestions, expresses emotional states, forms exclamatory address, and serves as a breath-catching/attention-getting filler at clause breaks.

### yori (より) — the core comparison particle
Paraphrased from pp.253–257. The default "than" comparison extends to boundary-marking (time/distance/position), the "yori hoka" + negation "no choice but to..." pattern, a formal/written "from" sense (where colloquial speech substitutes "kara"), a superlative sense paired with interrogative pronouns ("there's no better place than our home"), an evidential "on the basis of" sense, and the "to yuu yori" pattern for "more of a...than..." characterizations.

### ze (ぜ) and zo (ぞ) — the book's most explicitly register-restricted particles
Paraphrased from pp.257–258. "Ze" carries the dictionary's most specific social-register annotation: restricted to casual men's speech among colleagues or addressed to those of lower perceived social status, used to elicit agreement or emphasize the speaker's resolve ("this time, I'm really going to quit smoking!"). "Zo" is likewise explicitly marked men's language, emphatically asserting an opinion or judgment ("I'm taller than you!").
