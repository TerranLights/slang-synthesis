## Grammar points

### Place indicators: koko, soko, asoko, doko
A four-way demonstrative system parallel to English "here/there/over there/where": **koko** (this
place, near speaker), **soko** (that place, near listener), **asoko** (that place, distant from
both), **doko** (where?, interrogative). Each has a politer counterpart used in service contexts
(shops, hotels, restaurants): kochira, sochira, achira, dochira. p. 35-36.

### doko question structure
"Where is X?" uses X wa doko desu ka — same X wa Y desu topic-comment template as earlier units,
with doko filling the Y slot. p. 36.

### O'clock (-ji) and minutes (-pun/-fun)
Hours attach the counter **-ji**; three hours have irregular readings diverging from the plain
numeral series (4 o'clock = yo-ji not yon-ji; 7 o'clock = shichi-ji not nana-ji; 9 o'clock = ku-ji
not kyū-ji) — distinct irregularities from the day-of-month irregularities in Unit 2, showing the
same numeral root can drift differently across different counter domains. Minutes attach **-fun**
(after numbers ending in 2, 5, 7, 9) or **-pun** (otherwise), with some combinations triggering
consonant gemination (ippun "1 min", roppun "6 min", happun "8 min", juppun "10 min"). p. 39.

### gozen/gogo and han
a.m./p.m. markers **gozen**/**gogo** precede the time expression (gozen ni-ji "2:00 a.m."). "Half
past" can use either sanjup-pun "30 minutes" or the simpler **han** "half" (roku-ji han = roku-ji
sanjup-pun). p. 39.

### nan-ji / nan-pun and mae "to (the hour)"
"What time?" is nan-ji; the X wa ima Y desu template is used, with X wa commonly dropped when asking
the local time. An additional construction uses **mae** "before" to express minutes-to-the-hour
(ku-ji go-fun mae "5 minutes to 9"). p. 39.

### kara and made: from and until
Postpositions **kara** "from" and **made** "until" attach after a time (or place) expression
(Ginkō wa gozen 10-ji kara gogo 4-ji made desu "The bank is open from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m."); repeated
words (desu, the subject) are dropped when combining two such clauses into one. The question form
substitutes nan-ji for the time value (Ginkō wa nan-ji kara desu ka "From what time is the bank
open?"). p. 41.
