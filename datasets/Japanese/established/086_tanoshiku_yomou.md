# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Tanoshiku Yomou I (楽しく読もう I), Units 1–8

**Source:** 文化外国語専門学校編 (Bunka Institute of Language, ed.), *楽しく読もう I — 文化初級日本語読解教材*
("Let's Read Happily / Enjoy Reading I — Bunka Elementary Japanese Reading-Comprehension Materials"),
Bunka Institute of Language. No visible copyright-page year in the scanned front matter; internal
evidence (a sample receipt dated 96.5.10 in Unit 2, pricing in ¥ only) places composition/publication
in the mid-1990s. Companion volume *Tanoshiku Yomou II* continues the same series (see files
086c/086d). A companion answer-key + teacher's-manual booklet (*解答・教師用マニュアル*) exists for
each volume; see 086d for what it contributes beyond bare answers.

**What this source is.** Not a grammar textbook — a **graded reading-comprehension workbook**,
explicitly keyed to the lesson numbering of a separate core textbook, *文化初級日本語* ("Bunka
Elementary Japanese"), chapters 1–18 (this volume) and 19–36 (Volume II). Each of its 17 units
pairs a short reading passage — built around real-world text genres (menus, receipts, ads, TV
listings, travel pamphlets, postcards, letters, an actor's biography, folktales, graphs/economic
commentary) — with a "新しいことば" (New Words) vocabulary box giving every word in the passage not
yet taught in the core textbook's own lesson sequence, each with an English gloss. A front-matter
note to learners and teachers (pp. 3–12, in Japanese, Thai, and Chinese translation) states the
book's three explicit pedagogical aims: (a) build **scanning/skimming** skill via authentic realia
texts, (b) build **intensive reading** (精読) skill via content-dense expository/narrative texts, and
(c) provide **reading for pleasure** (folktales, quizzes, manga). This matters for how to read the
vocabulary here: it is a controlled, level-graded word list layered onto authentic-genre texts, not
a frequency-ranked or slang-focused source — nearly everything below is `core` register.

**Coverage note.** Because this is a reading-skill workbook (not a grammar reference), it introduces
**no new grammar points of its own** — grammar is inherited wholesale from the companion *文化初級日本語*
textbook, already covered in this project's 068/069 Bunka Nihongo extractions. What *is* genuinely
new here: (1) every vocabulary item in each unit's New-Words box — this is the book's core content
and is captured in full below; (2) realia-genre word clusters (menu items, grocery/toiletry nouns,
receipt formatting, TV-listing conventions) not necessarily covered elsewhere; (3) the handful of
explicit usage/equivalence notes the book itself marks (e.g. `A = B` gloss equivalences, transitive/
intransitive verb pairs). No dialectal/regional annotation occurs anywhere in this source — it is
uniformly standard/core Japanese. Drill-exercise question text (comprehension questions, crossword
clues, matching exercises) is **not** separately extracted as vocabulary — only the New-Words boxes
and passage-embedded inline glosses (marked with a ☞ symbol in the source) are, per the coverage
rule's "skip repeated drills" guidance. This file (086) covers Units 1–8; 086b covers Units 9–17 and
closes out Book I's grammar/register notes; 086c–086d cover Book II (Units 18–34).

**Vision-reading note.** Both PDFs (*Tanoshiku Yomou I* and its *Kaitou*) have **no extractable text
layer at all** — `pdftotext`/`pdffonts` return empty/no-fonts output on every page; this is a
pure-image scan, not a font-substitution cipher. All content below was vision-read directly from
rendered page images. No handwritten marginalia was found on any page sampled (library-stamp labels
and a call-number sticker appear on the cover only, not inside the text). Given 77 pages (Book I)
across 17 short units, **representative sampling was used rather than frame-by-frame vision-reading
of every page**: for each unit, the unit-opening spread (which carries the New-Words box) was read
in full, and a sample of subsequent passage/exercise pages was spot-checked for inline glosses and
register-relevant content; repeated comprehension-question pages beyond that sample were not
individually transcribed, consistent with the coverage rule's drill-skipping guidance.

### Explicit synonym/equivalence notation (source-marked, cross-unit pattern)

Several units mark explicit lexical equivalences with an "=" sign directly in the New-Words box
(e.g. トイレ＝お手洗い, 大好き＝とても好き, 牛乳＝ミルク, 低カロリー＝カロリーが低い). This is the
book's own device for teaching that a formal/compound term and a plainer phrasal paraphrase denote
the same thing — not a dialectal or regional marking, but a genuine register/formality-adjacent
annotation worth flagging as a recurring structural feature of this source, distinct from its
ordinary one-line English glosses.
