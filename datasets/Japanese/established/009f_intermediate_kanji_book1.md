## Grammar points

### Same-kun-reading kanji split a broad native verb/adjective into precise distinctions — Lesson 9 §①
Pairs (or occasionally triples) of kanji sharing a kun-reading mark fine-grained distinctions a
single English gloss would obscure: あう splits 合う(to fit/match) from 会う(to meet a person);
いたむ splits 痛む(pain, physical/emotional) from 傷む(deterioration, of objects/food); うつ splits
打つ(strike/type/send) from 撃つ(to shoot a firearm); きく splits 効く(a medicine/method works) from
利く(a sense/limb functions well, or being sharp-witted); おくる splits 送る(to send) from 贈る(to
give as a gift); つく splits 付く(to adhere/accrue) from 着く(to arrive); なく splits 泣く(people
crying) from 鳴く(animals/insects making sound); ひく splits 引く(to pull/consult) from 弾く(to play
a stringed/keyboard instrument); あたたかい splits 暖かい(warm weather/room) from 温かい(warm
food/hospitality); はやい splits 早い(early, time) from 速い(fast, speed); やわらかい splits
柔らかい(physically supple) from 軟らかい(gentle in manner/topic); かた splits 形(shape) from
型(type/model). A parallel set of intransitive/transitive verb pairs is built the same way:
あらわれる/あらわす splits 現れる・現す(to appear/reveal a person or place) from 表れる・表す(to be
expressed/to express a feeling); かえる/かえす splits 帰る・帰す(to go/send home) from 返る・返す(to
be returned/to return an object); たつ/たてる splits 立つ・立てる(to stand/set upright) from
建つ・建てる(a building rises/to construct a building); なおる/なおす splits 直る・直す(mechanical
repair) from 治る・治す(medical healing); はなれる/はなす splits 離れる・離す(to be apart/separate)
from 放れる・放す(to break loose/release an animal); and まざる/まじる・まぜる splits 交ざる/交じる・
交ぜる(mixing where components stay discretely identifiable) from 混ざる/混じる・混ぜる(mixing into
a homogeneous blend). The book extends this to 3+-way splits for words with no single transitive
counterpart (あく/あける: 開く・開ける "to open" generally vs. 空く・空ける "to become vacant" vs.
明ける, intransitive-only, "night/year to end"; うつる/うつす: 移る・移す "to relocate" vs. 写る・写
す "to be photographed/copied" vs. 映る・映す "to be reflected/projected"; とまる/とめる: 止まる・
止める "to stop motion" vs. 泊まる・泊める "to lodge overnight" vs. 留まる・留める "to catch/fix
attention"), and to a four-kanji homophone cluster for もと with no verb/adjective at all (本=the
essential part, 元=origin/former state, 下=under someone's guidance, 基=basis/foundation).

### Pitch accent and okurigana as secondary disambiguators for same-kun-reading kanji — Lesson 9 §②
Beyond kanji choice, some same-kun-reading pairs are distinguished purely by pitch accent with
IDENTICAL kanji-reading and okurigana: 暑い/熱い vs. 厚い (あつい), 書く vs. 欠く (かく), 降る vs.
振る (ふる), 葉 vs. 歯 (は), 花 vと 鼻 (はな) — all same spelling, different accent only. Other pairs
are distinguished by differing okurigana rather than accent: 起こる vs. 怒る (おこる); the four-way
かえる family 変える/代える/換える/替える (change/substitute/exchange/replace) is contrasted with the
separate かえる pair 帰る/返る (go home/be returned); and 別れる vs. 分かれる (わかれる, people
parting vs. a road/opinion splitting).

### Same-kun-reading verbs can belong to different conjugation classes — Lesson 9 §③
A more subtle disambiguation axis: two verbs can share an identical dictionary form (辞書形) yet
belong to different conjugation classes, so their other forms (ない-form, ます-form, て-form) and/or
pitch accent diverge even though the plain form looks identical — 帰る(ichidan-looking but actually
godan) vs. 返る, and 変える/代える (both ichidan) show this via accent contrast; 切る(godan) vs. 着る
(ichidan); 減る(godan) vs. 経る(ichidan). Separately, pairs with genuinely DIFFERENT dictionary forms
can converge onto an IDENTICAL て-form, which is the practical source of ambiguity in casual speech:
打つ・売る→うって; 老いる・置く→おいて; 追う・折る→おって; 着る・来る→きて (near-identical); 飛ぶ・
富む→とんで; 読む・呼ぶ→よんで; 言う・行く→いって (the one pair the book flags as fully identical in
BOTH て-form reading AND pitch accent, unlike all the others in this set). A final class of
same-kun-reading verbs differs instead by the particle(s) they require: 聞く(〜ヲ"to listen to" vs.
〜ニ〜ヲ"to ask someone something") vs. 効く(〜ガ〜ニ); 住む(〜ガ〜ニ) vs. 済む(〜ガ); 努める/勤める/
務める(all 〜ガ〜ニ or 〜ヲ depending on the specific verb) distinguished by object type rather than
particle alone; 似る(〜ガ〜ニ／〜ト) vs. 煮る(〜ヲ); 話す(〜ヲ) vs. 離す(〜ト〜ヲ) vs. 放す(〜ヲ); 降る
(〜ガ) vs. 振る(〜ヲ); わかれる as 分かれる(〜ガ〜ニ) vs. 別れる(〜ガ〜ト).

### Combining two synonymous kun-readings produces a Sino-Japanese compound — Lesson 9 §④
Pairing the kanji for two near-synonymous native verbs/adjectives yields a two-kanji Sino-Japanese
compound whose meaning is the shared core sense of both components: あう+あう→会合(a meeting);
あらわす+あらわす→表現(expression); うつ+うつ→打撃(a blow/impact); うつす+うつす→映写(projection,
of film); おさめる+おさめる→収納(storage); かえる+かえる→変換(conversion); たてる+たてる→建立
(founding a temple); つく+つく→付着(adhesion); つとめる+つとめる→勤務(being on duty); のぼる+のぼ
る→上昇(ascent); はかる+はかる→計量/計測/測量(three distinct measurement-register compounds);
わける+わかれる(a transitive/intransitive pairing rather than two synonyms)→分別(sorting/
discrimination); あたたかい+あたたかい→温暖(mild climate); はやい+はやい→早速(promptly, an
adverb rather than a noun/suru-verb); かわ+かわ→皮革(leather material); もと+もと→基本
(fundamentals).
