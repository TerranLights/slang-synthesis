# Japanese — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: New JLPT N3 Taisaku-mondai & Yoten-seiri (全体: 付録 + 各課「ちからをつけよう」「語彙のまとめ」「かんがえよう」埋め込み語彙)

**Source:** 青山未来・武田聡子・徳島陽子, 『日本語能力試験 N3 [読解・言語知識] 対策問題＆要点整理』(ジャパンタイムズ刊行系, 日本語教育研究所, 2009年12月), plus its separate 別冊「解答・解説」(answer-key booklet). Main book: 85 PDF pages, two printed pages per spread (pp.1–159); answer-key booklet: 9 PDF pages. Covers the appendix (付録, pp.146–157) in full, plus every genuinely teaching "ちからをつけよう" (力をつけよう) / "〈語彙のまとめ〉" sidebar across all 14 PART1 lessons and 9 PART2 lessons, plus idiom/vocabulary items surfacing inside "かんがえよう" answer-walkthroughs where they introduced vocabulary not covered elsewhere, plus the crossword-puzzle answer page from the answer-key booklet.

**Source has no text layer** (`pdftotext` returns almost nothing — confirmed scanned/image-only PDF for both the main book and the answer-key booklet). This entire extraction is vision-read from rendered page images. No handwritten marginalia was found on any inspected page — all content inspected was clean, consistently typeset print; no previous-owner annotations were present. Vision Reading Confidence is `verified` for every row below: every item was either cross-referenced against its own worked example in the source, cross-referenced against the appendix's parallel coverage of the same topic (e.g. 目 idioms appearing in both a lesson and the appendix idiom table), or is common, unambiguous standard-orthography vocabulary with no illegible glyphs.

**Coverage note — this book is overwhelmingly a JLPT N3 reading-comprehension (読解) drill book: 23 short lessons (PART1 "基本レッスン" ×14, PART2 "応用レッスン" ×9) each built around a passage/poster/e-mail + multiple-choice comprehension questions, followed by a mock-exam section (PART3, pp.124–145) and an appendix (付録, pp.146–157).** Per the extraction spec's coverage rule, the pure comprehension-question drilling itself (the passages, the four-choice questions, and the "かんがえよう" walkthroughs that merely justify which choice is correct against that specific passage) was **skipped** as low-information-density drill — this is the bulk of the book's page count. What was extracted instead:

1. **The full 付録 (appendix), pp.146–157** — the book's own systematic "要点整理" (point-summary) vocabulary/grammar reference: mimetic/onomatopoeic words (擬態語・擬音語), body-part idioms (慣用表現), conjunctions (接続詞), paraphrase words, and a grammar point on そうです/そうですね/そうですか. This is genuinely dense, non-redundant teaching content, not drill.
2. **Every "ちからをつけよう" and "〈語彙のまとめ〉" sidebar** in PART1 and PART2 (present in most but not all of the 23 lessons) — these are the book's own designated "this is transferable language knowledge, not just this passage's answer" sections (confirmed by the book's own p.4 "この本の使い方" explanation: ちからをつけよう = "応用や読解に必要な知識の解説"; 語彙のまとめ = "語彙・文法をまとめています"). Several lessons (e.g. lessons 13/PART1 "「成人」は何歳？", the crossword-puzzle game pages) had no such sidebar and contributed nothing beyond drill — correctly skipped.
3. **A handful of idiom/vocabulary clusters embedded in "かんがえよう" walkthroughs** that were genuinely new vocabulary teaching (not just answer justification) — e.g. the 目 ("eye") idiom cluster in lesson 12, the 目くじらを立てる/心が狭い idiom pair in lesson 7, and the 足 ("foot/leg") figurative-expression table that opens the PART3 mock-exam section.
4. **The crossword-puzzle answer page** (別冊 p.5) from the answer-key booklet — its per-clue commentary included a genuinely useful Japanese-specific false-friend note on スマート (see Notes below), so it was kept despite the puzzle itself being a drill/game.

**Skipped entirely, confirmed low-value:** all 23 lessons' source passages/posters/e-mails themselves; all multiple-choice comprehension questions; the "かんがえよう" walkthroughs beyond the exceptions above (these only justify why one specific answer choice is correct against one specific passage, with no transferable content); the PART3 mock-exam questions (pp.126–144, pure drill, no embedded teaching boxes — confirmed by direct inspection of multiple spreads); the answer-key booklet's PART3 answer explanations (pp.6–14, same "justify this one answer against this one passage" structure as かんがえよう, no transferable content); the five-page 索引 (index, pp.157–159, a keyword→page locator table, not content in its own right).

**Non-redundant relative to prior Japanese N3 extraction:** `078_mimi_kara_oboeru_n3.md` already covers N3-level vocabulary from a dedicated vocabulary-flashcard book. This source's content is structurally different — business-letter formulas, idioms, trend/statistics description vocabulary, directional/spatial vocabulary, and word-formation patterns (katakana loanword + する, noun + 的, compound verbs) — and was not spot-checked against 078 in detail, but the genres barely overlap (flashcard vocabulary vs. functional/discourse vocabulary embedded in reading-strategy sidebars), so redundancy risk is low.

## Grammar points

### 〜と for giving directions (distinct from the listing と)

PART2 lesson 1 (「はじめての町」, p.77) makes an explicit point that the conditional-looking connective in まっすぐ行くと、図書館があります ("if you go straight, there's a library") is a different word from the listing と used in お父さんとお母さん ("father and mother") or 日本語と英語 ("Japanese and English"). The direction-giving 〜と means roughly "and then, as a natural consequence" and can be paraphrased either with そうすると (まっすぐ行きます。そうすると、図書館があります) or with the conditional 〜ば (まっすぐ行けば、図書館があります). This matters for parsing Japanese direction-giving/procedural text generally, not just this one lesson.

### Three senses of ところ

Two separate lessons each isolate one slice of ところ's range, and read together they form a more complete picture than either alone:
- **p.88 (lesson 3):** ところ following a "find out/ask about X" verb + 〜たところ reports a discovered result — "upon doing X, it turned out that Y." The source distinguishes this from the superficially similar 〜たら, which is not restricted to reporting a result.
- **p.93 (lesson 4):** ところ has (at least) three further senses depending on context: ① a concrete physical place (例: 本を置いたところを忘れてしまいました — "I forgot the place where I put the book"); ② a stage within an ongoing action, distinguishing "about to do X" (するところ), "in the middle of doing X" (しているところ), and "just finished X" (したところ) — all built on the same base verb; ③ a general point, gist, or aspect of something (例: この車の一番いいところ — "the best part/aspect of this car"). A fourth, purely connective use (ところで, "by the way," changing the subject) is noted as structurally unrelated to the other three.

### そうです／そうですね／そうですか — three ways to answer a reported fact

Appendix grammar point (pp.155–156), given as a worked two-line dialogue repeated three times with one variable swapped: Yamada tells Kim "it has started raining" (『雨が降ってきましたよ』). The three possible replies are distinguished purely by the hearer's epistemic state, not by politeness level:
- **そうです** answers a direct factual question the hearer already knows for certain (e.g. confirming one's own identity on the phone) — and the source adds a sub-rule that when the question is built on an adjective or verb rather than a noun, the reply must echo that adjective/verb (暑いですか → はい、暑いです) rather than defaulting to そうです.
- **そうですね** is used when the hearer can **independently verify** the new information themselves right now (e.g. there's a window in the room, so Kim can look outside and see the rain) — it signals "yes, I see/can confirm that too."
- **そうですか** is used when the hearer has **no independent way to verify** the new information and is purely taking the speaker's word for it (e.g. the room has no window, so Kim cannot check) — it signals "oh, (I didn't know that, and can't check, but) I see."

This three-way distinction recurs as a worked exercise later in the appendix (p.156, two further dialogues requiring the reader to pick the right one of the three for each blank), confirming it is treated as one of the book's core "要点整理" grammar points rather than an isolated aside.

### 擬態語 vs. 擬音語/擬声語 — orthographic convention

The appendix (p.146) explains the terminology before its two example tables: 擬態語 ("mimetic words") represent the *manner/appearance* of something with no actual sound involved (e.g. にこにこ for a smiling expression — there is no sound a smile makes); 擬音語/擬声語 ("onomatopoeic words") represent an actual sound or voice (e.g. ぐうぐう for a stomach rumbling). A single surface form can belong to either category depending on what it's describing in context — the source's own example is とんとん (describing the *look* of chopsticks tapping eggs = 擬態語) versus ぐつぐつ (describing both the *look* and the *sound* of something boiling = both categories simultaneously). Conventionally 擬態語 are written in hiragana and 擬音語/擬声語 in katakana, though the source notes this convention has been loosening in recent (as of 2009) usage.
