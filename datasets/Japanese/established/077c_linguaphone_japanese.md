## Grammar points

### -nikui as a productive adjective-forming suffix ("hard/difficult to...")

minikui ("hard to see," Lesson 48/2) is glossed as mi- (stem of miru, "to see") + -nikui ("hard,
difficult, awkward"). This is a recognizably productive derivational pattern (attach -nikui to a
verb's conjunctive/i-stem to mean "difficult to [verb]"), distinct from the -te iru and resultative
-te aru patterns already catalogued in 077b — worth comparing against any -yasui ("easy to...")
counterpart if found in later lessons.

### Specialized-register vocabulary clusters appear once a lesson's topic narrows

Lessons 27–28 (postal/telegraph) and 47–48 (medical visit / broadcast media, sampled from p.102–103
and the facing p.182-183 grammar notes) each introduce a dense, topic-specific vocabulary cluster
(postal: kōkūbin, kakitome, kozutsumi, kyokuin, kōshū-denwa; broadcast: chūkei suru, hōsō suru,
jinkō-eisei, karā-terebi, channeru) that does not recur in the foundational Lessons 1–11 vocabulary.
This confirms the course's structure: an initial ~10–15 lessons build core grammar/particles with
domestic-life vocabulary, after which later lessons hold grammar roughly constant while cycling
through successive real-world topic domains (postal services, telephone, medicine, broadcasting) —
consistent with Linguaphone's stated "descriptive monologue... alternating with conversations" design
(handbook p. 16, cited in 077's front matter).
