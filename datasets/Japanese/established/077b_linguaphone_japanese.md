## Grammar points

### The resultative construction -te aru (state resulting from a prior action)

Recurs repeatedly across Lessons 6–7's vocabulary (uete arimasu "have been planted," oite atte "have
been put," ikete arimasu "have been arranged," tsukutte arimasu "has been made," nani mo oite
arimasen "nothing is put"). Each pairs a transitive verb's -te form with aru/arimasu to describe the
resulting state of a deliberate action, distinct from a simple passive — paraphrasing the pattern
across these near-identical five sentences (not individually glossed by the source as a named rule,
but the repetition across consecutive lessons makes the pattern unmistakable) (pp. 36–38).

### Register-paired vocabulary for the same referent (guest/visitor)

o-kyaku-san (Lesson 7) and o-kyaku-sama (Lesson 11) are both glossed as "guest," with the source
explicitly noting -sama is "more formal than o-kyaku-san" (p. 41) — a direct textual confirmation
that Japanese honorific suffixes form a graded register scale (-san < -sama) rather than a binary
polite/plain switch, extending the register-marking pattern already noted in 077 from prefixes to
suffixes.

### Native vs. Sino-Japanese numeral series completed through "ten"

Lesson 6 completes the native counting series begun in Lesson 5 (hitotsu, futatsu, mittsu, yottsu,
itsutsu, muttsu, nanatsu, yattsu, kokonotsu, tō) and flags that tō — "ten" — is irregular, not the
expected **tōtsu** (p. 34). This is the first full native-series paradigm captured in this source and
worth comparing against the Sino-Japanese series (ichi, ni, san...jū) tabulated piecemeal across
earlier lessons.

### mo as an emphatic/concessive particle after comparatives

yori mo ("than, even") in Lesson 9 is glossed with mo explicitly described as "emphasizes preceding
yori (than), even" (p. 38) — showing mo's function shifting from its more basic "also, too" sense
(seen in Lesson 1's watashi mo) to an intensifying role when it follows a comparative particle,
reinforcing a general theme that mo's meaning is heavily context-dependent on what it follows.
