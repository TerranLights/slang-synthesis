## Grammar points (continued)

### Group XI — Particles that Indicate a List of Objects, Qualities, or Actions (pp. 107–115)
Six particles/combinations: や…や, とか…とか, だの…だの, など, なんか, に. The author explicitly
cross-references Group III as covering some of the same particles "from a different perspective"
(p. 107) — a direct authorial signal that this group and III-2–III-8 overlap in territory but the
discrimination criteria differ. Register/connotation cline:
- や…や is the standard neutral partial-list marker for nouns only.
- とか…とか is more colloquial, gives concrete examples of a larger category, and can follow nouns,
  adjectives, and verbs (not just nouns) — among younger speakers it is often used for a *single*
  example rather than a list, interchangeable in that use with なんか (p. 108-109).
- だの…だの is similar in form to とか…とか/や…や but specifically carries a negative/annoyed
  connotation the other two lack (p. 109).
- など and なんか share two functions: marking a partial list (often after や…や or とか…とか to
  reinforce partiality) and marking a single word as "for example"; なんか is simply a less formal
  variant of など (p. 110-111).
- に marks a genuinely complete list (more structured/polite than や…や, とか…とか, だの…だの) unless
  followed by など, which then signals partiality (p. 112).

### Group XII — Particles that Indicate an Amount or Quantity (pp. 117–123)
Seven particles: くらい/ぐらい, ほど, ばかり/ばかし, ずつ, とも, だけ, も. くらい/ぐらい has the
widest range of use of the group and tends toward the *minimum* of an approximate amount; ほど is
more polite/written and tends toward the *maximum*; ばかり/ばかし focuses specifically on
*smallness* of amount and is not used with large figures (ばかし being casual, ばかり slightly
old-fashioned) (pp. 117-119). ずつ divides an amount equally; とも includes all specified items
("both"/"none" with negative verb); だけ sets an amount as an upper limit (usually but not
necessarily small); も (often with ば) indicates a ceiling or sufficient amount and is
interchangeable with くらい/ほど/ばかり/だけ, each carrying its own nuance (pp. 120-121).

### Group XIII — The Particle の (No) Indicates that the Noun Preceding It Modifies the Noun Following It (pp. 127–129)
A single-particle group (の only) — indicates that the preceding noun/person belongs to the group
or organization named by the following noun (e.g., あの大学の学生, "students belonging to that
university") (p. 128).

### Group XIV — Sentence-ending Particles Indicating What the Speaker Has Heard (pp. 131–133)
Two particles: って, だと. Both are everyday-conversational hearsay markers ("I hear," "they say"),
with そうです as their more formal equivalent (the author gives 中田さんは来るそうです as the formal
counterpart, p. 131). って is more typical of women and can be turned into a question with rising
intonation, or made more polite after ます/です forms (ますって, んですって, p. 132). だと is rougher
and more typically used by men, less common than って, and requires ん before a verb (食べるんだと)
(p. 132-133).

### Group XV — Particles that Indicate Emphasis (pp. 139–149)
Twelve particles: も, こそ, さえ, すら, ものなら, くせに, どころか, ものを, ぞ, ってば, ものか, ほど.
Key discriminations:
- **さえ vs. すら**: both mean "even," marking an extreme-position item, but さえ is more casual/
  everyday and (uniquely) can combine with a ば/たら-ending clause to mean "if only," while すら is
  more formal, doesn't pattern with ば/たら, and is often followed by a negative verb (pp. 140-141).
- **くせに**: similar to のに but far stronger in its condemnatory force, typically used when someone
  acts beyond their proper ability or station (p. 143).
- **ぞ, ってば, ものか/もんか**: all three are forceful, typically masculine or emphatic
  sentence-enders — ぞ warns of danger/self-encourages; ってば expresses irritation at
  non-compliance; ものか/もんか asserts a firm refusal ("I'll never...again") (pp. 145).
- **ほど (XV-12)** here marks an extreme *degree* ("so...that"), a third distinct sense from its
  amount-approximation use (XII-2) and its negative-comparison use (XVI-3) — the same particle form
  recurs across three of this book's nineteen groups with three genuinely distinct functions, worth
  flagging as a single surface-form/multiple-function case this comparative structure makes visible.

### Group XVI — Particles Used for Comparison (pp. 151–155)
Four particles: と, より, ほど, で. と lists items for the listener to choose among ("or"), and
answers conventionally add の方 ("[the one] on this side") after the chosen item — の方 works
regardless of how many items are being compared, contrasting with より, which is restricted to
exactly two items (p. 151). より marks the *lesser* of two compared items ("more than"); ほど marks
the *greater* item in a negative comparison ("not as...as," verb negated) — note the directionality
contrast: より follows the lesser item, ほど follows the greater (pp. 152-153). で marks the
scope/category within which three or more items are compared, contrasting with と's explicit
naming of the compared items (p. 154); the author notes that when 3+ items are compared, the final
item is often followed by の中で ("within"), itself built on this で (p. 152).

### Group XVII — Particles that Indicate a Means by which Something Is Done or Material from which Something Is Made (pp. 157–159)
Two particles: で, で/から. で alone marks the means/instrument of an action ("by," "with"). で and
から both mark material-of-origin ("from," "out of"), but で is preferred when the material is
visually self-evident from the finished object (wood, paper, leather, cloth, glass), per the
author's explicit usage note (p. 158).

### Group XVIII — Particles that Indicate a Purpose or Object of a Verb (pp. 161–163)
Two particles: に, を. に marks the purpose/goal of an action, attaching to a verb's ます-stem and
followed by a second verb of motion (食べに行く, "to go to eat"); を marks the ordinary direct
object of a transitive verb, and the two combine directly (イタリア料理を食べに行く, "to go eat
Italian food") (pp. 161-162).

### Group XIX — Particles that Come at the End of a Sentence and Indicate the Speaker's Feelings or Dictate the Tone of a Sentence (pp. 167–177)
Fourteen particles/combinations: か, さ, じゃん, けど, な/なあ, もの, の, ね, や, よ, わ, やら, が,
から/ので — by far the book's largest group, covering emotive/pragmatic sentence-final particles.
Major organizing axes the author draws out explicitly:
- **Gender register**: な/なあ, や, さ, ぞ, だと (XIV-2) skew masculine; もの, の, わ skew feminine; か,
  けど, ね, よ, が, から/ので are used by both genders (pp. 167-177, per-entry).
- **Agreement-seeking cluster**: ね (neutral, both genders) vs. よね (masculine-leaning) vs. わね
  (feminine-leaning) — same core "seeking agreement" function, split by the intensifying particle
  attached (p. 172).
- **Assertion-forcing cluster**: よ (forceful, both genders) vs. だよ (masculine) vs. のよ/わよ
  (feminine) — parallel structure to the ね cluster but for *pressing one's own view* rather than
  seeking agreement; the author explicitly contrasts よ with じゃん, noting じゃん's pressure on the
  listener is weaker than よ's (p. 168).
- **Unstated-reason-leaving cluster**: けど, が (XIX-13), から/ので (XIX-14) all leave an opposing
  view or reason implicit rather than stated outright, used as an indirect, polite way to express
  disagreement or decline a request; the author explicitly calls けど and が "quite similar," with が
  "somewhat more muffled in impact" (p. 169, p. 175-176).
- **もの/もん**: a self-justifying "reason" marker, explicitly contrasted by the author with
  から/ので (XIX-14) as "much softer and more feminine, much less direct, more of an appeal for
  leniency or indulgence" — もん (casual form) is used by both genders/all ages even though もの
  itself is principally feminine (p. 176... continued from p. 170).
