## Grammar points

### 1. Permission
~て(も) いいです／かまいません means "You may.../It is alright...". よろしいです substitutes for いいです
in formal situations. Answers to permission questions are explicitly gendered in the text:
うん、いいわよ。(女) vs. うん、いいよ。(男) — "Yeah, you can." (p. 40)

### 2. Prohibition
~ては いけません／だめです means "You must not...". てはcommonly contracts to ちゃ (では→じゃ) in
conversational Japanese: 本を見てはいけませんよ→見ちゃだめですよ. The prohibition examples are likewise
explicitly gender-tagged: たばこを吸っちゃだめだよ。(男) "You must not smoke" vs. 食べすぎちゃだめよ。(女)
"You must not overeat." (p. 40–41)

### 3. Advice
Verb-たほうがいいです ("should do.../had better do...") vs. Verb-ないほうがいいです ("shouldn't
do.../had better not do..."). Note the past-tense verb is used even for the affirmative advice
form: 少しやせたほうがいいですよ「You'd better lose a little weight.」(p. 41)

### 4. ~まで "until"
The plain present form precedes まで: 30になるまで、結婚しないつもりです「I intend not to marry until
I am 30.」(p. 41)

### 5. ~な（あ） — exclamatory sentence endings, explicitly gendered
A second explicit men/women register table, this time for exclamatory/wondering endings:

| Meaning | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| "You are stingy!" | けちだなあ。 | けちねえ。 |
| "How nice!" | いいなあ。 | いいわねえ。 |
| "How marvelous!" | すてきだなあ。 | すてきねえ。 |
| "I wonder if it'll rain" | 雨が降るかな。 | 雨が降るかしら。 |
| "I wonder if it's a holiday" | 休みかな。 | 休みかしら。 |

The men's forms keep だ/かな; the women's forms drop だ and substitute ねえ/かしら. (p. 42)

### 6. The Passive
-う verbs: -u → -areru (-wareru after a two-vowel-ending stem, e.g. 誘う→誘われる); -る verbs:
-ru → -rareru; irregulars する→される, 来る→来られる. Used to describe an event from the viewpoint of
the person acted upon. Three active→passive conversion patterns are given:
(A) XXがYYを...Verb → YYがXXに...Passive (先生が田中さんを呼ぶ。→ 田中さんが先生に呼ばれる。);
(B) XXがYYに...Verb → YYがXXに...Passive (子供がスミスさんに道をきいた。→ スミスさんは子供に道をきかれた。);
(C) XXがYYのZZを...Verb → YYがXXにZZを...Passive (先生がスミスさんの名前を呼んだ。→ スミスさんは先生に
名前を呼ばれた。— the possessed object ZZ keeps を, while the possessor YY becomes the passive
subject). (p. 42–43)

### 7. The Adversative Passive (めいわくの受身)
The passive form can also express that the subject is adversely affected by another's action —
a classic Japanese-specific "suffering passive" construction with no direct equivalent in English
passive voice, used even with intransitive verbs when someone is inconvenienced by the event.
(p. 43, continued from Note 6)
