## Grammar points

### to-wa (とは) conclusion, to-wa-ie (とはいえ) — exclamatory and adversative combination particles
Paraphrased from p.226. "To-wa" concludes with a sentence-final exclamatory sense expressing surprise, anger, or strong emotion ("to think that he didn't pass, after studying so hard!"). "To-wa-ie" is a standard adversative ("even though it's the rainy season, there have been many clear days this year"), interchangeable with "to wa ii nagara"/"to wa yuu mono no".

### to-yuu (という) and its further derivatives — the "yuu"-based quotative/nominalizer family
Paraphrased from pp.227–231. "To-yuu" is a workhorse combination particle: introducing proper nouns ("the mountain called Fuji-san"), marking a quantity has reached a stated total ("numbering fifty thousand"), nominalizing a clause into a noun-modifying "something that..." phrase, reporting hearsay, and distributing over repeated nouns ("every single car"). Three further combination particles build on it: "to-yuu-koto-wa" draws an inferential conclusion ("that means he probably hasn't come home yet"); "to-yuu-no-wa/-no-mo" supplies a reason/cause ("the reason for that is..."); and "to-yuu-to" expresses either a habitual correlation ("whenever X, Y always follows") or, sentence-initially, a natural-conclusion lead-in ("if we say that, then...").

### tte (って) — the colloquial catch-all built on "to"/"to yuu"/"tatte"
Paraphrased from pp.232–234. Functions as a casual quotation marker, an informal "to yuu" substitute, a casual topic-introducer, an interrogative-repetition/confirmation device, a hearsay marker, and (via "datte") a reported-speech particle, plus a casual hypothetical variant of "totte"/"tatte" ("even if I were to study very hard, I couldn't compete with Iida").

### wa (は/わ) — the core topic-marking particle, with an explicit orthographic rule
Paraphrased from pp.235–237 (entry continues in part 17). The book makes a point of spelling out the は-vs-わ orthographic split: は is read "wa" and marks a topic mid-sentence, while わ is pronounced identically but is a separate sentence-final particle following a copula/verb/adjective. As a topic marker, "wa" covers the basic affirmative topic-comment structure ("as for lions, they are ferocious animals" — distinguished from the subject-marking "ga" discussed at p.34), a negative-sentence use that implies comparison or restricted scope ("as for me, I won't go [implying someone else might]" — note "ga", not "wa", is still required when the subject is an interrogative pronoun), and an emphatic use when the topic is itself the object of the verb ("as for the money, I'll pay it [but not necessarily other costs]").
