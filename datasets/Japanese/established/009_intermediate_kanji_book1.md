## Grammar points

### Four ways to group kanji (Lesson 1)
The textbook teaches four systematic lenses for classifying/memorizing kanji, used throughout the
rest of the book: (1) **by shape** — kanji sharing a radical (e.g. 氵"water" in 海,温,涼,深,浅,濃;
頁"head" in 頭,題,願,顔,類; 艹"plant" in 花,茶,薬,苦,落,薄; 忄/心"heart" in 忙,性,情,快,憎,思,悪,
意,感,忘,悲,怒,恐; 辶"movement" in 道,週,近,遠,通,送; 广"roof" in 広,店,度,席,府,庁; 門"gate" in
問,間,開,閉,聞,関) or a shared non-radical component (口 in 品,古,加,商,器,局; 田 in 町,細,留,男,
界,胃; 土 in 赤,考,社,地,遠,壁; 王 in 全,国,現,理,球,環); (2) **by sound** — phonetic-compound
(形声文字) kanji sharing a 音記号 (phonetic marker) that cues the on-reading, e.g. 青→セイ(晴,静),
可→カ(何,歌), 交→コウ(校,効), 古→コ(故,個), 寺→ジ(持,時), 方→ホウ(訪,放); (3) **by meaning** —
semantic sets such as family members, locations, directions, seasons, colors, administrative
divisions, academic fields, country-name abbreviations (米=America,英=England,仏=France,独=
Germany,西=Spain,印=India,露=Russia,中=China,韓=Korea,豪=Australia,伯=Brazil; cf. 欧=Europe,
亜=Asia as region abbreviations, not countries), body parts, and government-office/organizational
terms (省,庁,局,部,課,係); (4) **by usage/part of speech** — i-adjectives, verbs, suru-verbs,
na-adjectives, place suffixes (〜所〜場〜地〜園〜館), occupation suffixes (〜家〜手〜者〜員〜人),
money suffixes (〜代〜費〜料〜金), negative prefixes (非〜不〜無〜未〜).

### Negative prefixes 不 and 無 change a word's grammatical behavior (Lesson 2, ①)
不 ("not ~") attaches to na-adjectives (親切な→不親切な) and to the plain stems of suru-verb nouns
(安定する→不安定な, 参加する→不参加), but the derived form does NOT retain the verbal (スル) usage
of the base word — i.e. ×不安定している and ×不参加する are both ungrammatical; the negated forms
behave as a na-adjective/bare noun respectively, not as suru-verbs. 無 ("without/lacking ~")
attaches to na-adjective/noun pairs expressing presence of a quality (有力な→無力な, 有効な→無効な,
有能な→無能な, 有料→無料).

### Paired opposite-meaning kanji are not a single consistent system (Lesson 2, ②–④)
The book explicitly warns that antonym pairing in Sino-Japanese vocabulary is lexically
idiosyncratic rather than a fully regular system: 出 pairs with 入 in 出・入 (出力/入力), but with 欠
in 出・欠 (出席/欠席); 入 pairs with 退 in 入・退 (入院/退院) but 進 also pairs with 退 (進化/退化);
勝 pairs with both 敗 (勝者/敗者, formal register) and 負 (勝負, idiomatic/colloquial register,
"match/game"). Similarly, 入学 "to enter school" is conventionally paired with 卒業 "to graduate,"
not with the phonologically-parallel 退学 "to withdraw from school." Paired na-adjectives built on
the same root kanji can carry opposite connotational register despite a shared base meaning:
強硬な/強固な (both "strong/firm") split into negative vs. positive image respectively, and
軟弱な/薄弱な (both roughly "weak") are both negative-register but used in different collocations
(意志が薄弱だ "to be weak-willed" vs 軟弱な外交 "weak-kneed diplomacy").

### Kanji-pair compound words with idiomatic meaning (Lesson 2, ⑥)
Compounding two antonymous kanji produces a noun meaning "whether X or Y" (高低="whether high or
low", 大小, 強弱, 増減, 勝敗, 進退, 出欠, 収支="income and expenses", 発着="departure and arrival",
前後, 左右, 上下, 内外, 公私="public and private"). A smaller set of such compounds have drifted to
idiomatic, non-compositional meanings that must be learned individually rather than derived: 多少
"more or less, a little" (not "many/few"), 加減 "allowance/adjustment" (not literally "add/subtract"),
始終 "always, all the time" vs. 終始 "from beginning to end" (the same two kanji in reversed order
yield a different idiom), and 勝負 "match, game, gamble" (not simply "win/lose").
