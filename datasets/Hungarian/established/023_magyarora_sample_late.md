# Hungarian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Magyaróra (New Paths to the Hungarian Language), Late Sample

**Source:** Szita Szilvia (with Görbe Tamás, Klementné Kecskeméti Beáta, Tóth Rózsa, and Molnár
Margit), *Magyaróra – New Paths to the Hungarian Language* (magyarora.com website, printed/archived
as PDF, materials dated 2002-2010), "19 Magyaróra New Paths to the Hungarian Language.pdf", 1324 PDF
pages. This is not a linear textbook but a graded website archive of short reading texts, worksheets,
and grammar summary sheets, each individually labeled by proficiency level ("1. szint" beginner,

articles, Újságcikkek/news articles, Irodalmi szövegek/literary texts, Beszélt nyelv/spoken language,

## Grammar points

### 1. Colloquial spoken-register markers explicitly flagged by the source (Ottlik "Iglbauer" unit, PDF pp. 1046-1064)

This is the highest register-value find in this sample. The source presents Ottlik Géza's short
story "Iglbauer" (from his collection *Próza*) as a level-3 (advanced) literary text, then follows it
with the source's own explicit stylistic commentary distinguishing Ottlik's narrator's own prose
(described as spare, "köznyelv"-based, deliberately unornamented — "Takarékoskodik a jelzőkkel,
mellőzi a díszítőelemeket") from the quoted-dialogue speech of the character who tells the narrator
about Iglbauer. The source names that character's speech pattern explicitly: "Iglbauer mondataiban
sok a **töltelékszó**, vagyis az olyan szó, amelynek nincs igazán jelentése. Ezek a szavak jellegzetes
elemei **a beszélt nyelvnek**" ("There are many filler words in Iglbauer's [reported] sentences, i.e.
words that don't really carry meaning. These words are characteristic elements of the spoken
language"), and states that Ottlik uses them deliberately to convey the character's plainspoken,
honest nature ("Ottlik segítségükkel Iglbauer egyszerű stílusát, becsületes voltát érzékelteti"). The
source then quotes four sentences and bold-tags the specific filler items in each: `aztán meg`,
`kérem`, `hát`/`ugyebár`, and a doubled first-person pronoun ("én, én") as a hesitation-repetition
device. This is a rare case of a pedagogical source explicitly naming and cataloguing colloquial
discourse-particle usage as a distinct register phenomenon, rather than leaving it for the reader to
infer — directly useful for the slang-mechanics phase's discourse-particle/filler-word category.

### 2. Imperative mood (felszólító mód), indefinite and definite conjugation (PDF pp. 1198-1199)

The source tabulates the imperative for all 6 persons across 4 verb-stem groups (regular; stems
ending `-s/-sz/-z/-t`; `-ik` verbs; and irregular verbs), separately for indefinite and definite
conjugation. Two structural notes not previously captured in this project's prior Hungarian
extractions:
- For `-s/-sz/-z/-t`-final stems, the imperative marker `-j-` **fully assimilates** to the stem-final
  consonant (`-s`→`-ssál/-sson`/etc., `-sz`→`-sszál/-sszon`, `-z`→`-zzál/-zzon`, `-t`→drops entirely,
  e.g. `festeni` "to paint" → `fess!`).
- The irregular-verb table flags `gyerünk!` ("let's go!") as an alternative 1pl imperative of `jönni`
  used as a lexicalized fixed exclamation distinct from the regular `jöjjünk`, with its own worked
  example distinguishing the two ("Mikor jöjjünk?" — a genuine question about timing — vs. "Gyerünk!"
  — a bare command to get moving). The table also marks `légy` (2sg imperative of `lenni`) as archaic
  ("régies") next to its living equivalent `legyél`, and `higgyed`/`igyad` (definite-conjugation
  2sg imperatives of `hinni`/`inni`) as archaic/regional ("régies... tájnyelvi") next to `hidd`/`idd`.

### 3. Plural and accusative suffix selection, and the shared "linking vowel" rule (PDF pp. 1200-1202)

Restates territory from `002_chapter5_nouns.md` (Rounds) with two formulations not seen there:
- **The `-ök`/`-öt` (rounded, front) alternant is explicitly restricted to two contexts**: monosyllabic
  stems in `ö`/`ő` (`kör`→`körök`, `bőr`→`bőrök`) and polysyllabic stems whose last vowel is any of
  `ö, ő, ü, ű` (`bőrönd`→`bőröndök`/`bőröndöt`) — with an explicit accusative-only exception carved out
  for participles (`sült`→`sültet`, not †`sültöt`).
- **The "Jó tudni" cross-identity rule**: the plural marker, the accusative marker, and the linking
  vowel of the `-s` adjectival suffix are frequently the *same* vowel for a given noun (`szerelem` →
  `szerelmek`/`szerelmet`/`szerelmes`; `ház` → `házak`/`házat`/`házas`) — offered as a mnemonic
  shortcut rather than an independent rule, and flagged with a caveat: after a further suffix (plural
  or possessive) is added, an accusative that would otherwise take `-ot` instead takes plain `-at`
  (`kabátot` "the coat, acc." but `kabátokat` "the coats, acc." — not †`kabátokot`).

### 4. Possessive suffixes and possessive pronouns, full paradigm (PDF pp. 1203-1204)

Full 7-person (én/te/ő/mi/ti/ők/Ön) table for both "one possession" (`kocsim` "my car" ... `kocsijuk`
"their car") and "multiple possessions" (`kocsijaim` "my cars" ... `kocsijaik` "their cars"), plus the
parallel possessive-pronoun table (`enyém` "mine," `tied` "yours," `övé` "his/hers," `mienk` "ours,"
`tietek` "yours (pl.)," `övék` "theirs"). One genuine ambiguity flagged by the source itself: `az ő
könyvük` is structurally ambiguous between "their book" (one book, several owners) and would need
`könyveik` for "their books" (several books, several owners) — the source calls this out explicitly
with a "Figyelem!" (attention) marker, since the 3rd-person-plural possessive marker doesn't
disambiguate possessor-number from possession-number the way the other persons' forms happen to.

### 5. Reported-speech verb-government catalogue (Idéző mondatok worksheet, PDF pp. 1250-1251)

A worksheet built around converting direct-quoted imperative/request speech into indirect
(`hogy`-clause) reported speech, organized as a drill on the specific case-government pattern each
reporting verb requires: `tanácsol valakinek valamit`/`hogy...` (advise), `megkér valakit
valamire`/`hogy...` (ask a favor of), `javasol valakinek valamit`/`hogy...` (suggest), `megtilt
valakinek valamit`/`hogy...` (forbid), `kér valamit valakitől`/`hogy...` (request from), and the
possessive-construction alternative `az az ötlete, hogy...` ("his/her idea is that..."). Structurally,
each reported clause takes the imperative mood in the embedded `hogy`-clause (e.g. "ne járjon
szoláriumba" — imperative — inside "azt tanácsolta..., hogy ne járjon szoláriumba") rather than a
separate subjunctive mood, confirming Hungarian's general pattern of using the imperative/subjunctive
form identically for both direct commands and embedded-clause "that s/he should" complements (a
pattern already noted for `Grammar points` in `015_fsi_vol1_units09-12.md` from a different source).

### 6. `ez`/`az` as forward- and backward-referencing pronouns (companion-book preview, PDF pp. 1314-1315)

This two-page excerpt (not from the main *Magyaróra* book itself, but a promotional sample spread
from Szita & Görbe's companion volume *Gyakorló magyar nyelvtan*, printed as this PDF's closing
pages) states a rule not previously captured in this project: `ez`/`az` can refer not only to
concrete objects/persons but to entire preceding or following clauses. `Ez` is used for **backward**
reference to something just said ("János bácsi százéves. Ez nagyszerű!" — "Uncle John is 100. This is
great!"), while `az` (typically in an oblique case form — `azt`, `arról`, `arra`) is used for
**forward** reference, anticipating a following `hogy`-clause, and co-occurs with a specific closed
list of verbs (`azt akarja`, `azt hallja`, `azt kérdezi`, `azt gondolja`, `azt mondja`, `azt állítja`,
`azt ígéri`, `örül annak`, `arról álmodik`, `arra kíváncsi`, `attól fél`, `emlékszik arra`, `azon
gondolkozik`). The source also notes the forward-referencing `utalószó` (reference word) can be
dropped when unstressed, with a worked minimal-pair example contrasting emphasis on the *question*
itself vs. emphasis on the *person being asked*. The `ezt`/`azt`-forms are flagged as **requiring the
definite conjugation** on their governing verb.

## Notable register/colloquial findings (summary)

The single strongest finding in this sample is item 1 above: the source's **own explicit naming and
tagging of colloquial filler-word usage** (`töltelékszó` / "beszélt nyelv" markers: `izé`, `szóval`,
`no`, `aztán meg`, `kérem`, `hát`/`ugyebár`, pronoun-doubling) as a deliberate literary device for
voicing an uneducated/plainspoken character — a rare case in this project's Hungarian sources of a
pedagogical text explicitly cataloguing discourse-particle/hesitation-marker vocabulary as its own
register category, directly relevant to a future slang-mechanics pass on Hungarian filler/discourse
particles. No taboo, argot, or otherwise "true slang" vocabulary was found in this sample — the
sampled sections skew toward standard written/spoken register and grammar-reference material, with
this one literary-text unit as the register-content exception.
