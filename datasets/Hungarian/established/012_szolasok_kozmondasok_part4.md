# Hungarian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Szólások és közmondások, Part 4 (final quarter)

**Source:** *Magyar szólások és közmondások* [Hungarian Sayings and Proverbs] (Gondolat
Könyvkiadó, Budapest, 1985 printing — OpenOffice-produced PDF, created 2011), PDF pages 626–835
of 835 total. This is a large numbered proverb/idiom dictionary organized alphabetically by
Hungarian keyword, with each entry giving the phrase, its figurative meaning after `=`, and
register tags in parentheses (`rég` = archaic, `táj` = dialectal/regional, `nép` = folk, `biz` =
colloquial/familiar, `durva` = coarse/vulgar, `gúny` = mocking, `tréf` = joking, `argó` = slang/
argot, `sajtó` = journalistic/press register, `vál` = elevated/literary, `elavulóban` = falling out
of use, `ritk` = rare).

**Coverage note — deliberate sample, not exhaustive.** This page range covers the tail end of the
alphabetical entries — the back half of **SZ** (from entry sz1273 "szükség" through sz1327
"szüzesség," PDF pp. 626–628), then the full letters **T** (t1–~881, PDF pp. 629–661), **TY**
(ty1–44, PDF pp. 662–664), **U** (u1–182, PDF pp. 665–672), **Ü/Ű** (ü1–57, PDF pp. 673–675), **V**
(the start of the section, v1–~45, PDF pp. 676–~678; the source's own V section continues well past
this to PDF p. 698, not fully surveyed here), **Z** (z1–45, PDF pp. 699–701), and **ZS** (zs1–69,
PDF pp. 702–705). **PDF pages 706–832 are the book's own back-matter keyword index ("Mutató") —
pure cross-reference material (keyword → entry-number lists), correctly skipped per this project's
extraction spec** — followed by the book's table of contents (pp. 833–834) and colophon (p. 835),
also skipped. The real proverb/idiom content in this dispatch's assigned range therefore actually
spans only PDF pp. 626–705 (~80 pages); this file samples roughly **50 of the several thousand**
entries in that span, chosen for interesting figurative derivation, an explicit register tag, or a
recurring idiomatic pattern — not exhaustive transcription, matching how this project's other large
glossaries (e.g. SCB) have been handled. Entry numbers restart at 1 for each new letter in the
source's own numbering scheme (its own cross-references use the form `sz1284`, `t8`, `u72`, `zs9`,
etc.) — that convention is preserved in the Notes column below for traceability back to the source.

**Text layer:** clean, real text layer via `pdftotext`; no vision-reading was needed anywhere in
this range.

## Recurring patterns worth flagging for future slang-mechanics analysis

1. **Livestock/farm-animal domain as a highly productive metaphor source.** The `tehén` ("cow")
   cluster alone runs roughly 40 consecutive numbered entries (t198–t239) covering laziness,
   dishonesty, fertility, temperament, and social standing; the `tyúk` ("hen") cluster in the TY
   section (ty1–44) does the same for talkativeness, gullibility, and domestic gossip. This mirrors
   `00_Usage_Tier_Taxonomy.md`'s `metaphor_productivity` derivation type (source tier `core`) — a
   concrete, densely-attested Hungarian example of one semantic domain (barnyard life) generating
   dozens of independent idioms, directly useful once Phase 3 mechanics analysis begins.
2. **"Handed/given an object" as a euphemism template for dismissal.** At least four separate
   idioms in this range (sz1291/sz1292 "kiteszik a szűrét," u179 "kikapja az útlevelet," u182
   "kikapja az útravalót," t32/t178 cross-referenced variants) independently encode "fired/evicted"
   as "handed [a garment/travel document/provisions]" — a productive template, not a one-off.
3. **Rhyme-paired proverbs as a mnemonic device.** `tanács`/`kalács` (advice/cake) recurs across at
   least four separate entries (t79, t82, t91, t95); `zsák`/`folt` (sack/patch) similarly recurs
   (zs29–30). This near-formulaic rhyme-pairing is a structural feature of the proverb genre itself,
   distinct from ordinary idiom formation, and may be worth its own note when Phase 3 gets to
   phonological/rhyme-driven slang mechanisms.
4. **Two confirmed `argó` (slang/argot) entries inside an otherwise archaic/dialectal-dominated
   proverb dictionary**: t254 "Leadja a tejet" (sexual euphemism via a commerce verb) and z37
   "Szintén zenész" (opaque occupational-metaphor slang for a fellow sufferer). Both are genuine
   attested slang, not folk/dialectal speech, and stand out as the highest-value finds in this
   sample for the eventual slang-mechanics phase.
5. **A single `sajtó` (press/journalistic register) tag** at u123 "uszályába kerül" — the only
   instance found in this sample of a register other than the book's standard rég/táj/nép/biz/durva/
   gúny/tréf set. Worth checking whether this recurs in other parts of the book or other Hungarian
   sources; if so it should be promoted to a `formal.journalistic` subcategory in
   `00_Usage_Tier_Taxonomy.md`.
6. **Apparent cross-linguistic idiom universals.** Two entries in this range structurally and
   semantically mirror well-known English idioms with no obvious borrowing relationship: ü55 "Ne
   hajigáljon az, kinek üvegből a háza!" (glass houses/stones) and zs33-34 "Nem árul/veszek
   zsákbamacskát" (cat-in-a-sack vs. English's pig-in-a-poke). Worth a note for later comparative
   work across this project's other languages, since a genuinely convergent idiom (rather than a
   loan-translation) says something about which metaphors are cognitively "obvious" across cultures.
