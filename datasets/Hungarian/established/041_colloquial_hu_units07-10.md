## Morpheme breakdowns

Hungarian is agglutinative; the following composed forms from this range are broken into their
component morphemes per the project's morphological-typology convention.

- **bejelentkezik** "checks in, registers" = `be-` (coverb, "in") + `jelent` (reports/announces,
  stem) + `-kezik` (reflexive/inchoative verbalizer) — lit. "reports oneself in."
- **egyágyas** "room for one" = `egy` (one) + `ágy` (bed) + `-as` (adjectival "having" suffix) —
  lit. "one-bedded."
- **fürdőszoba** "bathroom" = `fürdő` (bath, itself `fürdik` "bathes" + `-ő` participle) + `szoba`
  (room) — compound, "bathing-room."
- **konditerem** "exercise room" = `kondi(cionális)` (conditioning, clipped loan) + `terem` (hall) —
  compound.
- **szobafoglalás** "room reservation" = `szoba` (room) + `foglal` (reserves, stem) + `-ás`
  (nominalizer).
- **szállodaportás** "concierge" = `szálloda` (hotel) + `portás` (doorman) — compound.
- **idegenvezető** "guide" = `idegen` (foreign/stranger) + `vezet` (leads, stem) + `-ő` (agentive
  present-participle suffix) — lit. "one who leads strangers."
- **étterem** "restaurant" = `étel`/`ét-` (food) + `terem` (hall) — compound, "eating-hall" (the
  linking form drops the noun-forming `-el` before the compound boundary).
- **félemelet** "mezzanine" = `fél` (half) + `emelet` (floor/storey) — compound, "half-floor."
- **összeesküvés** "conspiracy" = `össze` (together, coverb) + `esküv-` (swears, stem, related to
  `esküszik` "to swear") + `-és` (nominalizer) — lit. "swearing-together."
- **születésnap** "birthday" = `születés` (birth, itself `születik` "is born" + `-és` nominalizer)
  + `nap` (day) — compound.
- **különszolgáltatás** "special service" = `külön` (separate/special) + `szolgál` (serves, stem) +
  `-tat` (causative) + `-ás` (nominalizer).
- **éjszakázik** "spends the night" = `éjszaka` (night) + `-zik` (verbalizer) — a noun turned into
  a verb of "doing/spending [the noun]."
- **időjárásjelentés** "weather report" = `idő` (weather/time) + `járás` (course/going, itself
  `jár` "goes" + `-ás` nominalizer) + `jelentés` (report, itself `jelent` "reports" + `-és`) —
  three-part compound, lit. "weather-course-report."
- **megreggelizik** "has breakfast" = `meg-` (perfectivizing coverb) + `reggeli` (breakfast) +
  `-zik` (verbalizer) — parallel formation to `éjszakázik` above, but with the perfective coverb
  added since the action (a full breakfast) is understood as completed.
- **megebédel** "has lunch" = `meg-` (perfectivizing coverb) + `ebéd` (lunch, noun) + `-el`
  (verbalizer) — same pattern as `megreggelizik`.
- **borjúpaprikás** "veal paprikás (dish)" = `borjú` (veal) + `paprika` (paprika, the spice) + `-s`
  (adjectivizer, here functioning as a dish-naming suffix, "in paprika [sauce]").
- **szénsavas** "carbonated" = `szén` (carbon) + `sav` (acid) + `-as` (adjectival "having" suffix)
  — lit. "having carbon-acid."
- **szénsavmentes** "non-carbonated" = `szénsav` (carbonic acid, see above) + `-mentes`
  ("free-of" suffix) — the same base noun with the opposite-polarity suffix, a productive pair the
  source itself presents as a direct contrast (p. 132-133).
- **könyvesbolt** "bookshop" = `könyves` (bookish, itself `könyv` "book" + `-es` adjectivizer) +
  `bolt` (shop) — compound.
- **bátorság** "courage" = `bátor` (brave) + `-ság` (abstract-noun-forming suffix).
- **turistacsoport** "tourist group" = `turista` (tourist, loanword) + `csoport` (group) —
  compound.
- **hazamegy** "goes home" = `haza` (homeward, an inherently directional adverb, distinct from the
  noun `haza` "homeland" seen in Unit 7's poem excerpt) + `megy` (goes) — not a coverb+verb pair in
  the strict sense (haza is a full adverb, not one of the seven core coverbs), but functions
  identically in the sentence, illustrating that directional adverbs and coverbs pattern alike.
- **napszakok** "times of day" = `nap` (day) + `szak` (part/section, bound stem) + `-ok` (plural).

### Nine-way locative case system (p. 117-119) — full paradigm, illustrative not exhaustive

The book presents this as a 3×3 grid (inside / outside / near-by, crossed with toward / no-motion /
from); worked with the noun stem in each cell rather than a single suffix chain, so it is
reproduced here as a structural table rather than a per-word morpheme breakdown:

| | Motion toward (hova?) | No motion (hol?) | Motion from (honnan?) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inside | -ba/-be | -ban/-ben | -ból/-ből |
| Outside | -ra/-re | -(o/e/ö)-n | -ról/-ről |
| Near by | -hoz/-hez/-höz | -nál/-nél | -tól/-től |

The demonstrative pronouns `ez`/`az` assimilate the case's initial consonant when case-marked
themselves (not merely suffixed): `ez + be > ebbe`, `az + ba > abba`, `ez + hez > ehhez`,
`az + hoz > ahhoz`, etc. — the final `-z` of `ez`/`az` is replaced by the first consonant of
whatever case follows, a genuine morphophonological assimilation rule distinct from ordinary
suffixation, worked out fully on p. 123.

## Grammar points

1. **Ordinal numbers 1st-1000th** (p. 101-102). Memorize 1st-20th (`első`, `második`, `harmadik`...
   `huszadik`); above 20, the pattern is fully regular and simply continues the teens' numerical
   endings (`huszonegyedik` "21st", `harmincadik` "30th", `századik` "100th", `ezredik` "1000th").
   Crucially, `első` "first" and `második` "second" are **not** used in the combining forms for
   teens/twenties/etc. — the combinatory forms `-egyedik` and `-kettedik` are used instead
   (`tizenegyedik` "11th," not *`tizenelső`*; `huszonkettedik` "22nd," not *`huszonmásodik`*).
2. **"Have" construction** (p. 102-104, major topic). Hungarian has no verb "to have." Three parts:
   (a) the possessor is marked dative (`nekem`, `Gábornak`) and may be omitted if it's a pronoun;
   (b) the possessed item takes a possessive suffix and **never** the definite article; (c) the
   verb `van` (or negated `nincs(en)`) appears as the finite verb. E.g. `(Nekem) nagy családom van.`
   "I have a large family." Word order: `Van`/`Nincs...?` commonly opens a question about
   possession (`Van a szállodának uszodája?` "Does the hotel have a pool?").
3. **`ugye?` tag question** (p. 105). Attached to the end of a sentence, functions like French
   "n'est-ce pas?" or German "nicht wahr?" — "isn't that right?"
4. **Double negation** (p. 105-106). A negative word (`sehol`, `senki`, `semmi`) requires a second
   negative (`nem` or `sem`) in the same clause: `Senkit nem/sem látok.` "I don't see anyone."
   `sem`/`sincs(en)` can only be the *second* negative; `nem` can be either first or second.
5. **`sem`/`sincs`: "neither/nor"** (p. 106). Same forms double as "neither do I" / "nor" outside
   pure negation: `Nincs testvérem. – Nekem sincs.` "I don't have siblings. – Neither do I."
   `Nem kérek sem kolbászt, sem sonkát.` "I'd like neither sausage nor ham."
6. **Plural possessive endings** (p. 108-109, major topic). Distinct from singular possessive
   endings learned earlier: `-im/-id/-i/-ink/-itok/-ik`, attached directly to vowel-final stems
   (with `a→á`/`e→é` lengthening) but requiring a `-(j)a-/-(j)e-` buffer (nearly always identical
   to the 3sg singular-possessive ending) before consonant-final stems: `szobáim` "my rooms" vs.
   `kulcsaim` "my keys."
7. **Plural possessive + "have" construction** (p. 109). Uses plural `vannak`/`nincsenek` instead
   of singular `van`/`nincs(en)`: `(Neki) mindig jó ötletei vannak.` "He always has good ideas."
8. **Irregular kinship terms** (p. 110). `bátya` "older brother," `öcse` "younger brother," `anya`
   "mother," `apa` "father" form irregular 3rd-person possessive stems (`bátyja`, `öccse`, `anyja`,
   `apja`) — the only other forms (plural possessive, etc.) are regular.
9. **Three new noun-declension classes** (p. 113-114, major topic): (1) **low-vowel nouns**
   (`ház`/`hely`) always use `a/e` linking vowels (never `o/e/ö`), always require a linking vowel
   before the accusative, and almost never take the j-initial 3rd-person possessive suffix; some
   lose their last-syllable vowel length when suffixed (`pohár`→`poharam`, `levél`→`levelem`), some
   don't (`ház`→`házam`, `hely`→`helyem`). (2) **Fleeting-vowel nouns** (`álom`, `tükör`) keep the
   regular `o/e/ö` linking-vowel choice but drop the noun's own last vowel when suffixed
   (`álom`→`álmom`, not *`álomom`*). (3) **`-alom`/`-elem` nouns** (`irodalom`, `történelem`,
   `jutalom`, `szerelem`) combine both quirks: `a/e` linking vowels *and* dropping the noun's last
   vowel (`jutalom`→`jutalmam`, `szerelem`→`szerelmem`). No way to identify these classes from the
   dictionary form alone (except the `-alom`/`-elem` ending itself); must be memorized per word.
10. **Nine locative cases (hol?/hova?/honnan? × inside/outside/near-by)** (p. 117-119, the chapter's
    central topic). See the full 3×3 table under Morpheme breakdowns above. No prepositions in
    Hungarian; instead nine cases handle "in/on/near," "into/onto/towards," "out of/off of/away
    from." Adjectives and articles never agree in case — only the last word of a phrase takes the
    case ending. Fleeting-vowel nouns drop their last vowel before the `-(o/e/ö)-n` case too
    (`sarok`→`sarkon`).
11. **Inside vs. outside cases and real-world geography** (p. 121-122, genuinely notable
    sociogeographic-grammar point). The **inside** locative cases (`-ban/-ben`, `-ba/-be`,
    `-ból/-ből`) are used for most city/country names **outside** Hungary (`Amerikában`,
    `Bécsben` "Vienna," `Párizsban`) — but the **outside** locative cases (`-on/-en/-ön`, `-ra/-re`,
    `-ról/-ről`) are used for city/country names **within** Hungary, including the country's own
    name (`Magyarországon`, `Budapesten`), and additionally for public transportation
    (`a villamoson` "on the tram"). The **near-by** cases (`-nál/-nél`, `-hoz/-hez/-höz`,
    `-tól/-től`) extend beyond literal nearness to a person's home or workplace (`Ferencnél` "at
    Ferenc's house," `az orvosnál` "at the doctor's office").
12. **`ez`/`az` case-agreement and consonant assimilation** (p. 122-123). Demonstrative pronouns
    agree in case and number with the noun they modify (as already true for plural/accusative), and
    the pronoun's own final `-z` assimilates to the first consonant of the case ending:
    `ez + hez > ehhez`, `az + tól > attól`, etc. — see full assimilation table above.
13. **Coverbs (major topic, Unit 8, p. 126-128)**. Verbal prefixes attached to a verb's front that
    refine or redirect its meaning; the seven directional coverbs introduced here: `be` (in/into),
    `fel` (up), `oda` (towards there), `el` (away), `ki` (out), `le` (down), `ide` (towards here).
    Freely combine with motion verbs (`bejön`/`bemegy`, `kijön`/`kimegy`, etc.) — full "come" vs.
    "go" contrast table given for all seven.
14. **Coverbs and word order — the "kicked out" rule** (p. 127-128). A coverb, though written
    joined to its verb, must detach and move to a position *after* the verb whenever a focus
    element (negation, a question word, an answer to a question, or emphasis) is introduced — the
    focus element itself occupies the slot immediately before the verb, displacing the coverb to
    right after it: neutral `Bemegyek a szobába.` vs. focus `Nem megyek be a szobába.`
15. **Adverb formation `-(a)n/-(e)n`** (p. 133-134). Most adjectives form adverbs by adding
    `-(a)n/-(e)n`, lengthening a final `a`/`e` first (`drága`→`drágán`). Some `-ó/-ő`-final
    adjectives take a linking vowel, some don't, unpredictably (`olcsó`→`olcsón` vs.
    `érthető`→`érthetően`); most other adjectives simply add `-an/-en` (`kíváncsi`→`kíváncsian`).
    A set of common irregular adverbs must be memorized (`jó`→`jól`, not *`jóan`*; `rossz`→`rosszul`;
    `lassú`→`lassan`; `könnyű`→`könnyen`; `szörnyű`→`szörnyen`; `hosszú`→`hosszan`).
16. **The coverbs `meg` and `el` as perfectivizers** (p. 134-135, major topic). Beyond directional
    coverbs, Hungarian uses coverbs (most commonly, but not exclusively, `meg` and `el`) to mark the
    completion (or intended completion) of a verb's action. Crucially, **Hungarian present tense can
    express future completion** when the correct coverb is attached — `Olvasom a könyvet.` "I am
    reading the book" (ongoing) vs. `Elolvasom a könyvet.` "I will read [finish] the book"
    (complete). Not all verbs use `meg`/`el` for this; which coverb (if any) attaches to a given verb
    for this function must be learned per-verb — "perhaps the most subtle... way to refine meaning
    in Hungarian."
17. **`tud` "can/is able" and `akar` "wants" — coverb-displacing verbs** (p. 137-138). Like the
    focus-word rule above, these two verbs pull a coverb off its original verb and place it
    immediately before themselves: `Megveszi a könyvet.` "He will buy the book" → `Meg akarja venni
    a könyvet.` "He wants to buy the book." If a focus element is also present, it takes the slot
    immediately before `tud`/`akar` and the coverb stays attached to its original verb instead:
    `Nem akarok bemenni a boltba.` "I don't want to go into the shop."
18. **`kell` "is necessary" + declined infinitives** (p. 139-140, major topic). The person who needs
    something is dative-marked; the needed thing is the grammatical subject of `kell`
    (`Nekem új számítógép kell.` "I need a new computer"). When what's needed is an *action*, the
    verb appears as a **declined infinitive** — the bare infinitive `-ni` with possessive endings
    attached (`várnom`, `menned`, `főznie`, etc., following back/front-unrounded/front-rounded vowel
    harmony); the final `-i` of `-ni` is dropped in 1st/2nd person forms but kept in 3rd person.
    `kell` (like `tud`/`akar`) also displaces coverbs from their verb: `(Nekem) el kell mennem.`
    "I have to go."
19. **`lehet` "is possible, may"** (p. 140). A form of `lesz` "will be," most often used
    impersonally (no specific actor implied — "one may," "you may," "we may" all possible readings).
    Also displaces coverbs like `tud`/`akar`/`kell`: `Be lehet menni.` "One may go in." Never occurs
    in the definite conjugation (true of `kell` as well).
20. **`lehet` vs. `talán`** (p. 140). Both translate "maybe," but `talán` carries the speaker's own
    hope that the possibility is real, while `lehet` is neutral, expressing mere uncontrolled
    possibility ("it depends on circumstances").
21. **Telling time: `negyed`/`fél`/`háromnegyed`** (p. 146-147, major topic). Hungarian tells
    non-hour time by referencing the *upcoming* hour, not the one just passed: 2:15 = `negyed három`
    lit. "a quarter (toward) three"; 7:30 = `fél nyolc` "half (toward) eight"; 11:45 = `háromnegyed
    tizenkettő` "three-quarters (toward) twelve." Colloquial abbreviations exist (`n3`, `f8`,
    `hn12`). Colloquial `délelőtt`/`délután` (morning/afternoon) vs. official 24-hour clock for
    schedules.
22. **`-kor` case: "at" (a time)** (p. 147). Attaches to a specific hour or certain other time
    expressions to mean "at that time" (`kilenckor` "at nine," `karácsonykor` "at Christmas");
    unlike most cases, final `a`/`e` is **not** lengthened before `-kor`.
23. **`-ig` case: "until, as far as" / "for how long"** (p. 147). Covers both temporal and spatial
    "until" (`fél háromig` "until 2:30," `Budapestig` "as far as Budapest") and duration
    (`sokáig` "for a long time," `tíz percig` "for ten minutes"). With the verb `tart`, means
    "lasts (until)": `Meddig tart az előadás? – Nyolcig.` "How long does the lecture last? – Until
    eight."
24. **Days of the week + `-(o/e/ö)-n` case** (p. 151). All days take this case to mean "on [day]"
    (`hétfőn`, `kedden`, `szerdán`...) **except `vasárnap`, which takes no case ending at all** for
    "on Sunday" — a genuine irregularity flagged explicitly by the source.
25. **Months + `-ban/-ben` case** (p. 151). "In/during [month]" uses the same inside-locative case
    already learned for places: `januárban`, `szeptemberben`, etc.
26. **`-ért` case: "for" (purpose, fetching, financial exchange)** (p. 153). Covers "for the purpose
    of / to fetch" (`Lemegyek a boltba kenyérért.` "I'm going to the shop for bread") and financial
    transactions, where it marks *either* the price or the item depending on which is the object of
    "for" in English (`Hatezer forintért vettem az inget.` "I bought the shirt for six thousand
    forints" vs. `Hatezer forintot fizettem az ingért.` "I paid six thousand forints for the
    shirt"). Related forms `azért` "that's why," `ezért` "this is why."
27. **`-val/-vel` case: "with"** (p. 153-154). The initial `-v-` assimilates to the final consonant
    of a consonant-final stem (`barát`→`baráttal`... actually shown as `baráttal`-pattern via full
    gemination, e.g. `lány`→`lánnyal`, `ceruza`→`ceruzával` for vowel-final stems, no assimilation
    needed). Frequently used with means of transportation to mean "by" (`vonattal` "by train,"
    `autóbusszal` "by bus," `kocsival` "by car," `metróval` "by metro"). `ez`/`az` have irregular
    doublets with this case: `ezzel`/`evvel`, `azzal`/`avval` (the forms with `zz` are more common).
