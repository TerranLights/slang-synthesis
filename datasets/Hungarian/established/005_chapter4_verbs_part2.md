# Hungarian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Chapter 4, Verbs (second half, §4.3.10–§4.7)

**Source:** Carol H. Rounds, *Hungarian: An Essential Grammar* (2nd edition, Routledge, 2009),
Chapter 4 "Verbs," printed pages 40–75 (PDF pages 55–90). Covers §4.3.10 Future tense (fog, lesz),
§4.4 Non-finite forms (participles, adverbial participle, infinitive, declined infinitives),
§4.5 Productive derivational endings (potential, causative, frequentative), §4.6 Irregular verb
stems (van/jön/megy, the "group of seven," fleeting-vowel stems, v-stems, -szik verbs), and
§4.7 Coverbs (direction, manner, aspect, the six most common coverbs individually, and a longer
list of additional coverbs). A sibling extraction covers §4.1–§4.3.9 (verb stems through the
conditional) from earlier in the chapter; this file begins cleanly at §4.3.10.2 "fog," the first
subsection following that sibling's assigned range, and ends at printed p. 75, immediately before
Chapter 5 "Nouns" begins on p. 76 (already extracted as `002_chapter5_nouns.md`).

**Coverage note:** every grammar point and every distinct vocabulary item in this range is
extracted, including the full irregular-verb-stem inventory (the "group of seven," the four
sz-alternation classes of *-szik* verbs, fleeting-vowel and v-stem classes) since these are exactly
the kind of finite, memorizable irregularity class the source itself tabulates — the same treatment
002's noun-stem classes received. Coverb + verb compounds are listed individually where the source
gives them as a distinct lexical/semantic unit (e.g. *kimegy* "go out" vs. bare *megy* "go"), since
coverb attachment in Hungarian is genuinely lexeme-forming, not merely inflectional. Repeated drill
sentences reusing already-captured vocabulary are skipped — see `../../00_Reference_Extraction_Spec.md`
for the full coverage rule.

**Morphological typology note.** Hungarian is agglutinative (see `../00_Extraction_Checklist.md`).
This chunk is the chapter's derivational core — potential, causative, and frequentative endings
stack onto verb stems and can combine with each other, and declined infinitives stack possessive-
style personal endings onto a reduced infinitive stem — so several entries below carry a
**Morpheme breakdown** note, continuing the convention established in `002_chapter5_nouns.md` and
`003_chapter6_case_system.md`.

## Grammar points

### §4.3.10 Future tense
There is no inflectional future tense in Hungarian. Three strategies express future time: (1) present
tense plus a time expression and/or coverb (p. 39); (2) the auxiliary **fog**, which conjugates in the
present tense (both conjugations) and combines with a bare infinitive — *pihenni fogok* "I will rest,"
*meg fogom várni* "I will wait for him" — used mainly with coverb-less verbs, and more emphatically
with coverb verbs; (3) **lesz** "will be, become," the suppletive future of *van* "be" (not an
auxiliary — it directly replaces *van* in any construction, including 'have' and adverbial-participle
passive constructions like *be lesz fejezve* "will be finished"). p. 40–41

### §4.4 Non-finite forms
**Participles (formation, §4.4.1).** Present participle *-ó/-ő* attaches directly to the stem (fleeting
vowels always elide first; v-stems always use the v-stem). Past participle is usually identical to the
3sg indefinite past (*adott*, *ismert*), with monosyllabic Class B verbs alternating a short and long
form (*állt ~ állott*). Future participle *-andó/-endő* attaches directly to the stem and is
considerably rarer in use than the other two. pp. 41–42

**Participles (usage, §4.4.2).** All three participles function as adjectives (*tanuló fiú* "studying
boy") and many nominalize as nouns (*tanuló* "student," *jövendő* "future"). In prose, participles
extend into long adjectival constructions that function semantically like relative clauses — the
source recommends identifying the modified noun and translating right-to-left through the participial
phrase (worked example: *Magyar főszereplővel forgatott film* "A movie made with a Hungarian leading
actor"). pp. 42–44

**Adverbial participle (§4.4.3).** *-va/-ve*, attached directly to the stem, expresses an action
concurrent with the main verb's action (*mosolyogva* "while smiling"). Colloquial usage with *van*
'be' is cross-referenced to §17.10 (outside this dispatch's range). p. 44

**Infinitive (§4.4.4).** *-ni*, attached directly to the stem; stems ending in two consonants or
long-vowel+t require a linking vowel a/e (exceptions: *áll, száll, varr, forr*). Infinitives complement
other conjugated verbs (*Szeretek olvasni*). When the infinitive's direct object is definite, the
matrix verb takes the definite conjugation — unless the matrix verb is impersonal (*kell, lehet*) or
inherently intransitive (*megy, igyekszik*), which are always indefinite. A topicalized-infinitive
construction (bare infinitive fronted, repeated as the finite verb) gives emphatic "well, I did X" or
concessive readings, usually followed by a *de* 'but' clause: *Olvasni olvastam a könyvet, de nem volt
jó* "(Well,) I read the book, but it wasn't good." pp. 45–46

**Declined infinitives (§4.4.5).** Used in impersonal constructions (cross-referenced to §17.7, outside
this dispatch's range). Formed by reducing the infinitive to *-(a/e)n* (dropping final *-i*) and
attaching personal (possessive-style) endings — 1sg *-om/-em/-öm*, 2sg *-od/-ed/-öd*, 3sg *-ia/-ie*,
1pl *-unk/-ünk*, 2pl *-otok/-etek/-ötök*, 3pl *-iuk/-iük* — with vowel harmony choosing among back,
front-unrounded, and front-rounded variants. See morpheme breakdown below. pp. 45–46

### §4.5 Productive derivational endings
Three endings attach to verb stems before tense/mood morphology and each creates a **new verb stem**
that then conjugates by its own regular pattern (a short-vowel-+t stem type), regardless of the
original verb's class:

- **Potential** *-hat/-het* (§4.5.1–4.5.2) — 'may, can, is possible'; also 'might, could' in expressions
  of doubt, and — combined with the conditional — a very polite request register (*Megnézhetném azt a
  könyvet?* "Could I have a look at that book?"), or — combined with the past conditional — 'could
  have + past participle' (*Megvehetted volna azt az inget* "You could have bought that shirt"). The
  potential of *van* is irregularly built from the *lesz* root, not from *van* itself. Combines
  regularly with the present participle *-ó/-ő* and *-atlan/-etlen* to derive adjective pairs like
  *érthető ~ érthetetlen* "understandable ~ incomprehensible." pp. 46–49
- **Causative** *-at/-et* or *-tat/-tet* (§4.5.3–4.5.4) — highly productive but semantically restricted
  (not usable on every verb). *-tat/-tet* attaches to vowel+t-final and most polysyllabic verbs;
  *-at/-et* attaches to consonant+t-final and most monosyllabic verbs; a further list of monosyllabic
  stems idiosyncratically takes *-tat/-tet* anyway (*ázik→áztat, bukik→buktat*, etc.). Marks that the
  subject has something done rather than performing the action directly; the performer, if named, takes
  the instrumental case (*Kivasaltattam az ingemet a férjemmel* "I had my husband iron my shirt") or,
  when there's no other direct object, the accusative (§4.5.4.1) — this use can shade into simply
  transitivizing an intransitive verb with the causative meaning largely bleached out (*bukik→buktat*,
  *fürdik→fürdet*, *változik→változtat*). The causative may itself take the potential (*olvastathat*
  "may have someone read"). pp. 50–52
- **Frequentative** *-gat/-get* (§4.5.5) — repetitive, prolonged, or aimless action; attaches directly
  to the stem, though most monosyllabic stems need a linking vowel o/e/ö (*üt→ütöget*). Can shift a
  verb's meaning more radically than a literal "repeated action" gloss suggests (*hall* 'hear' →
  *hallgat* 'listen, be quiet'; *lát* 'see' → *látogat* 'visit'). Also combines with the potential
  (*olvasgathat* "may read a bit"). pp. 53–54

### §4.6 Irregular verb stems
A closed set of memorizable irregularity classes, each internally regular once learned:

- **van/jön/megy (§4.6.1)** — three verbs each requiring a different stem per tense/mood; *jön* has
  variable present-tense forms including the colloquial imperative set *gyere/gyertek/gyerünk*.
- **The "group of seven" (§4.6.2)** — *lesz, tesz, vesz, hisz, visz, eszik, iszik* — a present-tense
  sz-stem alternating with other consonants across the paradigm (*tesz-/tett-/tegy-/ten-*); *eszik* and
  *iszik* additionally have irregular v-stem 3sg indefinite past forms (*evett, ivott*) with the rest of
  the past conjugating regularly off *ett-/itt-*.
- **Fleeting-vowel stems (§4.6.3)** — a large class where the stem-final vowel (always o/e/ö) elides
  before a vowel-initial suffix, yielding a consonant cluster whose members are drawn from a fixed set
  (j, l, ly, n, ny, m, r, z, zs). No shape-based way to predict membership; only the present participle
  *-ó/-ő* consistently triggers elision. Sole exception: *őriz* 'guard', which never elides.
- **V-stems (§4.6.4)** — a small closed class (*fő, nő, ró, lő, nyű, sző*) whose 3sg form ends in a
  vowel but which take a v-stem before vowel-initial suffixes; the final vowel shortens before the v in
  all of these except *nyű*.
- **-szik verbs (§4.6.5)** — verbs citation-formed in *-szik*; if a vowel precedes *-szik* the verb is
  regular (with two listed exceptions, *esküszik* 'swear' and *alkuszik* 'bargain,' which nonetheless
  pattern with the irregular group). If a consonant precedes *-szik*, the *sz* alternates with another
  consonant throughout the paradigm, in one of four patterns: **sz~d~v** (*alszik→alud-*, plus five
  frequentative-shaped verbs like *cselekszik~cselekedik* that allow either stem in the present tense),
  **sz~d** (the largest group, ~19 verbs, e.g. *öregszik~öregedik*), **sz~z** (*emlékszik~emlékezik* and
  two others), and **sz~z~v** (*igyekszik~igyekezik*, the sole member). Four verbs that look like *-szik*
  candidates conjugate entirely regularly instead: *hallatszik, játszik, látszik, tetszik*; a fifth,
  *alapszik*, is present-tense-only and uses the synonym *alapul* for all other forms. pp. 54–60

### §4.7 Coverbs
Coverbs are verbal prefixes, attachable and separable, with three broad functions plus a long tail of
idiosyncratic, verb-specific meanings — "sometimes coverbs change the meaning of the verb entirely" (p.
60). Only one coverb attaches per verb at a time; coverb position in word order shifts under focus,
auxiliary constructions, and imperatives (cross-referenced to Chapter 16, outside this range).

- **Direction (§4.7.1)** — *be, ki, fel, le, el, oda, ide, vissza* combine most naturally with verbs of
  motion (*megy/jön*) but can also add motion to an inherently stationary verb (*áll* 'stand' →
  *feláll* 'stand up'). pp. 60–61
- **Manner (§4.7.2)** — *végig, agyon, túl, újra* modify how or to what extent an action is carried out
  (*agyonver* 'beat to death', *túlél* 'survive', *újraválaszt* 're-elect'). p. 61
- **Aspect (§4.7.3)** — Hungarian has one form per tense (unlike English's several past-tense forms), so
  coverbs plus word order carry the imperfective/perfective distinction. In the past tense, a coverb
  marks a completed action; bare verbs mark an action in progress. In the present tense, coverb verbs
  often translate as English future (since completion typically postdates the utterance), while bare
  verbs mark ongoing action with no comment on completion — though this is not simply "add a coverb for
  future" (footnote 20: the converse doesn't hold cleanly). Coverbs still mark perfective aspect in the
  tenseless subjunctive/imperative. The future auxiliary *fog* can combine with a coverb verb (aspect
  stays perfective, meaning shifts toward emphasis) or a bare verb (marks imperfective future: *Sírok* →
  *Sírni fogok* "I am going to cry"). When imperfective aspect co-occurs with a coverb, the coverb is
  displaced to a post-verb position (*Mentem át az utcán* vs. perfective *Átmentem az utcán*). Coverbs
  can also mark the *beginning* of an action (*megszeret, meglát, elalszik*), and a handful of verbs
  occur only ever with a coverb attached (*megbénul, megsüketül, megbetegszik, megrészegszik*). pp. 62–63
- **The six individually profiled coverbs (§4.7.4)** — *meg* (completion; onset; single instance;
  expected/successful occurrence; brief communicative acts), *el* (away; perfective; onset; all-the-way;
  covering a wide area; done incorrectly; apart; 'along' with *előtt/mellett*; using something up;
  lengthy duration; communication to the end — and, paired against *meg*, the "unfortunate vs. expected
  change" contrast: *meghízik* 'get nicely fat' vs. *elhízik* 'get unfortunately fat'; *megfagyott*
  'froze (in the freezer, as expected)' vs. *elfagyott* 'got frostbite'), *be* (in/into; envelop/cover
  entirely; close/fasten), *ki* (out; undo; selection), *fel* (up; onto; furnish/supply with; turn
  upside-down; appear from somewhere; onset of a mental-state change; sudden beginning of an action —
  the semantically broadest of the six), *le* (down; off; remove/relinquish; cover a surface; copy/
  reproduce; lessen/reduce; a negative connotation added to the base verb, e.g. *lenéz* 'look down on
  someone', *leszid* 'put someone down'). pp. 63–73
- **More coverbs (§4.7.5)** — Coverbs derive from all parts of speech; sometimes only the orthographic
  convention (written as one word with the verb vs. two words) distinguishes a coverb from an adverb in
  the same shape (*jólesik* 'feel good' [coverb] vs. *Jól érzi magát* 'He feels well' [adverb];
  *egyetért* 'agree' [coverb] vs. *Egyet sóhajtott* 'She sighed once' [adverbial numeral]).
  Pronominalized case forms can serve as coverbs, usually restricted to 3sg except *bele* and *rá*:
  *bele, rá, rajta, hozzá, neki*. Many postpositions double as coverbs (*át, alá, ellen, keresztül,
  körül, mellé, túl, végig*). A further open-ended list of "additional important coverbs" is given:
  *elő, félre, fenn, hátra, haza, oda, ott, össze, szét, tovább, utána, vissza*. pp. 73–75

## Morpheme breakdown (agglutinative forms)

Continuing the convention from `002_chapter5_nouns.md` and `003_chapter6_case_system.md`, the forms
below — all directly attested in the source — show verbal-morphology stacking in this chunk.

> **tanítanom** = tanít (teach) + ani (infinitive, back-vowel two-consonant-stem linking form) + om
> (1sg declined-infinitive personal ending) — "[for] me to teach," from the declined-infinitive
> paradigm (p. 46). Three morphemes beyond the bare stem: linking-vowel infinitive, then a
> possessive-style personal ending stacked directly onto the reduced infinitive.

> **ülnöm** = ül (sit) + ni→n (infinitive, reduced by dropping final -i before the personal ending) +
> öm (1sg declined-infinitive personal ending, front-rounded variant by vowel harmony) — "[for] me to
> sit" (p. 46). Shows the same declined-infinitive template on a front-rounded-vowel stem, illustrating
> the three-way vowel-harmony choice (-om/-em/-öm) the paradigm requires.

> **érthetetlen** = ért (understand) + het (potential, "-can/-may-") + etlen (negative/'without'
> adjective-forming suffix, front-vowel variant of -atlan/-etlen) — "incomprehensible," lit.
> "not-understand-able" (p. 49). Two derivational suffixes stacked directly on the verb stem, both
> productive endings taught earlier in this same chunk (potential §4.5.1, this adjectival use noted at
> the end of §4.5.2).

> **olvashatatlan** = olvas (read) + hat (potential) + atlan (negative/'without' suffix, back-vowel
> variant) — "illegible, unreadable" (p. 49). Same template as *érthetetlen* on a back-vowel stem.

> **olvastathat** = olvas (read) + tat (causative, "have someone ___") + hat (potential, "may") —
> "may have someone read" (p. 51). The source's own explicit example of causative + potential stacking:
> once the causative redefines the stem, the potential attaches to the *new*, causativized stem, not
> the original.

> **rendeltethet** = rendel (order) + tet (causative) + het (potential) — "may have someone order"
> (p. 51). Companion example to *olvastathat*, front-vowel variant.

> **olvasgathat** = olvas (read) + gat (frequentative, "a bit / repeatedly") + hat (potential, "may")
> — "may read a bit" (p. 54). Frequentative + potential stacking, the source's parallel case to the
> causative + potential examples above — the same "derivational ending redefines the stem, then
> potential attaches to the new stem" pattern recurs across all three of §4.5's productive endings.

> **nézegethet** = néz (watch, look) + eget (frequentative, "-gat/-get" with linking vowel e, "about /
> repeatedly") + het (potential, "may") — "may look about" (p. 54).

> **be lesz fejezve** = be (coverb, perfective 'completely') + fejez (finish) + ve (adverbial
> participle, "-va/-ve") + lesz (auxiliary, future 'will be') — "will be finished," lit. "will be in a
> finished state" (p. 41). Not stem-internal stacking like the others, but a clause-level compositional
> unit worth flagging: Hungarian's nearest analog to an English future passive is built by combining a
> coverb-perfectivized adverbial participle with the future copula *lesz*, not with a dedicated passive
> morphology.
