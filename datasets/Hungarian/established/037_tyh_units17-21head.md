# Hungarian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *Teach Yourself Hungarian* (2nd ed.), tail of
Unit 17 + Units 18-20 + head of Unit 21

**Source:** Zsuzsa Pontifex, *Teach Yourself Hungarian*, 2nd edition (McGraw-Hill/Teach Yourself,
2003), "Teach Yourself Hungarian [2nd Ed (2003)].pdf" (scanned, no text layer — read visually),
printed pages 261-320 (PDF pages 271-330 of 388). Assigned dispatch range was PDF 271-330.

**Page-offset verification (mandatory check per the vision-reading gotcha):** empirically confirmed
by direct inspection, not assumed — PDF page 271 shows printed footer "261" (Unit 17, "mi a panasza?"
Practice section), PDF page 330 shows printed footer "320" (Unit 21 Grammar §1, imperative
indefinite conjugation table), and PDF page 327 shows the (rotated, sideways-scanned) Unit 21 title
page corresponding to printed p. 317. This confirms the consistent `printed_page = PDF_page − 10`
offset already established by sibling files `034` and `038`, and a flat single-page-per-image scan
throughout this range (no two-pages-per-image spread).

**Units covered, per the book's own table of contents (verified directly, PDF pp. 6-7):**
- **Unit 17 tail only** ("mi a panasza?", "what's the matter?") — printed pp. 261-265 (PDF 271-275):
  this unit itself runs printed pp. 250-265; only its final five pages (Practice-section drills,
  vocabulary boxes, and the closing "Do you understand?" dialogue) fall inside this dispatch's
  assigned range. The unit's opening Dialogue/Grammar sections (printed pp. 250-260) are outside this
  range and are expected to be covered by a sibling chunk assigned printed pp. ~82-260 (per the
  dispatch note, not yet landed in `established/` as of this extraction — see the coverage-gap note
  at the end of this file, same situation `038` flagged for Units 6-16).
- **Unit 18**, "Milyen emberek voltak a szülei?" ("What were your parents like?") — printed pp.
  266-281 (PDF 276-291). Full unit.
- **Unit 19**, "Sokkal jobban tetszik" ("I much prefer it") — printed pp. 282-299 (PDF 292-309). Full
  unit — this is the book's own most grammar-dense unit (comparatives/superlatives, the infinitive
  with possessive endings, `bennem`-type pronoun-suffix forms, `-t`/`-ra`/`-re`/`-ig` duration/
  deadline time-expression system).
- **Unit 20**, "Jobb lett volna, ha..." ("It would have been better if...") — printed pp. 300-316 (PDF
  310-326). Full unit — the conditional mood (present and past), verbal-particle meaning-change
  system, particle-separation rules, the `-hat`/`-het` "can/may/must" ending, indirect statements,
  and further `óta`/`-t`/`-kor` time expressions.
- **Unit 21 head only**, "Hová menjünk?" ("Where shall we go?") — printed pp. 317-320 (PDF 327-330):
  title page, opening Dialogue, culture note on `szalonnasütés` (Hungarian-style barbecue), and the
  start of the Grammar section (§1, the imperative mood, `-j` ending, indefinite conjugation table
  only). The unit's remainder (definite imperative conjugation, stem-change rules, remaining grammar
  points, Practice, "Do you understand?") continues at printed p. 321 (PDF 331), covered by sibling
  file `established/038_tyh_unit21_appendix_backmatter.md`, which explicitly begins its own coverage
  at printed p. 321 — confirmed a clean, non-overlapping handoff between this file and `038`.

**Relationship to prior Hungarian `established/` files:** continues the same source/genre already

### Morpheme Breakdown — agglutinative forms worth decomposing

- **`egyre szórakozottabb`** ("more and more absent-minded") = `egyre` ("increasingly," itself the
  sublative `-re` fossilized on `egy` "one") + `szórakozott` ("absent-minded," a participle-derived
  adjective from `szórakozik` "to be distracted/amuse oneself") + `-abb` (comparative suffix) — a
  productive "increasingly ADJ" intensifying pattern using the ordinary comparative morphology. Unit
  21, p. 319.
- **`disznóölés`** ("pig-killing") = `disznó` ("pig") + `öl-` ("kill," verb root) + `-és` (deverbal
  noun suffix) — a fully transparent compound-plus-derivation, the unit's own title concept. Unit 20
  Grammar/culture note, p. 303.
- **`szalonnasütés`** ("bacon-roasting") = `szalonna` ("bacon") + `süt-` ("roast/bake," verb root) +
  `-és` (deverbal noun suffix) — same `-és` nominalization pattern as `disznóölés`, confirming this is
  a productive Hungarian word-formation strategy (Noun-stem + Verb-stem + `-és` → "the activity of
  V-ing N") worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase as a general derivational template. Unit
  21, p. 320.
- **`ötszázasból visszaad`** ("give change from a 500[-forint note]") = `öt` ("five") + `száz`
  ("hundred") + `-as` (denominal "unit of X" nominalizer, here "a five-hundred[-forint note]") +
  `-ból` (elative "out of/from") + `vissza-` ("back," verbal particle) + `ad` ("give") — a fully
  agglutinative money/change-giving construction stacking a numeral-nominalizer, a case suffix, and a
  particle-verb compound in one phrase. Unit 20, p. 316.
- **`tanítanék`/`tanítanám`** ("I would teach," indefinite/definite) illustrate the `-ít`/two-
  consonant-stem linking-vowel rule for the conditional: `tanít-` (stem, ends in `-ít`) + `-a-`
  (linking vowel) + `-nék`/`-nám` (1sg conditional indefinite/definite). Contrasts with the vowel-less
  attachment on simple stems like `ad-nék`/`ad-nám`. Unit 20 Grammar §1, pp. 305-306.
- **`kaphatnék`** ("I could get/receive") = `kap-` ("get, receive") + `-hat-` (the `-hat`/`-het`
  "can/may" potential-mood suffix) + `-nék` (1sg conditional indefinite) — demonstrates the potential
  suffix stacking directly beneath the conditional-mood suffix, itself already agglutinatively
  layered on the bare verb stem. Unit 20 Grammar §4 / Practice ex. 2 dialogue, p. 313.

### Unit 19, Grammar §6 — Duration and deadline time expressions (`-t`/`-ra`-`-re`/`-ig`/`-ra`-`-re`
for "by")

A four-way system for expressing time periods: (a) a *negative* period extending to the present uses
a 3rd-singular-possessive-suffixed duration noun + `nem` + past tense, e.g. "Ezer éve nem láttalak"
("I haven't seen you for ages," lit. "for a thousand years"); made *positive*, the meaning shifts to
"X ago" rather than "for X" — "Két éve voltam Londonban" ("I was in London two years ago") vs. "Két
éve nem voltam Londonban" ("I haven't been to London for two years"), a genuine polarity-conditioned
meaning-shift worth flagging. (b) An ongoing period into the present also takes the 3rd-singular
possessive ending on the duration noun even without negation, e.g. "Már két hónapja... dolgozom"
("I've been working... for two months now"). (c) A period something is/was *to last* uses `-ra`/`-re`
with dynamic (motion) verbs ("Két hétre mentünk Olaszországba," "we went to Italy for two weeks") vs.
`-ig` with static verbs ("Két napig tartott a konferencia," "the conference lasted for two days") — a
genuine dynamic/static verb-class-conditioned case-ending split. (d) A deadline ("by X time") also
uses `-ra`/`-re`, e.g. "öt órára" ("by five o'clock"). Question words for each: `Mennyi ideje?`/
`Mióta?` (for how long, retrospective), `Mennyi időre?`/`Hány napra?` (for how long, prospective),
`Mennyi ideig?`/`Meddig?` (how long, duration), `Hány órára?`/`Hányra?`/`Mikorra?` (by when). pp.
290-292.

### Unit 19, Grammar §7 — The infinitive with possessive endings

Used for impersonal statements ("Itt nem szabad dohányozni," "smoking is not allowed here"); to
specify *who* is (dis)allowed/able, possessive endings attach directly to the infinitive, omitting the
linking `i` except in 3rd singular/plural, where `i` is retained (`tudnom`, `tudnod`, `tudnia`,
`tudnunk`, `tudnotok`, `tudniuk` — full back-vowel/front-vowel/ö-ő-ü-ű-final paradigms given). Governs
with `érdemes`, `kell`, `lehet`, `muszáj`, `sikerül`, `szabad`, `tilos` (see Vocabulary). To emphasize
or contrast the person, the *dative* ending is used instead on a separate pronoun/noun ("Editnek nem
szabad dohányoznia, mert terhes" — "Edit mustn't smoke because she's pregnant"). p. 292-293.

### Unit 20, Grammar §1 — The conditional mood (present and past)

The single richest grammar point in this dispatch's range. English's three conditional types map
onto Hungarian as: **Type i** ("if it rains...") → ordinary present or future tense, no special mood.
**Type ii** ("if it rained...") → the **present conditional**, required in *both* clause-halves
(unlike optional English backshifting) — indefinite conjugation `-nék/-nál/-na/-nánk/-nátok/-nának`
(back-vowel) or `-nék/-nél/-ne/-nénk/-nétek/-nének` (front-vowel, and notably back-vowel verbs
*also* take the front-vowel ending here — a genuine harmony exception flagged explicitly by the
source); definite conjugation `-nám/-nád/-ná/-nánk/-nátok/-nák` (back) or `-ném/-néd/-né/-nénk/
-nétek/-nék` (front). Stems ending in `-ít` or two consonants take a linking `a`/`e` vowel. A
full irregular-verb table is given for `megy/jön/eszik/iszik/lesz/van/tesz/vesz/hisz/visz/alszik/
fekszik` in both conjugations. **Type iii** ("if it had rained...") → the **past conditional**,
formed simply by past tense + `volna`, required in both clause-halves; `lenni`'s full past-
conditional paradigm is tabulated separately (`lettem volna`, etc.). The conditional additionally
covers: (a) polite requests ("Megmondaná, hol a jegypénztár?"), (b) wishes, often introduced by
`bárcsak`/`bár`/`csak` ("Bárcsak már péntek lenne!"), (c) *should/should have/ought to* via `kellene`/
`kellett volna` + infinitive, (d) *could/could have* via the plain conditional. pp. 304-308.

### Unit 20, Grammar §2 — Verbal particles changing the meaning of the verb

Beyond directional/aspectual particles from earlier units, `meg` (no direct English equivalent) is a
frequent further meaning-modifying particle. Two worked particle-families are tabulated: `beszél`
("speak") → `lebeszél (vkit) (vmiről)` "talk sy out of sg," `rábeszél (vkit) (vmire)` "persuade sy to
do sg," `megbeszél (vmit)` "discuss sg," `visszabeszél (vkinek)` "answer sy back"; and `vesz` ("take")
→ `kivesz`, `bevesz gyógyszert`, `levesz ruhát`, `felvesz ruhát`. The source explicitly advises
learning each particle-verb combination as a distinct lexical item rather than trying to derive
meaning compositionally. p. 308-309.

### Unit 20, Grammar §3 — Separating the verbal particle and the verb

A defined closed set of governing words (`kell`, `lehet`, `tud`, `fog`, `tetszik`, `akar`, `szokott`,
`szeretne`) can split a particle-verb compound when that whole complex (particle + governing word +
verb) is the sentence's focus: "Le kell beszélnünk Tamást a tervéről." If instead some *other*
sentence element is the focus, the particle reattaches directly to the infinitive as one word,
displaced after the focused element: "A filmre akarlak rábeszélni." If the governing word is negated,
the particle likewise stays attached to the infinitive as one word: "Sajnos ma nem tudok elmenni." In
past-conditional negated sentences, `volna` itself intervenes between the verb and its particle: "Ha
tudtam volna..., elmentem volna" but negated "...nem mentem volna el." pp. 309-310.

### Unit 20, Grammar §4 — The `-hat`/`-het` ending (can, could, may, might, be allowed to, must)

Expresses possibility, permission, or deduction; attaches to any verb stem and can itself then be
conjugated in present, past, or conditional (`moshatok` "I may wash," `cigarettázhatott` "he was
allowed to smoke," `kérhetnénk` "we could ask"). A closed irregular-stem list is given: `alszik→alhat`,
`eszik→ehet`, `fekszik→fekhet`, `fürdik→fürödhet`, `hisz→hihet`, `iszik→ihat`, `jön→jöhet`, `megy→
mehet`, `tesz→tehet`, `ugrik→ugorhat`, `lesz→lehet`, `vesz→vehet`, `visz→vihet`. Possibility is also
commonly expressed periphrastically via `lehet, hogy...` ("it is possible that..."). pp. 310-311.

### Unit 20, Grammar §5 — Indirect statements

Unlike English, Hungarian indirect (reported) statements do NOT backshift tense — "Péter azt mondta:
'Holnap nem jövök dolgozni'" (direct) becomes "Péter azt mondta, hogy holnap nem jön dolgozni"
(indirect), both clauses staying present tense; only the verb's *person* form changes (1st→3rd here).
`hogy` ("that") is always preceded by a comma in the indirect-statement construction. p. 311.

### Unit 20, Grammar §6 — Further time expressions: `óta`, the `-t` ending, `-kor`

`óta` ("since, for") is a postposition following the noun/time-expression; primary sense "since" (e.g.
"1986 óta Angliában élek"), but also expresses an unspecified negative-extending period as "for" (e.g.
"napok óta," "hetek óta," "évek óta" — "for days/weeks/years," typically with a negated main verb).
The `-t` ending marks a *period spent doing something* ("Három napot töltöttünk a tengerparton," "the
burglar spent 5 years in prison" — `A betörő 5 évet ült börtönben`). `-kor` ("at/on") marks fixed
calendar-type occasions: `karácsonykor`, `szilveszterkor`, `húsvétkor`. pp. 311-312.

### Unit 21 (head), Grammar §1 — The imperative mood (indefinite conjugation only, within this
dispatch's range)

Formed with a `-j` ending on the verb stem (e.g. `adj`, `kérj`, `küldj`), then the imperative's own
full personal-ending paradigm (not just 2nd person, since Hungarian's imperative also covers polite
commands to `maga`/`ön`/`maguk`/`önök`, which grammatically take 3rd-person forms, consistent with the
te/maga/ön system documented in `established/034`). Indefinite conjugation tabulated in full for
back-vowel, front-vowel, and ö/ő/ü/ű-final-vowel verb classes: `adjak/adj/adjon/adjunk/adjatok/
adjanak`; `kérjek/kérj/kérjen/kérjünk/kérjetek/kérjenek`; `küldjek/küldj/küldjön/küldjünk/küldjetek/
küldjenek`. The source explicitly flags a **colloquial-register variant**: some speakers use a longer
2nd-person-singular form — `adjál` instead of `adj`, `kérjél` instead of `kérj`, `küldjél` instead of
`küldj` — an explicitly source-annotated register/variant pair, directly comparable in kind to the
formal/informal pairs already catalogued in `established/034` (`krumpli`/`burgonya`, etc.), though
here the variation is *morphological length* rather than lexical choice. p. 320. (Remainder of Unit
