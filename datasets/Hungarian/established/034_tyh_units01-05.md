Books, 2003), "Teach Yourself Hungarian [2nd Ed (2003)].pdf" (scanned, no text layer — read
visually). Printed pages 1-81 (front matter, Introduction, Pronunciation guide, and Units 1-5, the
whole of "Part One"), corresponding to **PDF pages 11-91** of the 388-page scan.

**Page-offset verification (mandatory check per the vision-reading gotcha):** empirically confirmed
by direct inspection, not assumed — PDF page 5 shows printed roman numeral "iii," PDF page 17 shows
printed page "7," PDF page 25 shows the Unit 1 title page (printed p. 15, per the book's own table
of contents), PDF page 75 shows the Unit 5 title page (printed p. 65), and PDF page 91 shows the
explicit "welcome to Part Two!" sign-off at the end of Unit 5 (printed p. 81, immediately followed by
the Unit 6 title page at PDF 92/printed p. 82). This confirms a **flat, single-page-per-image scan**
(the "two printed pages per image" gotcha does NOT apply to this source) with a consistent
`printed_page = PDF_page − 10` offset throughout this range. Assigned dispatch range was PDF 1-90;
extended by one page to PDF 91 to complete Unit 5 and stop at the book's own "Part One" boundary
rather than an arbitrary mid-unit cutoff, per the coverage rule's preference for complete units.

**Units covered, per the book's own table of contents (verified directly):**

**Coverage note:** every distinct vocabulary item and every grammar point from the Dialogues, "Other
expressions"/cultural-notes sections, and Grammar sections of each unit. Per the coverage rule,
repetitive Practice-section drill items that only recombine vocabulary already captured are not
transcribed exercise-by-exercise; however, Practice-section vocabulary boxes that introduce genuinely
new lexical items (very common in this course — e.g. restaurant menus, fruit names, city-sight names)
ARE captured, since the coverage rule requires every distinct vocabulary item, not just Dialogue-
section items. Exercise scaffolding text itself (instructions, crossword-puzzle clue lists, true/false
statement prompts) is not extracted as vocabulary.

**Vision-reading note:** this PDF is a clean, modern (2005) digitally-typeset scan — no handwritten
marginalia or library stamps were found anywhere in this range. One page (Unit 1, printed p. 25, an
exercise showing a signature-style "Pontifex Zsuzsa vagyok..." sample) uses a script/cursive-look
font to illustrate handwriting for a reading exercise — this is the book's own printed typeset
apparatus (a font choice, not an added annotation) and is treated as legitimate printed content per
the spec's marginalia-guard distinction, not excluded. Text throughout this range was crisp and
legible; Vision Reading Confidence is `verified` or `plausible_unverified` for nearly every entry
(see per-row flags for the few `low_confidence` exceptions, mostly faint red-diacritic pronunciation
guides).

**Relationship to prior Hungarian `established/` files:** this is a fourth distinct genre/era of
source for Hungarian in this project (after Carol Rounds' modern reference grammar, the 1962 FSI
audio-lingual course, and the phraseology/proverb collections) — a 2003 UK-published communicative-
method course explicitly aimed at contemporary spoken Hungarian, with its own independent vocabulary
set organized around practical situations (restaurants, shopping, transport, addresses). Extracted in
full rather than as a non-redundant supplement: while some grammar territory overlaps with prior
sources (object/accusative -t ending, -ba/-be and -ban/-ben endings, vowel harmony, cardinal/ordinal
numbers, van/nincs), this course's own worked dialogues, culturally-grounded practical vocabulary, and
its own explicit register annotations (see Notable findings) are substantially independent.

### Morpheme Breakdown — agglutinative forms worth decomposing

- **`huszonhét`** ("27") = `húsz` ("20") + `-on` (linking consonant/vowel epenthesis) + `hét` ("7") —
  illustrates the general teens/tens-plus-ones compounding rule (`30+`: base ten-word + ones-word,
  no separate conjunction), Grammar points U4§1.
- **`harminckettő`/`harminckét`** ("32") = `harminc` ("30") + `kettő`/`két` ("2," free/bound forms) —
  same compounding rule, with the standalone/attributive `kettő`/`két` distinction carried through.
- **`négyszáznegyvenöt`** ("445") = `négy` ("4") + `száz` ("hundred") + `negyven` ("40") + `öt` ("5")
  — hundreds-tens-ones agglutinative stacking, no internal spaces or conjunctions in Hungarian
  numeral-words. Grammar points U4§1.
- **`huszonnegyedik`** ("24th") = `huszon-` (bound allomorph of `húsz` "20," used before another
  numeral) + `negyedik` ("4th") — ordinal suffix `-dik`/`-adik`/`-edik`/`-odik` attaches to the whole
  cardinal-number stem, itself already agglutinatively composed. Grammar points U5§1.
- **`Budapesten`** ("in Budapest") = `Budapest` + `-en` (superessive "in/at," front-vowel-harmony
  allomorph after the irregular-locative noun class) — see the full `mi?/hová?/hol?` three-way
  paradigm for `Budapest`, `Magyarország`, `repülőtér`, `tér`, `út`, `körút`, Grammar points U5§4.
- **`kerületben`** ("in the district") = `kerület` + `-ben` (superessive, front-vowel harmony) vs.
  **`kerületbe`** ("into the district") = `kerület` + `-be` (illative) — same noun, two different
  case suffixes showing the systematic -ba/-be (motion-into) vs. -ban/-ben (static-in) contrast that
  is one of this unit's two central grammar points (Grammar points U4§2, U5§4).
- **`tudnak`** ("they know" / "you [pl., formal] know") = `tud-` (root "know") + `-nak` (3rd person
  plural / formal-plural present-tense agglutinative suffix, back-vowel harmony allomorph of
  `-nek`) — paired with **`beszélnek`** = `beszél-` + `-nek` (front-vowel harmony allomorph),
  showing the vowel-harmony-conditioned suffix-allomorphy pattern general to this typology. Grammar
  points U3§1.

## Grammar points

### Unit 1A-1B (implicit; see FSI/Rounds cross-references) — te/maga/ön register system

The single richest grammar/register point in this range: Hungarian marks second-person address with
a three-way (or, counting plurals, six-way) formality system rather than a simple tu/vous binary —
`te`/`ti` (familiar), `maga`/`maguk` (neutral-to-formal, the everyday default with strangers/service
staff), and `ön`/`önök` (most formal, distant/official register, typical of written or highly formal
spoken contexts). Unlike French/German-style T-V systems, `maga`/`ön`/`önök` all grammatically take
**3rd-person** verb forms, not a dedicated 2nd-person-formal conjugation. Pertu (see Vocabulary) names
the social ritual of two people agreeing to move from formal to familiar address.

### Unit 3, Grammar §1 — Present tense, standard verbs, plural forms

Regular back-vowel and front-vowel plural present-tense paradigms given in full for `tud` and
`beszél` (mi/ti/maguk-önök/ők), completing the singular paradigm presumably given in Unit 2 (not in
this dispatch's page range in visible form, but referenced). Confirms the vowel-harmony-conditioned
`-unk/-ünk`, `-tok/-tek`, `-nak/-nek` suffix-allomorph system.

### Unit 3, Grammar §2 — `is` ("too, also, as well")

`is` always follows the word it modifies/scopes over, is unstressed, and is pronounced as if fused
onto the preceding word. Its position — not stress, as in English — carries the scope distinction:
`Én is kérek sört` ("I also want beer," i.e. someone else does too) vs. `Én sört is kérek` ("I want
beer as well," i.e. in addition to something else I'm ordering). p. 44.

### Unit 3, Grammar §3 — `pedig` ("and, however, but, on the other hand")

A contrastive connector that characteristically appears as the *second* element, after a pause, in
the second half of a two-part sentence (e.g. "Ica kávét kér, Ági **pedig** teát" — "Ica wants coffee,
but Ági wants tea"). p. 45.

### Unit 3, Grammar §4 — the `-t` (object/accusative) ending

The grammatical object takes obligatory `-t`. Vowel-final stems take `-t` directly (with the familiar
a→á, e→é lengthening before it: `mi`→`mit`, `kávé`→`kávét`); consonant-final stems usually need a
linking vowel (`o`/occasionally `a` for back-vowel words, `e`/occasionally `ö` for front-vowel words:
`étlap`→`étlapot`, `pörkölt`→`pörköltet`), though a subset of consonant-final stems (`sör`→`sört`,
`asztal`→`asztalt`) take no linking vowel at all — an unpredictable-by-rule subclass the source says
must simply be memorized per-word (hence the course's practice of always glossing a new noun's object
form alongside its dictionary form). Certain verbs (`kér`, and notably `köszönöm` in the fixed idiom
`Köszönöm a vacsorát`) obligatorily govern an object. p. 45-46.

### Unit 4, Grammar §1 — Cardinal numbers

Full 0-30 base forms plus the systematic compounding rule from 30 up (ten-word + ones-word, no
conjunction — see Morpheme Breakdown). `Kettő` (free-standing) vs. `két` (attributive-only) is a
genuine complementary-distribution pair, not free variation — though the source notes `kettő` is
sometimes substituted for `két` in noisy speech specifically to avoid confusion with `hét` ("seven"),
a rare explicit *phonetic-confusability-driven* register/usage note. Numerals also govern a singular
noun (`sok olasz turista volt` — "there were *many* [sg.] Italian tourists"), unlike English plural
agreement after quantifiers. No comma is used after thousands in Hungarian numerals (a comma instead
marks the decimal point); years are read out as one continuous compound number. pp. 57-58.

### Unit 4, Grammar §2 — the `-ba`/`-be` ending (illative, "into/to")

Marks motion toward an enclosed destination. Back-vowel words take `-ba`, front-vowel `-be` (with the
usual a→á/e→é lengthening). Governs (a) all foreign place names and some Hungarian ones, and (b)
nouns denoting enclosed spaces (`mozi`, `színház`, `opera`, `bank`, `uszoda`, `hotel`). `Budapest` and
`Magyarország` are explicitly flagged as irregular, taking `-ra`/`-re` instead — the sublative ending,
not otherwise covered in this range's grammar sections but named here as a genuine exception. p. 58-59.

### Unit 4, Grammar §3 — `Mennyibe kerül?` price-`-ba`/`-be` construction

The price/currency noun in a cost-answer takes `-ba`/`-be`, not the bare cardinal: "Ötszázhatvan
**forintba**" ("560 forints"). p. 59.

### Unit 4, Grammar §4 — `nincs`/`nincsen` ("is not")

Negative of `van`. Both forms are correct though `nincs` is more common; word order inverts relative
to the affirmative (`Ági nincs itt`, not *`Nincs Ági itt`). Also serves as "there isn't any"/"we
haven't got any" (existential negation), contrasted with affirmative `van` used for "is there
any?"/"have you got?" p. 59-60.

### Unit 4, Grammar §5-6 — `megy` ("go") and `lesz` ("will be") as irregular verbs

Both are irregular and must be learned outright rather than derived from the regular back-/front-
vowel present-tense pattern seen in Unit 3§1. `Lesz`'s 2nd-person-singular form `leszel` is flagged
as a specific irregularity within the irregular paradigm (not the expected `*leszesz`). p. 60.

### Unit 5, Grammar §1 — Ordinal numbers

Formed by suffixing the cardinal-number stem (see Unit 4§1) with `-dik`/`-(a/e/o/ö)dik` per vowel
harmony; irregular for `első` "1st" and (to a lesser extent) `második`/`harmadik`/`negyedik`. Ordinals
answer `Hányadik?` ("which [in a numbered sequence]?") and take a full stop when written in figures
(`1.` = "1st"). Ordinals mark Budapest floor levels (written in Roman numerals) and, per the source's
own cross-reference, districts. p. 73.

### Unit 5, Grammar §2 — `van` ("is"), consolidated usage inventory

The source consolidates five distinct `van` functions seen piecemeal across earlier units: location
("Tamás Bécsben van"), state-of-health ("Ildi rosszul van"), weather ("Hideg van," "Meleg van" — an
impersonal/subjectless construction), existential "there is" ("Van itt valahol egy posta?"), and
availability ("Van gyulai kolbász?"). p. 73-74.

### Unit 5, Grammar §3 — `innen`/`onnan`/`itt`/`ott`/`ide`/`oda` (three-way locative adverbs)

Hungarian systematically distinguishes *where-from* (`Honnan?` → `innen`/`onnan`), *where-at*
(`Hol?` → `itt`/`ott`), and *where-to* (`Hova?`/`Hová?` → `ide`/`oda`) with three formally distinct
adverb sets, where English collapses all three onto "here"/"there." This is the same three-way
locative distinction independently documented for the full noun-case system in the FSI Basic Course
extraction (`013_fsi_vol1_units01-04.md`, Unit 4G's `hol/itt/ott` vs. `hova/ide/oda`) — this source
extends the same pattern to `innen`/`onnan`, completing the third leg (*from*) not covered in the FSI
file's Unit 1-4 range. p. 74.

### Unit 5, Grammar §4 — the `-ban`/`-ben` ending (inessive, "in/at, static location")

Sister ending to `-ba`/`-be` (§Unit 4§2): same governing-noun class (enclosed spaces, most foreign
place names), same back/front vowel-harmony split, same a→á/e→é lengthening rule, but marking static
location rather than motion-into. `Budapest`, `Magyarország`, `repülőtér`, `tér`, `út`, `körút` are
explicitly tabulated as an irregular subclass across all three locative dimensions

## Notable findings (colloquial/register/period vocabulary)

Per this project's specific interest in informal/colloquial-register material, the following are
flagged from this extraction:

1. **`krumpli` (colloquial) vs. `burgonya` (formal/menu register)** for "potato" — the single
   highest-value finding in this range: the source states outright, "**Krumpli** is the colloquial
   word for potato (like 'spud' in English). The official word is **burgonya**. This is used in
   menus, etc." (Unit 4, p. 56) — an explicit, source-annotated colloquial/formal minimal pair, the
   clearest register contrast captured in this dispatch.
2. **`kösz`/`köszi` (informal) vs. `köszönöm` (neutral)** for "thanks" — explicitly flagged by the
   source as informal-register-only shortenings (Unit 2), directly analogous in kind to the
   `krumpli`/`burgonya` pair though for a function word rather than a content noun.
3. **`szia`/`szevasz` (current informal) vs. `szervusz` (dated/older-generation informal)** — a
   genuine intra-colloquial-register split by *age cohort/period*, not just formality tier: the
   source explicitly frames `szervusz` as now old-fashioned and associated with an older generation,
   while `szia`/`szevasz` are the current informal norm (Unit 2). Worth flagging for the
   mechanics-analysis phase as an example of slang/informal-register *churn* within a single
   politeness tier, not just a formal/informal axis.
4. **`buli`** ("party") — a distinctly colloquial/youth-register noun, introduced without hedging in
   a boxed vocabulary aside ("You are invited to a **buli** (*party*)," Unit 2, p. 36). Not flagged by
   the source itself as colloquial the way `krumpli` is, but its register is unmistakable relative to
   the surrounding formal/neutral vocabulary of the same unit.
5. **`kocsma`/`italbolt` vs. `söröző`** for "pub" — the source explicitly connotation-marks
   `kocsma`/`italbolt` as "rather basic places for local workmen" (i.e. lower-register, blue-collar
   social connotation) versus `söröző` as the closer Western-pub equivalent (Unit 3, p. 42) — a rare
   case of the source annotating *social* register/connotation, not just formality.
6. **`vendéglő`** explicitly glossed as "usually cheaper" than `étterem` — a price/prestige-tier
   register distinction among near-synonyms for "restaurant" (Unit 3), paralleling the `kocsma`/
   `söröző` pattern for "pub."
7. **`deka` as the short/informal form of `dekagram`**, and **`troli` as the short/informal form of
   `trolibusz`** — two milder short-form/full-form register pairs (Units 4 and 5 respectively),
   weaker than the `krumpli`/`burgonya` contrast but the same underlying mechanism (colloquial
   clipping of a longer formal/technical term), worth noting as a recurring Hungarian slang-formation
   pattern (clipping) distinct from the morphological-play patterns already documented from other
   Hungarian sources.
8. **Ticket-inspector cartoon (Unit 5, p. 81)**, captioned "SZIGOR" ("Strictness"), with dialogue "—
   Jegyeket, bérleteket! [Tickets, travelcards!] — De Lujza, én Béla vagyok, a férjed… [But Lujza, I'm
   Béla, your husband…]" — a genuine piece of printed Hungarian visual/verbal humor (public-transport-
   inspector stereotype), credited in the book to cartoonist "Szmodis Imre." Quoted here only as a
   short two-line excerpt per copyright discipline; the joke itself (a wife apparently not recognizing
   her own husband when demanding tickets) is a culturally-specific bit of everyday Budapest humor
   worth flagging for tone/register reference even though it isn't lexical slang per se.
9. **`néni`/`bácsi`** (Unit 1 culture note) — the customary respectful-affectionate address terms for
   older women/men (used with a first name, e.g. "Mari néni"), a register-conditioned address-term
   pattern parallel to the `úr`+profession pattern also noted this unit.
10. No `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]`-style dialectal tags or explicit comparative regional-variant annotations
    appear anywhere in this page range — like the FSI course, this is a single-dialect (standard
    Budapest-area spoken) course. `csárda` is the only word given an explicit `regional` usage-tier
    tag above, and even that is a connotation/setting note (rustic country inn) rather than a marked
    dialectal form.
