### Morpheme Breakdown

- **szégyelli magát** "be ashamed of oneself" — `szégyell` (verb stem, "to feel shame") + `-i`
  (3sg definite present marker) + `magát` (reflexive pronoun "oneself," accusative of `maga`).
  A reflexive verb-phrase construction, not a single fused word, but idiomatically lexicalized.
- **diákkoromban** "in my student days" — `diák` (student) + `kor` (time/age/era) + `-om`
  (1sg possessive) + `-ban` (inessive "in"). Literally "in my student-time."
- **gyerekkoromban** "in my childhood" — `gyerek` (child) + `kor` (time/age/era) + `-om` (1sg
  possessive) + `-ban` (inessive "in"). Same `-kor` "life-stage/era" compounding pattern as
  `diákkoromban` immediately above — a productive Hungarian pattern for "during my X years."
- **öreg fejjel** "when one is not so young" — `öreg` (old) + `fej` (head) + `-jel` (instrumental
  "with"). Literally "with an old head" — idiomatic instrumental-case metaphor for age-conferred
  wisdom/hindsight.
- **időjárásjelentés** "weather forecast" — `idő` (time/weather) + `járás` (going, course,
  derived from `jár` "to go/walk" + nominalizing `-ás`) + `jelentés` (report, derived from
  `jelent` "to report" + `-és`). A three-noun compound; the glossary explicitly flags it `(c)` for
  taking front-vowel case endings despite `idő`/`járás` being back-vowel-looking components —
  compound-word vowel harmony is fixed by the final element, `jelentés` (front-vowel), not the
  compound's first element.
- **kérhetek/kérhetem** "I can ask" (indefinite/definite) — `kér` (verb stem "ask, request") +
  `-het` (potential/"can, may" suffix, front-vowel allomorph of `-hat`) + `-ek`/`-em` (1sg present
  personal ending, indefinite vs. definite conjugation). The `-hat/-het` suffix itself stacks
  further tense/mood/person morphology on top, e.g. `kérhettem` (`kér`+`het`+`t`[past]+`em`[1sg
  def.]) "I could ask/I was allowed to ask," and `kérhetném` (`kér`+`het`+`né`[conditional]+`m`[1sg
  def.]) "I would be able to ask" — see the full paradigm in Grammar points below.

## Grammar points

### 1. The imperative mood: full conjugation system

Unit 21's grammar section (spanning into this chunk from p.317, with the material captured here
running p.321-324) tabulates the imperative's **definite conjugation** in full: for back-vowel verbs
(`ad`→`adjam, add, adja, adjuk, adjátok, adják`), front-vowel verbs (`kér`→`kérjem, kérd, kérje...`),
and front-vowel verbs whose final vowel is ö/ő/ü/ű (`küld`→`küldjem, küldd, küldje...`). Note that
except for the 2nd-person singular, the definite-conjugation imperative personal endings match the
definite past tense; some speakers use a longer 2sg form (`adjad` instead of `add`).

**Pronunciation assimilation with the `-j` ending** (a fixed, spelling-vs-pronunciation mismatch
system, distinct from ordinary vowel harmony):
- `l`+`j` → pronounced `jj` (e.g. `induljon` → pronounced `indujjon`)
- `ll`+`j` → pronounced `jj` (e.g. `állj` → pronounced `ájj`)
- `n`+`j` → pronounced `nny` (e.g. `köszönjek` → pronounced `köszönnyek`)
- `d`+`j` → pronounced `ggy` (e.g. `adjunk` → pronounced `aggyunk`)
- `gy`+`j` → pronounced `ggy` (e.g. `hagyjátok` → pronounced `haggyátok`)

**Four groups of verb-stem-plus-`-j` changes:**
1. Verbs ending in a sibilant (`s`, `sz`, `z`, `dz`): the `-j` assimilates fully to the sibilant
   (`s+j=ss`, `sz+j=ssz`, `z+j=zz`, `dz+j=ddz`), e.g. `olvas`→`olvass`, `néz`→`nézz`.
2. Verbs ending in `-st`/`-szt`: the `t` drops and the same sibilant-assimilation applies
   (`st+j=ss`, `szt+j=ssz`), e.g. `fest`→`fess`, `választ`→`válassz`.
3. Verbs ending in a short vowel + `t`: the final `t` and `-j` both become `ss`, e.g. `fut`→`fuss`;
   `lát` and `megbocsát` are explicitly grouped here too (`lásd`).
4. Verbs ending in a long vowel + `t`, or a consonant + `t`: `-j` becomes `s` (not doubled), e.g.
   `tanít`→`taníts`, `ért`→`érts`; pronounced `ccs` after a vowel, `cs` after a consonant.

**Fully irregular imperatives:** `eszik, iszik, lesz, tesz, vesz, visz, hisz` (eat, drink,
become/will-be, put, buy/take, carry, believe) each have a complete irregular indefinite/definite
paradigm (`egyem, edd/egyed*, egye...`; `*both forms correct, first more frequent`). A separate
irregular class covers `-ik` verbs whose imperative is built from the **infinitive** stem rather than
the 3sg present — `alszik`(`aludni`)→`aludj`, `fekszik`(`feküdni`)→`feküdj`, `megy`(`menni`)→`menj`,
`ugrik`(`ugorni`)→`ugorj`, `fürdik`(`fürödni`)→`fürödj`, `mosakszik`(`mosakodni`)→`mosakodj`. `jön`
"come" is separately irregular (`gyere`/`jöjj`/`jöjjél`, `gyertek`/`jöjjetek`), with the source
explicitly noting `gyere`/`gyertek` are far more frequent than the alternates; `gyerünk` "let's go"
functions as a synonym of `menjünk`.

**Five uses of the imperative**, each illustrated: (a) orders/commands/requests/suggestions/advice —
note the verbal particle *follows* the verb here (`Ülj le!`); (b) negative commands, using `ne`
"don't" and `se` "don't...either" (which replaces `sem` in imperative sentences: `Sehova se
menjetek egyedül!` "Don't go anywhere on your own!"); (c) indirect statements reporting someone's
command/request/suggestion (`A titkárnő azt mondja..., hogy foglaljon helyet` "The secretary tells
the client to take a seat"); (d) inviting suggestions/instructions ("shall I/we...?" — `Kinyissam az
ablakot?` "Shall I open the window?"); (e) making a suggestion ("let's..." — `Menjünk haza!` "Let's
go home!").

### 2. Verbal particles marking completion or inception of an action

`meg`, `el`, and `ki` are the most frequent particles marking **completion** (`megtanul`, `elmond`,
`kiolvas` "finish reading"); `meg` and `el` (a subset) also mark the **beginning** of an action
(`megszólal` "start to ring," `elindulni` "to start (moving)"). This is presented as a semantic/
functional grammar point layered on top of the verbal-particle system already noted in earlier
Hungarian `established/` files — this source's specific completion-vs.-inception two-way split for
the same particle set is new information not previously captured.

### 3. Indirect question-word questions

When reporting a question-word question indirectly, tense and word order remain unchanged from the
direct question — no sequence-of-tense shift, e.g. `János azt kérdezte: "Hová jártok szórakozni?"` →
`János azt kérdezte, hogy hová járunk szórakozni.` ("János asked where we go out to.")

### 4. The `-ért` ending ("for," purpose)

`-ért` has only one invariant form (no vowel-harmony allomorphs — final `a`/`e` still change to
`á`/`é` before it as usual, e.g. `kenyérért`/`ruhámért`). It expresses the purpose of an action or "in
exchange for/for the sake of." The question words `Kiért?` ("Who for?") and `Miért?` ("What for?")
pair with it; the source explicitly flags **`Mért?`** as a colloquial contraction of `Miért?` — a
clean marked-register example. Purpose can alternatively be expressed with a bare purposive
infinitive (`Lemegyek kenyeret venni a boltba` "I'm going down to the shop to buy bread").

### 5. `Mellé` and the three-way Hol?/Hova?/Honnan? postposition system

Place-postpositions in Hungarian have three parallel forms depending on whether they answer
"Where (static)?", "Where to (motion toward)?", or "Where from (motion away)?" — fully tabulated:
`előtt/elé/elől` (in front of), `mögött/mögé/mögül` (behind), `alatt/alá/alól` (under),
`felett`/`fölött`/`fölé`/`fölül` (above — variant spellings for the static form), `körül/köré/—`
(around — no attested "from around" form given), `mellett/mellé/mellől` (next to), `között/közé/közül`
(between/among). `után` (after) and `felé` (towards) are noted as having only a single invariant
form each, an exception to the three-way pattern. All these postposition forms can additionally take
possessive-style personal endings (`előttem` "in front of me," `mögötted` "behind you").

### Appendix: The `-hat/-het` ("can, may" — potential) suffix, full paradigm

A single-page appendix giving the complete `-hat`/`-het` potential-suffix paradigm for a back-vowel
verb (`vár` "wait") and a front-vowel verb (`kér` "ask/want") side by side, across six full
person/number tables: present indefinite, present definite, past indefinite, past definite, present
conditional indefinite, and present conditional definite. E.g. indefinite present: `várhatok,
várhatsz, várhat, várhatunk, várhattok, várhatnak` / `kérhetek, kérhetsz, kérhet, kérhetünk,
kérhettek, kérhetnek`; present conditional definite: `várhatnám, várhatnád, várhatná, várhatnánk,
várhatnátok, várhatnák` / `kérhetném, kérhetnéd, kérhetné, kérhetnénk, kérhetnétek, kérhetnék`. This
confirms the suffix stacks productively with every tense/mood/conjugation-type combination already
established for the plain verb paradigm elsewhere in this language's `established/` files — i.e.
`-hat/-het` behaves as a genuine derivational-then-inflectable layer, not a frozen fixed form.
