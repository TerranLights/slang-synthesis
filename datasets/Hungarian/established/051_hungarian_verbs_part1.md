# Hungarian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *Hungarian Verbs and Essentials of Grammar*, Part One (Ch. 1–13)

**Source:** Miklós Törkenczy, *Hungarian Verbs and Essentials of Grammar*, 2nd Edition (McGraw-Hill,
2008), "Hungarian Verbs.pdf" (scanned, no text layer — read visually via `pdftoppm` + vision). PDF
pages 1–70 of 140 assigned; printed-page offset verified by direct inspection: PDF page 7 shows
printed page "3" (start of Chapter 1, "Pronunciation"), PDF page 62 shows printed page "58," and PDF
page 65 shows printed page "61" ("Part Two: Essentials of Grammar" divider) — confirming a flat,
single-page-per-image scan with a consistent `printed_page = PDF_page − 4` offset. This is a
front-matter-plus-two-part reference grammar (Part One "Hungarian Verbs," Part Two "Essentials of
Grammar"), not a per-verb dictionary/paradigm-table book in the "501 Verbs" sense — its actual
organization is topic-by-chapter, closely paralleling Carol Rounds' Chapter 4 in scope.

**Scope decision:** Part One ("Hungarian Verbs," Chapters 1–13) ends cleanly at printed p. 59 = PDF
page 63, immediately before the "Part Two: Essentials of Grammar" divider page (PDF 64) and Chapter
14 "The Article" (PDF 65, printed p. 61). Although the assigned PDF range technically extended to
page 70 (printed p. 66), this extraction **stops at the end of Part One** (PDF 63) rather than
continuing into Part Two's Article and Noun chapters — that content is a different topic (nouns/
articles, not verbs), is out of scope for a file titled "verbs," and already has its own established
coverage via `002_chapter5_nouns.md`. PDF pages 64–70 are left for a separate Part-Two-scoped
dispatch if this book is ever extracted further. This mirrors the boundary-respecting practice used
in `034_tyh_units01-05.md`.

**Non-redundant-supplement note (mandatory per the dispatch pattern):** Rounds' Chapter 4 (already
extracted as `004_chapter4_verbs_part1.md` and `005_chapter4_verbs_part2.md`) covers almost exactly
the same topical ground as this book's Part One: definite/indefinite conjugation, present/past/
conditional/conjunctive-imperative, the periphrastic future, -ik verbs, verb-stem irregularity
classes (including the "group of seven" — here called the "ten irregular verbs," since Törkenczy
folds *van*/*jön*/*megy* into the same discussion Rounds treats separately — v-stems, -szik classes),
infinitives, tense usage, verbal derivational suffixes (causative, potential/-hat/-het, frequentative),
and verbal prefixes (coverbs). **Skipped as redundant:** the core grammatical rules themselves
wherever this book states the same rule Rounds already documents (definiteness triggers, the
periphrastic future's two word orders, the "group of seven"/*van*/*jön*/*megy* irregular paradigm
shapes, the causative/potential/frequentative suffix mechanics, the coverb-separability rule). Full
6-person paradigm tables for the ten irregular verbs and the reflexive suffix are **not**
bulk-transcribed here (copyright discipline; Rounds already documents equivalent forms in less
complete but sufficient detail). **Kept as genuinely new:**
1. A far more systematic IPA-referenced phonology chapter than any prior Hungarian source in this
   project (full vowel/consonant charts with narrow-IPA transcription, explicit voice-assimilation
   rule).
2. A much fuller **vowel-deleting verb-stem class** inventory (~40 verbs) — Rounds' Ch. 4 only
   mentioned a handful of fleeting-vowel examples in passing.
3. A dedicated **reflexive-suffix** derivational category (-kodik/-kezik/-ködik etc.) that Rounds
   treats only incidentally (as part of a couple of -ik-verb glosses), not as its own productive class.
4. A near-exhaustive **verbal-prefix (coverb) inventory** (~48 prefixes) — substantially fuller than
   Rounds' "six most common + a longer list."
5. Two fairly complete **modal/quasi-modal predicate inventories** (verbs-plus-complex-verbs taking a
   bare infinitive; verbs-plus-complex-verbs taking a dative subject) that Rounds only illustrates
   with scattered individual examples across many pages, never as a consolidated list.
6. A handful of new sample/paradigm verbs and stem-class members not seen in any prior Hungarian
   `established/` file.
7. A genuine register/period annotation on the *jön* imperative forms not previously captured (see
   Notable findings).

**Vision-reading note:** clean, modern (2008), digitally-typeset scan throughout this range. No
handwritten marginalia, underlining, library stamps, or any non-print annotation was found on any of
the ~55 pages inspected. Vision Reading Confidence is `verified` for all entries below (crisp,
unambiguous print) unless individually flagged otherwise.

**Morphological typology note.** Hungarian is agglutinative (see `../00_Extraction_Checklist.md`).
Several entries below carry a **Morpheme Breakdown** note where the source's own stem-alternation
tables expose compositional structure.

### Morpheme Breakdown — agglutinative forms worth decomposing

- **`fésülködtél`** ("you combed your hair") = `fésül-` ("comb") + `-köd-` (reflexive suffix,
  front-vowel/-k- allomorph) + `-t-` (past tense) + `-él` (2sg indefinite) — illustrating the
  productive reflexive-suffix category (`-kodik/-kedik/-ködik`, `-kozik/-kezik/-közik`, `-ódik/-ődik`,
  `-ó(d)zik/-ő(d)zik`), a category this book treats as its own dedicated derivational class where
  Rounds only names it in passing. p. 53
- **`sodorja`** ("he/she/it rolls it") vs. **`sodrom`** ("I roll it") = `sodor-` ("roll," a
  vowel-deleting stem) + `-ja`/`-om` — the stem vowel before the final consonant deletes before a
  vowel-initial suffix (`sodor+om→sodrom`) but is retained before a consonant-initial suffix
  (`sodor+ja→sodorja`); the general shape of the ~40-verb vowel-deleting class in Ch. 9. p. 35
- **`kalap+jai+m+é+i+t`** ("those of my hats," accusative) = `kalap` ("hat") + `-jai` (plural) +
  `-m` (1sg possessive) + `-é` (external-possessed) + `-i` (external-possessed plural) + `-t`
  (accusative) — the book's own formal five-slot noun-structure schema (Stem + NUMBER + POSSESSIVE +
  (EXTERNAL POSSESSED + EXTERNAL POSSESSED NUMBER) + CASE), cited here only as a cross-reference
  since it belongs to Ch. 15 (Nouns), outside this dispatch's own scope, but directly informative for
  this project's morphological-mechanics analysis of Hungarian's agglutinative noun as well as verb
  system. p. 62

## Grammar points

### Chapter 1 — Pronunciation (largely new, not covered this precisely by prior sources)

A full 44-letter alphabet with IPA-transcribed letter names, followed by complete vowel (14
phonemes, in 7 short/long pairs) and consonant (~25 phonemes) charts with narrow IPA and an English-
or German-comparison gloss for each. States explicitly that vowel and consonant letters have
constant phonetic values (nearly 1:1 grapheme-phoneme correspondence), with archaic-spelling proper
names as the main exception (`Kossuth` [koʃut], `Dessewffy` [dɛʒø:fi]). Documents an explicit
**voice-assimilation rule**: voiced obstruents (b, d, gy, g, dz, dzs, v, z, zs) devoice before a
following voiceless obstruent, and voiceless obstruents voice before a following voiced obstruent,
regardless of spelling (`adhat` [ɔthɔt], `zsákból` [ʒa:gbo:l]). This is a considerably more precise
phonetic treatment than any prior Hungarian source in this project (FSI Vol. 1, Rounds, or Teach
Yourself Hungarian). pp. 3–5.

### Chapters 2–8 — Definite/indefinite conjugation, present, past, conditional, conjunctive-
imperative, future, -ik verbs (redundant with Rounds Ch. 4)

These chapters cover the same ground as Rounds' §4.1–§4.3.9 (already extracted): the definite vs.
indefinite conjugation trigger conditions, the four finite paradigms across both conjugations, and
-ik-verb peculiarities. The grammar rules themselves are not re-documented here. Genuinely new value
found only in the specific *sample verbs* used (see Vocabulary) and in one fuller lexical-class
inventory (the `-ad/-ed` past-tense-irregularity class, ~10 verbs, p. 24–25, fuller than any
equivalent list in Rounds).

### Chapter 9 — Stems and Irregularities (the chapter's flagship non-redundant content)

Three genuinely fuller stem-alternation inventories than Rounds provides:
1. **Vowel-deleting stems** (~40 verbs, p. 35–36) — stems ending in vowel+single-consonant that
   delete the vowel before a vowel-initial suffix (`sodor`→`sodrom`) but keep it before a
   consonant-initial suffix (`sodor`→`sodorja`). Rounds mentions only a handful of examples in
   passing (`mosolyog`, `érez`, `üdvözöl`); this book gives a list roughly 6× as long.
2. **v-adding stems** (`lő`, `nő`, `sző`, `ró`, `fő`, `nyű`) — same class Rounds documents, confirmed
   here with matching paradigm shapes; `lő` and `ró` are new-to-this-project members.
3. **sz/d stems** (-uszik/-üszik verbs whose stem-final -sz becomes a vowel+d before a
   consonant-initial suffix: `alszik`→`aludtam`, `esküszik`→`esküdtem`) — confirmed matching Rounds'
   "-szik, sz~d~v class," with two new dual-variant members (`mosakszik~mosakodik`,
   `öregszik~öregedik`) and an explicit exception list (`tetszik`, `látszik`, `játszik` — these three
   conjugate as regular two-consonant stems instead).

The chapter's own "ten irregular verbs" section (`tesz`, `vesz`, `hisz`, `visz`, `lesz`, `eszik`,
`iszik`, `van`, `megy`, `jön`) gives complete 6-person paradigm tables across present, past,
conditional, and conjunctive-imperative for all ten — fuller in raw completeness than Rounds' partial
"group of seven" tables, but the same underlying paradigm shapes Rounds already documents (not
re-transcribed here per copyright discipline against bulk table reproduction). p. 38–42.

### Chapter 10 — Infinitives and Infinitival Constructions (partly new)

The core infinitive-formation rule (`-ni`/`-ani`/`-eni` depending on stem shape) matches Rounds
§4.4.4. New value: (a) a fuller worked list of which consonant-cluster/long-vowel+t stems take the
vowel-initial variant (~20 verbs, p. 43–44, including several verbs — `dönt`, `gyárt`, `hord`, `sért`,
`szánt`, `vonz` — not seen in prior Hungarian `established/` files); (b) the infinitive-with-
possessive-suffix paradigm (a nominal-like possessive agreement system attaching directly to the bare
infinitive stem, e.g. `adnom`, `nézned`, `ölnie`) — Rounds' declined-infinitive coverage
(`005_chapter4_verbs_part2.md` §4.4.5) documents the same construction with different sample verbs,
so this is a confirming rather than novel finding; (c) two substantial **complex-verb inventories**
(verbs/complex-predicates taking a bare infinitive with a nominative subject, ~30 items; and taking a
dative subject with either an impersonal infinitive or a possessive-suffixed infinitive, ~17 items) —
these consolidated lists are the single most useful new content in this chapter, since Rounds only
ever illustrates individual members of these classes scattered across many pages, never as a
complete inventory. pp. 43–48.

### Chapter 11 — Use of Tenses (mostly confirms Rounds, no major new rule)

Confirms the same core facts Rounds documents: no dedicated perfect/continuous tenses (coverbs supply
perfective meaning instead); present tense covers both present and future time; past tense covers
simple/continuous/perfect/perfect-continuous English equivalents; no formal sequence-of-tenses rule
(subordinate clauses may be in any tense regardless of the main clause's tense); the three-way
conditional-construction system (type 1: present/future in both clauses; type 2: present conditional
in both; type 3: past conditional in both). No new grammar rule beyond what Rounds §4.3 already
covers, though the illustrative sentence set is independently drawn. pp. 48–51.

### Chapter 12 — Verbal Suffixes (partly new — reflexive as its own category)

Causative (`-at/-et`/`-tat/-tet`) and potential (`-hat/-het`) sections confirm Rounds' §4.5.1/§4.5.4
mechanics exactly, including the same "group of seven"+`van`/`jön`/`megy` irregular -hat/-het forms
(`ehet`, `hihet`, `ihat`, `jöhet`, `mehet`, `tehet`, `lehet`, `vehet`, `vihet`) and a few idiosyncratic
causatives Rounds doesn't name (`csikorgat`, `altat`, `itat`, `etet`). Genuinely new: **Reflexive** is
presented as its own dedicated productive derivational suffix category (four suffix-shape families:
`-kodik/-kedik/-ködik`, `-kozik/-kezik/-közik`, `-ódik/-ődik`, `-ó(d)zik/-ő(d)zik`), with an explicit
note that not every verb bearing one of these suffix shapes is semantically reflexive
(`jelentkezik` "report/apply," `gondolkozik` "think" — reflexive-shaped but non-reflexive in
meaning). Rounds mentions reflexive-forming morphology only incidentally (e.g. `bemutatkozik` as "a
reflexive-forming variant of mutat"), never as its own named productive category — this is the
single clearest genuinely-new grammar point in the whole dispatch. pp. 52–54.

### Chapter 13 — Verbal Prefixes (fuller inventory, same mechanics as Rounds' coverbs)

Same core mechanics as Rounds' coverb chapter: prefixes can carry concrete spatial meaning
(`beugrik` "jump into," `kiugrik` "jump out of"), aspectual meaning (`meg-`/`el-`/`ki-` marking
completion, `meg-`/`el-` marking inception: `megcsinálta` "completed doing," `megszólalt` "started to
talk"), can be separated from the verb and moved for focus/emphasis (`Nem vettem meg a könyvet`), and
can combine with a verb non-compositionally (idiomatic opacity: `kicsinál` "kill, finish off" ≠
"do-out"; `átver` "cheat on someone" ≠ "beat-across"). The **prefix inventory itself is substantially
larger** than Rounds' "six most common + longer list" — this book tabulates ~48 distinct prefixes
with individual glosses in one place (p. 55), the fullest single-source coverb inventory captured for
Hungarian in this project so far. pp. 55–58.

## Notable findings (colloquial/register/period vocabulary)

Per this project's specific interest in informal/colloquial-register material, the following are
flagged from this extraction:

1. **`jöjj` (formal/full 2sg conjunctive-imperative of "come") explicitly called "practically
   extinct in colloquial Hungarian," with `gyertek` (2pl) explicitly marked "more colloquial than
   the long form `jöjjetek`"** — a genuine period/register-churn finding on a single irregular
   verb's imperative paradigm, similar in kind to the `szia`/`szervusz` age-cohort split already
   flagged in `034_tyh_units01-05.md`, but here it is the *grammar reference itself* (not a
   conversational-course culture note) making the colloquial-register call explicitly. p. 42.
2. **`kicsinál`** ("kill, finish off," lit. "do-out") and **`átver`** ("cheat on someone," lit.
   "beat-across") — the source's own two examples of semantically opaque coverb+verb combinations,
   both landing in colloquial/informal registers (a finish-off-a-person euphemism and a cheating/
   swindling verb) — genuinely slang-adjacent idiomatic compounds worth flagging for the
   mechanics-analysis phase as a coverb-driven idiom-formation pattern distinct from clipping
   (already documented from the TYH source) or morphological derivation. p. 57.
3. **`muszáj`** — a German-loanword modal ("must, have to," from *muss sein*) sitting alongside the
   native `kell` in the dative-subject predicate inventory; not explicitly register-tagged by the
   source itself, but recognizable as the more colloquial/informal member of the `kell`/`muszáj`
   near-synonym pair on general Hungarian-sociolinguistic grounds — flagged per this project's
   "explicit loanword register" convention rather than the source's own annotation. p. 47.
4. **`feled`** — a poetic/archaic doublet of the standard `elfelejt`/`felejt` ("to forget"), appearing
   unmarked in the `-ad/-ed` stem-class list; flagged here as an archaic-register item even though the
   source itself does not register-tag it, since it is a recognizable literary/older form. p. 24–25.
5. **`lót-fut`** — a reduplicative-idiom compound ("to run around, never stop, bustle about"),
   syntactically treated as a single irregular-infinitive verb (`lótni-futni`) alongside `lát`; a
   colloquial-register construction type (rhyming/near-synonym reduplication) not previously
   documented from any Hungarian source in this project. p. 44.
6. No `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]`-style dialectal tags or explicit comparative regional-variant annotations
   appear anywhere in this range — like every other Hungarian source extracted so far, this is a
   single-dialect (standard) reference grammar with no regional/dialectal marking at all.

No handwritten marginalia, library stamps, or non-print annotation of any kind were found anywhere in
this page range — this is a clean modern scan.
