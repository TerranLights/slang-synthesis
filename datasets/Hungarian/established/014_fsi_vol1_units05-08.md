# Hungarian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: FSI Basic Course Vol. 1, Units 5–8

**Source:** Augustus A. Koski & Ilona Mihalyfy, *Hungarian Basic Course* (Foreign Service
Institute, Washington D.C., 1962; PDF scan of the "Units 1–12" Student Text volume, Paper Capture
OCR by U.S. GPO, digitized 2006–2008). Units 5–8, printed pp. 85–178 (PDF pages 98–191 of 279).
Unit boundaries were located by direct inspection of the running headers/footers (each page's
footer gives its spelled-out printed page number plus the current unit number), not by arithmetic
page-offset estimation, per the extraction spec's PDF-gotcha guidance:

- **Unit 5** "Johnsonék vacsorára mennek" ("The Johnsons go to dinner") — printed pp. 85–105
  (PDF pp. 98–118)
- **Unit 6** "Szép város Budapest" ("Budapest, a beautiful city") — printed pp. 106–129
  (PDF pp. 119–142; basic sentences begin p. 107, p. 106 is an interstitial illustration page)
- **Unit 7** "Hivatal után Budapesten" ("After work in Budapest") — printed pp. 130–155
  (PDF pp. 143–168; basic sentences begin p. 131)
- **Unit 8** "Külföldi diplomaták Budapesten" ("Foreign diplomats in Budapest") — printed
  pp. 156–178 (PDF pp. 169–191; basic sentences begin p. 157)

**Coverage note.** Each unit follows the same drill-course structure: Basic Sentences (new
vocabulary + dialogue), Notes on Pronunciation, Notes on Grammar (For Home Study), then a long
sequence of Substitution/Transformation/Variation/Response drills, Conversation Practice,
Situations, and a Narrative. Per the extraction spec's coverage rule, this file captures **every
distinct vocabulary item introduced in each unit's Basic Sentences** (the vocabulary-introduction
point of the course) plus any new item surfacing in a unit's Narrative, **every grammar point**
from the Notes on Grammar sections, and the one genuinely new phonology topic (consonant
assimilation, unaspirated stops) is paraphrased rather than reproducing the drill word-lists
verbatim. The hundreds of drill sentences themselves are skipped wherever they only recombine
vocabulary already captured from the same unit's Basic Sentences — this is overwhelmingly the
case; drills in this course are built by substituting already-taught words into fixed frames, not
by introducing new ones. A "Supplementary List" of ~120 nouns at the end of Unit 7 (pp. 153–155)
is explicitly framed by the source itself as **a review list of nouns already used in Units 1–6**
("nouns which have occurred in the first six units") — this is intentionally skipped here as
redundant with prior extraction, not overlooked.

**OCR-quality caveat.** This PDF is a Paper Capture (OCR) scan of a 1962 typewritten government
document; `pdftotext` extraction shows real but localized character-level noise (e.g. curly
apostrophes/quotes rendered as stray characters, occasional `í`/`ő`/`ű` misreads, digit/letter
confusion in page-footer running numbers). This noise is heaviest in prose paragraphs and page
footers, essentially absent from the tabular Basic-Sentences vocabulary lines (which are short,
isolated, and cross-checked against their own English gloss and against repeated occurrences of
the same word elsewhere in the unit). No vocabulary entry below was left in genuine doubt; per
spec, `Vision Reading Confidence` is `n/a` throughout (a real machine text layer was used, not
vision-reading), and any row with residual uncertainty is flagged individually in its Notes field
rather than only in this header.

**Register/colloquial finding (flagged per the extraction spec's register-annotation rule):** Unit
8's grammar note on telling time (§D) contains an explicit, source-marked distinction between the
"citation" quarter-hour system taught in the Basic Sentences and **actual colloquial usage** —
see Grammar point 8.D below. This is the one explicit register annotation in this page range and
is a genuine colloquial-register data point (how Hungarians actually state non-round times in
speech), not a generic usage note.

**Morphological typology note.** Hungarian is agglutinative (see
`../00_Extraction_Checklist.md` and `../../00_Word_Concept_and_Morphological_Typology_Guide.md`).
Unit 7's central grammar topic is the **possessive suffix paradigm** — a full second layer of
person-marking suffixes that stack with case suffixes on a single noun stem — so this file's
Morpheme Breakdown section is concentrated there and in Unit 8's locative-suffix material.

## Morpheme breakdown

> **hivatalában** = hivatal ("office") + á (3sg possessive linking vowel, "his/her") + ban
> (inessive, "in") — "in his/her office." Illustrates the same double-stacking (possessive + case)
> that Unit 7's whole Grammar section is organized around; taken from Nagy's line about his
> sister's workplace. p. 145

> **külügyminisztériumban** = kül ("foreign") + ügy ("affair, matter") + minisztérium
> ("ministry") + ban (inessive) — "in the Ministry of Foreign Affairs." A three-root compound
> (itself built from `kül-ügy`, "foreign affair(s)," + `minisztérium`) with a case suffix stacked
> on top — contrast the simpler suffix-only stacking of `hivatalában` above. p. 172

> **leszállni** / **felszállni** = le- ("down," directional verbal prefix) + száll(ni) ("to
> fly/board") vs. fel- ("up," directional verbal prefix) + száll(ni) — a minimal pair showing how
> Hungarian's productive directional-prefix system (parallel to `meg-`'s completive function
> covered in Unit 6 Grammar E) distinguishes "get off" from "get on" a vehicle by prefix alone,
> not by a different root. p. 108

> **háziasszonynak** = házi ("of the house," adjectival) + asszony ("woman") + nak (dative, "to")
> — "to the lady of the house." Compound noun (`házi` + `asszony`) with a case suffix added on
> top, contrasted in this chapter's grammar notes with pure suffix-stacking on a simple stem. p. 85

> **öccse** = öcs ("younger brother," bound stem, not used bare) + cs [gemination of the stem-final
> consonant before the 3sg possessive] + e (3sg possessive, "his/her") — "his/her younger
> brother." One of the irregular possessive stems the source lists explicitly (alongside `apa`,
> `anya`, `bátya`), where the possessive suffix is added with a stem alternation not predictable
> from the general rule. p. 148

> **háromnegyed tizenegykor** = háromnegyed ("three-quarter[s]," itself három "three" + negyed
> "quarter") + tizenegy ("eleven") + kor (temporal suffix, "at") — "at a quarter to eleven."
> Illustrates how Hungarian's forward-looking time system (see Grammar point 8.D) builds a single
> compound time-word (numeral fraction + upcoming hour) before the invariant `-kor` suffix is
> even added — three layers of composition in one orthographic word. p. 182

## Grammar points

### 5.A The suffixes -ban/-ben vs. -ba/-be

`-ban`/`-ben` ("in, inside, within an enclosure") marks static location and is generally used
with verbs that don't indicate motion (or, if they do, motion confined within the area). `-ba`/
`-be` ("to, into") marks motion from outside to inside an enclosure, and is used with motion
verbs. Both suffixes attach to the noun's "basic form" (nominative), except that a stem-final `-a`
or `-e` lengthens to `-á-`/`-é-` before either suffix; for the plural, both attach to the already-
formed plural stem (cf. established/002 §5.3). pp. 91–93

### 5.B Present tense of irregular verbs: enni, menni, venni, inni, vinni, jönni

These six verbs ("to eat, go, take/buy, drink, carry, come") share irregular stems but mostly
regular personal endings, fully regular in the plural. The source tabulates all six across all six
persons; alternate 1sg forms are noted for some (`eszem`/`eszek`, `iszom`/`iszok`), with the `-m`
ending flagged elsewhere (Unit 8 Grammar A) as the more standard/preferred variant over the `-k`
variant some speakers use. p. 92

### 5.C Negation of "to be": nincs/nincsen, nincsenek

The negative counterparts of `van`/`vannak` are irregular: `nincs`/`nincsen` (singular) and
`nincsenek` (plural), not a regular `nem van`/`nem vannak`. For every other person of `lenni` ("to
be"), the regular rule applies (`nem` before the verb). In an equational sentence with no overt
verb, the negative is `nem` alone. p. 93

### 5.D Postpositions: mögött

Hungarian expresses concepts like English "behind" with a **postposition** — a word that follows,
rather than precedes, the noun it relates to (`A ház mögött kert van` "Behind the house there's a
garden," lit. "the house behind"). The noun governed by a postposition stays in its basic
(nominative) form. p. 94

### 5.E Haza vs. otthon

Both mean "home," but `haza` is used only where motion is expressed or implied ("to home"), while
`otthon` is used for static location ("at home") — paralleling the `ott`/`oda` ("there" static vs.
directional) distinction covered earlier in the course. p. 94

### 6.A Definite vs. indefinite verb conjugation

Every Hungarian transitive verb has two complete sets of personal endings — **definite** and
**indefinite** — chosen according to the direct object: a definite-reference object (marked by the
definite article, a proper noun, a personal pronoun like `magát`/`őt`, an interrogative like
`melyiket`, or a `hogy`-clause used as object) triggers the definite conjugation; an indefinite,
unmodified, quantity-limited, or first/second-person-pronoun object triggers the indefinite
conjugation (identical in form to intransitive-verb endings). The source tabulates the full
Present Definite paradigm for three vowel-harmony classes (`várom/várod/várja...`,
`kérem/kéred/kéri...`, `töröm/töröd/töri...`) and gives a summary chart of which object types
require which conjugation. This distinction has no equivalent in English and is one of the
course's largest single grammar topics to this point. pp. 113–116

### 6.B Assimilation of -j- in the Present Definite

When a definite-conjugation ending beginning in `-j-` (`-ja/-je`, `-juk/-jük`, etc.) attaches to a
stem ending in a sibilant (`s`, `z`, `sz`), the `-j-` fully assimilates: `s+j → ssz` written `ss`
(`olvas-` → `olvassa`), `z+j → zz` (`hoz-` → `hozza`), `sz+j → ssz` (`halász-` → `halássza`). p. 115

### 6.C Definite article before nouns used in a general sense

Where English omits an article for a category-wide statement ("freighters bring fruit and
vegetables"), Hungarian requires the definite article: `A teherszállító hajók hozzák a gyümölcsöt
és a zöldséget.` This generalizing use of the definite article was already noted for singular
nouns in established/002 §5.4.1.3; here the source extends it explicitly to plural/mass-reference
objects as well. p. 117

### 6.D The verbal prefix meg

Beyond its conjunction use ("and"), `meg-` functions as a highly productive separable verb prefix
that adds a **completive/perfective** sense to the verb — roughly paralleling English "up" in "eat
up the food," "burn up the house." `meg-` can separate from its verb and, under conditions to be
covered later, follow it. This is the first systematic introduction of Hungarian's verbal-prefix
aspect system, which recurs constantly from here on (`leszállni`/`felszállni` in Unit 6's own
Basic Sentences is a directional-prefix cousin of the same mechanism). p. 117

### 6.E Nem before a non-verb word (contrastive negation)

`Nem` doesn't always sit immediately before the verb to negate the whole sentence; placed before
some other word or phrase, it negates just that element in contrast to another
(`Nem magyar könyvet olvas, hanem angolt` "She isn't reading a Hungarian book, but an English
one"). This is a distinct construction from ordinary sentence negation (Unit 1), built around the
`nem X, hanem Y` frame. p. 118

### 7. The possessive system

**The core mechanism.** Hungarian has no possessive pronouns or apostrophe-`s`; possession is
marked entirely by suffixes on the possessed noun's stem, for every person (`ceruzám` "my pencil,"
`ceruzád` "your pencil," `ceruzája` "his/her pencil," `ceruzánk` "our pencil," `ceruzátok` "your
(pl.) pencil," `ceruzájuk` "their pencil"). The source tabulates this across 16 nouns spanning
every stem-alternation class already established (vowel-final, v-stem, low-vowel, consonant-
final) — the possessive auxiliary vowel before `-m` ("my"), `-d` ("thy"), and `-tok` ("your, pl.
familiar") is identical to the plural-`-k` linking vowel already learned; `-nk` ("our") attaches
directly to a vowel-final stem (with the usual `a→á`/`e→é` lengthening) or as `-unk/-ünk` to a
consonant-final stem. pp. 137–139

**Third-person `-j-`.** The 3sg/3pl possessive endings (`-ja/-je`, `-juk/-jük`) drop the `-j-` for
stems ending in `i, gy, ly, ny, s, sz, z, zs, cs` — otherwise whether `-j-` appears is largely
unpredictable and must be memorized per word (the source flags this explicitly rather than
claiming a clean rule). Irregular possessive stems noted this unit: `apa` ("father"), `anya`
("mother"), and `bátya` ("older brother") all lose their final vowel before 1st/2nd-person
possessive suffixes (`apám`, `anyám`, `bátyám`) but keep an irregular 3rd-person form (`apja`,
`anyja`, `bátyja`); `fiú` ("son") and `öcs` ("younger brother") have their own irregular paradigms.
pp. 139

**Possessive patterns.** The English "possessive adjective + noun" pattern (`my friend`) is
generally preceded by the definite article in Hungarian (`a barátom`) *except* with a proper noun
or a noun used in direct address. To emphasize possession, Hungarian adds the definite article
plus an explicit personal pronoun before the usual possessive form (`Ez az én barátom` "This is
*my* friend"). `Ő` disambiguates "his/her" vs. "their" *only* by which possessive-ending set
follows it (`az ő kabátja` "his/her coat" vs. `az ő kabátjuk` "their coat") — the plural `ők` is
never used this way. When the possessor is itself a noun (John's book, the girls' family), the
pattern is: possessor noun (basic form) + possessed noun with 3rd-person possessive suffix
(`János könyve`, `a lányok családja`); if the possessor noun is *itself* modified by a possessive
adjective, both possessor and possessed carry possessive endings (`a barátom ceruzája` "my
friend's pencil"). An alternative possessive construction is deferred to Unit 9. pp. 140–141

**Possessive as direct object.** Any possessed noun used as a direct object requires the linking
vowel `-a-`/`-e-` (or `-á-`/`-é-` after a possessive ending in `-a`/`-e`) before the accusative
`-t`, and — because the object is inherently definite once possessed — the verb must be in the
**definite** conjugation (`Nagyon szeretik a feleségemet` "They like my wife a lot"), directly
applying Unit 6's definite/indefinite distinction. p. 141

### 8.A "Ik" verbs

A subset of verbs (mostly intransitive) take `-ik` rather than a "zero" ending in the 3rd person
singular present (`teniszezik` "she plays tennis," `dolgozik` "he works," `érkezik` "she arrives,"
`búcsúzik` "he says goodby"). Most are irregular only in the singular. The source tabulates five
representative patterns (`fázni` "to be cold," `érkezni` "to arrive," `ugrani` "to jump," `fürdeni`
"to bathe," `feküdni` "to lie") and separately gives the definite-conjugation forms of `enni`/
`inni` (which, being transitive `ik`-verbs, have both definite and indefinite paradigms — cf.
Unit 5's irregular-verb table). **Colloquial-register note:** some speakers use `-k` instead of the
standard `-m` for the 1sg present indefinite of `ik`-verbs; the source explicitly flags `-m` as
"more prevalent and considered preferable" — a genuine, if brief, register/prescriptive-norm
observation, not a hard rule. pp. 163–164

### 8.B The infinitive

The infinitive ending `-ni` attaches directly to the root for most verbs (`állni`, `fázni`,
`várni`, `látni`, `adni`, `főzni`, `ülni`); other verbs require an auxiliary vowel `-a-`/`-e-`
before `-ni` (`mondani`, `küldeni`, `tanítani`, `segíteni`, `érteni`) to break up what would
otherwise be a word-medial three-consonant cluster (a combination Hungarian doesn't otherwise
tolerate except word-initially). The source tabulates the full present-tense conjugation for this
auxiliary-vowel class and notes that when the stem ends in `t` and the following ending begins
with `t`, the auxiliary vowel can be dropped with assimilation. The infinitive functions as a
dependent infinitive with another verb (`Hol kell leszállni?` "Where do I have to get off?") and
as a verbal noun, e.g. as the object of a verb (`Nagyon kellemes úszni` "It's very pleasant to
swim"). pp. 164–165

### 8.C The suffixes -ból/-ből and -n/-on/-en/-ön

`-ból`/`-ből` ("out of, from inside") is the motion-away counterpart of `-ba`/`-be` ("into"),
completing the three-way `-ban/-ben` (static in) / `-ba/-be` (motion into) / `-ból/-ből` (motion
out of) system. Separately, `-n`/`-on`/`-en`/`-ön` ("on, on top of, over," used with non-motion
verbs) contrasts with `-ban`/`-ben` ("in, inside of") the same way `-ba`/`-be` contrasts with
`-ból`/`-ből`. **A specific, closed list of words takes `-n` (rather than `-ban`/`-ben`) to mean
"in"**: `Magyarország`, `Buda`, `Pest`, `Szeged`, `Földvár`, `külföld`, `állomás`, `tér`,
`környék`, `konzulátus`, `egyetem`, and the directional/seasonal nouns `észak`, `dél`, `kelet`,
`nyugat`, `tél`, `nyár`, `hét` — an irregular-government list that must simply be memorized, not
derived from any general rule. pp. 165–166

### 8.D Telling time — **explicit colloquial-register note**

Hungarian tells time by referencing the **upcoming** hour rather than the one just past — the
opposite of English's typical past-hour reference. `negyed kettő` = "a quarter of the way to two"
= 1:15; `fél három` = "half of the way to three" = 2:30; `háromnegyed négy` = "three-quarters of
the way to four" = 3:45. **The source states explicitly that in colloquial speech, Hungarians
reckon non-round minutes by their proximity to the *nearest* quarter-hour landmark** (rather than
building a time expression purely off the clock hour the way English does), giving worked
examples: 1:05 = `öt perccel múlt egy` ("five minutes past one"), 10:16 = `egy perccel múlt negyed
tizenegy` ("one minute past a-quarter-to-eleven"), 2:32 = `két perccel múlt fél három` ("two
minutes past half-past-two"), 5:51 = `hat perccel múlt háromnegyed hat` ("six minutes past
three-quarters-to-six"). This is the one place in this page range where the source itself labels a
usage pattern as belonging to actual colloquial speech, distinct from the citation-form paradigm
taught in the Basic Sentences. pp. 166–167

### 8.E The suffix -kor

`-kor` ("at," used only with time expressions) is invariant under vowel harmony and causes no stem
change — unlike virtually every other suffix covered so far in the course (`Hány órakor?` "At what
time?," `ötkor` "at five o'clock," `Máskor` "At another time"). p. 167
