### Unit 13 — The future tense with `fog`
Hungarian expresses the future analytically with the auxiliary verb **fog** (conjugated to agree with
the subject) followed by the infinitive of the main verb, e.g. **horgászni fogok** ("I will fish/go
fishing"), **fogunk vásárolni** ("we will shop"). This is distinct from the simple present, which can
also carry future meaning with an explicit time expression (a pattern already noted in earlier units),
but `fog` + infinitive is the dedicated, unambiguous future construction. A companion table
systematically presents time expressions used with the future (e.g. "a jövő héten," next week).

### Unit 16 — Grammar (numbered, verbatim structure from the source)
1. **Expressing habitual actions (`szokott`).** The verb **szokott** is conjugated only in the past
   tense but is used to express present habitual actions, followed by the infinitive. Full paradigm
   (indefinite/definite):
   | | Indefinite | Definite |
   |---|---|---|
   | (én) | szoktam | szoktam |
   | (te) | szoktál | szoktad |
   | (maga/ön/ő) | szokott | szokta |
   | (mi) | szoktunk | szoktuk |
   | (ti) | szoktatok | szoktátok |
   | (maguk/önök/ők) | szoktak | szokták |

   To express a *past* habit (rather than present), `szokott` is combined with an explicit past-time
   expression, e.g. **Régen itt szoktam vásárolni.** ("I used to shop here.")

2. **The plural of nouns.** Formed with the **-k** ending. Vowel-final nouns just take -k (with a→á,
   e→é shift before it, e.g. nő→nők, anya→anyák, lecke→leckék). Consonant-final nouns take the same
   linking vowel before -k as before the object ending (e.g. étlap→étlapok, ház→házak,
   gyerek→gyerekek, gyümölcs→gyümölcsöt→gyümölcsök). Words needing no linking vowel before the object
   ending still need one before the plural (e.g. asztal→asztalt→asztalok; ember→embert→emberek;
   sör→sört→sörök). Irregular plurals: férfi→férfiak, szó→szavak. Crucially, **Hungarian nouns are not
   always singular/plural in parallel with English**: numbers and quantity words (sok, kevés, pár)
   always take the singular noun (Két körtefa van a kertben — "There are two pear trees"); fruit names
   are always singular (Szereted a narancsot? — "Do you like oranges?"); and singular is used when an
   item is understood as one-per-person even across multiple people (Anyu karácsonyra pulóvert kötött
   nekünk — "one pullover each," vs. pulóvereket — "two or more each"). The definite article is
   required before indefinite plural nouns in generic statements (A gyerekek nem tudnak sokáig
   koncentrálni).

3. **The plural of adjectives.** Add -k to vowel-final adjectives (a→á, e→é before it: jó→jók,
   buta→buták, fekete→feketék). Back-vowel adjectives ending in ú/i take an -a- linking vowel before
   -k (lassú→lassúak); front-vowel adjectives ending in ű/i take -e- (gyönyörű→gyönyörűek,
   eredeti→eredetiek). Irregular: kicsi→kicsik. Consonant-final back-vowel adjectives mostly take -a-
   (piros→pirosak), with several irregularly taking -o- instead (boldog, fiatal, gazdag, nagy, olyan —
   and all nationality adjectives, e.g. angol→angolok). Consonant-final front-vowel adjectives take -e-
   almost without exception (kedves→kedvesek, zöld→zöldek). Irregular: nehéz→nehezek, kevés→kevesek.
   Adjectives only take the plural ending when standing alone (predicatively), not when modifying a
   following noun: *Ezek drága cipők* ("These are expensive shoes") vs. *Ezek a cipők drágák* ("These
   shoes are expensive"). Substantivized plural adjectives express "the [X] people," e.g. az öregek
   ("the old"), a fiatalok ("the young"), az angolok ("the English").

4. **Ezek "these," azok "those"** — the plural demonstrative forms, used with plural nouns (Ezek a
   lányok elég csinosak — "These girls are quite pretty"; Azok a férfiak nagyon jóképűek — "Those men
   are very handsome").

5. **Plural forms followed by other endings.** Plural nouns take the same case/other endings already
   learnt, with the linking vowel always **-a-** after back-vowel plurals and **-e-** after front-
   vowel plurals before further endings (e.g. asztalokat, kertekbe, lányoknak).

6. **The '-szor'/'-szer'/'-ször' "times" ending**, added directly to the stem with vowel harmony,
   used in three contexts: (a) after cardinal numbers (egyszer, kétszer, háromszor...; with
   pronunciation assimilations t/sz→cc and gy→ty before sz, e.g. kétszer pron. kéccer, egyszer pron.
   etyszer); (b) after ordinal numbers without -ik (először, másodszor, harmadszor...; d/sz→cc, e.g.
   másodszor pron. másoccor; the sole exception is előszőr for "the first time"); (c) after certain
   quantifier words (néhányszor "a few times," párszor "a couple of times," sokszor "a lot of times,"
   számtalanszor "countless times," többször "several times").

7. **The '-féle' and '-fajta' "sorts of/kinds of" endings**, interchangeable after numbers and certain
   words: kétféle ("two kinds of"), többféle ("several kinds of"), mindenfajta ("all sorts of"),
   különféle ("various kinds of"), sokfajta ("many kinds of").

8. **Magam "myself"** and its full paradigm (magam/magad/maga/magunk/magatok/maguk), used
   reflexively/emphatically like English myself/yourself/etc., and able to take case endings when
   required by the verb (Vettem magamnak egy szép blúzt — "I bought myself a beautiful blouse"). The
   idioms **jól érzem magam** / **rosszul érzem magam** ("I have a good/bad time" or "I feel
   well/unwell") use the irregular-present verb **érez** ("feel"), whose full conjugation with `magam`
   forms is given (e.g. jól érzem magam(at), jól érzed magad(at), jól érzi magát, jól érezzük
   magunkat, jól érzitek magatokat, jól érzik magukat) — the object ending on magam/magad can be
   dropped in 1st/2nd person singular.

9. **Érdekel "be interested in."** (Vkit) érdekel (vmi/vki) — "(something/somebody) interests
   (somebody)." Full-person conjugation given (engem/téged/minket/titeket-benneteket → érdekel/
   érdekelnek [indefinite-agreeing with subject]; magát/önt/őt and magukat/önöket/őket → érdekli/

10. **Fürdik "have a bath, bathe"** — irregular past-tense conjugation given in full (fürödtem,

### Unit 17 — Grammar (numbered, verbatim structure from the source)
1. **Possessive endings.** English *my/your/his/her* etc. are expressed via possessive suffixes rather
   than separate possessive pronouns. Vowel-final words just take the ending (a/e shift to á/é before
   it); full paradigms given for both back-vowel (autó-, kutya-) and front-vowel (cipő-, zsemlé-)
   stems, singular and plural persons. Note: 2nd person plural uses **-tök** instead of **-tek** after
   stems whose final vowel is ö/ő/ü/ű (cipőtök, sörötök). Consonant-final words take the same linking
   vowel as before plural/object endings; in the 3rd person singular/plural the ending may appear
   *with* or *without* a linking **-j-** (-ja/-a, -juk/-uk or -je/-e, -jük/-ük) — there is no
   predictive rule, this must be memorized per word, though a word taking -ja/-je in 3rd singular will
   consistently take -juk/-jük in 3rd plural (barátja→barátjuk, kertje→kertjük; háza→házuk,
   söre→sörük). Pronunciation note: t+j is pronounced as **tty** between vowels and **ty** after a
   consonant (barátja pron. baráttya, kertje pron. kertye). Irregular 3rd-person stems: ajtó
   ("door") → ajtaja/ajtajuk; idő ("time/weather") → ideje/idejük. Words with possessive endings
   always require the definite conjugation of an accompanying verb (A feleségemet várom). The definite
   article normally precedes a possessive-marked noun (a barátom), though the indefinite article or
   "az egyik" ("one of") can substitute when introducing a not-yet-specific referent (Egy barátom
   mondta... / Az egyik barátom mondta...).

2. **Possessive forms followed by other endings.** All previously learnt case/other endings (except
   -kor) can follow a possessive-marked noun; the 3rd-person-singular possessive's final -a/-e changes
   to -á/-é before them (kertjében). An accent alone distinguishes meaning: Látom a házat ("I can see
   the house") vs. Látom a házát ("I can see your/his/her house"). The object ending is often dropped
   after 1st/2nd person singular possessive forms (Nem találom a fésűmet / a fésűm — "I can't find my
   comb").

3. **Possessive constructions ("Péter's father," "the door of the car").** The possessor precedes the
   possessed item, and the possessed item takes the 3rd-person-singular possessive ending: Péter
   édesapja ("Péter's father"), a kocsi ajtaja ("the door of the car"). The possessor itself can also
   carry a possessive ending in constructions like az édesapám neve ("my father's name") — a doubly-
   marked possessive-of-possessive structure.

4. **"I have" (possession).** Expressed with **van** (positive) / **nincs** (negative), followed by
   the possessed item marked with the possessor's possessive ending: van/nincs kalapom ("I have/don't
   have a hat") through all persons. The person who possesses, when named explicitly as a noun (not a
   pronoun), takes the **dative** ending: Péternek nincs új barátnője; A háznak fehér ajtaja van; A
   gyerekeknek új angol tanáruk van. Past tense uses volt/nem volt; future uses lesz/nem lesz (Sajnos
   nem volt kertünk; Nemsokára nekünk is lesz videónk).

5. **Adverbs of manner.** Formed from adjectives with the **-n** ending, answering Hogy?/Hogyan?
   ("How?"). Vowel-final adjectives just add -n (a→á, e→é before it: olcsó→olcsón, drága→drágán); back
   -vowel adjectives ending in ú/i take linking -a- (szomorú→szomorúan), front-vowel ending in ű/i take
   linking -e- (gyönyörű→gyönyörűen). Consonant-final back-vowel adjectives take -a- (komoly→komolyan);
   front-vowel take -e- (kedves→kedvesen). A substantial list of **irregular** adverb forms is given:
   lassú→lassan, könnyű→könnyen, szörnyű→szörnyen, rossz→rosszul, jó→jól, kiváló→kiválóan,
   kitűnő→kitűnően, felelőtlen→felelőtlenül, hosszú→hosszan; and consonant-final irregulars nagy→nagyon,
   szabad→szabadon, gazdag→gazdagon, vastag→vastagon, fiatal→fiatalon, bátor→bátran, nehéz→nehezen.

6. **Both, either, neither.** (a) "... is ... is" (or "..., ... is") = *both ... and ...*, with both
   particles placed *after* the words they modify, unlike English (Mari szép is és okos is / Mari szép
   is, okos is — "Mari is both beautiful and clever"). (b) "nem is ... és nem is ..." / "nem is ...,
   nem is ..." / "se nem ..., se nem ..." = *neither ... nor ...* (Edit nem is szép és nem is okos /
   Edit se nem szép, se nem okos); **sem** can replace se (Sem a zöld, sem a kék kalap nem tetszett).
   (c) "vagy ..., vagy ..." = *either ... or ...* (Vagy színházba, vagy moziba megyünk).
