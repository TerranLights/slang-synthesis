### Morpheme Breakdown — agglutinative forms worth decomposing

- **`dolgot`** ("thing," accusative) = `dolog` ("thing," nominative) with the second-syllable vowel
  *elided* before any vowel-initial suffix (`dolg-` + `-ot`) — the defining behavior of the "fleeting
  vowel" noun class also shown by `álom`→`álmot` and `torok`→`torkot`-type words elsewhere in this
  language's lexicon (not itself glossed on this page, but the same alternation pattern). Appendix
  II, p. 273.
- **`pohárban`** ("in the glass") = `pohár` + `-ban` (inessive, back-vowel allomorph) — contrasted
  with **`pohárba`** ("into the glass") = `pohár` + `-ba` (illative) — the same static/motion contrast
  already documented for `kerület`/`Budapest`-class nouns in `034_tyh_units01-05.md`, here shown
  systematically across all 13 cases for a single "loses length" noun. Appendix II, p. 271-272.
- **`házaimban`** ("in my houses") = `ház` + `-ai-` (plural possessive linking morpheme) + `-m` (1sg
  possessor) + `-ban` (inessive) — a three-morpheme stack (case + possessor-person + possessor-number)
  illustrating the possessive-declension paradigm's full productivity; contrasted with the
  simpler **`házam`** ("my house," nominative-possessive, no case suffix) = `ház` + `-am` (1sg
  possessive, no linking `-ai-` since it's singular "my house" not "my houses"). Appendix II, p. 275.
- **`lányaiba`** ("into his/her girls") = `lány` + `-ai` (plural possessive linking) + `-ba`
  (illative) — parallel structure to `házaimban` above but 3rd-person possessor (`lánya` "his/her
  girl" + plural `-i` + case). p. 275.
- **`kicsoda?`** ("who? [emphatic]") = `ki` ("who") + `-csoda` (an emphatic-question intensifying
  suffix, cf. `micsoda!` "what a...!") — noted in the glossary sample as a `(emphatic)`-tagged
  question-word variant, contrasting with plain `ki?`. Hungarian-English glossary, p. 285; also
  English-Hungarian glossary, p. 313.

## Grammar points

### Appendix I (tail) — Irregular verb paradigm class, continued

This chunk picks up mid-table from a sibling chunk's coverage of Appendix I's opening pages. The
irregular verbs completed in this chunk's range (`vesz`, `visz`, `hisz`, `eszik`, `iszik`, `megy`,
`jön`, `van`) fall into two structural subclasses the book itself distinguishes: (1) verbs taking the
full indefinite/definite conjugation contrast throughout all moods (`vesz`, `visz`, `hisz`, `eszik`,
`iszik`) — each showing the familiar `-lak/-lek` "I-you" object-agreement form in the "*me* +
téged/benneteket/titeket" row; and (2) verbs the source explicitly marks as **using only indefinite
conjugations** (`megy`, `jön`, `van`) — a genuine, non-obvious irregularity, since these are among the
most frequent verbs in the language and a learner might otherwise expect the definite forms to exist
by analogy. `Jön`'s doublet imperative stems (`gyere`-series vs. `jöjjél`-series) is a further,
source-flagged usage-frequency note distinguishing ordinary command use from subjunctive-triggered
contexts — worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase as a live morphological alternation, not
free variation. pp. 267-270.

### Appendix II — The 13-case declension system, worked across noun classes

The book organizes Hungarian's full case system as thirteen ordered case slots — nominative,
accusative, illative, inessive, elative, sublative, superessive, delative, allative, adessive,
ablative, dative, instrumental, causal-final, terminative (a fourteenth, temporal `-kor`, is handled
elsewhere per the Grammar index) — and works this same ordered list across eight paradigm classes to
show how each noun-final-segment type interacts with vowel harmony, vowel length, and epenthetic
"fleeting" vowels. Reproduced in full below is the back-/front-vowel "no loss of length" pair (`ház`
"house" / `könyv` "book"), the class explicitly presented first and treated as the default/regular
pattern; the other seven classes are summarized by their distinguishing behavior rather than
reproduced cell-by-cell, per copyright discipline:

| case | ház (sg.) | házak (pl.) | könyv (sg.) | könyvek (pl.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominative | ház | házak | könyv | könyvek |
| accusative | házat | házakat | könyvet | könyveket |
| illative | házba | házakba | könyvbe | könyvekbe |
| inessive | házban | házakban | könyvben | könyvekben |
| elative | házból | házakból | könyvből | könyvekből |
| sublative | házra | házakra | könyvre | könyvekre |
| superessive | házon | házakon | könyvön | könyveken |
| delative | házról | házakról | könyvről | könyvekről |
| allative | házhoz | házakhoz | könyvhöz | könyvekhez |
| adessive | háznál | házaknál | könyvnél | könyveknél |
| ablative | háztól | házaktól | könyvtől | könyvektől |
| dative | háznak | házaknak | könyvnek | könyveknek |
| instrumental | házzal | házakkal | könyvvel | könyvekkel |
| causal-final | házért | házakért | könyvért | könyvekért |
| terminative | házig | házakig | könyvig | könyvekig |

The other seven classes, by their distinguishing behavior relative to this default:
- **Low vowel, loses length** (`pohár`/`kéz`): the stem-final long vowel (á/é) shortens before every
  suffix except the bare nominative (`pohár`→`poharat` not `*pohárat`; `kéz`→`kezet`).
- **Regular, ending in vowel** (`táska`/`csésze`): the stem-final a/e lengthens to á/é before every
  suffix (the familiar a→á/e→é rule already documented from other Hungarian sources), otherwise
  patterns exactly like the `ház`/`könyv` default.
- **Regular, ending in consonant, with front-rounded subclass** (`lány`/`szék`/`őr`): adds a genuine
  third vowel-harmony dimension — front-rounded stems (`őr` "guard") take yet another suffix-vowel
  allomorph (`őrben`, `őrre`, `őrnek`...) distinct from both back-vowel and front-unrounded patterns,
  the "three-way" vowel harmony split (back / front-unrounded / front-rounded) that this project's
  other Hungarian sources have flagged as a recurring cross-chapter theme (cf. the vowel-harmony
  discussion noted from the Hungarian test-run graphify pass, per project memory).
- **Fleeting vowel** (`dolog`/`iker`/`tükör`): the stem's second-syllable vowel drops before any
  suffix beginning with a vowel or directly attaching (`dolog`→`dolgot`, not `*dologot`) — see
  Morpheme Breakdown above.
- **Possessive declension** (two full tables, pp. 275-276): shows the same 13-case list applied
  *after* a possessive-person suffix is already attached (`házam` "my house" → `házamba` "into my
  house" → full case paradigm), worked for a no-loss noun (`ház`), a loses-length noun (`levél`
  "letter"), a regular noun (`lány`), and a fleeting-vowel noun (`jutalom`/`álom`/`kép`) — confirming
  that possessive-marking and case-marking stack independently and productively across all noun
  classes, not just the "regular" one.

This is the single most systematic, exhaustively-tabulated presentation of the Hungarian case system
found in this project's Hungarian corpus to date — more complete in raw paradigm-cell coverage than
the case-system chapters extracted from Carol Rounds' standalone reference grammar
(`003_chapter6_case_system.md`) or the FSI course, though those sources give richer usage/pragmatic
framing per case that this appendix (being a bare reference table) does not attempt. pp. 271-276.

## Notable findings (colloquial/register/period vocabulary)

Per this project's specific interest in informal/colloquial-register material, the following are
flagged from this extraction:

1. **`Viszontlátásra!` (formal) vs. `viszlát` (informal)** for "goodbye" — the clearest explicit
   register-tagged minimal pair found in the sampled glossary pages, directly parallel in kind to the
   `krumpli`/`burgonya` and `szia`/`szervusz` pairs already documented from *Teach Yourself Hungarian*
   (`034_tyh_units01-05.md`). Hungarian-English glossary p. 296; English-Hungarian glossary p. 299.
2. **`Viszonthallásra`** ("goodbye, on the phone") — a genuine context-restricted register variant
   (telephone-specific sign-off), distinct from both the formal and informal in-person goodbyes above.
   p. 296.
3. **`bocs` (informal) vs. (implicitly) `bocsánat`/`elnézést`** for "excuse me/sorry" — the source's
   own glossary explicitly tags `bocs` `(informal)`; this is the same clipped-informal-shortening
   pattern already flagged for `kösz`/`köszi` and `deka`/`troli` in the *Teach Yourself Hungarian*
   extraction (`034_tyh_units01-05.md`) — worth noting as a recurring Hungarian slang-formation
   mechanism (clipping of a longer, more formal base word) now confirmed across a second,
   independently-authored source. p. 278.
4. **`kérem` (formal) vs. `szívesen` (neutral/general)** for "you're welcome" — a formality-tier pair
   for the same function, though milder than the leave-taking pair above; both entries appear
   adjacent in the English-Hungarian glossary under the same headword "you're welcome," making the
   contrast a direct source-adjacency finding rather than an inference. p. 313.
5. **`well, well ...` (informal)** glossed to `nocsak` — an interjection explicitly tagged informal
   on the English-headword side of the glossary, the reverse-direction confirmation of the same kind
   of register tagging seen in the Hungarian-English direction. p. 313.
6. **`kicsoda?` (emphatic)** vs. plain `ki?` for "who?" — not a formality-register tag but a genuine
   pragmatic-intensity register distinction the source itself marks, worth flagging alongside the
   formal/informal pairs as a second axis (emphasis/intensity, not politeness) of lexical-choice
   register variation in this language.
7. **No vulgar/taboo-register tags** (`(vulgáris)`-style or equivalent) were found anywhere in the
   sampled glossary pages of this book, consistent with the *Teach Yourself Hungarian* glossary
   sample's finding (`038_tyh_unit21_appendix_backmatter.md`) — both of this project's back-of-book
   glossary samples so far skew toward polite-register teaching vocabulary with only mild
   informal/clipped-form register variety, not stronger slang or taboo material. The explicit
   `(vulgáris)`-tagged material found elsewhere in this project's Hungarian corpus remains isolated to
   `021_magyarora_sample_early.md` (a different genre of source — authentic forum/spoken-sample
   material, not a teaching-course glossary).
8. **`jön`'s doublet imperative stems** (`gyere`-series as the more common plain imperative vs.
   `jöjjél`-series as the more common subjunctive-triggered form) — a source-flagged usage-frequency
   distinction between two co-existing forms of the same verb, worth flagging for the
   mechanics-analysis phase as a case of morphological doublets sorted by *syntactic context*
