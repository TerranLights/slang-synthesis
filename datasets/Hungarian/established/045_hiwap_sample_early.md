# Hungarian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: "Hungarian in Words and Pictures" (Magyar nyelvkönyv), Early Sample

**Source:** Erdős József, Kozma Endre, Prileszky Csilla, Uhrman György, *Hungarian in Words and
Pictures — A Textbook for Foreigners (Magyar nyelvkönyv)*, 3rd edition (Tankönyvkiadó, Budapest,
1990), "Hungarian-in-words-and-pictures-a-textbook-for-foreigners-Magyar-nyelvk-nyv.djvu" (scanned
.djvu, no text layer confirmed via `djvutxt` — read entirely visually via `ddjvu`-rendered page
images). 611 physical pages total.

**Page-offset verification (mandatory check per the vision-reading gotcha):** empirically confirmed
by direct inspection, not assumed. PDF page 9 shows printed page "7" (CONTENTS, page 1 of TOC), PDF
page 10 shows printed "8", PDF page 15 shows printed "13" — a consistent `printed_page = PDF_page − 2`
offset through the front matter. This offset **shifts by one page** somewhere between PDF 15 and PDF
20: PDF page 20 shows printed page "19", and this new `printed_page = PDF_page − 1` offset was then
confirmed consistently at PDF 22 (printed "21", start of Introduction to Phonetics), PDF 30 (printed
"29", Lesson 1 title page), PDF 40 (printed "39"), and PDF 50 (printed "49", Lesson 2). The shift is
most likely an unnumbered plate or half-title page inserted somewhere in the front matter; it does
not recur later. This is a **flat, single-page-per-image scan** — no two-printed-pages-per-image
issue found anywhere in the inspected range.

**Format of this source:** this is explicitly a pictogram-driven pedagogical grammar ("words and
pictures") rather than a dialogue-based course like the project's other Hungarian sources. Each
numbered grammar box (boxes `1` through `139` across the whole book, per the table of contents) pairs
a schematic pictogram/diagram with a minimal sentence pattern; running prose dialogues, an end-of-
lesson "Szavak" (Words) list, and "Közmondások" (Proverbs) round out each lesson. Confirmed by direct
inspection to be considerably denser in continuous grammar prose than a strict picture-book, but
still vocabulary-light relative to a phrasebook — most lessons introduce 40-70 new lexical items via
their official end-of-lesson word list.

## Coverage — what was sampled, what was skipped

Per dispatch instructions (extract a genuinely representative SAMPLE from PDF pages 1-200 / roughly
printed pages 1-199, not exhaustive coverage of the full 611-page book), the following four sections
were extracted **in full** (all grammar boxes, full official word lists, illustrative dialogue quoted
selectively per copyright discipline):

- **Introduction to Phonetics** (printed pp. 21-28, PDF 22-29) — full Hungarian alphabet/IPA table,
  syllabication rules, accent, and the six intonation-pattern (A-F) system.
- **ELSŐ LECKE / Lesson 1, "Ki ön?"** (printed pp. 29-45, PDF 30-46) — extracted in full: all grammar
  boxes 1-8, the "A pályaudvar előtt" reading passage, the full Szavak (word list), and the Szórend
  (word-order) chart system.

For **MÁSODIK LECKE / Lesson 2** (printed pp. 46-71, PDF 47-72) and **HARMADIK LECKE / Lesson 3**
(printed pp. 72-91, PDF 73-92), a **narrower slice** was taken rather than full-lesson coverage,
since Lesson 1 alone already gave a complete worked example of this source's box-by-box format and
the coverage rule's "skip repetitive drill" principle applies especially strongly to this pictogram
course (whose numbered exercises 1-14 per lesson very heavily recombine the same box-vocabulary):

- **Lesson 2**: the core grammar boxes actually read were box `9` (Mik?/Kik? — plural of `ez`/`az`),
  the plural-formation chart, box `12` (van/nincs plural `vannak`/`nincsenek`), box `14` (`van`-`nincs`
  contrast), box `15` (`hány?`/`mennyi?` and the cardinal numbers 1-20), and the full official Szavak
  word list (p. 63/PDF 64). Boxes 10, 11, 13, 16-18 and the Budapest/Gabi reading passages were **not**
  read in this pass and are not represented below.
- **Lesson 3**: box `19` (the `hol?`/`miben?`/`min?`/`minél?` three-tier locative-case system — the
  richest single grammar point sampled in this file) and box `23` (`sem`/`sincs`, the negative-`is`
  counterpart), plus the "Az étteremben" reading passage and the full official Szavak word list
  (pp. 85-86/PDF 86-87). Boxes 20-22 and the "Hol van az étterem?"/"A szállodában"/"A megállónál"
  reading passages were **not** read in this pass.

**Everything from Lesson 4 onward (printed p. 92/PDF 93 through the end of the assigned PDF 1-200
range, and all of PDF 200-611) was skipped entirely** for this dispatch — a genuine sample, not an
attempt at exhaustive coverage of the assigned range.

**Vision-reading note:** this is a clean, modern (1990 reprint) digitally-typeset scan. No handwritten
marginalia, library stamps, underlining, or pencil annotation of any kind was found on any page
inspected in this pass. All text was crisp and legible; Vision Reading Confidence is `verified` for
essentially every entry below, since each item was cross-checked against its appearance in both a
grammar box/reading passage *and* the lesson's own official Szavak list wherever both were sampled.

**Relationship to prior Hungarian `established/` files:** this is a fifth distinct genre/era of
Hungarian source in this project (after Carol Rounds' reference grammar, the 1962 FSI audio-lingual
course, the phraseology/proverb collections, and *Teach Yourself Hungarian*). Unlike *Teach Yourself
Hungarian* (`034_tyh_units01-05.md` etc.), which explicitly source-annotates several colloquial/formal
register pairs (`krumpli`/`burgonya`, `kösz`/`köszönöm`, etc.), this pictogram course is markedly
**register-neutral** — see Notable findings below. Its case-system presentation (box 19's `Miben?/
Min?/Minél?` three-way locative paradigm) is more systematic/complete than the equivalent material
in the FSI course sampled so far and is worth flagging for the morphology-mechanics phase.

### Morpheme Breakdown — agglutinative forms worth decomposing

- **`tizenegy`...`tizenkilenc`** ("11"-"19") = `tizen-` (bound allomorph of `tíz` "10," linking form
  used before another numeral) + ones-word (`egy`, `kettő`, `három`...) — same general teens-
  compounding rule independently documented in `034_tyh_units01-05.md` Morpheme Breakdown for `huszon-`
  (20s). Lesson 2 box 15, PDF 58 (printed 57).
- **`az autóban`** ("in the car," inessive) vs. **`az autón`** ("on the car," superessive) vs.
  **`az autónál`** ("by/at the car," adessive) = `autó` + `-ban`/`-n`/`-nál`, the three-way locative-
  case suffix set taught as a single paradigm in box 19. Back-vowel allomorphs shown here (`-ban`,
  `-on`/`-n`, `-nál`); the box's own chart also gives the front-vowel counterparts `-ben`, `-n`/`-en`/
  `-ön`, `-nél` and a third (rounded/`ö`-final-stem) allomorph column for the `Min?` row specifically —
  a three-way vowel-harmony split (back/front/front-rounded) for the superessive `-n` ending that is
  *more granular* than the two-way back/front split sufficient for the other two cases in the same
  paradigm. PDF 74 (printed 73).
- **`autókban`** ("in the cars") = `autó` + `-k` (plural) + `-ban` (inessive) — plural noun preceding
  the locative-case suffix, shown directly beside the singular paradigm in box 19's illustrative
  vocabulary box, confirming plural-then-case suffix ordering.
- **`kabátzseb`** ("coat-pocket") = `kabát` "coat" + `zseb` "pocket"; **`nadrágzseb`** ("trouser
  pocket") = `nadrág` "trousers" + `zseb` — a parallel pair of N+N compounds sharing the same head
  noun, both introduced together in Lesson 3's official Szavak list, printed p. 86.
- **`buszmegálló`** ("bus-stop") = `busz` "bus" + `megálló` "stop, station" — Lesson 2 official Szavak
  list, printed p. 63.
- **`sincs`** ("is not ... either") = `is` ("too, also") + `nincs` ("is not") with obligatory

## Grammar points

### Introduction to Phonetics — vowel/consonant system, syllabication, accent, and intonation

Hungarian has 14 vowel sounds and 25 consonant sounds (one consonant, `j`/`ly`, traditionally written
two ways). Vowels split into a short/long pairs by length (with `a`/`á` and `e`/`é` as the two
quality-changing exceptions — `á` is not simply a long `a`, nor `é` simply a long `e`), and the full
vowel-harmony classification table (unrounded/rounded × front/back) is given explicitly as "the basis
for choosing the correct endings of words" — i.e. this source frames vowel harmony as *the* structural
key to the whole suffix system, consistent with its centrality across all three lessons sampled.
Stops `p`, `t`, `k` are always unaspirated (unlike English). Syllabication rules: two adjacent vowels
split across syllables; a single consonant between vowels joins the following syllable; two
consonants between vowels split one-each; three-plus consonants keep the first two (three) with the
preceding vowel; compound words may also be split at their own internal morpheme boundaries. Word
accent is always on the first syllable. Six distinct sentence-intonation patterns (A-F) are
catalogued by function: A (neutral declarative), B (yes/no question), C (question-word questions,
also greetings/commands/strong contrast), D (sharp logical contrast, non-closing), E (non-closing
neutral/compound-sentence parts, sometimes astonishment), F (strong-stress first clause of a compound
sentence). PDF 22-29 (printed 21-28).

### Lesson 1 — `te`/`ön`/`ő` personal-pronoun register system

A three-way singular personal-pronoun system paralleling (but described more tersely than) the
`te`/`maga`/`ön` system documented in `034_tyh_units01-05.md`: `te` (informal, "like French tu"), `ön`
(formal, "like French vous," explicitly noted as "logically second person but grammatically third
person"), and `ő` (third person, genderless — covers both "he" and "she," with a separate pronoun `az`
reserved for inanimate "it"). Note explicitly given: personal pronouns in 1st/2nd person are usually
omitted unless emphasized — a pro-drop rule. PDF 38 (printed 37). This source's two-way formal system
(`ön` only) is *coarser* than `034_tyh_units01-05.md`'s three-way `te`/`maga`/`ön` system — no `maga`
is introduced in the range sampled, suggesting either a simpler pedagogical model or that `maga` is
covered later in the book (outside this dispatch's range).

### Lesson 1 — Word order (Szórend) as an emphasis-driven system

The book's signature "Szórend" charts formalize word order not by grammatical role but by logical
emphasis, using a four-column schema (A / B~— / —~C~C / D) where C marks the verb's position and B
marks whichever word/phrase immediately precedes the verb and carries the sentence's logical focus.
The basic rule given explicitly: either the verb itself is emphasized (nothing occupies B) or the
element in B is emphasized (and then C carries no independent stress). Elements in A (before B) and D
(after C) are always unstressed. This is presented as a general framework reused for every lesson's
own "Szórend" section, not a one-off — PDF 44 (printed 43) states this explicitly ("Charts with the
title Szórend refer to the word order of the sentence... shown in the respective sections of the part
Grammar"). PDF 43-45 (printed 42-44).

### Lesson 1 — Noun+postposition word order

Postpositions (`előtt`, `mellett`, `fölött`, `alatt`, `mögött`, `között`) always immediately follow
their governed noun; no word may intervene between noun and postposition. PDF 39 (printed 38).

### Lesson 2 — Plural formation (nouns and adjectives)

Regular plural suffix is `-k` after vowel-final stems (with the familiar á/é-lengthening pattern
before it, e.g. `autó`→`autók`), and a linking-vowel-plus-`k` (`-ok`/`-ak`/`-ek`/`-ök`, conditioned by
vowel harmony, with `-ök` specifically triggered by an `ö`/`ü` in the stem's last syllable) after
consonant-final stems. Adjectives pluralize identically. A short list of lexically-irregular plurals
is flagged directly beside the main chart (`szó`→`szavak`, `férfi`→`férfiak`, `híd`→`hidak`,
`tér`→`terek`, `madár`→`madarak`, `lassú`→`lassúak`, `régi`→`régiek`) rather than left for the learner
to infer — the source explicitly commits to flagging all further such exceptions in each lesson's own
word list going forward. PDF 51 (printed 50).

### Lesson 2 — `van`/`nincs` existential-locative pair, singular and plural

`van` ("is/there is") and its negative `nincs` ("is not/there isn't") both have distinct plural forms
(`vannak`/`nincsenek`) used when the logical subject is plural. Word order inverts in the negative,
matching the same `nincs`-inversion rule independently documented in `034_tyh_units01-05.md` Grammar
U4§4. PDF 55-56 (printed 54-55).

### Lesson 2 — `hány?`/`mennyi?` count vs. mass-noun quantifier questions and the cardinal numbers 1-20

`hány?` ("how many?") is used with count nouns, `mennyi?` ("how much?") with mass nouns — the same
count/mass quantifier-question split found in many languages, here given its own dedicated grammar
box with matched example sentences (`Hány lány fut...?`/`Négy lány fut...` vs. `Mennyi gyümölcs van
itt?`/`Sok gyümölcs van itt.`). The book's full cardinal-number chart 1-20 is given immediately below
this box, with the `kettő` (free-standing)/`két` (attributive) complementary-distribution pair
demonstrated by direct paired example (`két lány` vs. standalone `kettő`) rather than stated as a
prose rule — the same distinction independently documented as a prose rule in `034_tyh_units01-05.md`
Grammar U4§1. PDF 58 (printed 57).

### Lesson 3 — The three-way locative case system: `Miben?`/`Min?`/`Minél?` (and animate `Kiben?`/`Kin?`/`Kinél?`)

The richest single grammar point sampled in this dispatch. Hungarian's static-location cases split
into three distinct semantic/case dimensions taught as one paradigm: the inessive `-ban/-ben` ("in
what?", `Miben?`), the superessive `-n`/`-on`/`-en`/`-ön` ("on what?", `Min?`), and the adessive
`-nál/-nél` ("by/at what?", `Minél?`) — each governed by a different verb in the box's own worked
example (`ül` "sit" → inessive, "sits *in* the car"; `van` "is" → superessive, "is *on* the car";
`áll` "stand" → adessive, "stands *by* the car"). A parallel animate-pronoun paradigm (`Kiben?`/
`Kin?`/`Kinél?`, "in/on/by whom?") is given alongside the inanimate one. Vowel harmony conditions each
suffix's allomorphs, with the superessive row uniquely split three ways (back-vowel `-on`, front-vowel
`-en`, and a third front-rounded `-ön` allomorph) where the other two cases in the paradigm need only
a two-way back/front split — a genuine phonological asymmetry within a single paradigm worth flagging
for the morphology-mechanics phase. This three-way case system is more granular than the two-way
where-at/where-to (`hol`/`hova`) contrast alone documented from the FSI course and *is* the same
underlying case-inventory the book's own later Grammar sections evidently build on (the un-sampled
box 20's `Melyik___n?` and box 21's `Melyik___alatt?` continue this same case-system thread). PDF 74
(printed 73).

### Lesson 3 — `sem`/`sincs`: negative-scope counterpart of `is`

`sem` ("neither, not ... either") is the negative-polarity counterpart of the additive particle `is`
("too, also," itself documented as a grammar point in `034_tyh_units01-05.md` Unit 3§2), and `sincs`
is the correspondingly contracted negative-existential form (`nincs`+`is`→`sincs`, not the
compositionally-expected but ungrammatical `*is nincs`) — the book's own diagram makes this
contraction explicit with a struck-through incorrect form beside the corrected one, for both singular
and plural (`sincs`/`sincsenek`). PDF 83 (printed 82).

## Notable findings (colloquial/register/period vocabulary)

Per this project's specific interest in informal/colloquial-register material, the following are
flagged from this extraction:

1. **This source is markedly register-neutral compared to the project's other Hungarian sources.**
   Across all four sections sampled (phonetics, full Lesson 1, and partial Lessons 2-3), not one
   explicit colloquial/formal register annotation, dialectal tag, or "this word is more casual than
   that word" comment was found in the source's own text — a sharp contrast with
   `034_tyh_units01-05.md` (*Teach Yourself Hungarian*), which explicitly source-annotates several
   such pairs (`krumpli`/`burgonya`, `kösz`/`köszönöm`, `szia`/`szervusz`, `kocsma`/`söröző`, etc.) in
   a comparable page range. This pictogram-driven pedagogical course appears to be built almost
   entirely around a single neutral written/spoken register, with lexical variety expressed instead
   as plain synonym sets (see below) rather than register-marked pairs.
2. **`szálloda`/`szálló`/`hotel`** — three apparent synonyms for "hotel" spread across Lessons 1 and 3
   with zero register or connotation commentary from the source, unlike the analogous `étterem`/
   `vendéglő`/`csárda` restaurant-register set that *is* explicitly connotation-marked in
   `034_tyh_units01-05.md`. Worth flagging as a case where near-synonymy is present in the lexicon but
   the pedagogical source itself gives no register signal — the mechanics-analysis phase would need
   another source (or corpus data) to determine whether a real register distinction exists among the
   three.
3. **`autóbusz`/`busz`** — full and clipped forms treated as plain interchangeable synonyms with no
   "clipping = informal" comment, contrasting with `034_tyh_units01-05.md`'s explicit register framing
   of the parallel `trolibusz`/`troli` pair (there, `troli` is stated to be the informal clipped form).
4. **`mama`** (Lesson 2, alongside neutral `anya` "mother") is the one item in this dispatch flagged
   with a tentative `colloquial` usage tier on distributional grounds alone (a diminutive/child-
   register-feeling loanword-adjacent form appearing in a plain word list beside the neutral term,
   itself unremarked upon by the source) — genuinely uncertain, hence `plausible_unverified` rather
   than `verified` Vision Reading Confidence, flagged for the same reason as the `öreg`/`régi`
   "old" near-synonym pair (both real words, no register signal from the source, real semantic
   distinction — animacy for `öreg`/`régi`, formality-of-address for `mama`/`anya` — that a corpus
   source would need to confirm).
5. **`szervusz`** is glossed here as a plain informal greeting/farewell with **no generational/period
   marking**, unlike `034_tyh_units01-05.md`'s explicit note that `szervusz` is now old-fashioned and
   associated with an older generation relative to `szia`/`szevasz`. Since this source (1990) predates
   *Teach Yourself Hungarian* (2003) by 13 years, this is plausibly a genuine period snapshot: worth
   flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase as indirect evidence that `szervusz`'s "dated" register
   marking may itself be a development of the 1990s-2000s rather than already true in 1990.
6. No `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]`-style dialectal tags or comparative regional-variant annotations of any kind
   appear anywhere in the sampled range — like both `034_tyh_units01-05.md` and the FSI course, this
   is presented as a single-dialect (standard Budapest-area) course with no internal register/dialect
   marking at all in the sections sampled.

No handwritten marginalia, library stamps, or non-print annotation of any kind were found anywhere in
this page range — this is a clean 1990 scan.
