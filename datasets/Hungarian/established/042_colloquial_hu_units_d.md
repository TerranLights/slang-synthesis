### Morpheme Breakdown — agglutinative/suffix-stacking forms worth decomposing

- **`szeretlek`** ("I love you") = `szeret-` (root "love") + `-lek` (the portmanteau
  1st-person-subject/2nd-person-object present-tense ending) — this ending, glossed in full at
  Grammar points below, replaces the ordinary indefinite ending whenever the subject is `én` and the
  object is `téged`/`titeket`/`benneteket`; the pronouns need never be stated. Unit 13 title word,
  p. 212.
- **`kerestelek`** ("I was looking for you") = `keres-` (root "look for") + `-t-` (past-tense
  marker) + `-elek` (past-tense allomorph of the same 1sg-subject/2sg-object portmanteau) — same
  portmanteau slot, past tense. p. 212.
- **`mögötte`** ("behind him/her/it") = `mögött` (postposition "behind") + `-e` (3sg possessive
  ending) — postpositions take possessive endings, not case endings, to combine with pronominal
  referents (see Grammar points, "Postpositions and pronouns"), p. 207.
- **`nélkülem`** ("without me") = `nélkül` (postposition "without") + `-em` (1sg possessive ending,
  front-vowel harmony) — same pattern, p. 207.
- **`hiányoztál`** ("you were missing/I missed you") = `hiány(o)z-` (fleeting-vowel verb stem "be
  missing") + `-tál` (past 2sg ending, consonant-initial so the fleeting vowel `o` is retained) vs.
  **`hiányzol`** ("you are missing," present) = `hiányz-` (vowel-initial ending `-ol`, fleeting
  vowel dropped) — the general fleeting-vowel-verb pattern (parallel to fleeting-vowel nouns already
  documented in prior Hungarian `established/` files), p. 172.
- **`vigyél`** ("take!," te-form imperative) = `vi-` (suppletive imperative stem of irregular verb
  `visz` "carries") + `-gy-` (imperative marker, irregular allomorph) + `-él` (te-ending, long form)
  — contrasted with the short form `vidd`, itself `vi-` + `-dd` (definite short 2sg ending) — p. 189.
- **`Nehéz fába vágtam a fejszémet`** ("I slammed my axe into a tough tree") — `fejsze` ("axe") +
  `-m` (1sg possessive) + `-et` (accusative, linking vowel `-e-` inserted before the possessive-then-
  case double-suffix stack) — a possessive+case double-stack on a common noun inside a fixed idiom,
  p. 170.
- **`Budapesten`-class irregular locative nouns** (`tér`→`téren`/`térre`, `út`→`úton`/`útra`,
  `körút`→`körúton`/`körútra`) recur in this dispatch's Text 1 (`Kálvin tér mögött`, p. 202) — cross-
  referencing the same irregular locative-declension class already fully tabulated in the
  established Teach Yourself Hungarian file (`034_tyh_units01-05.md`, Unit 5§4), confirming the
  pattern is source-independent, not particular to one course's own presentation.

## Grammar points

### Unit 10 (tail) — Past tense: Class A/B/C verb-stem system

The past tense uses a short (`-t`) or long (`-ott/-ett/-ött`) marker depending on verb class: **Class
A** verbs (ending in two consonants, e.g. `hall`, `ért`; a long vowel + `-t`, e.g. `segít`, `fordít`;
or a short closed list `fut`/`hat`/`jut`/`köt`/`nyit`/`süt`/`üt`/`vet`) always take the long form.
**Class B** verbs (ending in `j`/`l`/`ly`/`n`/`ny`/`r`, e.g. `beszél`, `kér`; many but not all
two-syllable `-ad/-ed` verbs, e.g. `ébred`, `marad`) always take the short form. **Class C** (all
other verbs) take the long form only in 3sg indefinite, short elsewhere. Exceptions exist in both
directions (`kezd`/`küld`/`küzd`/`lát`/`mond` look like Class A but conjugate as C; `áll`/`száll`
look like Class A but conjugate as B). Irregular past stems: `van`→`volt-`, `jön`→`jött-`,
`megy`→`ment-`, `hisz`→`hitt-`, `lesz`→`lett-`, `tesz`→`tett-`, `vesz`→`vett-`, `visz`→`vitt-`,
`eszik`→`ett-`, `iszik`→`itt-`. pp. 156-158.

### Unit 10 (tail) — Completed vs. ongoing action via coverbs

Hungarian has only one past-tense form; the English distinction between "I was reading"/"I read"/"I
had read" is expressed by using (or omitting) a coverb, typically signalling completion:
**`Olvastam a könyvet`** ("I was reading the book," ongoing) vs. **`Kiolvastam a könyvet`** ("I read
the whole book," completed). A coverb alone can serve as a full yes/no answer to a question
containing the same coverb (`Elmentél moziba?` → `El.` "Yes[, I went]."). p. 158.

### Unit 11 — `te`/`maga`/`ön` register system (cross-reference)

Confirmed again in this range's dialogues: Hungarian's three-way (six-way counting plurals) second-
person formality system — `te`/`ti` (familiar), `maga`/`maguk` (neutral-to-formal default with
strangers/service staff), `ön`/`önök` (most formal/distant, official register) — all with `maga`/
`ön`/`önök` grammatically taking 3rd-person verb forms. This matches the system already fully
documented from the *Teach Yourself Hungarian* course (`034_tyh_units01-05.md`).

### Unit 11 — Pronouns and cases

Nominative pronouns (`én, te, ő, mi, ti, ők`) contrast with irregular accusative forms
(`engem, téged, őt, minket/bennünket, titeket/benneteket, őket`) — 3rd-person accusative pronoun
objects are definite (trigger definite conjugation); 1st/2nd-person accusative pronoun objects are
indefinite; pronoun objects, especially `őket`, are often simply omitted since the verb conjugation
already signals them. Most Hungarian cases have a distinct pronominal form built by adding a
possessive-like ending to a case-specific stem (e.g. `-ban/-ben` → `bennem, benned, benne...`;
`-val/-vel` → `velem, veled, vele...`) — a systematically different paradigm from ordinary noun
declension, tabulated in full pp. 166-167. Several coverbs (`bele, rá, hozzá, neki`) derive
transparently from this pronoun-case system when used with 3rd-person objects (`nekimegy a falnak`
"bumps into the wall," `beleszeret a hangjába` "falls in love with his/her voice"). pp. 165-168.

### Unit 11 — The future with the auxiliary `fog`

Hungarian has no dedicated future-tense conjugation; `fog` + infinitive expresses the future,
conjugating with ordinary present-tense endings (`fogsz hiányozni` "you will be missed/missing").
With a coverb, the coverb separates and sits directly before `fog` (same word-order slot as `kell`,
`lehet`, `akar`, `tud`), often carrying an emphatic reading: `Meg fogom csinálni!` "I *will* do it!"
pp. 170-171.

### Unit 11 — Fleeting-vowel verbs

Parallel to fleeting-vowel nouns, some verb stems (e.g. `hiányzik` "is missing," `érez` "feels")
drop an internal vowel before a vowel-initial ending but retain it before a consonant-initial one
(`hiányzol` vs. `hiányoztál`). p. 172.

### Unit 11 — The reflexive pronoun `maga`

Beyond its formal-`you` function, `maga` (and its full possessive-style paradigm `magam, magad,
maga, magunk, magatok, maguk`) serves as a reflexive pronoun when a speaker refers back to themself
within the same sentence, and takes possessive-style case endings across the full case system
(tabulated p. 174: `magamat/magadat/magát...`, `magamba/magadba/magába...`, etc.). Common reflexive
idioms: `jól érzi magát` ("feels good/has a good time," lit. "feels oneself well"), `meggondolja
magát` ("changes one's mind," lit. "rethinks oneself"), `magában beszél` ("talks to oneself").
pp. 173-174.

### Unit 12 — The imperative

Formed by attaching `-j-` to the verb stem plus personal endings; a short `te`-form exists alongside
the full one (`várj`/`várjál`). Coverbs move to directly after the verb in imperative/request use;
negation uses `ne`/`se` instead of `nem`/`sem`; imperatives always take an exclamation mark in
writing. Requests may add `légy szíves`/`legyen szíves` "please." Verb stems ending in `s`/`sz`/`z`
assimilate the imperative `-j-` (`olvas`→`olvasd`, `néz`→`nézzük`); verbs ending in `-t` follow three
distinct assimilation patterns depending on what precedes the `-t` (`-st/-szt` verbs drop `-t` and
assimilate `-j-` to `s`/`sz`; other consonant+`-t` or long-vowel+`-t` verbs read `-j-` as `-s-`;
short-vowel+`-t` verbs read both `-t` and `-j-` as `-s-`). Irregular imperative stems: `eszik`→
`egy-`, `iszik`→`igy-`, `hisz`→`higgy-`, `lesz`/`van`→`legy-`, `tesz`→`tegy-`, `vesz`→`vegy-`,
`visz`→`vigy-`, `alszik`→`aludj-`, `megy`→`menj-`; `jön` uses its own suppletive
`gyere/gyerünk/gyertek` forms far more often than the regular `jöjj-` paradigm. pp. 181-189.

### Unit 12 — The subjunctive use of the imperative

The subjunctive conjugation is formally identical to the imperative and is used (a) after verbs of
wanting/requesting/suggesting/communicating, and (b) in "in order to"/"so that" clauses, even when
the English equivalent doesn't use a distinct mood: `Azt javasolta, hogy sétáljak a friss levegőn`
"He recommended that I take a walk in the fresh air"; `Hazament, hogy segítsen a szüleinek` "She went
home (in order) to help her parents." pp. 193-194.

### Unit 12 — "I need you!" (`szükségem van rá`)

Need is expressed via `szükség` ("necessity") in a possessive "have"-construction: `szükség` takes a
possessive ending for the person who needs, and the needed person/thing is marked with `-ra/-re`:
`Gabinak szükksége van új cipőre` "Gabi needs new shoes"; `Nincs szükségem rá` "I don't need it."
p. 192.

### Unit 13 — Postpositions

Where cases substitute for many English prepositions, postpositions (following, not preceding, the
noun/phrase they govern) cover the rest (`után` "after," `előtt` "before/in front of," `nélkül`
"without," `alatt` "under/during," `miatt` "because of," `között` "between," `mögött` "behind,"
`fölött` "above," `felé` "in the direction of," `körül`/`köré` "around"). Several have a three-way
motion-towards/no-motion/motion-away paradigm parallel to case suffixes of motion (e.g.
`alá`/`alatt`/`alól` "towards under/under/from under" — full nine-postposition table p. 204). To
express "ago," use the postposition `ezelőtt`, with the preceding time phrase in the `-val/-vel`
case: `Két nappal ezelőtt telefonáltam nekik` "I called them two days ago." pp. 200-201, 208.

### Unit 13 — Postpositions and pronouns

A postposition cannot attach directly to a pronoun; instead it takes a possessive-style ending, the
same principle as the pronoun-case system in Unit 11 (`mögötte` "behind him," `nélkülem` "without
me," `miattunk` "because of us," `mellém` "beside me"). The polite pronouns `maga`/`ön`, however,
behave like ordinary nouns here and take no possessive ending (`Ön mögött`, `Maga mellé`).
pp. 207-208.

### Unit 13 — The adverbial participle `-va/-ve`

Expresses two simultaneous actions where one serves as background to the other, analogous to an
English "-ing" adverbial clause: `Énekelve lépett be a szobába` "She came into the room singing";
`Könyörögve nézett rám` "He looked at me pleadingly." The participle attaches to the verb stem and
functions as an adverb, typically preceding the main verb. p. 202.

### Unit 13 — Postpositions and motion / postpositions and time

Several postpositions of location double as time expressions with the same form (`előtt` "before,"
`után` "after," `között` "between," `alatt` "under, *during*" — e.g. `A szerződést fél óra alatt meg
tudjuk írni` "We could write the contract in under an hour"). pp. 208.

### Unit 13 — The verb ending `-lak/-lek` ("I ... you")

A dedicated portmanteau verb ending marks the specific subject/object combination `én` (subject) +
`téged`/`titeket`/`benneteket` (object): present tense `-lak/-lek`, past/subjunctive `-alak/-elek`.
The pronouns need never be stated — the ending alone conveys both subject and object:
`Megvárlak (téged)` "I'll wait for you"; `Sokáig kerestelek (titeket)` "I was looking for you for a
long time"; `Meg akarlak látogatni` "I want to visit you"; `Elvigyelek a moziba?` "Should I take you
to the cinema?" This is the single grammar point the chapter's own dialogue title ("Szeretlek!" "I
love you!") is built around. p. 212.

## Notable findings (colloquial/register/idiom vocabulary)

Per this project's specific interest in informal/colloquial-register material, the following are
flagged from this extraction:

1. **`Ne fesd az ördögöt a falra!`** ("Don't paint the devil on the wall!") — a vivid superstition-
   flavored idiom for "don't think the unthinkable, it may just happen," reinforced by the source
   with its own illustrative cartoon (Unit 12, pp. 186, 195). High mechanics value: figurative
   imagery built on a taboo/superstitious domain (invoking the devil).
2. **`Szállj le rólam!`** ("Get off my back!") — confrontational colloquial imperative idiom (Unit
   13, p. 200), a literal "climb down off me" body-contact metaphor for "stop pestering me."
3. **`Fél füllel hallottam, hogy...`** ("I happened to overhear that...," lit. "I heard with half an
   ear that...") — a partial-body-part idiom for incomplete/overheard information (Unit 13, p. 207),
   structurally parallel to English "I caught wind of."
4. **`komolyra fordítva a szót`** ("speaking seriously," lit. "turning the word to serious") — a
   discourse-register-shift marker explicitly used mid-conversation by a character to signal a
   change from joking to sincere tone (Unit 13, p. 211) — directly useful for future
   register-shift-mechanism analysis.
5. **`rávág`** (colloquial "gives a hasty/unthinking answer") and **`apropó`** (French-loanword
   discourse particle "by the way," Unit 13, p. 199) — both explicitly colloquial-register discourse
   items appearing without a source-provided register tag but clearly informal relative to
   surrounding neutral vocabulary.
6. **`rögzítő`** (colloquial clipped form of `üzenetrögzítő` "answering machine," Unit 13, p. 207) —
   another example of the clipping word-formation pattern (cf. `troli`/`deka`/`csoki`/`foci`/`matek`
   already documented across the *Teach Yourself Hungarian* established files) recurring
   independently in a second course by different authors — increases confidence this is a genuine,
   productive Hungarian slang-formation mechanism, not an artifact of one course's own vocabulary
   choices.
7. **`Tessék??` / `Tessék???` / `Tessék????`** (Unit 13, p. 211) — the ordinarily neutral/polite
   request-to-repeat `Tessék?` is repeated with escalating question marks as a deliberate written-
   register device to convey mounting surprise — a notable orthographic/paralinguistic-register
   pattern worth flagging for `analysis/`'s eventual treatment of written informal registers.
8. **`findzsa`/`dzsida`/`handzsár`** (Ottoman-era loanwords, Unit 10 tail cultural note, p. 163) —
   three further members of the `dzs`-digraph Ottoman-loanword family not previously captured in
   this project's Hungarian corpus (which had `dzsungel`/`bridzs`/`dzsem`/`menedzser`/`dzsessz` from
   the *Teach Yourself Hungarian* Pronunciation section) — confirms this is a real, extended
   productive loanword cluster rather than a handful of isolated items.
9. **Handwritten marginalia found and excluded** (Unit 13, Dialogue 1, printed p. 198/PDF 212): see
   the file-opening note above — a previous owner's cursive-ink strikethrough-and-replacement of part
   of the printed dialogue. Excluded per the marginalia guard; the original printed sentence was used
   instead.
10. No `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]`-style dialectal tags or explicit comparative regional-variant annotations
    appear anywhere in this page range — like the other Hungarian communicative courses already
    extracted, this is a single-dialect (standard Budapest-area spoken) course. `csárda`-style
    regional tags do not recur in this range; `függőfolyosó` (hanging interior-courtyard corridor,
    p. 203) is tagged `regional` here as a distinctively Budapest-apartment-block architectural term,
    though not a dialectal-speech marker per se.
