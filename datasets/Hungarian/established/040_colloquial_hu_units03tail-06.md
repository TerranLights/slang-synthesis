## Morpheme breakdowns

Hungarian is agglutinative; the following stacked/compound forms from this range are broken into
their component morphemes rather than glossed as atomic items, per the project's morphological-
typology convention.

- **útlevélellenőrzés** "passport control" = `út` (road) + `levél` (letter) → `útlevél` "passport"
  (lit. "road-letter") + `ellenőrzés` (inspection, itself `ellenőr` "inspector" + `-zés` nominalizing
  suffix) — a three-part compound, p. 38.
- **határátlépés** "border crossing" = `határ` (border) + `átlépés` (crossing over, itself `át-`
  "across" prefix + `lép` "steps" + `-és` nominalizing suffix), p. 38.
- **reggelizik** "eats breakfast" = stem `reggeliz-` (from `reggel` "morning" + `-i` denominal-verb
  suffix) + `-ik` (3sg -ik-class marker); full paradigm given p. 50 (`reggelizem/-el/-ik/-ünk/-etek/
  -enek`), alongside model verb `zongorázik` = `zongora` (piano) + `-áz-` (denominal-verb suffix) +
  `-ik`.
- **rózsaszínű** "pink" = `rózsa` (rose, flower) + `színű` (colour-having, itself `szín` "colour" +
  `-ű` possessive-adjective suffix), p. 52.
- **narancsszínű** "orange" = `narancs` (orange, the fruit) + `színű` (see above), p. 52.
- **vérvörös** "blood-red" = `vér` (blood) + `vörös` (red, marked register), p. 53.
- **vöröshagyma** "onion" = `vörös` (red) + `hagyma` (a bulb vegetable) — lit. "red bulb," p. 53/56.
- **vöröshangya** "red ant" = `vörös` (red) + `hangya` (ant), p. 53.
- **sötétbarna** "dark brown" = `sötét` (dark) + `barna` (brown), p. 52.
- **világoskék** "light blue" = `világos` (light) + `kék` (blue), p. 52.
- **taxiállomás** "taxi stand" = `taxi` (loanword) + `állomás` (station, itself `áll` "stands" +
  `-omás` nominalizing suffix), p. 48/80.
- **útlevélellenőrzés** — see above.
- **elvámolnivaló** "items to declare" = `el-` (perfective prefix) + `vámol-` (to pay customs duty on,
  itself denominal from `vám` "customs") + `-ni` (infinitive) + `-való` ("-able/-ible"-style
  participial suffix, "thing to be [verbed]") — lit. "thing that needs to be customs-declared," p. 90.
- **unokatestvér** "cousin" = `unoka` (grandchild) + `testvér` (sibling, itself `test` "body" + `vér`
  "blood") — lit. "grandchild-sibling," p. 96.
- **édesapa** "father (formal/affectionate)" = `édes` (sweet) + `apa` (father), p. 96.
- **édesanya** "mother (formal/affectionate)" = `édes` (sweet) + `anya` (mother), p. 96.
- **légiutaskísérő**-style compounding recurs in **vámhivatalnok** "customs official" = `vám`
  (customs) + `hivatalnok` (official, itself `hivatal` "office" + `-nok` agentive suffix), p. 89.
- **taxisofőr** "taxi driver" = `taxi` (loanword) + `sofőr` (driver, a French loanword `chauffeur`),
  p. 73.
- **a barátnőm/-öd/-je** "my/your/his-her girlfriend" (vowel-final possessive paradigm) = `barátnő`
  (girlfriend, itself `barát` "friend" + `nő` "woman") + possessive suffix; `-m` (1sg), `-d` (2sg),
  `-ja/-je` (3sg), `-nk` (1pl), `-tok/-tek/-tök` (2pl), `-juk/-jük` (3pl) — full paradigm, p. 91.
- **a bőröndöm/-öd/-je** "my/your/his-her suitcase" (consonant-final possessive paradigm) = `bőrönd`
  (suitcase) + possessive suffix; `-öm` (1sg), `-öd` (2sg), `-je` (3sg, j-initial because `bőrönd`
  ends in `d`), `-ünk` (1pl), `-ötök` (2pl), `-jük` (3pl) — full paradigm, p. 91.
- **a nővéremnek/-ednek/-ének** "to my/your/his-her sister" = possessed noun `nővér` + possessive
  suffix (`-em/-ed/-e`) + dative case `-nek`, demonstrating that a case attaches only after the
  possessive suffix is already in place, p. 93.
- **a csomagomat/-odat/-ját/-unkat/-otokat/-jukat** "my/your/his-her/our/your(pl)/their luggage"
  (accusative of a possessed noun) = `csomag` + possessive suffix + linking vowel `a`/`e` (except
  after the vowel-final 3sg possessive) + accusative `-t`, p. 93.

## Grammar points

1. **Regular present-tense (indefinite) conjugation, pp. 42-45.** Endings follow three-way vowel
   harmony (back `-ok/-sz/Ø/-unk/-tok/-nak`; front unrounded `-ek/-sz/Ø/-ünk/-tek/-nek`; front rounded
   `-ök/-sz/Ø/-ünk/-tök/-nek`), attached to the verb stem = the bare 3sg dictionary form. Two regular
   sub-classes need special handling: (a) verbs ending in `-ít` or two consonants (e.g. `tanít`,
   `ért`, `tölt`) need a linking vowel for the `te`/`ő k` endings (`-asz/-esz`, `-anak/-enek`, and
   `ti`-form `-otok/-etek/-ötök`); (b) verbs whose stem ends in `s`, `sz`, or `z` take `-ol/-el/-öl`
   (not `-sz`) for the `te` form, since a doubled sibilant is hard to pronounce (`olvas → olvasol`,
   `vesz → veszel`, `főz → főzöl`).
2. **The infinitive `-ni` ending.** Attaches directly to the stem (`tudni`, `szeretni`); stems ending
   in two consonants or `-ít` need a linking vowel `a`/`e` (`tanítani`, `érteni`).
3. **The `-ik` verb class (major topic, pp. 49-50).** A large class of verbs whose dictionary
   (3sg) form itself ends in `-ik` (e.g. `reggelizik`, `zongorázik`, `dohányzik`, `vacsorázik`,
   `hiányzik`, `érkezik`, `találkozik`); the stem is found by stripping `-ik`. Prescriptively the `én`
   form takes `-om/-em/-öm`, though colloquial spoken usage often substitutes the regular indefinite
   `-ok/-ek/-ök` instead — an explicit register/prescriptive-vs-actual-usage note from the source
   itself.
4. **Expressing a preference: `inkább`.** Hungarian has no verb "to prefer"; the adverb `inkább`
   does this work, either alone (`Inkább olvasok` "I prefer to read") or paired with `szeret`
   (`Inkább énekelni szeretek`). A `mint` "than" clause can compare two preferences (`Inkább
   zongorázom, mint tanulok`). Word order: `inkább` + the preferred action goes immediately before
   the conjugated verb.
5. **Expressing yourself in a language: `-ul/-ül`.** This case ending attaches to a language name
   whenever you speak/read/write/understand *in* that language (`Beszélek magyarul`); back/front
   vowel-harmony pair, with the usual `a→á`/`e→é` lengthening before it.
6. **`ez`/`az` modifying a noun directly (Part II), pp. 51-52.** Two rules apply whenever `ez`/`az`
   directly modifies a noun rather than standing alone: (a) a definite article always follows `ez`/
   `az` before the noun, with no equivalent in English (`Ez a fekete csomag nehéz`); (b) `ez`/`az`
   must agree in number with the noun they modify (`Ezek a fekete csomagok nehezek`) — but a
   preceding adjective (here `fekete`) never itself agrees in number, staying singular regardless.
7. **Colour vocabulary and the `piros`/`vörös` register split (Unit 3 Cultural notes, p. 53).** Both
   words translate as "red," but they are not interchangeable: `vörös` is preferred for nature/
   biological referents (wine, blood, hair/beard colour, animal names), institutions (Vöröskereszt
   "Red Cross"), and communism/Soviet-era political referents specific to Hungary (vörös zászló,
   Vörös Hadsereg, the young pioneers' red scarf); `piros` is the register-neutral default elsewhere.
   This is presented by the source as a genuinely tricky point that "even fewer non-native speakers
   get right" — a real sociolinguistic/collocational split within a single colour concept, not just
   two synonyms.
8. **The accusative `-t` ending (major topic, pp. 57-58).** Marks the direct object; formation rules
   parallel the plural (Unit 1) with one key difference: nouns ending in the consonants `r, l, ny, n,
   j, ly, s, sz, z, zs` take the accusative with **no** linking vowel (unlike the plural, which always
   uses one after a consonant-final stem) — e.g. `baj → bajt` (accusative) vs. `bajok` (plural).
   Nouns ending in any other consonant use a linking vowel `o/e/ö` per vowel harmony, matching the
   plural pattern.
9. **Use of the singular after quantity expressions.** Hungarian uses the singular noun form after
   any number or other expression of quantity (`Harminc deka téliszalámit`, `Öt pár virslit`), and
   commonly uses the singular for fruits/vegetables generally, reserving the plural for distinguishing
   different varieties.
10. **`két` vs `kettő`; `kis` vs `kicsi` (p. 65).** Both "two" and "small" have a short form used when
    the word directly precedes what it modifies (`két saláta`, `kis kocsi`) and a longer form used
    elsewhere/standing alone (`kettő`, `kicsi`): "Hány kiló alma ez? Kettő." / "Ez a kocsi kicsi."
11. **Future time, two strategies.** (a) The present tense often does double duty for the future when
    context makes the future meaning clear, exactly as in English ("Azután készítek szendvicseket" =
    "Then I am going to prepare sandwiches"). (b) The verb `lesz` (infinitive `lenni`) is the
    irregular future form of `van` ("will be, becomes"), conjugating regularly in the present tense
    once you know its irregular stem.
12. **Cardinal numbers 1-9999+ (pp. 64-65).** 1-10 basic forms; 11-29 built on combinatory stems
    `tizen-` and `huszon-` + the base numeral; 30-99 formed by directly juxtaposing the tens word and
    the 1-9 word (as in English) but written as one word; all numbers 1-999 are written as a single
    word in Hungarian, with compound numbers above 1000 optionally hyphenated at the thousands break
    (e.g. `kilencezer-kilencszázkilencvenkilenc`).
13. **Apologies and responses (p. 66).** Three apology phrases with a register split: `Bocsánat` and
    `Elnézést` work in both formal and informal contexts, while `Bocs` is informal-only (a clipped
    form). Responses are register-neutral across the board: `Semmi baj`/`Nem baj` ("no problem"),
    `Semmi gond` ("no trouble"), `Ugyan` ("oh well/never mind"), `Nem történt semmi` ("nothing
    happened").
14. **`Parancsol` as a polite-service register form (p. 66).** Literally "orders/demands," but used
    by salespeople as a courteous "What would you like?"/"What can I do for you?" (`Mit parancsol?`
    singular, `Mit parancsolnak?` plural) — a semantically counter-intuitive but firmly conventional
    politeness form, parallel to `tessék`'s own multi-use flexibility (see next point).
15. **The multi-use word `tessék` (Unit 4 Cultural notes, p. 68).** A single short word covering at
    least five distinct pragmatic functions: "please come in" (answering a knock), a phone-answering
    greeting, an offer of food/an object/a seat ("please take it"/"please take a seat"), a shop
    assistant's "how can I help you," a request to repeat unclear speech ("pardon?"), and the doubled
    market-vendor call `Tessék, tessék...` ("come look here"). A genuinely dense single-word
    pragmatics item.
16. **The definite conjugation (major topic, pp. 77-79).** Used whenever the sentence's direct object
    is definite; the object counts as definite if it (1) has a definite article, (2) is or is modified
    by `ez`/`az`, (3) is a proper noun, (4) has a possessive ending, (5) is a third-person pronoun
    (even unstated — the verb ending alone can supply "him/her/it/them"), (6) is the reciprocal
    pronoun `egymás` "each other," (7) is or is modified by `melyik` "which," or (8) is understood as
    the unstated `azt` introducing a subordinate `hogy`-clause. Endings (`-om/-od/-ja` etc.) follow
    vowel harmony; an additional regular process applies to stems ending in `s/sz/z/dz`, where a
    `j`-initial ending's `j` assimilates fully into the preceding sibilant (`olvas+ja → olvassa`,
    `vesz+jük → vesszük`, `hoz+játok → hozzátok`, `edz+jük → eddzük`).
17. **The dative case `-nak/-nek` (major topic, pp. 84-86).** Attaches directly to a noun (even a
    name), lengthening final `a`/`e` to `á`/`é`; back words take `-nak`, front words `-nek`; the
    personal-pronoun dative forms (`nekem, neked, neki, nekünk, nektek, nekik`) are irregular/
    suppletive rather than built by attaching the case ending. Core uses covered: indirect object
    ("to/for" someone); several verbs that require the dative where English does not use an indirect
    object (`telefonál`, `örül`); and the `tetszik` "to be pleasing to" construction, where the liked
    thing is grammatically the subject and the person who likes it takes the dative (`Nekem nagyon
    tetszik Budapest!` lit. "Budapest is very pleasing to me").
18. **Two irregular verbs of motion: `megy` "goes" and `jön` "comes" (p. 86-87).** Both irregular in
    the present tense (`megyek/mész/megy/megyünk/mentek/mennek`; `jövök/jössz/jön/jövünk/jöttök/
    jönnek`); as verbs of motion they never take a direct object, so they never have a definite
    conjugation form to worry about.
19. **Possessive endings (major topic, pp. 90-93).** Vowel-final nouns lengthen a final `a`/`e` to
    `á`/`é` before attaching `-m, -d, -ja/-je, -nk, -tok/-tek/-tök, -juk/-jük`; consonant-final nouns
    attach `-om/-em/-öm, -od/-ed/-öd, -(j)a/-(j)e, -unk/-ünk, -otok/-etek/-ötök, -(j)uk/-(j)ük`, with
    third-person forms not fully predictable — though nouns ending in `c, cs, gy, j, ly, ny, s, sz,
    ty, z, zs` never take a j-initial third-person ending, and back-vowel nouns ending in `b, d, g, p,
    t, k` very often do. A stressed/contrastive possessor uses the ordinary subject pronoun before the
    possessed noun, with one exception: "their" uses `ő` (not `ők`) in this contrastive position.
20. **Possession combined with case (p. 93).** Any case attaches *after* the possessive ending, never
    before. Adding the accusative to a possessed noun needs a linking vowel `a`/`e` after every
    possessive ending except the vowel-final 3sg form; adding most other cases (e.g. the dative) needs
    no linking vowel at all, though the 3sg possessive's final vowel is still lengthened as usual.
21. **`Hol?`/`Hova?`/`Merre?` (p. 94).** Three near-synonymous "where" question words with distinct
    scope: `Hol?` = static location ("where is X"); `Hova?` = direction of motion ("where to");
    `Merre?` = usable for either a static or moving referent, glossed as "which way, in what
    direction."
22. **Hungarian currency and numbers-as-nouns (Unit 6 Cultural notes, pp. 87-89).** The forint (Ft)
    is the unit, formerly divisible into 100 fillér (now largely obsolete); coins/notes in circulation
    range 1 Ft to 10,000 Ft. Numeral roots can be nominalized with a `-s`-type suffix to name a
    specific coin/note or a numbered public-transport line (`ötös` "the number 5," "a five-forint
    coin"; `kettes villamos` "tram number 2"; `tizenötös busz` "bus number 15").
23. **Hungarian kinship terminology (Unit 6 Cultural notes, pp. 96-97).** Distinguishes sibling age
    with entirely distinct nouns for each combination of gender and relative age (`báty`/`öcs` for
    older/younger brother, `nővér`/`húg` for older/younger sister) — a category English does not mark
    lexically at all. `édes-` ("sweet") prefixed to `apa`/`anya` yields a more formal/respectful
    register variant. The clipped in-law-derived address terms `bácsi`/`néni` are conventionally
    attached to a stranger's first name by children and are unmarked for actual family relation.
