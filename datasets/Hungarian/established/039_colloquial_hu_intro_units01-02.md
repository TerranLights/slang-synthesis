# Hungarian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Colloquial Hungarian (Rounds & Sólyom, 2002), Introduction + Units 1-2

**Source:** Carol H. Rounds and Erika Sólyom, *Colloquial Hungarian: The Complete Course for
Beginners* (Routledge, 2002), "Colloquial-Hungarian-Разговорный-Венгерский-.pdf" (scanned, no text
layer confirmed via an empty `pdftotext` test — read entirely visually). Front matter (cover, title
page, copyright, Contents, Preface, Acknowledgements) + Introduction (pronunciation guide) + Unit 1
"Ismerősök" (Acquaintances) + Unit 2 "A repülőút" (The flight), printed pp. i-xii, 1-37, corresponding
to **PDF pages 1-51** of the 334-page scan.

**Page-offset verification (mandatory check per the vision-reading gotcha):** empirically confirmed
by direct inspection, not assumed. Front-matter roman-numeral pages follow `printed_roman = PDF − 2`
(e.g., PDF page 8 shows printed "vi" at the foot of the Contents' second page). Arabic-numbered body
pages follow a **different, but equally consistent**, `printed_page = PDF − 14` offset starting once
the book's own arabic pagination begins: PDF page 15 shows the "Introduction" title page (printed
p. 1); PDF page 19 shows printed p. 5 (end of the Introduction's Intonation section); PDF page 20 is
the Unit 1 title page (printed p. 6, matching the Contents' own listing); PDF page 35 shows printed
p. 21, the confirmed last page of Unit 1's own content (the "Names" cultural note's tail, ending
mid-paragraph with "good luck to the language learner and the genealogist!"); PDF page 36 is the
Unit 2 title page (printed p. 22, matching Contents); PDF page 51 shows printed p. 37, the confirmed
end of Unit 2 (the "pertut inni" cultural note, immediately followed by the Unit 3 "Határátlépés"
title page at PDF 52/printed p. 38). This is a **flat, single-page-per-image scan** — the "two
printed pages per image" gotcha does **not** apply to this source.

**Scope decision — stopped short of the assigned PDF 1-56 range:** the dispatch's assigned range
(PDF 1-56) would have cut roughly a third of the way into Unit 3 "Határátlépés" (printed pp. 38-53,
PDF 52-67 — only PDF 52-56, 5 of 16 pages, would have been covered). Per the coverage rule's
preference for complete units, this chunk stops at the clean Unit 2/Unit 3 boundary (PDF 51) instead,
leaving all of Unit 3 for a follow-on dispatch — flagged below and in the checklist for follow-up.

**Vision-reading notes:** every page in this range is genuinely printed/typeset — **no handwritten
marginalia was found anywhere in this range.** However, this specific scanned copy shows a
consistent **duplex bleed-through/ghosting artifact**: faint mirror-image text and the book's own
running headers/page numbers from the *facing* printed page show through faintly on many pages
(most visible on the Contents, Preface, and Acknowledgements pages, e.g. PDF page 12 shows a faint
reversed "Colloquial Hungarian" title bleeding through under the real "Acknowledgements" heading).
This is a **scanning/printing artifact, not reader-added marginalia** — it was never mistaken for or
extracted as content, and is flagged here only because it is visually similar to the marginalia risk
the extraction spec warns about and is worth naming explicitly so it isn't rediscovered as a false
alarm by a future chunk. All extracted vocabulary below was read from clearly legible primary-layer
print; Vision Reading Confidence is marked `verified` where a word is independently cross-checked
(appears in both a dialogue and its own Vocabulary box, or recurs across multiple dialogues) and
`plausible_unverified` where a word appears clearly but only once.

## Morpheme breakdowns

Hungarian is agglutinative; the following stacked forms from this range are broken into their
component morphemes rather than glossed as atomic items, per the project's morphological-typology
convention.

- **amerikaiak** "Americans" = `amerikai` (American) + `-ak` (plural, linking vowel *a* required
  because the stem ends in *-i*, per the "adjectives ending in i/ú/ű" plural rule, p. 18).
- **egyetemisták** "students" = `egyetemista` (student) + `-k` (plural; final *-a* lengthens to *-á*
  before the plural marker, the regular noun-plural vowel-lengthening rule, p. 17).
- **ismerősök** "acquaintances" = `ismerős` (acquaintance) + `-ök` (plural; front rounded linking
  vowel, because the stem's last vowel *ő* is front-rounded, p. 17). Worked example in the source
  itself for the o/e/ö three-way linking-vowel choice.
- **tanárok** "teachers" = `tanár` (teacher) + `-ok` (plural; back linking vowel, because the stem
  is a back-vowel word, p. 17).
- **szülők** "parents" = `szülő` (parent) + `-k` (plural; final *-ő* is a vowel already, so the
  plural marker attaches with no linking vowel and no lengthening, p. 17).
- **drágák** "expensive/dear ones" (adj. plural) = `drága` (dear) + `-k` (adjective plural; final
  *-a* lengthens to *-á*, p. 18).
- **hosszúak** "long ones" (adj. plural) = `hosszú` (long) + `-ak` (adjective plural; linking vowel
  *a* required because the stem ends in *-ú*, same class as `amerikai`, p. 18).
- **budapesti** "from/of Budapest" = `Budapest` (place name) + `-i` (adjectivizer denoting origin/
  residence, p. 11 — the general place-name-to-adjective rule covering `bostoni`, `londoni` as well).
- **Szabóné** "Mrs. Szabó" = `Szabó` (surname) + `-né` (suffix marking a married woman, traditionally
  attached to the husband's name; the source's Cultural Notes on Names, p. 20-21, lay out the full
  paradigm of alternatives, e.g. `Szabó Zoltánné`, `Szabóné Egri Zsófia`, `Szabó Zoltánné Egri Zsófia`,
  `Szabó Zsófia`).
- **repülőjegy** "plane ticket" = `repülő` (flying/airplane, from `repül` "to fly" + `-ő` present-
  participle-forming suffix) + `jegy` (ticket) — a compound, not a suffixed form, but its own first
  component is itself derived.
- **repülőút** "flight" = `repülő` (flying, see above) + `út` (road/journey) — compound, "flying-
  journey."
- **légiutaskísérő** "flight attendant" = `légi` (aerial, from `lég` "air" + `-i` adjectivizer) +
  `utas` (passenger, itself `út` "road/journey" + `-as` agentive/possessive-adjective suffix) +
  `kísérő` (escort/companion, from `kísér` "to accompany" + `-ő` present-participle suffix) — a
  three-root compound literally "aerial passenger-escort."
- **utaslista** "passenger list" = `utas` (passenger, see above) + `lista` (list, a loanword) —
  compound.
- **gépen** "on the plane" = `gép` (machine/plane) + `-en` (superessive case ending, "on/at," front-
  vowel-word variant of `-on/-en/-ön`).
- **Örülök, hogy megismertelek** "I'm glad that I got to know you" (the informal "nice to meet you")
  = `örül-` (be glad, stem) + `-ök` (1sg present indefinite) `, hogy` (that) `meg-` (perfective/
  completive verbal prefix) + `ismer-` (know, stem) + `-te-` (past tense marker) + `-lek` (the
  portmanteau ending marking simultaneously a 1st-person singular subject acting on a 2nd-person
  singular object — "I [verbed] you"). The formal counterpart **megismertem** swaps this portmanteau
  for the plain 1sg indefinite past `-tem`, since the formal `Ön`/`Maga` are grammatically 3rd
  person and take no special subject-object portmanteau.

## Grammar points

1. **No verb "to be" for simple identification (3rd person).** Sentences identifying a 3rd-person
   subject via a noun or adjective predicate use no copula at all (`Ő történész.` "S/he is a
   historian."); the verb `van` exists but is reserved for locative/existential sentences (see point
   6) and is introduced only in Unit 2.
2. **Definite article `a(z)`.** `a` before a consonant-initial word, `az` before a vowel-initial word
   (`a művész`, `az anya`) — the same consonant/vowel-triggered allomorphy pattern as English "a/an,"
   but conditioned on the *following* word.
3. **Third person singular pronoun `ő`.** Gender-neutral ("he" and "she" both); frequently omitted
   entirely when context is clear, since Hungarian is a pro-drop language for subject pronouns.
4. **Place-name-to-adjective derivation.** Add `-i` to a place name to form an uncapitalized
   adjective of origin/residence (`Amerika → amerikai`, `Boston → bostoni`). Ethnonyms not already
   ending in `-i` (e.g. `magyar`, `angol`, `finn`, `horvát`, `cseh`) instead behave as ordinary nouns
   for pluralization purposes; only `-i`-final origin words decline as adjectives (`budapesti`,
   `bostoni`, `amerikai`, `londoni`).
5. **Vowel harmony (major topic, pp. 15-17).** Hungarian endings mostly come in front/back variants
   (occasionally three-way front-unrounded/front-rounded/back) to match the vowel quality of the
   word they attach to. Back vowels: `a á o ó u ú`. Front unrounded: `e é i í`. Front rounded:
   `ö ő ü ű`. The neutral vowels `e é i í` are formally front but may appear in back-vowel ("mixed")
   words, which are then treated as back-vowel words for suffixation purposes (`papír`, `tanít`,
   `iroda`, `fiú`); a word containing only neutral vowels is treated as front.
6. **Plural of nouns: `-k`, with a linking vowel `o/e/ö` before consonant-final stems** (vowel-
   harmony-governed: `o` for back-vowel stems, `e` for front-unrounded, `ö` for front-rounded), and
   vowel lengthening (`a→á`, `e→é`) before the plural when the stem itself ends in `a` or `e`.
7. **Plural of adjectives: `-k`, but with a linking vowel `a/e` (not `o/e/ö`)** when required —
   different from the noun-plural linking-vowel set. Adjectives ending in `a/e` lengthen the same
   way as nouns; adjectives ending in `i/ú/ű` always take a linking vowel; adjectives ending in
   `ó/ő` are unpredictable (some take the linking vowel, some don't, some allow either);
   consonant-final adjectives always use `a/e`.
8. **Interrogative/pronoun number agreement.** `ki?`/`kik?` "who?" (sg/pl) and `mi?`/`mik?` "what?"
   (sg/pl) both show explicit plural marking to agree in number with their answer — a pattern that
   extends to all subjects, including pronouns.
9. **Existential/locative "is": `van`/`vannak`, negative `nincs(en)`/`nincsenek` (major topic,
   Unit 2).** `van` is obligatory whenever "is" is modified by a locative/existential adverb (`Itt
   van az újság.`) but must NOT appear in a bare predicate-identification sentence (`Ez német
   újság.`, no `van`). Negation replaces `van`/`vannak` with `nincs`/`nincsen` (either acceptable)
   and `nincsenek`, rather than adding `nem`.
10. **The full present-tense paradigm of "to be" (`van`), pp. 31-32:** singular `(én) vagyok`,
    `(te) vagy`, `(ő) van`; plural `(mi) vagyunk`, `(ti) vagytok`, `(ők) vannak`. The formal 2nd-
    person pronouns (`Ön/Maga`, `Önök/Maguk`) grammatically trigger 3rd-person verb forms
    (`Ön van...`/`Önök vannak...`) since they are formally 3rd-person pronouns — a genuinely
    important register-syntax interaction, not just a vocabulary choice.
11. **Focus-word word order (major topic, pp. 32-33).** A "focus word" — negation (`nem`, `nincs`,
    `nincsenek`), a question word (`ki?`, `mi?`, `hol?`), its answer, or any emphasized word/phrase —
    must sit immediately before the verb, displacing whatever would otherwise occupy that slot to
    immediately after the verb instead. This is described as "a crucial word-order rule... almost
    never broken." Yes/no questions with no focus word keep declarative word order, distinguished
    from statements purely by the special rise-fall intonation pattern on the final two syllables
    (cross-references the Introduction's Intonation section).
12. **Number agreement in predicate-adjective/noun sentences.** Hungarian requires plural agreement
    on predicate nouns/adjectives where English uses no plural (`A lányok magyarok.` "The girls are
    Hungarian[s]." — lit. "Hungarians").
13. **Personal-pronoun/address-register system (major topic, Unit 2, pp. 31, 36-37).** Six "you"
    pronouns split across informal `te/ti` (`tegezés`) and two formal registers, `Ön/Önök`
    (recommended default, `önözés`) and `Maga/Maguk` (`magázás`, explicitly flagged as a "delicate"
    register call even native speakers can get wrong). The source gives concrete situational rules
    (official vs. private settings, age, in-group vs. out-group, gender-conditioned default for men
    addressing women) and introduces the **`pertut inni`** ritual — literally "to drink a pertu" —
    as the culturally marked act of a toast that licenses a permanent switch from formal to informal
    address between two people.
14. **`Csókolom!`** is presented as a genuinely three-way register form distinct from a simple
    formal/informal binary: derived from `Kezét csókolom!` ("I kiss your hand"), conventionally used
    by men greeting women and by children/younger people greeting older adults — simultaneously
    formal *and* affectionate, not reducible to either register alone.
