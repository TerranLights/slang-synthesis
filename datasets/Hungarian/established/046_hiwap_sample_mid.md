### Morpheme Breakdown — agglutinative forms worth decomposing

- **`nélkülem`** ("without me") = `nélkül` ("without") + `-em` (1sg possessive-style suffix on a
  postposition) — full paradigm: `nélkülem` (1sg), `nélküled` (2sg fam.), `nélküle` (3sg/formal-2sg
  `ön nélkül`), `nélkülünk` (1pl), `nélkületek` (2pl fam.), `nélkülük` (3pl/formal-2pl `önök nélkül`) —
  Hungarian postpositions of this class inflect for person/number exactly like possessed nouns, not
  via a separate pronoun. Lesson 13, p. 293.
- **`ahogy(an)`** ("as, in the way that") = `a-` (relative-linking element) + `hogy(an)` ("how") — pairs
  with `úgy` ("so, that way") in the correlative construction `úgy...ahogy(an)`, e.g. `Tamás úgy utazik,
  ahogyan szeret` ("Tamás travels the way he likes to"). An alternative correlative uses `mint` ("as,
  like") in the second slot instead of `ahogyan` with no change in meaning. Lesson 13, p. 294.
- **`Laci autójának a lámpája`** ("the lamp of Laci's car") = `Laci` (possessor-1, bare) + `autó-já-nak`
  (`autó` "car" + 3sg possessive `-ja` + dative/genitive-linking `-nak`, obligatory on the *first*
  possessor in a double-possession chain) + `a lámpá-ja` (`lámpa` "lamp" + 3sg possessive `-ja` again,
  on the final possessed noun). The rule stated explicitly by the source: **in double possession, the
  first (outer) possessed noun obligatorily takes `-nak/-nek`**, marking it simultaneously as
  "possessed" (relative to Laci) and "possessor" (relative to the lamp) — a genuine chained-possession
  morphological pattern distinct from the single-possession `-ja/-je` seen elsewhere in this project's
  Hungarian extractions. Lesson 16, p. 376.
- **`mostam`/`mostad`/`mosta`...** (Class I past tense of `mos` "wash") = `mos-` (root) + `-t-` (past
  tense marker) + person/number suffix (`-am`/`-ad`/`-a` indefinite vs. definite 1-3sg, etc.) — the
  past-tense marker `-t-` is common to all three conjugation classes (see Grammar points), with the
  classes differing only in what precedes it (a linking vowel, or none). Lesson 14, p. 319-320.
- **`tudnak`-type present-tense allomorphy is not repeated here** (already documented in the `034_tyh`
  file); this dispatch's parallel past-tense case is **`néztem`/`néztem` (indefinite/definite 1sg of
  `néz` "look")** = `néz-` + `-t-` (past) + `-em` (1sg, front-vowel harmony allomorph of `-am`/`-em`) —
  illustrating that the past-tense person-suffixes are a *distinct* paradigm from the present-tense
  ones documented in `034_tyh`, not a simple tense-swap of the same endings. p. 319.

## Grammar points

### Lesson 9 — Definite vs. indefinite conjugation

One of Hungarian's most structurally distinctive features: verbs are conjugated differently depending
on whether their direct object is *definite* (has a definite article, is a proper noun, or is otherwise
specific) or *indefinite*/absent. `Péter fut` ("Péter runs," no object → indefinite) vs. `Péter lát egy
autót` ("Péter sees a car," indefinite object → still indefinite conjugation) vs. `Péter az autót
látja` ("Péter sees the car," definite object → definite conjugation, `látja` not `lát`) and `Katit
várjuk` ("We are waiting for Kati," a proper-noun object → definite conjugation `várjuk` not `várunk`).
Full definite-conjugation paradigms given for `rajzol`/`kér`/`üt` and `olvas`/`vesz`/`néz`, with the
suffix set `-om/-em/-öm, -od/-ed/-öd, -ja/-i, -juk/-jük, -játok/-itek, -ják/-ik` abstracted out
separately (vowel-harmony-conditioned, as usual). p. 215-216.

### Lesson 12 — SZÓREND (word order) for `akar`("want") + infinitive constructions

A four-column word-order template (A / B~focus / negation~C~C / D~infinitive-clause) systematically
walks through how Hungarian topic/focus/negation structure reorders a sentence like "Péter sétálni
akar" ("Péter wants to go for a walk") under wh-question, yes/no-question, and negation
transformations — e.g. focus-fronting a locative prefix (`Be akar menni Péter a moziba?` → "Does Péter
want to go INTO the cinema?" with `be` fronted for focus) rather than simply negating the whole clause.
This is presented as a general template applicable beyond `akar`, extended to `tud` ("can/know how to")
later on the same pages. pp. 276-277.

### Lesson 12 — The `-e` interrogative/reported-question particle

`-e` (equivalent to English "whether"/"if") attaches directly to the end of a conjugated verb or
predicate to mark an embedded yes/no question with no interrogative pronoun of its own: `Éva azt
kérdezi, jó idő van-e` ("Éva asks whether the weather is good," from the direct question "Jó idő
van?"). Explicitly contrasted with wh-word embedded questions (`Erika azt kérdezi, hogy milyen idő
van`), which use `hogy` + the wh-word instead and take no `-e`. p. 281.

### Lesson 13 — `nélkül` ("without") as an inflecting postposition

See Morpheme Breakdown for the full person/number paradigm. Governs a bare noun for a general "without
X" and can itself carry the possessive-style person suffixes directly when the missing item is a
person/pronoun rather than a repeated noun (`Gabi a testvére nélkül megy` → `Gabi nélküle megy`,
"Gabi goes without him/her"). p. 293.

### Lesson 13 — `úgy...ahogy(an)`/`mint` comparative-manner construction

See Morpheme Breakdown. Used for manner-parity comparisons ("X does something the way/as Y does"),
distinct from the more familiar degree-comparison constructions (`mint` alone, "than") — the source
distinguishes this correlative usage from simple comparison by requiring the paired `úgy` in the main
clause. p. 294.

### Lesson 13 — The `-val/-vel` instrumental/comitative suffix

Marks "with" in both the instrumental sense (tool/means: `kanállal eszünk`, "we eat with a spoon") and
the comitative sense (accompaniment: `az orvossal beszélget`, "converses with the doctor"). The `-v-`
assimilates to match a preceding consonant (not tabulated in full on the sampled pages, but demonstrated
across the Ex. 12 drill's varied noun-final consonants — `vízzel`, `szappannal`, etc., inferable from
the fill-in-the-blank pattern though the answer key itself is not printed on these pages). pp. 297-298.

### Lesson 14 — Past tense indicative: three conjugation classes + irregular verbs

The past tense marker is uniformly `-t-`, but its formation splits into three classes by the verb
stem's final sound(s):
- **Class I** — most verbs: `-t-` attaches directly with a linking vowel that harmonizes
  (`-ott/-ett/-ött`) in the 3rd person singular indefinite, e.g. `mosott`, `nézett`, `lökött`.
- **Class II** — stems ending in `-l, -r, -n, -ny, -j, -ly`, or two-syllable stems in `-ad/-ed`: take
  `-t` with no linking vowel in most forms (`tanult`, `pihent`, `kért`, `köszönt`, `ébredt`) — except
  `enged` ("allow"), explicitly flagged as an exception that instead follows Class I.
  - **Class III** — verbs ending in two consonants, in `-ít`, or one-syllable verbs ending in `-t`: also
  take a linking vowel (`ugrott`, `ébresztett`, `tanított`, `töltött`... [`tölt`→`töltött`], `ütött`)
  — but the source notes `mond`, `kezd`, `küld`, and `lát`-type verbs actually pattern with Class I
  despite superficially resembling this class, while `áll`/`száll`-type verbs pattern with Class II.
- **Irregular stems** (suppletive/altered past roots, not derivable from the present stem by the above
  rules): `tesz→tett-`, `vesz→vett-`, `visz→vitt-`, `hisz→hitt-`, `eszik→ett-`, `iszik→itt-`,
  `jön→jött-`, `megy→ment-`, `alszik→aludt-`, `fekszik→feküdt-`, `nő→nőtt-`, `lő→lőtt-`.

A single unified six-person suffix table (indefinite `-am/-em, -ál/-él, —/—, -unk/-ünk, -atok/-etek,
-ak/-ek` vs. definite `-am/-em, -ad/-ed, -a/-e, -uk/-ük, -átok/-étek, -ák/-ék`, plus the 1sg→2sg
"objective" forms `-alak/-elek`) applies uniformly across all three classes once the class-specific
stem-plus-`-t(-)` form is derived. pp. 319-320.

### Lesson 16 — Double possession and the obligatory `-nak/-nek` marker

When a possessed noun is itself the possessor of a further noun (a possession chain, "X's Y's Z"), the
*first* possessor obligatorily takes the dative/genitive-linking suffix `-nak/-nek`, even though a
single, non-chained possessor normally appears bare (`Laci autója`, "Laci's car," no suffix — vs. `Laci
autójá-nak a lámpája`, "the lamp of Laci's car," suffix now obligatory on `Laci` because `autó` is
itself simultaneously possessed-by-Laci and possessor-of-the-lamp). p. 376.
