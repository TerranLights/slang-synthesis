### Morpheme Breakdown — agglutinative forms worth decomposing

- **`leg-magas-abb`** ("the highest/tallest," Unit 18 exercise, "hogy a legmagasabb polcot is eléri" —
  "that he can even reach the highest shelf") = superlative prefix `leg-` + `magas` ("tall/high") +
  comparative/superlative suffix `-abb` — the standard Hungarian superlative-formation pattern
  (superlative = `leg-` + comparative), illustrating that Hungarian marks superlative morphologically
  on the *stem itself* rather than with a separate word ("most"). p. 428.
- **`ki-egyez-és`** ("the Compromise," historically the 1867 Ausgleich) = verbal prefix `ki-` ("out") +
  root `egyez-` ("agree") + nominalizing suffix `-és` — literally "coming-to-agreement," a transparent
  compositional gloss on a term whose historical referent (the compromise) is otherwise opaque from
  the surface form alone. Unit 25, p. 548.
- **`fel-kel-és`** ("uprising") vs. **`le-ver-és`** ("suppression") = a directly paired
  prefix-contrast: `fel-` ("up") + `kel-` ("rise") + `-és` (nominalizer) vs. `le-` ("down") + `ver-`
  ("beat") + `-és` (nominalizer) — the verbal-prefix system encodes the historical-political
  up/down opposition (rising vs. being beaten down) directly in the morphology, a clean illustration
  of how Hungarian's productive verbal-prefix system carries real semantic/aspectual weight beyond
  simple directionality. Unit 25, historical-vocabulary cluster, pp. 540-548.
- **`szok-ott`** ("[habitually] does," Unit 17) — surface identical to the past-tense 3rd-person
  singular suffix `-ott`, but the source explicitly flags that this form's *function* has drifted from
  its historical past-tense morphology to a present-habitual meaning; worth flagging for
  mechanics-analysis as a case where synchronic Hungarian morphology and synchronic *meaning* have
  come apart from each other for one specific lexical item's grammaticalized construction, unlike the
  fully regular past-tense paradigm it otherwise shares its shape with. p. 407.

## Grammar points

### Unit 17 — Future tense: `fog` + infinitive vs. `majd` + present

The "real" grammatical future (`fog` conjugated + infinitive, e.g. "Be **fogok menni** a szobába" — "I
am going to enter the room") is distinguished from the already-covered `majd`+present-tense construction
(Lesson 12): the `fog` construction "generally expresses an intention or a feeling of certainty in
respect to the future action or event," implying a firmer commitment than the more open-ended `majd`
construction. Both indefinite and definite conjugations of `fog` are given in full. p. 401 (PDF 401).

### Unit 17 — `lesz` as the suppletive future of `van`/`nincs`

`Van`("is")/`nincs`("is not") have no future-tense forms of their own; `lesz`/`nem lesz` supplies the
future in both the locative ("Péter **nem lesz** a szobában" — "Péter won't be in the room"),
possessive ("Péternek **lesz** pénze" — "Péter will have money"), and predicate-nominal ("Péter
**mérnök lesz**" — "Péter will be an engineer") constructions already established for `van`/`nincs` in
earlier lessons — this is a clean unification point showing `van`'s suppletive paradigm extends
consistently across all of `van`'s functional uses. `Lesz` is separately flagged as also meaning
"become" (cross-referenced to a later Section 123 not in this dispatch's sampled range). pp. 402-403.

### Unit 17 — `szokott` + infinitive: grammaticalized habitual construction

See Vocabulary entry above; grammatically the single most notable point in this unit — a
morphologically past-tense-shaped verb form whose construction-level meaning is present-habitual, with
the source explicitly warning learners against the false-friend mapping to English "used to." p. 407.

### Unit 18 §111 — `olyan/akkora ..., hogy` (result/consequence clauses)

A clause introduced by `hogy` following a main clause containing `olyan` (quality) or `akkora` (size)
expresses the result or consequence of the degree named in the main clause — structurally parallel to
English "so ... that," but built compositionally from a demonstrative-adjective + subordinator rather
than a single correlative pair. Cross-referenced by the source to its own Section 112. p. 428.

### Unit 18 §112 — Subordinate `hogy`-clauses after fixed governing predicates

A grammar box tabulates three classes of Hungarian predicates that obligatorily take a `hogy`-clause
object, each with its own required "dummy" pronoun placeholder: (1) evaluative predicates (`jó`,
`igaz`, `érdekes`, `hasznos`, `érdekel`, `tetszik`) take optional `(az)` + `hogy`; (2) cognition/report
predicates (`tudja`, `reméli`, `látja`, `hallja`) take optional `(azt)` + `hogy`; (3) emotion/mental-state
predicates (`örül`, `fél`, `gondol`, `gondolkozik`) take one of `(annak)/(attól)/arra/azon` + `hogy` —
i.e. the case-marked dummy pronoun's *form* is lexically selected per governing verb, not free. p. 429.

### Unit 20 — Imperative mood, full paradigm

A complete imperative-suffix drilling exercise spans regular verb classes (`keres`, `száll`,
`vacsorázik`, `vesz`, `hisz`, `alszik`, `választ`, `iszik`, `ért`, `néz`, `mutat`, `vált`, `jön`, `hűt`),
covering both indefinite and definite conjugation and multiple person/number/politeness combinations
via the book's own numeral-icon key system (not fully resolvable from the image alone without that
key, hence the `plausible_unverified` flag on the specific vocabulary row above); still useful as
confirmation this course covers the imperative as a single unified drilling unit rather than
introducing it piecemeal. Notably, the drilling exercise's own example uses the colloquial
`legyél`-type informal-imperative form (`Ne legyél kíváncsi!`) rather than the more conservative
`légy`-type form, without flagging it as informal — see Notable findings. pp. 459-460.

### Unit 25 — "Történelmi lecke" reading-comprehension structure

The unit is explicitly structured as a historical reading ("TÖRTÉNELMI LECKE (I.)" — "History Lesson
(I.)", implying at least a second installment elsewhere in the book, likely just before or after this
sampled range), moving from the 896 CE Magyar conquest and King István's state-founding (1000-1038)
through, per its own comprehension questions, a "szabadságharc" (freedom fight/war of independence)
that failed, the 1867 "kiegyezés" (Compromise), and unspecified 20th-century events, ending with an
open reading-comprehension question about what "we hope for today" (Q12, "Mit remélünk ma?") — framing
the whole historical arc pedagogically as leading to the book's own contemporary (1988/1990) moment.
This is a distinct genre from any of the practical-situational vocabulary units and from any other
Hungarian source extracted so far in this project — worth flagging for mechanics-analysis as the
richest single source of period-specific/historical-register Hungarian vocabulary encountered to date.

## Notable findings (colloquial/register/period vocabulary)

1. **`szia` explicitly glossed in the back-matter dictionary as "(very familiar greeting, used mostly
   among young people)"** — the single clearest register/age-cohort annotation found in this dispatch,
   directly corroborating (from an independent source) the same word's treatment in
   `034_tyh_units01-05.md` (Notable finding #3), where `szia`/`szevasz` were framed as the "current"
   informal norm against `szervusz` as dated.
2. **`szervusz(tok)` given with no register qualifier here**, in contrast to the TYH course's explicit
   "now old-fashioned" framing for the same word — a genuine *disagreement in register-marking
   granularity* between two independently-authored Hungarian courses on the very same lexical item,
   worth flagging for mechanics-analysis: one course (TYH, UK-authored, 2003) treats `szervusz` as
   dated/age-marked; this course (Hungarian-authored, 1988/1990, and thus actually the *earlier*
   publication) marks no such distinction, suggesting either that the "datedness" judgment emerged or
   sharpened between 1990 and 2003, or that the two courses' authors simply made different editorial
   choices about how much register nuance to encode in a glossary vs. a running commentary.
3. **`ne legyél` (colloquial informal-imperative variant) used unmarked as the course's own drilling
   example**, alongside the more conservative `légy`-type form — the source treats this without
   comment as ordinary informal register, itself a soft data point that the `legyél`-type imperative
   was unremarkable/standard informal usage by the time of this book's editions (1988 second edition,
   used for this 1990 printing).
4. **`nyugalom!` ("take it easy!") as a colloquial exclamatory extension of a neutral noun** ("rest,
   calmness") — a clean example of a core-register noun acquiring an idiomatic colloquial-interjection
   use documented directly in the back-matter glossary's own boxed sub-entry.
5. **`amit az ember szeme-szája kíván`** ("whatever one's eye and stomach can wish") and **`Egy-kettő!`**
   ("Hurry up!," literally "one-two!") — two vivid idiomatic/colloquial phrases from the Unit 21
   end-vocabulary "Kifejezések" list, notable for their figurative/exclamatory register relative to the
   surrounding neutral practical vocabulary of the same list.
6. **Historical/political register cluster in Unit 25** (`forradalom`, `szabadságharc`, `kiegyezés`,
   `felkelés`, `leverés`, `függetlenség`, `honfoglalás`, `munkásosztály`, `paraszthaború`) — not
   colloquial/slang register, but a genuinely distinct *domain* register (historical/political
   discourse) not documented from any prior Hungarian `established/` source in this project; worth
   flagging as a vocabulary domain the mechanics-analysis phase should track separately from
   everyday-conversational registers, since slang/informal register often derives its charge from
   ironic or subversive reuse of exactly this kind of formal-register historical/political vocabulary.
7. **Two independent "NÉPDAL" (folk song) inclusions** (Unit 17's "A csitári hegyek alatt," Unit 25's
   "Elindultam szép hazámból") confirm folk song is a recurring pedagogical device throughout this
   book, not a one-off; both are quoted here only by title/incipit per copyright discipline.
8. **No `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]`-style dialectal tags or explicit comparative regional-variant annotations**
   were found anywhere in this sample, consistent with every other Hungarian source extracted in this
   project to date — this remains, across all sampled Hungarian sources, a single-standard-dialect
   teaching tradition with no source yet marking regional/dialectal variation explicitly.

No handwritten marginalia, library stamps, or non-print annotation of any kind were found anywhere in
this page range — this is a clean, consistently-typeset scan throughout the sampled span.
