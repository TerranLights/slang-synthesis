# Hungarian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Chapter 4, Verbs, Part 1 (§4.1–§4.4.4)

**Source:** Carol H. Rounds, *Hungarian: An Essential Grammar* (2nd edition, Routledge, 2009),
Chapter 4 "Verbs," printed pages 15–45 (PDF pages 30–60). Covers §4.1 Verb stems through §4.4.4
Infinitive — i.e., verb stem types, the definite/indefinite conjugation system, the four
tense/mood paradigms (present, past, subjunctive/imperative, conditional), the periphrastic future,
and the non-finite forms (participles and the infinitive). Stops immediately before §4.4.5 Declined
infinitives, which begins a distinct, more specialized sub-topic (impersonal constructions) and is a
clean sub-section boundary very close to the assigned page-60 cutoff. Part 2 of this chapter
(§4.4.5 onward, including the productive derivational endings §4.5 and coverbs) is a separate,
not-yet-extracted dispatch.

**Coverage note.** Every grammar point and effectively every distinct verb (this chapter's central
lexical category — Hungarian verb-stem class is the chapter's whole organizing principle) in this
page range is captured, along with the nouns/adjectives/adverbs from illustrative example sentences
that carry independent lexical content. Proper names (Zsuzsa, Budapest, Lajos, Szeged,
Franciaország, Magyarország, Balaton, Mátyás) are omitted per this project's established
name-vs-lexicon convention. A handful of items already documented in
`002_chapter5_nouns.md` or elsewhere are **not** re-added as duplicate rows when this chapter's
own examples simply reuse them unchanged: `ház`, `elolvas`, `este`, `levél`, `szó`, `hó`, `drága`,
`nő`, `lakik`, `gyerek`. Repeated drill-style example sentences that reuse already-captured
vocabulary purely to illustrate a personal-ending slot already shown for a different verb (e.g. the
6-person paradigm tables) are not re-transcribed cell-by-cell — the pattern is paraphrased once per
paradigm and illustrated with 1–2 forms, per the copyright-discipline rule against bulk-reproducing
tables. No handwritten marginalia or scan artifacts were encountered; the source PDF has a clean
text layer (confirmed via `pdftotext -layout`) apart from the expected cosmetic
"< previous page / page_N / next page >" navigation-text noise, which is ignored throughout.

**Register annotations (explicit in source):** this chapter carries genuine archaic/colloquial
register tags on certain *inflectional endings* rather than standalone lexical items — see
§4.1.1 in Grammar points below (the `-ik`-verb subjunctive/conditional endings and the `-ék` 3sg
ending are explicitly called archaic; the `-om/-em/-öm` 1sg present ending is explicitly noted as
"always correct, if not always colloquial"). These are flagged in the grammar-points prose since
they attach to conjugation slots, not to individual vocabulary rows.

**Morphological typology note.** Hungarian is agglutinative (see `../00_Extraction_Checklist.md`).
This chapter is where verbal agglutination is most dense: a single conjugated verb form routinely
stacks stem + derivational/mood marker + linking vowel + personal ending (and, for 1sg-subject/
2sg-object forms, a fused subject-object portmanteau ending). Several vocabulary entries below carry
a **Morpheme Breakdown**.

### Morpheme breakdown

> **utazzam** = utaz- (verb stem of `utazik` "travel," -ik removed) + zz (subjunctive marker j
> assimilated to the sibilant-final stem) + am (archaic 1sg -ik-verb subjunctive ending, `~
> utazzak` with the regular ending) — "that I travel." Shows both the regular subjunctive-j
> assimilation rule and the archaic vs. regular -ik-verb ending alternation the source explicitly
> flags. pp. 16, 28

> **olvassa** = olvas ("read") + ja (3sg definite present ending) → the j assimilates fully to the
> stem-final sibilant s — "s/he reads it." One of four worked assimilation examples the source
> gives (also `vesz+jük→vesszük`, `hoz+játok→hozzátok`, `edz+jük→eddzük`, the last showing the
> "double only the first letter of a digraph" orthography rule). p. 20

> **ébresszek** = ébreszt- ("wake someone") minus its final -t (lost before the assimilating
> subjunctive marker) + ssz (j assimilated to the resulting sibilant cluster) + ek (1sg subjunctive
> indefinite ending) — "that I wake (someone)." Illustrates the -szt-stem-specific subjunctive rule
> (t-loss + j-to-sz assimilation), distinct from the plain-sibilant rule above. p. 28

> **mutass** = mutat ("show") + j (subjunctive marker) → both the stem-final t and the marker j
> become s — "show!" (imperative/subjunctive stem). Illustrates the fourth and most-fused of the
> source's four subjunctive-marker assimilation classes (short-vowel+t stems). p. 28

> **tanítanék** = tanít ("teach") + a (linking vowel, required before the conditional marker after a
> long-vowel+t stem) + né (1sg conditional marker) + k (1sg indefinite ending) — "I would teach."
> Source's own worked example of the conditional's stem-conditioned linking vowel, explicitly
> compared to the identical rule for the infinitive (`tanítani`). p. 35

> **várjalak** = vár ("wait") + j (subjunctive marker) + alak (fused 1sg-subject/2sg-object
> portmanteau ending, "that I ... you") — "that I wait for you." A distinct personal-ending slot
> that exists specifically for a 1sg subject acting on a 2nd-person object, found across all four
> mood/tense paradigms in this chapter (cf. `ütöttelek` "I struck you," `felhívlak` "I'll call you,"
> `szeretnélek` "I would love you"). p. 31

> **felhívlak** = fel (coverb, "up/completive") + hív ("call") + lak (1sg-subject/2sg-object present
> indefinite ending) — "I'll call you." Present-tense sibling of `várjalak`/`ütöttelek` above, shown
> in the future-tense section as an example of the present tense used with a coverb + time
> expression to express future meaning. p. 39

> **elmentem volna** = el (coverb, "away/completive") + ment (irregular past stem of `megy` "go") +
> em (1sg past indefinite ending) + volna (invariant fixed particle marking past conditional) — "I
> would have left." Illustrates the past conditional as a periphrastic (two-word) construction built
> on an ordinary past-tense form rather than a single further-agglutinated verb form — the one place
> in this chapter's core paradigms where Hungarian steps outside pure stem-suffix stacking. p. 37

> **mosolyogva** = mosolyog ("smile") + va (adverbial participle, "while ~ing") — "(while)
> smiling." Simple stem+suffix form, included as the paradigm case of the adverbial participle
> (front-vowel counterpart: `énekel+ve→énekelve` "while singing"). p. 44

> **érteni** = ért ("understand") + eni (infinitive -ni, with the front linking vowel e required
> after a long-vowel+t/two-consonant stem) — "to understand." Paired in the source with
> `tanít+ani→tanítani` (back-vowel counterpart) and contrasted with vowel-final stems that take no
> linking vowel at all (`olvas+ni→olvasni`). p. 45

> **vádlott** = vád ("accusation," underlying noun, not itself glossed as a separate entry in this
> excerpt) + ol (verb-forming derivational suffix, yielding `vádol` "to accuse") + t (past
> participle) — "defendant," i.e., "the one who has been accused." A three-layer example
> (noun → verb → participle-as-noun) of the same participle-as-noun pattern also seen in `befő→
> befőtt` "preserves" and `szerkeszt→szerkesztő` "editor." p. 43

> **főszereplő** = fő ("main, chief") + szerepel ("to act, play a role" — not itself glossed as a
> separate entry in this excerpt) + ő (present participle) — "lead actor/actress," lit. "main
> role-player." Compounding (fő + a full derived participial noun) layered on top of the ordinary
> present-participle derivation. p. 44

## Grammar points

### 4.1 Verb stems

The Hungarian verb stem is, for most verbs, identical to the 3rd person singular present tense
indefinite form — the citation form used in dictionaries — and stays constant throughout the
conjugation. Some verb classes alter their stem under certain suffixes; these are treated in later
sections (and more fully in §4.6, outside this excerpt). p. 15

### 4.1.1 -ik verbs

A large class of verbs end in `-ik` in the 3sg present indefinite/citation form (e.g. `utazik`
"travel," stem `utaz-`). Beyond the stem-final `-ik` being dropped for suffixation, -ik verbs show
two further alternations:

- In the present tense, `-om/-em/-öm` may replace the regular 1sg indefinite ending
  (`-ok/-ek/-ök`): `utazom ~ utazok` "I travel." **Register annotation (explicit in source,
  footnoted):** "the `-om/-em/-öm` endings are always correct, if not always colloquial" — i.e.
  grammatically acceptable in all registers, but not automatically felt as colloquial in every
  context; usage varies by idiolect and by individual verb, some verbs favoring the -ik-style
  ending, others the regular one.
- The same alternation appears in the subjunctive and conditional 1sg (`utazzam ~ utazzak`,
  `utaznám ~ utaznék`), and a further, 3sg-specific alternate ending `-ék` exists in the
  subjunctive/conditional (`utazzék ~ utazzon`, `utaznék ~ utazna`). **Register annotation
  (explicit in source):** both the 1sg -ik-style endings in the subjunctive/conditional and the
  3sg `-ék` ending are explicitly called **archaic**, with the regular endings having "all but
  replaced" them in speech; `-ék` specifically is called "rarely used in speech." p. 16

### 4.2 Definite and indefinite conjugations

Every Hungarian verb has two parallel conjugations for each tense/mood: the **definite**
conjugation is used when the sentence has a definite direct object; the **indefinite** elsewhere.
Contrast `Látok egy házat` "I see a house" (indefinite) vs. `Látom a házat` "I see the house"
(definite). p. 17

### 4.2.1 Determining the definiteness of an object

A direct object counts as definite in seven distinct environments, each illustrated with a `lát`
"see" example: (a) preceded by the definite article `a/az`; (b) a demonstrative pronoun (`ez/az`,
`ezek/azok`) or modified by one — including cataphoric (forward-referring) uses, overt or implied,
with a footnoted exception where `azt` meaning "that kind of" instead takes the *indefinite*
conjugation (`Azt kérek` "I would like that kind"); (c) has a possessive suffix (`a házadat` "your
house") — including possessive/reflexive pronouns, which themselves contain possessive suffixes;
(d) a proper noun; (e) a 3rd-person pronoun, overt or implied (including the polite `maga/maguk`
and formal `ön/önök` forms) — the pronoun need not be spoken aloud, so a bare `Látom` is ambiguous
among "I see him/her/them/you(formal)" without context, though `őket` "them" is commonly spoken
even when context would allow omitting it; (f) the reciprocal pronoun `egymás`; (g) a modifier
ending in `-ik` (`melyik`, `hányadik`) or preceded by one. pp. 17–18

### 4.2.2 -lak/-lek

A unique conjugational slot — listed with the definite forms though the object isn't inherently
definite — is used specifically when the subject is `én` "I" and the direct object is a 2nd-person
pronoun (`téged`, `benneteket`, `titeket`): `Látlak (téged)` "I see you." With any subject other
than `én`, an ordinary indefinite conjugation is used for a 2nd-person object instead. p. 18

### 4.3.1 Present tense: conjugation

The present tense has no tense marker of its own; personal endings attach directly to the stem.
**Indefinite** (§4.3.1.1): endings vary by vowel harmony (2–3 vowel variants), with a linking
vowel required after stems ending in two consonants or long-vowel+t, and a special 2sg ending
`-ol/-el/-öl` (replacing `-sz`) after sibilant-final stems (`s, sz, z, dz`). **Definite**
(§4.3.1.2): additionally, the `j`-initial endings (`-ja, -juk/-jük, -játok, -ják`) regularly
assimilate to a sibilant-final stem's final consonant, with digraph doubling written by doubling
only the digraph's first letter (`olvas+ja→olvassa`, `vesz+jük→vesszük`, `hoz+játok→hozzátok`,
`edz+jük→eddzük`). Both paradigms are illustrated across five contrasting verb classes (back
vowel, front unrounded, front rounded, sibilant-final, long-vowel+t, and -ik-verb-with-sibilant-
stem); full six-person tables are not reproduced here — see the Morpheme Breakdown entries above
for representative forms. pp. 19–21

### 4.3.2 Present tense: usage

Beyond ordinary present/habitual reference (`Sétálunk a parkban` "We are walking in the park"),
the present tense covers four further environments in this chapter: (§4.3.2.2) future reference,
typically combined with a coverb and/or a future time expression (`Jövő télen veszek egy új autót`
"Next winter I will buy a new car"); (§4.3.2.3) narrating a past event as if it were happening now,
in colloquial storytelling register — paralleled in colloquial English; (§4.3.2.4) reported speech,
where Hungarian (unlike English) always preserves the tense the speaker originally used, rather than
"backshifting" to match the reporting context; (§4.3.2.5) an action begun in the past that continues
into the present, paired with `óta` "since" (`Január óta dolgozom a könyvtárban` "I have been
working in the library since January"). pp. 22–23

### 4.3.3 Past tense: conjugation

The past-tense marker sits between stem and personal endings, in a long form (`-ott/-ett/-ött`,
vowel-harmony-governed) or a short form (`-t`). Three stem classes determine which form(s) a verb
takes:

- **Class A** (always long form): verbs ending in long-vowel+t; verbs ending in two consonants; and
  exactly eight monosyllabic short-vowel+t verbs (`fut, hat, jut, köt, nyit, süt, üt, vet`).
- **Class B** (always short form): verbs ending in a single `j, l, ly, n, ny,` or `r`; many
  bisyllabic verbs ending in `-ad/-ed`.
- **Class C** (short form throughout except the 3sg indefinite, which is long): the
  negatively-defined default — everything not in A or B.

Exceptions are explicitly flagged: `lát, küld, mond, kezd, függ, fedd` look like Class A (ending in
two consonants) but conjugate as Class C; `áll, száll, varr, forr` look like Class A but conjugate
as Class B; `fürdik` "bathe" conjugates as either A or C in most forms but only the long form in
3sg. After the correct marker is chosen, personal endings attach unchanged (indefinite §4.3.3.1,
definite §4.3.3.2); full paradigm tables for `süt/marad/szeret/olvas` (indefinite) and
`üt/kér/bemutat` (definite) are given but not reproduced verbatim here. pp. 23–26

### 4.3.4 Past tense: usage

(§4.3.4.1) Ordinary past reference, as in English. (§4.3.4.2) Because Hungarian has only one past
tense form, coverbs and time expressions do the work that English's compound tenses (simple past,
present perfect, past perfect) do — a coverb-prefixed past verb is inherently perfective
(`Elolvastam a könyvet` "I read/have read/had read the book"), while the same verb without a coverb
is aspectually ambiguous (`Olvastam a könyvet`). (§4.3.4.3) The defective auxiliary `szokott`
"usually" occurs only in past-tense form, though its meaning may be past- or present-habitual.
(§4.3.4.4) In compound sentences, the past tense can indicate the completion of an action that is
itself in the future relative to the main clause (`Ha megebédeltem, lefekszem egy órára` "When I
have finished my lunch, I will lie down for an hour"). pp. 26–27

### 4.3.5 Subjunctive/imperative: conjugation

The subjunctive doubles as the imperative and is referred to by the source under one label for
brevity. Its marker `j` sits between stem and personal endings and undergoes four regular
alternations depending on the stem's final segment: (1) sibilant-final stems (`s, sz, z, dz`) —
`j` fully assimilates (`keres+j→keress-`); (2) stems ending in `-st`/`-szt` — the stem-final `t` is
lost and `j` assimilates to the sibilant (`ébreszt+j→ébressz-`); (3) stems ending in
long-vowel+t or consonant+t (other than the -st/-szt case) — `j` becomes `s` (`segít+j→segíts-`);
(4) stems ending in short-vowel+t — both the stem-final `t` and `j` become `s`
(`mutat+j→mutass-`). In the **definite** conjugation (§4.3.5.2) the 2sg ending has both a long form
(`kérjed`) and a short form arrived at by dropping `j` (or its assimilated reflex) and the following
vowel (`~kérd`) — except for the long-vowel+t/consonant+t class, where only the vowel after `j`
drops and `j`(→s) is retained (`tartsad~tartsd`). pp. 28–31

### 4.3.6 Subjunctive: usage

(§4.3.6.1) As imperative, for polite or blunt commands; a coverb normally moves to post-verb
position (`Csukja be az ajtót!`) but stricter/more threatening commands keep the coverb preverbal
(`Megcsináld!` "Do it!"); negative commands use `ne`/`se` for `nem`/`sem`, and strict negative
commands place the coverb before the negative particle (`Meg ne edd azt a barackot!`). The
subjunctive 1pl also expresses "let's" (`Menjünk moziba!`), and combines with the fixed particle
`hadd` to express permission. (§4.3.6.2) The subjunctive is used in 1sg/1pl questions meaning
"should/shall I/we...?" (coverb stays preverbal) and in indirect questions with the same sense.
(§4.3.6.3) In subordinate clauses, the subjunctive appears after: (3.3.1) verbs of will or
communication conveying a wish/command (`akar, kér, javasol, ajánl, mond, ír, üzen`) — coverb moves
postverbal for requests/commands, though prohibitive main-clause verbs keep it preverbal; (3.3.2)
clauses of purpose ("in order to/so that"), optionally introduced by `azért` and negated with
`hogy...ne`/`nehogy` (the latter keeping the coverb preverbal); (3.3.3) subordinate clauses after
impersonal main clauses (`Fontos, hogy...`); (3.3.4) subordinate clauses where the main clause
expresses lack, absence, or inability. pp. 31–34

### 4.3.7 Conditional (non-past): conjugation

The conditional marker is `-na/-ne` (3sg indefinite), `-né` (1sg indefinite, no back-vowel
variant), or `-ná/-né` (all other persons/definite), sitting between stem and personal endings. A
linking vowel `a/e` is required before the marker when the stem ends in long-vowel+t or two
consonants (`tanít+ané+k→tanítanék`; exceptions again `áll, száll, varr, forr`). Because of these
vowel-length/harmony interactions, the source gives combined marker+ending tables directly rather
than separate marker and ending tables, for both the indefinite (§4.3.7.1 — note: unlike every
other paradigm in this chapter, the regular 1sg ending shows *no* vowel-harmony variation, always
`-nék`) and definite (§4.3.7.2) conjugations. pp. 35–37

### 4.3.8 Past conditional

Formed periphrastically, not by further suffixation: the substantive verb conjugated in the past
tense, followed by the invariant particle `volna` (`elmentem volna` "I would have left" — see
Morpheme Breakdown). Negation places the negative particle immediately before the conjugated verb,
with the coverb moving to a position immediately after `volna` (`Nem mentem volna el` "I would not
have gone"); any other focused/stressed element causes the same word-order shift. Because it is
built directly on the past-tense forms, no separate full paradigm is given. pp. 37–38

### 4.3.9 Conditional: usage

(§4.3.9.1) Hypothetical "if...then..." conditions, with both clauses in the conditional. (§4.3.9.2)
Polite requests (`Kölcsön adnál egy ezrest?` "Would you loan me a thousand forints?"). (§4.3.9.3)
Wishes and desires, always used after `bárcsak` "if only." (§4.3.9.4) Clauses introduced by
`anélkül` "without," `ahelyett` "instead of," and `mintha` "as if." pp. 38–39

### 4.3.10 Future tense

Hungarian has no inflectional future; three strategies express it. (§4.3.10.1) The present tense,
combined with a coverb and/or a future time expression (`Holnap felhívlak` "I'll call you
tomorrow"). (§4.3.10.2) The auxiliary `fog` (itself conjugated in the present tense, definite or
indefinite), used mainly with coverb-less verbs (`pihenni fogok` "I will rest"); when combined with
a coverbed verb, the construction is more emphatic (`Meg fogom várni` "I will wait for him").
`fog` is footnoted as homonymous with the ordinary lexical verb `fog` "catch," which conjugates
normally in all tenses/moods. (§4.3.10.3) `lesz` "will be, become," the suppletive future of `van`
"be" (not an auxiliary) — it can replace `van` in any construction, including existential/"have"
constructions and constructions with the adverbial participle, to express future meaning. pp.
39–41

### 4.4.1 Past, present, and future participles: formation

(§4.4.1.1) Present participle `-ó/-ő`, attached directly to the stem; fleeting-vowel verbs always
elide their last vowel and v-stem verbs always use the v-stem before it (`ír→író`,
`sző→szövő`). (§4.4.1.2) Past participle is usually identical to the 3sg indefinite past tense
form (`ad→adott`); a footnoted exception among monosyllabic Class B verbs allows an additional
long-form variant alongside the regular short-form past tense (`áll→állt~állott`,
`ír→írt~írott`). (§4.4.1.3) Future participle `-andó/-endő`, attached directly to the stem
(`marad→maradandó` "enduring," `kel→kelendő` "(easily) marketable") — described as considerably
rarer in use than the other two. pp. 41–43

### 4.4.2 Present, past, future participles: usage

All three participle types can function (§4.4.2.1) as ordinary adjectives (`tanuló fiú` "studying
boy," `kifestett szoba` "painted room," `leendő anya` "mother-to-be") and (§4.4.2.2) — commonly for
present, less commonly for past/future — as nouns (`tanuló` "student," `vádlott` "defendant,"
`teendő` "task, agenda"). (§4.4.2.3) In prose, participles are frequently extended into longer
adjectival constructions (adding adverbs, postpositional phrases, etc. before the participle) that
function semantically like relative clauses; the source advises translating these right-to-left,
identifying the modified noun first (`Zsuzsa egy múlt században épült házba akar beköltözni`
"Zsuzsa wants to move into a house built in the last century"). pp. 42–44

### 4.4.3 Adverbial participle

Formed with `-va/-ve`, attached directly to the stem (`mosolyog→mosolyogva`, `énekel→énekelve`).
It modifies a conjugated verb, expressing an action continuing simultaneously with the main verb's
action (`Mosolyogva lépett be a szobába` "She entered the room smiling"). Colloquial usage of the
adverbial participle with `van` "be" is cross-referenced to §17.10, outside this excerpt. p. 44

### 4.4.4 Infinitive

Formed with `-ni`, attached directly to the stem; stems ending in two consonants or long-vowel+t
require the linking vowel `a/e` before it (`olvas+ni→olvasni` vs. `tanít+ani→tanítani`,
`ért+eni→érteni` — the same conditioning environment as the conditional marker, §4.3.7). Infinitives
can complement other conjugated verbs (`Szeretek olvasni` "I like to read"); when the infinitive's
own direct object is definite, the *conjugated* (matrix) verb takes the definite conjugation,
unless that matrix verb is impersonal (`kell, lehet`) or inherently intransitive (`megy, igyekszik`)
— `Szeretném befejezni ezt a dolgozatot` "I would like to finish this paper." A distinct emphatic,
topical use doubles the infinitive before the conjugated form of the same verb, typically followed
by a `de` "but" clause: `Olvasni olvastam a könyvet, de nem volt jó` "(Well,) I read the book, but
it wasn't good." pp. 44–45
