# Hungarian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *Hungarian Verbs and Essentials of Grammar*, Part Two (Ch. 16–End + Verb Index)

**Source:** Miklós Törkenczy, *Hungarian Verbs and Essentials of Grammar*, 2nd Edition (McGraw-Hill,
2008), "Hungarian Verbs.pdf" (scanned, no text layer — read visually via `pdftoppm` + vision, spot-
checked against `tesseract` OCR for cross-verification of ambiguous characters). PDF pages 71–140 of
140 assigned (second half, through the end of the book). Offset check: PDF page 71 shows printed page
"68" (Ch. 16, "Nouns: Case"); PDF page 129 shows printed page "126" ("Index" title page); PDF page 140
shows printed page "137" (final page of the book, "Verb Index"). The offset drifts slightly across
this range (roughly `printed = PDF − 3`, but not perfectly linear — the Index/Verb Index back-matter
sections do not always carry a printed folio on their own title pages), consistent with `051`'s
finding that this scan is a flat single-page-per-image sequence; both PDF and printed page numbers are
cited per entry below where available.

**Scope decision (mirrors `051`'s boundary-respecting practice).** This assigned range is
overwhelmingly **not verb content**. PDF pages 71–128 (printed 68–125) continue Part Two, "Essentials
of Grammar," through Chapter 16 ("Nouns: Case") and onward — cases, pronouns (personal, possessive,
demonstrative, interrogative, relative, reflexive), adjectives, numbers, postpositions, negatives,
word order/focus, and a long run of topic-vocabulary lists (colors, family, food, house, clothing,
travel, education, holidays, forms of address, etc.). None of this is verb-system material, and it is
out of scope for a file titled "Hungarian Verbs Part Two" the same way Part One's dispatch correctly
declined to bulk-extract Chapter 14 ("The Article")/Chapter 15 ("Nouns") — this noun/pronoun/adjective/
topic-vocabulary territory belongs to a separate, differently-scoped dispatch (nouns/pronouns/topic-
vocabulary), not this one, and is **not extracted here**. PDF pages 129–130 (printed 126–128) are the
book's general topical **Index** (a page-number finder for its own chapters, not source content in
its own right) — also not extracted.

**The genuinely verb-relevant content in this range is the back-matter "Verb Index," PDF pages
131–140 (printed 129–137), the last nine pages of the book** — and it is the flagship non-redundant
find of this dispatch.

**Non-redundant-supplement note (mandatory per the dispatch pattern):** Rounds' Chapter 4 (extracted
as `004`/`005`) and this same book's own Part One (extracted as `051`) both already document Hungarian's
core conjugation classes (definite/indefinite present, past, conditional, conjunctive-imperative;
vowel-deleting/v-adding/sz-d stem classes; the "ten irregular verbs"). **Skipped as redundant:** the
underlying phonological rules themselves (already fully stated in `051`). **Kept as genuinely new:**
the Verb Index is not a restatement of the rules but a **reverse-lookup classification key** — it
takes ~110 distinct, mostly basic/high-frequency verbs (many of which had never appeared in any prior
Hungarian `established/` file, since `051`'s sample verbs were chosen for phonological illustration,
not core-vocabulary coverage) and sorts them, tense-by-tense and mood-by-mood, into the exact suffix-
allomorph class each belongs to. This is a distinct pedagogical artifact — a "find your verb's type"
index — not documented in this form by Rounds, FSI, or TYH. It also surfaces a large amount of
previously-undocumented **basic core-verb vocabulary** (`akar` "want," `gondol` "think," `mond`
"say," `tud` "know," `szeret` "like/love," `vár` "wait," `keres` "look for," `hallgat` "listen,"
etc.) that no prior Hungarian source in this project had captured as headwords in their own right.

**Vision-reading note:** clean, modern (2008), digitally-typeset scan throughout this range, same as
`051`. No handwritten marginalia, underlining, library stamps, or non-print annotation was found on
any of the ~70 pages inspected (both the skipped noun/pronoun/topic-vocabulary chapters and the
extracted Verb Index). Vision Reading Confidence is `verified` for all vocabulary entries below —
each verb's spelling was cross-checked against `tesseract` OCR output of the same page and, in every
case of disagreement (OCR is unreliable on this font for `á/é/í/ó/ö/ő/ú/ü/ű` diacritics, frequently
misreading them as `4/6/1/il` etc.), resolved in favor of the clearly legible direct-vision reading
recognized as a real, known Hungarian word.

**Morphological typology note.** Hungarian is agglutinative (see `../00_Extraction_Checklist.md`).
The Verb Index's own organizing principle is itself a morphological classification (which suffix
allomorph a given stem shape selects); see Grammar points below rather than a per-word morpheme
breakdown, since the entries here are not composed forms but bare infinitive-citation stems.

## Grammar points

### The Verb Index as a reverse-lookup classification key (the flagship new content of this dispatch)

PDF pp. 131–140 (printed 129–137, the book's last nine pages) are headed simply "Verb Index," with an
explicit stated purpose (paraphrased): to let a learner take a verb they've encountered, find which
of the book's many tense/mood paradigm-tables it patterns after, and thereby predict its full
conjugation without needing to look each verb up individually. This is a distinct pedagogical
artifact from anything in Rounds, FSI, or TYH — none of those sources organize their verb coverage as
a searchable classification key running the other direction (verb → class), only as class → example.

The index is organized by tense/mood, and within each, by phonological class of the verb stem:

1. **Present Tense Indefinite** — four classes: (a) "standard" verbs (the `ad/fej/öl` type, by far the
   largest group, ~65 verbs listed); (b) verbs ending in a sibilant (`sz/z/s/dz/zs` — the `hoz/vés/főz`
   type, ~14 verbs); (c) verbs ending in two consonants or in `-ít` (the `old/fest/küzd` type, ~18
   verbs); (d) `-ik`-final verbs (~19 verbs), with an explicit note that a subset of these (e.g.
   `dolgozik`, stem `dolgoz-`) must simultaneously apply both the `-ik` rule and the sibilant rule.
2. **The Past Tense** (cross-referenced to pp. 17–21, i.e., `051`'s already-documented rules) — three
   suffix-allomorph classes: (a) verbs taking `-ott/-ett/-ött` in all persons (the `ajánl` type, ~24
   verbs); (b) verbs taking the vowelless `-t` variant in all persons (the `énekel` type — essentially
   the same ~50-verb "standard" list from the present-tense section, restated for the past); (c) verbs
   taking the vowelless `-t` variant everywhere *except* 3sg indefinite, which instead takes
   `-ott/-ett/-ött` (the `küld` type, ~50 verbs — mostly overlapping with the sibilant/`-ik`/standard
   classes from the present-tense section).
3. **The Present Conditional** (cross-referenced to pp. 22–24) — two suffix-variant classes: (a)
   vowelless `-n` (the large majority of verbs indexed, ~95 verbs); (b) vowel-initial `-an/-en` (the
   `old/fest/küzd`-type consonant-cluster/`-ít` stems, ~17 verbs — the same class as present-tense
   class (c) above).
4. **The Conjunctive-Imperative** — three classes mirroring the present-tense ones: (a) verbs taking
   `-j` before the personal suffixes (`ad/fej/öl` type, ~60 verbs); (b) verbs ending in a sibilant
   (~14 verbs); (c) verbs ending in a short vowel + `t` (the `alkot/fizet/üt` type, ~13 verbs).
5. The book's final page (PDF 140, printed 137) closes with two further classes not given their own
   heading elsewhere: verbs ending in a consonant + `t` or a long vowel + `t` (the `ront/sért/önt`
   type — a small, ~7-verb residual list, all already appearing under the two-consonant/`-ít` class
   above) and verbs ending in `st` or `szt` (the `oszt/fest` type, ~9 verbs) — the single genuinely
   new stem-shape class not previously named in `051` (which documented `-ad/-ed` and vowel-deleting
   irregularity classes, but not this specific `st/szt`-cluster class).

**Net assessment:** the index's *organizing principle* (tense-by-tense, mood-by-mood classification
keyed to suffix allomorph) is itself the new content — a systematic cross-reference tool neither
Rounds nor TYH provide in this form — while the great majority of its ~110 individual verb entries
are, for the mechanics-analysis phase's purposes, simply previously-undocumented **basic core
vocabulary** (very high-frequency verbs like "want," "know," "say," "think," "wait," "look for,"
"listen," "answer," "buy," "drive") that happened not to have been used as sample/illustration verbs
in any prior extraction from this book or from Rounds/FSI/TYH. Only the final `st/szt` class (item 5
above) is a genuinely new *stem-shape category* beyond what `051` already named.

## Notable findings (colloquial/register/period vocabulary)

1. **`focizik`** ("to play football/soccer") and **`teniszezik`** ("to play tennis") — both denominal
   `-ik` verbs built productively from a sport-name noun (`foci`, `tenisz`) + `-zik`. `foci` itself is
   a recognizable colloquial clipping of `futball`/`labdarúgás` (parallel in kind to the clipping
   pattern already documented from the TYH source, e.g. `szia` register-splits), making `focizik` a
   colloquial-register-adjacent verb worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase as a second,
   denominal-verb-formation route into informal vocabulary (distinct from the coverb-idiom route
   already flagged in `051`, e.g. `kicsinál`/`átver`).
2. **`válaszol`** ("to answer") vs. **`választ`** ("to choose," already documented in `051`) — a
   near-minimal-pair worth flagging explicitly since the two verbs share an obvious surface
   resemblance (`válasz-` "answer" as a noun root underlies both) but diverge in meaning and
   conjugation class; a genuine false-friend risk for a language-learner-facing derived conlang.
3. No `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]`-style dialectal tags or explicit comparative regional-variant annotations
   appear anywhere in this range, consistent with every other Hungarian source extracted so far in
   this project — a single-dialect (standard) reference grammar with no regional/dialectal marking.
4. No handwritten marginalia, library stamps, or non-print annotation of any kind were found anywhere
   in the ~70 pages inspected for this dispatch (both the skipped noun/pronoun/topic-vocabulary
   chapters, pp. 71–130, and the extracted Verb Index, pp. 131–140) — this is a clean modern scan
   throughout, consistent with `051`'s finding for Part One.
