# Swedish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Astrid Lindgren, *Vi på Saltkråkan* (native-text sample)

**Source:** Astrid Lindgren, *Vi på Saltkråkan* ("We on Saltkråkan" — a Stockholm-archipelago
island-summer family novel). Publication year 1964, confirmed from the back-matter list of Astrid
Lindgren's "samlingsbibliotek" printed at the end of this scan ("Vi på Saltkråkan (1964)"). Vision-read
sample spanning the cover, front matter, and printed pages 6–9, 12–13, 16–17, 20–21, 70–71, 110–111,
150–151, 190–191, 230–231, 270–271, 310–311, 350–351, and 354–357 (181 PDF pages total, scanned as
two-page spreads per image — confirmed empirically; the full novel runs to roughly printed page 362,
based on the back-matter location at PDF page 180 ≈ printed page 360).

**Coverage note:** this is a native-language usage-corpus sample (a longer, wider-age-range family
novel — not an instructional text), so per the dispatch instructions this file samples meaningfully
across the opening, several well-separated middle sections, a dialogue-heavy stretch, and the ending,
rather than transcribing exhaustively. It pulls out genuinely notable vocabulary, idioms, colloquial
compounds/coinages, archipelago/nautical vocabulary, and register contrasts across the book's unusually
wide character age range (a seven-year-old, young teens, a nineteen-year-old, a grown man, and an
elderly island woman all get extended dialogue). No handwritten marginalia of any kind was found on any
sampled page — all content below is genuinely printed/typeset. The only non-source mark found was a
diagonal red software watermark band ("A-PDF Merger DEMO: Purchase from www.A-PDF.com to remove the
watermark") stamped across the cover image by the PDF-merging tool used to assemble this scan — this is
a tool artifact, not handwriting and not source content, and was excluded entirely. No usable PDF text
layer exists (confirmed via `pdftotext`, which returned only the watermark string); every entry below
was vision-read from rendered page images.

## Grammar points

### Dialogue vs. narration: "mej/dej/sej" vs. "mig/dig/sig"

Across all sampled pages, the book consistently spells the first/second/third-person object pronouns
phonetically ("mej", "dej", "sej") whenever they occur inside quoted spoken dialogue — e.g. Petter's
"Låt mej se" (p. 270), fru Sjöblom's "när jag gifte mej" (p. 356) — while the narrator's own prose and
Malin's written diary entries consistently use the standard written forms "mig"/"dig"/"sig", e.g. Malin's
diary: "jag bar mig åt på ett sätt jag inte själv begriper" (p. 310). This is a systematic
orthographic device, not an inconsistency: it marks the spoken/written register boundary typographically,
letter by letter, every time a character actually speaks versus when the narrating or diary-writing voice
takes over.

### Age-graded dialogue register (adult vs. teen vs. child)

This novel's cast spans a wider age range than Lindgren's other Saltkråkan-series siblings sampled so
far in this project, and the sampled pages show a clear, consistent register ladder:
- **Young child (Tjorven, age ~5–7; Pelle, age 7):** short, blunt, present-tense-anchored declaratives
  and exclamations — "Skönt", "Nä då", "Jag håller allting hemligt" — with invented/nonsense vocabulary
  ("bongalo") and concrete, literal logic (treating "vifta med öronen" or santa-lore as plainly true).
- **Teen (Malin, 19; Petter, an older teen suitor):** self-aware, reflective speech capable of irony and
  precise hedging — Petter's deadpan "exakt tio dagar och tolv timmar och tjugo minuter" (p. 270)
  satirizes his own romantic seriousness with mock-nautical-logbook precision — plus, uniquely to Malin,
  a separate *elevated/poetic* register reserved for her private diary ("Malin, Malin, var har du varit
  så länge?", p. 16), markedly more literary than her spoken dialogue.
- **Adult (Melker, the father):** performative, nostalgic, mock-theatrical register full of rhetorical
  questions and stage directions for his children's reactions ("Nå, men varför jublar ni inte då... Får
  jag be om lite jubel!", p. 6) — adult speech self-consciously playing at childishness.
- **Elderly rural islander (fru Sjöblom):** marked by old-fashioned exclamations and affirming particles
  not used by any other character sampled — "Vad i alla mina dar", "minsann", "i all sin dar" — the
  clearest explicit generational/regional flavor-marking found in this sample, though the source does not
  tag it with an explicit dialect label the way some comparative grammars do.

### Productive ad hoc compounding as a register marker

Several entries above (`specialvidrig`, `gammelgädda`, `lusehund`, `åbäke`-as-insult) are not fixed
dictionary vocabulary but compounds/insults improvised in the moment by characters, especially in
sibling banter. Swedish's very permissive open compounding (any noun/adjective can combine with another
on the fly) is exploited here as a comic device — the joke is often the compound's inventiveness itself,
not just its meaning.
