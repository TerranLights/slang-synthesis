# Swedish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Astrid Lindgren, *Emil i Lönneberga* (native-text sample)

**Source:** Astrid Lindgren, *Emil i Lönneberga*, illustrations by Björn Berg (Rabén & Sjögren,
Guldböcker edition). Text © 1963; this scan is the 12th printing ("Tolfte upplagan"), printed 2002,
ISBN 91-29-65593-5. Vision-read sample spanning the book cover, front matter/title/copyright pages,
and printed pages 5, 6–9, 18–19, 28–29, 38–39, 48–49, 58–59, 68–69, 78–79, 88–89, 98–99, 110–111,
120–121, and the back cover (63 PDF pages total, scanned as two-page spreads per image — confirmed
empirically; the book itself runs to roughly printed page 121+).

**Coverage note:** this is a native-language usage-corpus sample (children's novel), not an
instructional text — per the dispatch instructions, this file samples meaningfully across the
opening, several middle sections, and dialogue-heavy stretches rather than transcribing
exhaustively. It pulls out genuinely notable vocabulary, idioms, child-speech patterns, exclamations,
and — the main reason this source was prioritized — explicit regional/dialectal markers, since *Emil
i Lönneberga* is a flagship Smålandsdialekt (Småland dialect) text with a distinctive folksy
narrator who addresses the reader directly. No handwritten marginalia of any kind was found on any
sampled page — all content below is genuinely printed/typeset. No usable PDF text layer exists
(confirmed via `pdftotext`); every entry below was vision-read from rendered page images.

## Grammar points

### Narrator direct address to the reader
Throughout the sample the narrator steps outside the story to address the reader in second person —
"du vet väl" (p. 9), "Nu har du hört vad Emil gjorde" and "Fråga Krösa-Maja! Nej, gör inte det
förresten" (p. 121), "Hohoho, jag måste skratta ... men jag berättar det inte, för det har jag lovat
Emils mamma" (p. 121). This is a pervasive stylistic feature, not a one-off: an oral-storytelling
register (rhetorical questions, direct address, a narrator who claims to be withholding information
out of a promise made to a character) is folded into otherwise plain written narrative prose. Worth
flagging because it blends registers within a single sentence rather than marking a clean shift.

### Dialect vs. narrator-prose register split
The sampled pages show a consistent pattern: narrator prose is in standard written Swedish, while
quoted speech from Lönneberga villagers, Lina, and especially Emil's own child-speech carries
explicit phonetic dialect spellings — "mi mysse" (p. 6), "te pojk" (p. 9), "Jag mä" (p. 98), "Di
kommer bara bort" (p. 98), and the apostrophe-elided place-names in Alfred's sung verse ("sta'",
"Hultsfre'", p. 89). The narrator also names the dialect directly on p. 5 ("han talade
småländska ... Det gör man i Småland"), making this one of the clearest self-identified
regional-dialect sources sampled in this project so far — the source marks its own dialect content
rather than requiring external inference.

### Object-fronted/topicalized word order in dialect speech
p. 9: "Honom blir det aldrig nånting av" fronts the object pronoun "Honom" as topic, more marked than
the standard written order "Det blir aldrig något av honom." Appears only in quoted village gossip,
not narrator prose — consistent with the register split noted above.
