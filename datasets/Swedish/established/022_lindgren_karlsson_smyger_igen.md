## Grammar points

*(Brief, as appropriate for a usage-corpus sample rather than a grammar reference — see spec.)*

### Spoken-register orthography in dialogue
The book consistently marks colloquial pronunciation in dialogue through non-standard spelling:
`dom` for `de`/`dem`, `mej`/`dej` for `mig`/`dig`, and in the closing song, apostrophe-elided forms
like `da'` (dag), `ja'` (jag), and bare `va` (vara). This is a deliberate, book-wide register
choice — narration leans more standard, dialogue (and the one song) leans phonetic — rather than
an inconsistency, and it holds across every sampled page from the opening (p. 11) through the
ending (p. 196).

### Interjections as complete utterances
Child-speech and Karlsson's own speech frequently use a bare interjection as a full turn in
dialogue, with no clause attached: *"Hoj, hoj"*, *"Ut!"*, *"Fy!"*, *"Nä"*. This is consistent with
how exclamatory/affective register works across the sampled dialogue generally — a one-word
reaction is treated as a complete conversational move, not an elliptical fragment of a fuller
sentence.

### Nonce word-formation by direct analogy
Karlsson's self-declared "three methods" (p. 195, *tirritera och filura och figurera*) place one
real word (*figurera*) next to one lightly-mangled real word (*tirritera*, from *irritera*) and one
likely nonce coinage (*filura*) in a single rhythmic, rhyming triplet — the formation pattern
itself (not just the individual words) is the notable feature: Karlsson invents/mangles vocabulary
specifically to fit a rhyming cadence, a structural habit rather than a one-off joke.
