**Morpheme breakdown.** Swedish is predominantly fusional/analytic with productive but transparent
compounding — most entries above are simple, non-agglutinative lexical items or fixed idioms and
don't need a morpheme breakdown. The one item worth flagging compositionally is `tjuvrökning`
(`tjuv-` "thief-, done secretly" + `rökning` "smoking (nominalization of röka 'to smoke')") because
the `tjuv-` element is a productive slang-forming prefix recurring across many unrelated Swedish
colloquialisms (`tjuvlyssna`, `tjuvkika`, `tjuvåka`), not a one-off compound — worth tracking as a
reusable derivation mechanism for the later mechanics-analysis phase.

## Grammar points

### Informal pronoun/reflexive spellings used throughout narration, not just dialogue

Lindgren's prose consistently uses the spoken-register spellings `mej`/`dej`/`sej`/`dom` (for
standard written `mig`/`dig`/`sig`/`de`/`dem`) not only in characters' dialogue but in the narrative
voice itself (e.g. p. 9 "Kalle suckade... Det gjorde Anders också"; throughout, reflexive verbs and
object pronouns consistently take the `-ej` spelling). This is a deliberate register choice — it
makes the whole book read as closer to spoken Swedish than the more formal `mig`/`dig`/`sig`/`de`
forms a stricter written register would use, appropriate for a children's book, and distinct from a
grammar source's "formal vs. colloquial" footnote-style marking: here it's the baseline register of
the entire text, not a flagged exception.

### A further step of dialectal elision is reserved for marked moments of excitement

Against that baseline, one line goes further: "Di Röa kommer!" (p. 99, a child shouting) uses `Di`
for `De` and `Röa` for `Röda`, an extra reduction beyond the book's normal `mej/dej/dom` convention.
This suggests Lindgren uses at least two tiers of informality in the same text — a default
mildly-informal narrational register, and an even more phonetically reduced register reserved for
direct quoted excitement/shouting.

### Children's register borrows adult institutional jargon for play

A recurring comic mechanism in the sampled gang-rivalry passage (pp. 98–99) is children deploying
genuinely formal/technical adult vocabulary — military (`spanare`, `kringgående rörelse`,
`överrumpling`), institutional (`högkvarter`, `rapporterade`) — completely earnestly for their own
backyard reconnaissance game. This isn't a single lexical item so much as a patterned register
contrast worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase as a `register_inversion`-adjacent
mechanism: formal/technical terms imported wholesale into a child-play register for comic
seriousness, rather than slang terms replacing formal ones.
