# Swedish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: The Svenssons (Texts 1–22)

**Source:** *The Svenssons (Texts 1–22)*, SFI-style graded Swedish reading book (no author/publisher/
year printed on the scanned pages; PDF metadata shows only a 2012 scan date, not an original
publication date). 23-page PDF, no text layer (vision-read). Sampled pages/texts: Text 1 ("Gunillas
brev," pp. 8–9), Text 2 ("Hemmakväll," pp. 10–11), Text 3 ("Gunilla och pojkarna köper kläder," pp.
12–13), Text 5/6 boundary ("Innan dagen börjar...," pp. 18–19), Text 8/9 ("Nu är det jul igen...," pp.
24–25), Text 11 ("Livet är urtrist – Livet är stenkul," pp. 30–31), Text 12 ("Morfars död," pp. 34–35),
Text 16 ("Svenssons bjuder på middag," p. 41, comic-panel layout), Text 21 ("Semester," pp. 51/57),
Text 22 ("Gunillas brev II," pp. 58–59).

**Coverage note:** This is a pedagogically-controlled graded reader for language learners (the
Svensson family's daily life, told across 22 short texts), not organic native prose — register is
deliberately flat and vocabulary deliberately simple/repetitive for most of the book, as expected for
this genre. Per dispatch instructions, this file does **not** attempt exhaustive per-text coverage;
instead it samples across the early/middle/late range to characterize (a) the vocabulary-ladder
progression, if any, and (b) genuinely colloquial/register-marked material where it does occur —
rather than forcing slang findings onto texts that are plainly register-neutral. `Source Type` is
`literary_text` per this project's established convention for Swedish reading-book material (as
opposed to `grammar_reference`, used for instructional-course sources elsewhere in the Swedish
dataset). All vocabulary below was vision-read from scanned page images (no text layer); one
instance of handwritten pencil marginalia (a previous owner's annotation, illegible beyond a short
fragment resembling a date/page note) was found in the top-left margin of the Text 22 page (p. 58)
and is excluded entirely — not transcribed as source content.

## Grammar points

### Vocabulary-ladder progression: topic-driven, not strictly monotonic

Sampling across the full range (Texts 1, 2, 3, 5/6, 8/9, 11, 12, 16, 21, 22) shows the ladder is
real but not a simple linear ramp from "easy" to "hard." The earliest texts (1–3) are plain,
declarative narration — a mother's letter home, a family evening, a shopping trip — built almost
entirely from `core` vocabulary with minimal idiom. Texts 5/6 introduce the book's one clear
*explicit* register lesson: a child's colloquial clippings ("jumpa," "matan") are voiced in dialogue
and then immediately corrected by a parent to their standard forms ("gymnastik," "matbespisningen")
in the same exchange — the source teaching the colloquial/standard contrast directly, not just
exhibiting it. Texts 8/9 (Christmas) widen the *domain* vocabulary (Lucia-tradition, baking, folk
countdown rhymes) without raising register difficulty. The clearest colloquial-register spike in the
whole sampled set is Text 11 ("Livet är urtrist – Livet är stenkul"), built around a teenage
daughter's relationship conflict — its title itself pairs two slangy intensifying coinages
("ur-"+trist, "sten-"+kul), and the text supplies a small cluster of genuine youth-colloquial items
("plugga," "matte," "kondis," "gänget," "Nehej!") not seen elsewhere in the sample. By contrast, the
late texts sampled (21, 22) return to the plain declarative register of the opening texts — more
syntactically elaborate (longer sentences, more subordinate clauses, a dialogue with more turns) but
not more colloquial or slang-bearing. **Conclusion: the controlled register loosens specifically
when the text's point-of-view shifts to a teenager (Text 11), rather than progressing uniformly with
text number** — a content/character-driven register shift rather than a pure graded-reader difficulty
curve.

### Productive intensifying prefixes: "jätte-," "ur-," "sten-"

Three distinct intensifying prefixes recur across the sampled texts, all attaching to ordinary
adjectives to mean roughly "really/extremely ___": `jätte-` (lit. "giant-"; jättekul, jättegullig,
jättetrött — Text 6), `ur-` (urtrist, uruselt — Text 11), and `sten-` (stenkul — Text 11, title only).
`jätte-` is the most neutral/common of the three and appears in a kid's everyday dinner-table
dialogue; `ur-` and `sten-` cluster specifically in the one text with elevated teen-colloquial
register, suggesting the source is using prefix choice itself as a register marker (the "giant-"
prefix reads as a child's casual intensifier; "ur-"/"sten-" read as a teenager's).

### Register-correction as explicit pedagogical content

Text 6 is notable for directly dramatizing a colloquial-vs-standard contrast rather than just using
one register throughout: a child uses a clipped/slang form, and a parent immediately supplies and
names the standard equivalent, within the same line of dialogue. This is the strongest instance in
the sampled material of the source's own explicit register annotation (as opposed to inferred
register from context) and was treated as a priority find per the extraction spec.

### Comic-panel layout reduces running text

Text 16 ("Svenssons bjuder på middag") is laid out as a sequence of captionless comic-strip panels
rather than running prose or dialogue — essentially no extractable vocabulary on that page beyond
the title itself. Noted here rather than silently skipped, since a "no running text" page is a
distinct finding from "checked and found nothing interesting."
