**Morpheme breakdown.** Swedish is a moderately fusional/compounding Germanic language (see this language's own typology profile); most entries above are simple compounds whose parts are already glossed inline in the Notes column rather than requiring a separate breakdown block. One worth breaking out explicitly:

> **Pippilotta Viktualia Rullgardina Krusmynta Efraimsdotter Långstrump** = Pippilotta (diminutive/pet form built on "Pippi") + Viktualia (← *viktualier* "provisions") + Rullgardina (← *rullgardin* "roller blind" + feminine-coded *-a* ending) + Krusmynta (← *krus-* "curled" + *mynta* "mint") + Efraimsdotter (← *Efraim* + *-s-* genitive linker + *dotter* "daughter," a patronymic) + Långstrump (← *lång* "long" + *strump* "stocking") — demonstrates Lindgren building an absurdist name entirely out of recognizable common-noun compounds given invented feminine-sounding endings, rather than from any real naming convention.

## Grammar points

### Emphatic adjective/verb reduplication for intensification
Several passages double a word rather than using a single intensifier: *blodiga, blodiga hämnd* ("bloody, bloody revenge," p.88), *teg och teg* ("kept silent and silent," p.119), *länge, länge* ("for a long, long time," p.129). This reads as a genuine Swedish stylistic device for extending/intensifying duration or intensity, distinct from the more familiar English-style single-intensifier pattern (*mycket blodig* "very bloody") — worth tracking as a productive intensification mechanism separate from adverbial modification.

### Archaic plural verb agreement and strong adjective declension survive only in quoted/sung material
The book's narration and dialogue use uniformly modern Swedish (no person/number agreement on verbs, weak adjective forms in definite contexts). The one exception found, *Här komma de svenske med buller och bång!* (p.127, a quoted song line), preserves two pre-20th-century features: the old plural verb form *komma* (vs. modern invariant *kommer*) and the strong/definite-plural adjective ending *-e* in *de svenske* (vs. modern *de svenska*). This is a clean, contained example of an older verb/adjective paradigm surviving specifically inside quoted song material embedded in an otherwise fully modernized text — useful as a model for how a conlang might similarly fossilize an older grammatical paradigm inside ritual/sung language while the spoken register has moved on.

### Fronted-predicate exclamatory clauses
*Vad vi har det härligt!* (p.89) fronts the predicate complement around the verb phrase instead of the more literal "Vad härligt vi har det" — a natural but structurally marked word order for exclamations, worth noting for how Swedish marks exclamatory force through constituent order rather than (only) through a dedicated exclamatory particle.

### Nonce word-coinage as Pippi's core idiolect marker
Across the sample, Pippi repeatedly invents new words or nonsense phrases on the spot rather than drawing on an established slang register: *pluttifikation* (p.38, blending *plutt* + *multiplikation*), *U buj uje muj!* (p.118-119, pure nonsense representing garbled speech), *Si guda, guda* (p.19, mock-courtly nonsense greeting). Unlike ordinary colloquialisms, these are one-off coinages invented within the scene itself and not attested as reused slang elsewhere in the sample — grammatically they still slot into normal Swedish syntax (nouns take normal noun-phrase positions, interjections sit where interjections go), suggesting a productive model for synthesized conlang slang: invent the lexical item freely, but keep it grammatically "legal" within the existing syntax.

### Tall-tale discourse formula: "Jag kände/träffade [en + occupation] i/på [exotic place] som..."
At least three separate tall tales in the sample open with the same template: *Jag kände en gång en kakelugnsmakare i Kalkutta, som...* (p.118), *jag har sett större ormar... I Bortre Indien* (p.68), *På Cuba träffade jag en gång en finsnickare som...* (p.129, cut off by the narrative before completion — itself a running gag, the tall tale is never actually finished). This is a recognizable discourse-level register marker for Pippi's boastful/fantastical register, distinct from ordinary narrative or dialogue syntax, and a strong candidate pattern for a "tall-tale register" marker in synthesized slang/storytelling conventions.
