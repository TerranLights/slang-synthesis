### Grammar points

- **Linking main clauses/words with coordinating conjunctions**: *och* (and), *eller* (or), *men* (but), *utan* ("but" specifically after a negated clause), *för* (as, because); correlatives *antingen...eller*, *varken...eller*. These do **not** affect word order in the clauses they join — a contrast with the inversion-triggering fronting rules covered in earlier units.
- **End-article plural — three rules**, extending the end-article-singular (p. 35/82) and plural-prediction (p. 83) rules already covered: (1) plurals ending in vowel + -r add -na (*flickor→flickorna*; -are nouns first drop -e: *lärare→lärarna*); (2) plurals ending in other consonants add -en (*barn→barnen*; -el/-en/-er stems drop -e: *lager→lagren*); (3) ett-word plurals ending in vowel + -n add -a (*yrken→yrkena*).
- **Possessive pronouns — two formal types**, now given in full: the -s type (3rd person: *hans, hennes, dess, deras*) takes no agreement endings at all; the other type (*min/mitt/mina*, *din/ditt/dina*, *vår/vårt/våra*, *er/ert/era*) agrees with en-word/ett-word/plural exactly like an adjective.
- **Three ways to express future time, none using a special verb ending**: (1) present tense + time marker (*Jag åker imorgon*), including *bli* and *tänka* as specially future-flavored present-tense verbs; (2) *kommer att* + infinitive for an event independent of the speaker's will (*Det kommer att regna ikväll*); (3) *ska* + infinitive for intention/warning/threat with a personal subject (*Det här ska du få ångra!* — "You will regret this!").
- **i vs. på rule of thumb for "in/on/at", with named exceptions.** Default: *på* + surface, *i* + volume (*duken på bordet* vs. *duken i skåpet*). Exceptions: *på* with public buildings/workplaces/entertainment venues (*på kontoret*, *på bio*) but *i* with school/shop/church (*i skolan, i affären, i kyrkan*); *hos* for people/professions (explicitly compared to French *chez*, German *bei*: *hos tandläkaren*); *vid* for lengthwise-extended things (*vid kusten*).
- **Location/direction/origin adverb triplet system**, a structurally important three-way split worth preserving for conlang spatial-adverb design: *här/hit/härifrån* ("here"/"to here"/"from here"), and parallel triplets for *var/vart/varifrån*, *hemma/hem/hemifrån*, *inne/in/inifrån*, *ute/ut/utifrån*, *uppe/upp/uppifrån*, *nere/ner/nerifrån*, *framme/fram/framifrån*. A directional adverb alone can stand in for an omitted verb of motion when a modal is present (*Jag ska hem* — "I'm off home").
- **"Where" splits by both Q-word status and location/motion**: as an interrogative, *var* (location) vs. *vart* (motion); as a relative pronoun, *där* (location) vs. *dit* (motion) — e.g. *ett konditori där han kommer att få bakelser* (location) vs. *ett konditori dit vi kan gå* (motion).
- **Silent-letter and spelling/pronunciation-mismatch pronunciation notes, explicitly tabulated by the book** — a direct, systematic register-of-pronunciation list rather than scattered pron. notes: silent -g (*jag, dag, någon* [shortens vowel to "nån"]), silent -d (*med, vad, sedan* [→ "va," "sen"]), silent -k (*hemskt, kritiskt*), silent -l (*karl, världen*), silent -t (*det, mycket, litet*); plus words whose everyday pronunciation diverges further from spelling: *de/dem* → "dom," *att* (infinitive marker) → "å" (vs. *att* "that," pronounced as spelled), *och* → "å"/"ock," *lade* → "la," *sa(de)* → "sa," *mig/dig/sig* → "mej/dej/sej." This is the book's most explicit, consolidated colloquial-pronunciation reference point so far.
- **Money/currency naming conventions**, including colloquial note/coin nicknames (*tjuga* = 20-kronor note, *femtilapp*/*hundralapp* = 50/100-kronor notes) alongside the plain descriptive terms (*femtioöring*, *enkrona*, *femkrona*) — a register-doubling pattern (slang nickname vs. literal descriptive compound) for the same referent, worth modeling for a conlang's own currency-naming conventions.

### Grammar points

- **Full regular verb-conjugation paradigm (I–IV), explicitly labeled weak vs. strong.** I (*parkera/parkerar/parkerade/parkerat*), IIa — voiced-consonant stem (*bygg/bygger/byggde/byggt*), IIb — unvoiced-consonant stem (*hjälp/hjälper/hjälpte/hjälpt*), III — non-a vowel stem (*sy/syr/sydde/sytt*), IV — strong/stem-vowel-changing (*skriv/skriver/skrev/skrivit*). I–III form the past tense by suffixation ("weak," explicitly compared to English park/parked); IV changes the stem vowel ("strong," compared to English drink/drank) — the book notes this weak/strong split is a structural parallel shared with English, a useful cross-linguistic anchor for conlang verb-class design.
- **Perfect/pluperfect formed with *har*/*hade* + supine.** The supine is an invariant form (always ending in -t/-tt/-it depending on conjugation) corresponding to the past participle in most European languages; conjugation determines which supine ending applies (I → -t, IIa/IIb → -t, III → -tt, IV → -it).
- **Postponing the subject with formal subject *det*.** When a new/indefinite subject would awkwardly open a sentence, it is postponed and replaced in subject position by *det* (always *det* in Swedish, unlike English's "there"/"it" split): *Någon sitter i köket* → *Det sitter någon i köket*. Crucially, **Swedish allows any intransitive verb** in this construction, not just "to be" as in English (*Det bor 9 miljoner människor i Sverige* — "There are 9 million people living in Sweden"). *Finnas* is the most frequent verb used this way. In questions, the formal subject immediately precedes the real subject (*Finns det inget öl hemma?*).
- **Subordinate clauses categorized by function** (MPT-expression, subject, or object within the main clause) with link words organized by meaning: *att* (indirect-speech "that"), *om* (indirect yes/no-question "whether"); time (*när/då/sedan, medan, innan*); cause (*för att, eftersom, därför att*); condition (*ifall, om*); concession (*fast(än)*). Fronted sub-clauses trigger the same main-clause subject–verb inversion as any other fronted MPT-expression.
- **som vs. att, both translating "that," cleanly disambiguated.** *att* is a subordinating conjunction following a verb (*Jag sa att hon talade...*); *som* is a relative pronoun following a noun (*Kön som hon ställde sig i...*) or used in emphatic/cleft constructions. Both can be omitted — *som* when not the clause's subject, *att* especially in speech after verbs of saying/thinking/perceiving — mirroring the optionality of English "that"/"which."
- **Indeclinable adjectives — four classes**: ending in -s (*gammaldags*; exception: -ös adjectives like *nervös* still decline), ending in -e (including all present participles in -(a)nde/-ende used adjectivally, *växande*), ending in -a (*medeltida*), and a miscellaneous group (*slut, fel, sönder*).
- **Voiced→voiceless consonant pronunciation shift before s or t, systematically tabulated**: d→t (*onsdag* → "onsta"), g→k (*hur dags* → "hur daks," *högt* → "hökt"), v→f (*livstid* → "lifstid"), b→p (*substantiv* → "suppstantiv," *snabbt* → "snappt"). A clean, rule-governed assimilation process directly transferable to conlang phonological-rule design.

### Grammar points

- **Weak-verb past tense, with four sub-conjugation exceptions newly detailed beyond Unit 8's basic pattern.** Conjugation I (two-thirds of all Swedish verbs, all -era verbs and new loanwords: *jobba, lifta, parkera, nationalisera*). Conjugation II splits by stem-final voicing (IIa voiced → -de; IIb unvoiced -k/-p/-s/-t/-x → -te) and has four irregular sub-patterns: consonant+d stems add only -e in the past and drop -d in the supine (*använda→använde→använt*); -mm/-nn stems drop one consonant before -de/-t (*glömma→glömde→glömt*, *känna→kände→känt*); consonant+t stems add only -e and drop -t in the supine (*gifta→gifte→gift*); short-ä/ö-with-extra-j stems drop the -j and lengthen the vowel elsewhere (*välja→valde→valt*, *dölja→dolde→dolt*). Conjugation III (long-vowel-final stems other than -a) shortens that vowel before -dde/-tt (*tro→trodde→trott*; also *ske, bero, avsky, bry, strö, klä, rå*).
- **Full irregular-weak-verb table** given by the book across all three weak conjugations (I: *betala, koka*; II: *göra, ha, heta, kunna, lägga, måste, skola, säga, sälja, sätta, veta, vilja*; III: *be, dö, få, ge, gå, le, se, stå*) — a comprehensive irregular-verb reference directly reusable for conlang irregular-verb-class modeling.
- **Definite adjective construction — three-part marking of definiteness**, more systematically presented than earlier fragments: (1) a front/adjectival article (*den/det/de* — identical in form to the pronoun "it" and demonstrative "that"); (2) the adjective takes the definite ending -a (same form as plural indefinite); (3) the noun takes its ordinary end article. Result: *den goda maten*, *det goda ölet*, *de goda druvorna*. **Exception**: with a masculine singular noun referring to one male person, written Swedish usually changes the adjective's -a ending to -e (*den store pojken*, *den långe mannen*) — all other genders/numbers keep -a.
- **Emphatic cleft construction**: *Det är/var X som...* ("It is/was X who/that...") parallels English exactly, with *som* translating "that/who" in this position; common in questions too (*Var det Kalle och Sven som åkte...?*).
- **Comprehensive time-expression table** (past / habitual / present / future, crossed with seasons-and-festivals / days / parts-of-day / years-and-months) — a dense, systematic reference directly useful for conlang tense/aspect-adverb design, e.g. showing the past/future symmetry breaking down for some categories (days: *i söndags* "last Sunday" vs. *på söndag* "next Sunday," with no habitual/present-tense slot filled the same way).
- **Vowel-hiatus avoidance**: the book explicitly warns English speakers against inserting an intrusive -r- between adjacent vowels across word boundaries (as in "Africa(r) and Asia") and gives a drilled list of Swedish phrases to practice gliding cleanly between vowels instead (*inte_alls*, *lite_av varje*) — a cross-linguistic L1-interference note of direct relevance to a conlang's own phonotactic design around vowel-vowel boundaries.
- **sådan/någon/ingen pronoun series, with register notes.** *sådan/sådant/sådana* ("such a") shows inverted word order relative to English (*en sådan röra* — "such a mess," lit. "a such mess"); *sådan + här/där* = "this/that kind of," commonly pronounced "sån/sånt/såna." *någon/något/några* functions adnominally, standalone ("someone/anyone," "something/anything"), or with a negative, commonly pronounced "nån/nåt/nåra." *ingen/inget/inga* parallels this for "no/none/no one/nothing" (*inget* also = *ingenting*). All three series show the same colloquial vowel-reduced pronunciation pattern already flagged for other function words in this book, reinforcing that this is a systematic register layer, not isolated exceptions.

### Grammar points

- **Strong verb (Conjugation IV) past tense formed by stem-vowel change**, often changing again for the supine, grouped into productive vowel-alternation classes the book presents "in order of importance": i–e–i (*skriva→skrev→skrivit*), y–ö–u (*flyga→flög→flugit*), ju–ö–u (*bjuda→bjöd→bjudit*), i–a–u (*sitta→satt→suttit*), plus a miscellaneous group (*dra, bära, komma, äta, vara*). The book notes irregular Conjugation III verbs (Unit 9) follow the same vowel-change logic. Most strong verbs take present -er and supine -it.
- **Sub-clause word order, fully formalized**: a sub-clause always opens with its link word and (crucially) keeps **straight** subject–verb order, unlike main clauses which frequently invert. The **SIV mnemonic** (Subject – *Inte* – Verb, actually a girl's name) is explicitly given by the book as a memory aid: in a sub-clause, any sentence-adverb (*inte, aldrig, alltid, ju, egentligen*, etc.) sits **before** VERB1, exactly the opposite of the main-clause rule (adverb immediately **after** VERB1, established in Units 3–4). This SIV/main-clause contrast is one of the most structurally important word-order facts in the book and a prime candidate for direct transfer into a derived conlang's own subordinate-clause word-order rule.
- **ingen vs. inte någon** (both negate an indefinite): no further elaboration beyond what Unit 9 already covered for the *ingen* series, but the unit title flags this as a distinct learning point, implying free or near-free variation between the synthetic negative pronoun and the analytic "not + any" construction — worth testing against real usage data if available later.
- **Time-duration/frequency prepositions, a second confusable cluster for English speakers** (extending "Expressions of time (I)" from Unit 9): *för...sedan* ("...ago"), *i/om/på* + time-span showing a three-way aspectual distinction — *om en timme* (future: "in an hour('s time)"), *på en timme* (duration-to-completion: "it will take an hour"), *i en timme* (ongoing duration: "for an hour"); *i* + time span with negation = "not for" (*Han har inte bott där på ett år* — "He hasn't lived there for a year," note: **på**, not *i*, under negation) — a genuine non-obvious polarity-sensitive preposition-switch worth flagging distinctly for conlang temporal-adverbial design.
- **Foreign-loanword pronunciation exceptions**, a short but systematic list: g/j/ch all realized as Swedish *sj* in certain loans (*genial, journalist, chef*); silent p (*psalm*); eu realized as "ev" (*pseudo-, pneumatisk*).
- **Slangy intensifying prefixes for opinion-adjectives — the single highest-value colloquial/slang finding in this unit, explicitly labeled by the source itself** ("In colloquial, often somewhat slangy, Swedish it is usual to modify adjectives by using a strengthening prefix"): *jätte-* ("giant-," usually positive: *jättebra, jättefin, jättekul, jättestor*), *toppen-* ("top-," always positive: *en toppenbil, en toppenkille*), *ur-* ("ultra-," usually negative: *urdålig, urdum, urfånig*), *skit-* ("shit-," can go either way: *skitbillig, skitdålig, skitbra, skitbussig*) — with the book making an explicit, directly quotable cross-linguistic calibration claim: **"This last prefix is not nearly as strong in Swedish as in English"** — i.e. Swedish *skit-* has weaker taboo force than its literal English gloss would suggest, a genuinely load-bearing register fact for any conlang borrowing or adapting a scatological intensifier. This four-prefix system (positive-only / positive-only-different-flavor / negative-leaning / valence-neutral-taboo) is a clean, complete model for a derived conlang's own colloquial-intensifier morphology.
- **Opinion-adjective vocabulary list** (less slangy register, contrasted explicitly with the prefixed forms above): *snäll, elak, snygg, ful, vacker, vänlig, pålitlig, jobbig, livlig, ytlig, blyg, öppen, kylig, stel, varm, flitig, lat* — a compact personality-trait lexicon useful for conlang character-description vocabulary.
- **Patronymic/surname history**, factually dense: first names were the only names until surnames became obligatory in the 19th century; -son patronymics (originally for both sons and daughters, later male-only in form) competed with nature-descriptive surnames (*Lindström, Sjögren, Forsberg* — element compounds from nature vocabulary); nobility surnames (17th century) often described a coat of arms (*Gyllenkrook* = "three golden hooks"); Renaissance-era clergy/scholars sometimes Latinized their names after their home village (*Hassela → Hazelius*).

### Grammar points

- **Definite adjective declension systematized into four named types**, building directly on Unit 9's basic pattern: Type 1 — front article/demonstrative + end article (*den stora bilen*, *den här stora bilen*); Type 2 — genitive/possessive/demonstrative *denna* with **no** end article (*Olles nya bil*, *min nya bil*); Type 3 — **no** front article but **with** end article (*svenska språket*, *hela dagen*); Type 4 — **no** front article and **no** end article (*lilla vän*, *första klass*). This four-way grid is one of the book's more systematic grammar presentations and directly transferable as a definiteness-marking typology template.
- **Adjectival nouns — Swedish goes further than English.** Plural definite omission of an "obvious" noun parallels English (*de unga* = "the young"), but Swedish additionally permits this with indefinite forms (*en blind* = "a blind person"), singular definite en-/ett-forms (*den gamle* = "the old man" vs. *den gamla* = "the old woman" — gender distinguished purely by the masculine -e/-a alternation from Unit 9; *det nya* = "the new thing"), and present participles (*de närvarande* = "those present"). Also covers the "a big one"/"the white one" construction (*Jag tar den röda* — "I'll have the red one").
- **S-verbs: three functionally distinct categories sharing one morphological marker (-s), fully disambiguated by the book.** Passive (*Bilen lagas* — "The car is being mended," to be covered fully in Unit 17); reciprocal (*Vi träffas kl 2* — "We'll meet at two," with the plural subject acting as both agent and patient — explicitly equated with *varandra* "each other": *Vi kramades* = *Vi kramade varandra*); deponent (*Det finns inga bananer*) — active in meaning, with **no corresponding non-s form** at all. The present-tense -s form is built by deleting the regular present ending (-r or -er) and appending -s directly to the stem (*lagar→lagas*, *behöver→behövs*), with Conjugation II forms sometimes retaining the -e in formal register (*Bär köpes!* — "Berries bought," a classic sign-register construction). This is a clean three-way disambiguation of a single bound morpheme by function — valuable as a model for a conlang voice/valence system that similarly overloads one marker.
- **Pet-name (hypocoristic) formation**, given as a full correspondence table between formal and informal given names — directly modelable for conlang nickname-derivation rules (e.g. truncation + -e: *Bertil→Berra*; -an suffixation: *Elisabeth→Bettan*; consonant alternation: *Birgitta→Bibbi/Gittan*).
- **vem/vad/vilken + obligatory *som* when functioning as the subject of an indirect question** — a genuinely non-obvious syntactic rule with no English equivalent: *Jag undrar vem hon älskar* ("...who she loves," *vem* = object) vs. *Jag undrar vem som kysser henne nu* ("...who's kissing her," *vem* = subject, **must** take *som*). This subject-marking *som* is purely a syntactic requirement, not a separate lexical choice, and is a clean example of an obligatory resumptive/subject-marking particle worth testing for conlang relative/interrogative-clause design.
- **An explicit "Slang (I)" section — the single highest-value, most directly labeled slang content so far in this book.** The book gives an 11-word slang-vs.-standard-Swedish table for friend/family terms (*kille/tjej/kompis/polare/grabb/brorsa/farsa/morsa/syrra/gubbe/gumma/fruga/kärring/unge*), and singles out exactly **one** entry — *kärring* — with an explicit "(derogatory)" tag, sharply distinguishing ordinary affectionate/informal slang from a genuinely pejorative term within the same semantic field (both denote "woman/wife," but *kärring* alone carries negative force) — a clean minimal pair for modeling register-graded synonym sets in a derived conlang's slang layer.
- **Text-messaging register, given its own dedicated abbreviation table** — a rare explicit written-informal-register data point distinct from spoken colloquialisms covered elsewhere in the book. Patterns observed: phonetic respelling of function words (*ja*="jag", *oxå*="också", *me*="med", *de*="det"), numeral-for-homophone substitution (*3vligt*="trevligt", using "tre" for the "tre-" sound), and whole-phrase clipping to acronym-like forms (*cs*="vi ses", *pok*="puss och kram", *hare*="ha det bra"). This is a self-contained, systematized written-slang register worth treating as its own register tier distinct from spoken colloquial Swedish when modeling a derived conlang's informal written forms.
