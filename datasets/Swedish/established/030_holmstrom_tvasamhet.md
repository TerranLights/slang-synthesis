## Grammar points

### `-samhet` as a productive abstract-noun suffix, and the collection's title as a coinage on it

Swedish has a productive derivational pattern where an adjective or (less commonly) a numeral takes
the abstract-noun suffix `-samhet` to produce a noun denoting the corresponding state or quality:
`ensam` ("alone") -> `ensamhet` ("loneliness"), `gemensam` ("common/shared") -> `gemensamhet`
("togetherness/community"), similar formations from other adjective stems. Holmström's title
`tvåsamhet` ("twosomeness," roughly "being-a-couple-ness") is built the same way from the numeral
`två` ("two") rather than from an adjective -- a legible, half-colloquial coinage that reads as a
deliberate echo of (and contrast with) the existing word `ensamhet` ("loneliness/aloneness"): the
one-word swap from "one" to "two" carries the book's whole thematic argument (that being a couple
doesn't resolve, and can even sharpen, the isolation of being alone). Confirmed used inside the
prose itself, not just as the cover title (p. 108), so it is functioning as real running vocabulary
within the stories, not only paratext.

### Frozen subjunctive oath `förbanne mig` as a synchronic intensifier

`förbanne mig` ("damn it," lit. something like "may it damn me") preserves an archaic subjunctive
verb form (`förbanne`, from `förbanna`, "to curse/damn") that is no longer productive in ordinary
modern Swedish verb morphology -- the subjunctive mood survives only in a small set of fossilized
interjections like this one (compare `Gud bevare` "God preserve [us]"). In the "Leaving Paradise"
dialogue sample (p. 148) it is inserted mid-clause ("Klockan är förbanne mig inte ens tio ännu") the
way a modern speaker would insert `jävlar` or `fan` -- i.e. it now functions as a slotted
intensifier particle rather than a semantically active verb clause, even though its internal
grammar is frozen from an earlier stage of the language.

### Dialogue formatting convention

Consistent with standard contemporary Swedish literary typesetting (confirmed across every
dialogue-bearing page sampled: pp. 8, 68-69, 88-89, 108-109, 148-149): direct speech is marked with
an initial em dash (`–`) rather than quotation marks, with no closing dash, e.g. "– Jag vet inte,
säger hon..." This is a general Swedish-publishing convention, not specific to this author, but
worth recording as the baseline format this project's other `established/` Swedish sources should
be compared against.
