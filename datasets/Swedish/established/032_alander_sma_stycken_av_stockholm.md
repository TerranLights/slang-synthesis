# Swedish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: "Små stycken av Stockholm" (sampling pass)

**Source:** Rainer Alander, *Små stycken av Stockholm* (Bokförlaget Abacus, Mariehamns Tryckeri Ab, 1996), ISBN 952-5064-05-0 — a collection of short personal essays/vignettes about Stockholm by a Finland-Swedish author (sailor, novelist, visual artist, resident of Stockholm/Pargas). Sampled across chapters: "En polska" (p.7), "Nyår" (p.14-15), "Levande malm" (p.25), "En äkta Kylberg" (p.24, dialogue continuation), "En landsman" / "Hundskit och presidentval" (p.42-43), "En landsman" dialogue continued, "Finska flickor" (p.34-35), "Antastaren" (p.44-45), "En uteliggare" (p.54-55), "Samtal vid en pissoar" (p.57), "Missriktad vänlighet" (p.75), "Hundar och kärlek" (p.89), "Rakas, rakas." (p.99), toilet-economics dialogue in "Samtal vid en pissoar" continuation (p.58-59), and the closing chapter "De polska hästarna" (p.110-111).

**Coverage note:** This is native-language usage-corpus material (personal essays/vignettes), not an instructional source — per the extraction spec this is a representative sampling pass (opening, two middle sections, and the ending) pulling out genuinely notable vocabulary/idiom/register, not an exhaustive transcription of all 57 PDF pages (~110 printed pages). PDF has no text layer (confirmed via `pdftotext` test); all entries below are vision-read from rendered page images (`pdftoppm -r 150`). No handwritten marginalia was found on any sampled page — all sampled pages are clean printed text with the author's own line-drawing illustrations (also printed, not handwritten). Page-numbering gotcha found and resolved: PDF pages 1-4 are cover/title/colophon/TOC (irregular single-page and spread scans), but from PDF page 5 onward the book is scanned as two-printed-pages-per-image spreads with a consistent offset `printed_left_page = 2×PDF_page − 2` (verified against 12 independently rendered spreads, all matching exactly).

**Register finding:** This source leans noticeably more toward natural literary/discursive register than the children's-fiction dialogue already extracted elsewhere in this dataset, but it is NOT a purely formal source — Alander's dialogue passages (the book is heavily dialogue-driven despite being essayistic) carry real taboo/colloquial Stockholm street register (profanity, drug slang, sailor slang, dialect-flagged speech) sitting right alongside more literary narrative prose. The essay/narration voice itself (first-person reflective passages) is measured and descriptive; the quoted-speech voice of other characters is where almost all the slang/taboo material below was found. This mirrors a common real-world register split: narrator-literary vs. character-colloquial.

## Grammar points

*(Sparse by design — this is a native-prose usage source, not a grammar reference; points below are stylistic/register observations, not systematic paradigms.)*

### Unmarked dialogue with em-dash only, no quotation marks

Alander follows the standard Swedish literary convention of marking direct speech with a line-initial em-dash (–) and no closing quotation mark, rather than English-style quotation marks (seen throughout every sampled page, e.g. p.42-43, p.58-59). Worth noting for any downstream corpus-processing step that might otherwise misparse dialogue boundaries using quote-mark heuristics tuned for other sources.

### Historical present alternating with narrated past within a single chapter

The opening chapter "En polska" (p.7) opens in present tense ("Jag sitter på Café Lindex...", "Jag bläddrar i Dagens Nyheter...") for scene-setting immediacy, then several chapters sampled later (e.g. "Antastaren," p.44) are told entirely in simple past as completed anecdotes. The tense choice tracks whether the vignette is framed as "happening now, as I write" vs. "a story I'm recounting" — a deliberate stylistic device rather than a grammatical rule, worth flagging for anyone modeling register/tense conventions in literary Swedish narration.

### Elliptical one-word/short-fragment dialogue turns

Dialogue throughout (e.g. p.42-43's "–Där ser man." / "–Just det." / "–Jaha.") relies heavily on terse, copula-less or verb-less response fragments as a naturalistic spoken-register marker — distinct from the fuller-sentence dialogue typical of pedagogical/children's-fiction sources already in this dataset. This fragment style is one of the clearest register fingerprints distinguishing adult literary dialogue from instructional or children's material.

### Compound-noun taboo/blunt titling

Chapter titles themselves carry register signaling independent of body text — "Hundskit och presidentval" (dog shit and presidential election) juxtaposes a taboo compound noun with a formal/political one purely for ironic effect at the title level; the chapter body itself is comparatively measured political dialogue. Worth noting as a case where `Usage Tier` for a single lexical item (the title noun) doesn't predict the register of the surrounding prose.
