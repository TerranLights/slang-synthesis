## Grammar points

*(Brief/sparse by design for a native-literature usage sample — see the extraction spec.)*

### Narrative present tense for dramatic immediacy

Lindgren's past-tense narration occasionally shifts into historical present mid-scene for comic or
dramatic beats — e.g. "Sixten slår sig på knäna och skrattar mera än någon annan... Han gick fram till
de rödas ledare" (pp. 38-39) slides from present back to past within the same paragraph. The device is
used selectively, not as a sustained tense, and tracks moments the narrator wants to render as if
watched live rather than reported.

### Spoken/written register split inside the same text

The written narration consistently uses *de*/*dem* ("they"/"them"), while quoted child and adult
dialogue freely uses the colloquial leveled form *dom* for both (see the `dom` vocabulary entry above).
This is a clean, visible register marker the book deploys deliberately — dialogue is written closer to
actual spoken Swedish than the surrounding prose.

### Productive "-unge" diminutive for dismissive child-nicknames

Both *snorvalp* and *flickungen* (see above) show the same pattern: an insulting modifier or bare noun
stem + a diminutive/child-marking element (*-valp* "puppy," *-unge* "kid/brat") to belittle a child
specifically as a child, distinct from adult-directed insults. Worth tracking across the gang-slang
register generally — children in this book insult each other primarily through exaggerated comparison
(bison oxen, foghorns) rather than through adult-register taboo vocabulary, which appears mostly in the
kidnapper-villain's dialogue instead (e.g. *tusan*).

### Contraction/elision as a child-speech marker

*Heter'u* and *Aktare* (see above) both render a reduced/fast spoken form directly in print rather than
spelling out the full standard form. This appears selectively — mostly in fast, emotional, or
breathless dialogue (an eager child's retelling, a casual question) — functioning as a light
visual/phonetic cue to the reader rather than a systematic orthographic convention across the book.
