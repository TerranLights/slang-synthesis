## Grammar points

### "dom" for de/dem in narration and dialogue alike

The text consistently uses the spoken-Swedish form **dom** for both the subject pronoun *de* ("they")
and the object pronoun *dem* ("them") — not only in character dialogue (where this would be
unsurprising) but in the narrator's own prose (e.g. p. 7: "Dom är ju så söta!"; throughout, "dom"
appears far more often than the written-standard "de"/"dem"). This is a deliberate register choice —
Lindgren's narration leans toward a spoken, child-adjacent voice rather than formal written Swedish,
and it stays consistent across the whole sampled range (pp. 7–170), not confined to quoted speech.

### Final -g dropping in "sej"/"mej"/"dej"

The reflexive/object pronoun forms consistently appear as **sej, mej, dej** (not the written-standard
*sig, mig, dig*) throughout both dialogue and narration (e.g. "jag tycker om dej ändå", p. 31; "jag
betalar själv vad jag slår sönder... Här!", p. 169). Paired with the "dom" convention above, this is
the book's two main orthography-level markers of colloquial spoken register, used uniformly rather
than only inside quotation marks — i.e., the narrator's own voice is written in the same informal
register as the characters speak, not in a more formal contrasting register.

### Apostrophe-marked clipping for colloquial contraction

Beyond "me'" for "med" (see vocabulary table), the clipping convention marks informal, rapid or
heightened-emotion speech specifically — it is not used for ordinary dialogue throughout, only for
Karlsson's most agitated lines (p. 130). This suggests the apostrophe-clipping is reserved as a
register-escalation device rather than a blanket transcription convention.

### Exclamations functioning as complete, verb-less utterances

Several one- or two-word interjections stand as full conversational turns with no surrounding
clause — e.g. "Försvinn!" (p. 130), "Nej, Karlsson... låt bli!" (p. 168), "Äntligen" (p. 150). This
is simply a natural feature of transcribed-feeling dialogue, but worth noting for conlang-synthesis
purposes: child-register exclamation can carry a full speech-act (command, relief, refusal) with no
finite verb at all.
