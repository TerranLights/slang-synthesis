# Swedish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Advanced Spoken Swedish (Söderbäck)

**Source:** Martin Söderbäck (North Park College), *Advanced Spoken Swedish* (Augustana Book Concern,
Rock Island, Illinois), Upsala, Sweden, February 1947 — a 15-lesson advanced conversational/idiom
course (not the FSI companion course the dispatch speculated it might be; this is an independent,
earlier work). Each lesson has a dialogue (A), explanatory Notes (B), and vocabulary; the preface
states the book is deliberately idiom-focused, built around colloquial expressions and set phrases
rather than core grammar (which it assumes the student already has).

**Rendering note:** vision-only scan (no usable text layer), `pdftoppm -png -r 150`. **Each rendered
PDF page image is a two-printed-page spread** (85 PDF pages ≈ 170 printed pages, matching the book's
own pagination through its two end vocabularies to ~p.160+).

**Relationship to this dataset's other Swedish sources:** this book is explicitly idiom/colloquial-
register-focused by design (per its own preface: "Notes permit a great range of use... various groups
of idioms may be studied and learned thoroughly"), making it structurally different from Croghan `008`,
McClean `009`, and FSI `015a-c` (all grammar-forward courses). Accordingly this file captures
substantially *more* of its content than the "non-redundant supplement" treatment given to McClean/FSI
— idiom density here is high and low in grammatical redundancy with this project's other sources.

**Coverage note:** Lessons I-XV sampled across the book; dialogue vocabulary and idiom-notes captured
per lesson, drill/translation exercises (sections C-F) skipped per the coverage rule (repeated
practice material, no new content). No handwritten marginalia found; scan quality clean throughout.

## Lesson I (pp. 7-13)

**Dialogue premise:** Herr Noreen and Herr Strömberg meet on the street; Strömberg has just returned
from a long trip through the United States (as far as California).

**Note 1 (p.8) — hemma vs. hem, and the wider rest/motion adverb family:** `hemma` ("at home," rest)
vs. `hem` ("home," motion-toward) — the same rest/motion distinction already documented from Croghan
`008` Unit 14, here extended with a fuller paired list: `ut/ute`, `fram/framme`, `in/inne`,
`bort/borta` (motion/rest pairs), plus a differently-formed set `var/vart` ("where"/"whither"),
`här/hit` ("here"/"hither"), `där/dit` ("there"/"thither"). Also: `utifrån` ("from outside"),
`hemifrån`/`norrifrån`/`därifrån` ("from home"/"from the north"/"from there"), and the directional
`-ut` compounds `västerut, österut, norrut, söderut` ("westward, eastward, northward, southward").

**Note 2 — paratactic motion+activity, reconfirmed:** `Jag trodde du fortfarande var ute och reste`
("I thought you were still out traveling") — the same "two finite verbs joined by `och`" pattern
already documented from Croghan `008` Unit 8 (`komma och bada`), here with further examples: `Han går
ut och jagar` ("he goes out hunting"), `Han sitter vid fönstret och skriver` ("he sits at the window
writing"), `Hon springer omkring och skvallrar` ("she runs around gossiping" — genuinely new
vocabulary, `skvallra` "to gossip"). Also notes that spoken Swedish frequently **omits `att`** at the
start of a clause: `Jag trodde du fortfarande var...` is a reduced form of the fuller `Jag trodde att
du fortfarande var...`.

**Note 3 — time expressions "last ___":** a systematic `i ___s` pattern built on festival/day/
season/time-of-day names: `i söndags morse` ("last Sunday morning"), `i julas` ("last Christmas"),
`i onsdags` ("last Wednesday"), `i våras` ("last spring"), `i somras` ("last summer"), `i vintras`
("last winter"), `i morse` ("this [past] morning"), `i middags` ("this [past] noon") — but `i går`
("yesterday") and `i förgår` ("day before yesterday") do **not** take the `-s`. Future equivalents use
`nästa` or bare `i` + simple form (`i morgon`, `i övermorgon`, `i jul`/`nästa jul`). Past-time month/
year reference uses `förra`/`förliden`: `förliden april`, `förra året`/`förlidet år`. `om` marks future
elapsed-time ("in X time": `om ett år`, `om fjorton dagar`); `(för)...sedan` marks past elapsed time
("X ago": `för ett par veckor sedan`); plain duration uses `i`: `i två år` ("for two years").

**Note 4 — the discourse particle `då` (unstressed vs. stressed):** unstressed, sentence-final `då` in
a question is untranslated filler (`Hur står det till med din syster då?`); stressed `då` means "then,
at that time, under those circumstances" and *should* be translated (`Då kom han`, "Then he came").
Exception: `då` in the fixed question words `När då? Var då? Hur då? Vad då?` is stressed but still
untranslated.

**Note 4B — the discourse particle `nog`:** adds "no doubt, probably" — `Det går nog bra` ("It will
turn out well, no doubt").

**Note 4C — the discourse particle `ju`:** gives "a complexion of affirmation and certainty," often
best rendered as an English tag question ("...didn't you?/...as you know"): `Men du sade ju, att du
skulle komma` ("But you said you would come, didn't you?"); `Han betalar ju aldrig sina skulder` ("He
never pays his debts, as you know").

**Note 4D — the discourse particle `väl` (unstressed):** implies "probability, supposition,
uncertainty, or hope," or may be pure filler: `Han vill väl inte gå ut i det här regnet` ("I don't
suppose he wants to go out in this rain"); `Det är väl inte så noga` ("It is not so particular [I
suppose]").

**Note 5 — `tycka` vs. `tänka` (both loosely "think"):** `tycka` is used for (1) opinion (`Jag tycker
att du borde komma`, "I think you ought to come") and (2) reporting an impression received through any
of the five senses (`Jag tycker tavlan är vacker`; `det smakar/luktar gott`; `det känns för mjuk`).
(The contrast with `tänka`, presumably given on the following page, was not captured in this pass.)

## Lesson II — "I Butiken" ("In the Shop") (pp. 16-21)

Shopping dialogue (buying a hat, gloves, Christmas presents) with several genuinely new idiom/register
notes:

- **`vad kan jag stå till tjänst med?`** ("what can I do for you?" lit. "what can I stand to service
  with") — formal shop-clerk opening formula.
- **`det duger`** ("that'll do, that's good enough") — colloquial approval of a purchase.
- **`får det lov att vara något annat?`** ("will there be anything else?") — formal clerk phrase.
- **`det går väl an`** (`gå an` = "be all right, be acceptable"): `Det gick väl an, fast det var för
  långt` ("It was all right, though it was too long" — reviewing a lecture). `väl` here, with long
  stressed `ä` and equal stress on the following word, means "rather/quite" — **a second, distinct
  sense of `väl` from the Lesson 1 unstressed-filler sense**, explicitly contrasted by the source:
  `Han är väl ung för en sådan syssla` ("He is rather young for such a task"); `ganska` can substitute
  for this stressed sense of `väl` but not the unstressed one.
- **`hålla på och + infinitive`** — a continuous/progressive-action construction: `jag har hållit på
  och köpt` ("I have been buying" — `och köpt` is explicitly flagged as **colloquial** for `att köpa`).
  `hålla på` implies continued action: `Han håller på och skriver en bok` ("He is writing a book").
  With point-action verbs, `hålla på att + infinitive` instead means "be on the point of, almost":
  `Jag höll på att falla i sjön` ("I almost fell into the lake"); `Jag höll på att sätta mig på din
  hatt` ("I almost sat down on your hat").
- **Denomination-clipping nouns**: `femman` ("the five," short for `femkronosedel`, a five-crown
  bill), `tian` (`tia`, short for `tiokronosedel`, a ten-crown bill) — directly parallel to Croghan
  `008` Unit 5's `hundralapp`, confirming this clipped-denomination-noun pattern is productive and
  general: **any** number can form a noun this way (`ettan` "the number one," `tolvan` "the number
  twelve," `sjuan`/`sexan` "number seven/six" as race-position nouns) — these are first-declension
  nouns (`två ettor`, "two ones").
- **Price-per-unit construction**: the noun takes the **definite** form as the normal pattern
  (`Mjölken kostar tjugofem öre litern`, "Milk costs 25 öre per liter"; `Tyget kostar en och femtio
  metern`); the exception is `per styck` ("apiece"), which commonly keeps the indefinite instead
  (`fem öre styck`).

## Lesson III (pp. 24-29)

**Dialogue premise:** Fröken Andersson and Herr Rydberg talk about family — an only child's envy of
his conversation partner's many cousins, an ailing grandmother on the mend, and arranging a future
meeting.

- **`rätt (så) bra`** ("pretty good") — `rätt (så)` conveys "rather": `Det är rätt så kallt ute` ("It
  is rather cold out"); `Han talar svenska rätt så bra` ("He speaks Swedish fairly well") — a further,
  *third* distinct intensifier alongside `väl` (Lesson 2) and `väldigt` (Lesson 1) for the "rather/
  quite" semantic space, confirming Swedish has several non-interchangeable near-synonymous degree
  adverbs worth distinguishing for conlang mechanics.
- **`dålig`** explicitly flagged as polysemous: "sickly" (of a person's health, as in the dialogue's
  `farmor har varit dålig`) alongside its more basic sense "bad, poor" (`Det är dålig skörd i år`, "The
  harvest is poor this year").
- **`ta hand om`** ("to take care of [someone]," e.g. `vi tar hand om henne`) vs. **`ta vara på`**
  ("take care of/look after," also "make use of [an opportunity]" — `Ett sådant tillfälle måste man ju
  ta vara på`, "One must of course make use of an opportunity like that") — explicitly noted as
  interchangeable in some contexts but not all.
- **`gifta sig med`** ("to get married to") / **`förlova sig`** ("to get engaged") — `På juldagen
  förlovade de sig, och de tänker gifta sig i vår` ("They got engaged on Christmas Day, and intend to
  marry this spring").
- **`gott om`** ("plenty of, lots of") — `Han har gott om pengar` ("He has lots of money"); `Nästa
  vecka har jag gott om tid` ("Next week I'll have lots of time").
- **The proclitic-vs-suffixed definite article split, systematized**: certain adjectives take **no**
  suffixed definite article on the following noun even though the adjective itself signals
  definiteness — `följande` ("following"), `förliden` ("past, last"), `samma` ("same"), `kommande`
  ("coming"), `nästa` ("next") — e.g. `följande dag`, `förlidet år`, `kommande vecka` (no `-en`/`-et`
  suffix on the noun). By contrast, `första` ("first"), `förra` ("past"), `enda` ("only"), `halva`
  ("half"), `hela` ("whole"), `rätta` ("right"), `senare` ("latter"), `senaste` ("last") **do** take
  the suffixed article: `första dagen`, `förra året`, `halva taket`, `hela huset`. **This is a
  genuinely new, systematic grammar point with no equivalent in this dataset's other Swedish
  sources.**
- **`gå till` / `ske` / `hända`** — three near-synonyms for "to happen": `gå till` is used for "how
  something happened" as an ongoing process, typically in past tense (`Hur gick det till?`, "How did
  it happen?"); `ske`/`hända` are more general "happen" verbs, substitutable for `gå till` but also
  usable for a single unexpected event (`Det hände alldeles oförväntat`, "It happened entirely
  unexpectedly" — this phrasing would sound odd with `gå till`).
- **`gå sönder`** ("to break, go to pieces") — `Glaset föll i golvet och gick sönder` ("The glass fell
  on the floor and broke").
- **`för resten`** ("besides, furthermore, as far as that goes," also "by the way" depending on
  context) — a versatile discourse connector: `Och för resten är det för sent nu` ("And furthermore,
  it is too late now"); `Vad gjorde du i går, för resten?` ("What did you do yesterday, by the way?").

## Lesson IV (pp. 32-37)

**Marginalia note (vision-reading guard):** p.32-33 of this copy carries a previous owner's faint
handwritten pencil annotations in the left margin (what appears to read "probably not" and "anyway" —
written vertically, inconsistent pen-stroke style versus the printed typeface, positioned in the blank
margin rather than in any printed answer space). **Excluded entirely from extraction** per the
extraction spec's vision-reading guard; flagged here for the record.

**Dialogue premise:** Herr Landquist and Herr Sundén discuss Christmas and New Year's plans — a rich
source of Swedish holiday-custom vocabulary that both confirms and extends Croghan `008` Unit 10
("Midsommar i Dalarna") and FSI `015c` Unit 15's holiday content.

- **`namnsdag`** ("name day," a saint's-day-derived personal calendar observance, historically tied to
  when a child was named) vs. `födelsedag` ("birthday") — the dialogue explains these used to coincide
  ("Förr i tiden var det ju vanligt, att man gav barnen namn så att namnsdag och födelsedag kom
  samtidigt") and traces the custom to the Catholic-era practice of naming children for the saint's
  day they were born on (`ett arv från den katolska tiden`).
- **`det räcker`/`räcka till`** ("to last, be enough"): `så det räcker för ett helt år` ("so that it
  lasts a whole year"); `Kaffet räckte inte till åt alla` ("There was not enough coffee to go
  around").
- **`hålla med om`** ("to agree [with a statement/idea]") vs. **`hålla med`** (without `om`, "to side
  with/stand up for [a person]") — a minimal preposition-drop contrast: `Hon håller alltid med sina
  egna barn` ("She always stands up for her own children"); `Pojken håller med sin syster fast hon har
  orätt` ("The boy stands up for his sister though she is wrong").
- **Holiday greetings**: `en glad jul och ett gott nytt år` ("a merry Christmas and a happy New
  Year"), `glad påsk` ("Happy Easter"), `lycka till` ("good luck"), `lycklig resa`/`lycka till på
  resan` ("bon voyage"), `hjärtliga lyckönskningar` ("my best wishes").
- **Regional holiday-pilgrimage customs, stated as specific received wisdom**: "Midsummer, for
  example, one really should celebrate in **Dalarna**" and "on **Walpurgis night** (`Valborgsmässo-
  afton`) one ought to be in **Upsala**" — confirming Croghan's choice of "Midsommar i Dalarna" as a
  unit title reflects a real, named cultural association (Dalarna = the quintessential Midsummer
  destination), and adding a second, parallel regional association (Uppsala = the quintessential
  Valborgsmässoafton/April 30 student-festival destination) not found elsewhere in this dataset.
- **Rural Christmas Eve customs described**: fish and porridge (`fisk och gröt`), a sheaf of grain set
  out for the birds (`ett kassie för fåglarna`, i.e. `en kärve`/sheaf), and a horse-drawn sleigh ride
  with torches to the Christmas morning matins service (`till julotta med släde och facklor`).

## Lesson V (pp. 39-45)

**Dialogue premise:** two friends discuss sports, a boating trip, and an upcoming Sweden-Finland
soccer match.

- **`idrott` vs. `sport`** (both "sport"): `idrott` (the native Swedish term) applies to sports based
  primarily on physical training/ability; `sport` (an English loanword) is used for sports depending
  largely on equipment/an external agent (hunting, sailing, horse racing) — a semantic domain split
  with no single-word English equivalent.
- **Cultural note on sport popularity (1947)**: `fotboll` (soccer) is explicitly stated as "the most
  popular sport in Sweden"; American football, baseball, basketball, and golf are "of little
  importance compared with other sports such as soccer, track, and swimming." Lotteries are legal in
  Sweden specifically for purposes like charity fundraising and promoting theater.
- **`spela` vs. `leka`** reconfirmed (matches Croghan `008` Unit 4's `spela`/`leka` distinction) with
  new example idioms: `spela bort sina pengar` ("to gamble away one's money"); `det spelar ingen roll`
  ("that makes no difference," lit. "it plays no role").
- **`på tiden`** ("about time") — `Det är på tiden` ("It is about time").
- **`på länge`** ("for a long time," used specifically with a negative to indicate a lapse between
  occurrences): `Jag har inte sett honom på ett helt år` ("I haven't seen him for a whole year").
- **`vem som helst`** ("anybody") confirmed as part of the same `som helst` free-choice-indefinite
  family already documented from Croghan `008` Unit 10 and McClean `009`.
- **`här om året`** ("the other year," i.e. recently) / **`här om dagen`** ("the other day").
- **`det stämmer`** ("that is right, that matches") — `stämma` can also mean "agree, tally": `Deras
  uppgifter om olyckan stämmer inte` ("Their accounts of the accident do not agree").
- **`ha ont`/`göra ont`** ("to have a pain" / "to hurt, cause pain"): `Var har du ont?` or `Var gör det
  ont?` ("Where does it hurt?").
- **`få fatt på`** ("to get hold of [someone]") — `Jag ska försöka få fatt på honom`.

## Lesson VI (pp. 46-53) — idiom notes sampled from Section B

- **`trädgård`** explicitly flagged as a false-friend-adjacent point: means "flower or vegetable
  garden," **not** "orchard" as might be assumed — "orchard" is specifically `fruktträdgård`.
  Fruit-tree vocabulary: `äppelträd` (apple), `päronträd` (pear), `körsbärsträd` (cherry), `plommonträd`
  (plum).
- **`när som helst`** ("anytime") and the full `som helst` family reconfirmed with `helst` identified
  as the superlative of `gärna` ("preferably, rather, best of all"); combined with `som` it becomes a
  general "any-" indefinite: `vem som helst` ("anybody"), `vad som helst` ("anything at all"), `hur som
  helst` ("either way, anyhow"), `vilken dag som helst` ("any day now"), `när som helst` ("any time").
- **`läggas om`** ("be replaced/redone, repeated, duplicated") — a productive `om`-prefixed verb
  family: `Verandagolvet måste läggas om helt och hållet` ("The porch floor must be replaced
  entirely"); `Tre år senare gifte han om sig` ("Three years later he remarried" — `gifta om sig`).
  `klä om sig` ("to change clothes") and `göra om` ("to do over again") are given as further examples
  of the same `om`-prefix productivity.
- **`helt och hållet`** ("completely, entirely") — `Han förstörde den helt och hållet` ("He destroyed
  it completely").
- **Vague-degree comparative construction**: Swedish sometimes uses an adjective's comparative form
  not for direct comparison but to convey "rather [old/young/etc.]" — `utan större kostnader`
  ("without great expense," not "without *greater* expense"); `en äldre herre` ("an elderly
  gentleman," not necessarily "older than someone specific"); nouns modified this way stay in the
  indefinite declension.
- **`låsa upp`** ("unlock") vs. bare `låsa`/`låsa igen` ("to lock").
- **`om jag får be`** ("if you please" — a formal request softener).
- **`eller hur?`** reconfirmed as a general tag question "expecting confirmation" (cross-source match
  with `015a`'s FSI finding): `Den är väl inte för stor, eller hur?` ("It isn't too big, is it?").

## Lesson VII (pp. 53-60) — idiom notes sampled from Section B

- **`draga`/`dra`** ("to pull") identified as highly polysemous, with a cluster of idiomatic
  extensions: `Han drar på sig skorna` ("He puts on his shoes"); `Det drar här` ("There is a draft
  here"); `Han drog på munnen` ("He smiled faintly," lit. "pulled on the mouth"); `Det drar ut på
  tiden` ("It takes a long time," lit. "it pulls out on the time"); `Han drar sig tillbaka` ("He
  retires," lit. "pulls himself back"); `Jag ligger bara och drar mig` ("I am just stretching out for
  a minute," lit. "lying and pulling myself").
- **`Dra åt skogen, din dumbom!`** ("Go jump in the lake, you blockhead!" lit. "Pull off to the
  forest, you blockhead!") — **a genuine mild-insult/dismissal idiom**, directly comparable in
  function to the English figurative dismissal it's glossed with; `dumbom` ("blockhead") independently
  cross-confirms McClean `009`'s identical insult noun (`en sådan dumbom!`).
- **`Morgonstund har guld i mund, säger ordspråket`** ("The early bird catches the worm, says the
  proverb" — lit. "the morning hour has gold in its mouth") — explicitly labeled `ordspråket` ("the
  proverb") by the source.
- **`sjusovare`** ("sleepyhead, sluggard," lit. "seven-sleeper") — the source gives its etymology: not
  a reference to sleeping until seven o'clock, but from an old legend (first recorded in the sixth
  century on the European continent) about seven men who slept for 198 years — a genuinely interesting
  etymological/cultural note on a colloquial insult-adjacent term.
- **Three-way "to know" distinction**: `känna` ("to be acquainted with [a person]" — `Jag har känt
  honom i många år`; also `känna igen` "to recognize"); `veta` ("to have knowledge of a fact" — `Det
  visste jag redan i går`); `kunna` ("to be capable of / have knowledge of [a skill/fact]" — `Kan han
  engelska också?`, `Jag kan inte den sången`, `Han kan ingenting`).
- **Two-way "to ask" distinction**: `bedja`/`be` (short colloquial form, cross-confirming McClean
  `009`'s §73 `be/ber` note) means "ask" in the sense of *request*, and also "pray" (`Han bad till
  Gud`, "He prayed to God"); `fråga` means "ask" in the sense of *question* (`Han frågade, när vi
  tänker flytta`, "He asked when we intend to move").
- **`har du lust?`** ("would you like [to]?") — `lust` here is a noun ("desire/inclination"), **not**
  to be confused with the adjective `lustig` ("funny": `Han talade om en lustig historia`, "He told a
  funny story") nor with `olust`/`olustig` ("a feeling of discomfort": `Jag känner mig olustig`, "I
  feel uncomfortable/ill at ease").

## Lesson VIII (pp. 60-65) — idiom notes

- **Mild insult/address terms, a genuine cluster**: `ditt nöt!` ("you fool!," lit. "you nut/beast"),
  `din drummel` ("you chump"), `era odågor` ("you good-for-nothing fellows"), `din lilla parvel` ("you
  little fellow" — affectionate rather than hostile, "How sweet you are, you little fellow").
- **`göra av med`** ("to spend freely, squander"): `Han gjorde av med sitt arv på en kort tid` ("He
  spent his inheritance in a short time"). Bare `göra av (med)` more generally means "to put, to
  leave" (`Var gjorde du av tidningen?`, "Where did you put the newspaper?"). Unstressed `göra av`
  (without a following prepositional phrase) means "to make from" (`Leksakerna är gjorda av trä`, "The
  toys are made from wood").
- **`jag kom över dem för rampris`** ("I got them for a song" — a bargain-purchase idiom).
- **Paper-bound vs. hardbound books cultural note**: `obundna böcker`/`häftade böcker` ("paper bound
  books") are explicitly noted as "far more common in Sweden than in America."
- **`stå` used where English uses "write/say/read"**: `Det står i bibeln...` ("The Bible says...");
  `Det stod i tidningen i morse` ("I read it in the paper this morning").
- **`kanhända`** ("maybe, perhaps," a contraction of "can happen").
- **`antingen...eller` / `varken...eller`** ("either...or" / "neither...nor").
- **`för...skull`** ("for the sake of...") — a possessive-requiring construction, directly paralleling
  English "for ... sake": `för pappas skull` ("for father's sake"), `för artighets skull` ("for the
  sake of politeness").
- **`säga upp`** ("to resign / give notice," used both of quitting a job and of a landlord/tenant
  vacating): `Han har sagt upp sin plats` ("He has resigned"); `Han har blivit uppsagd` ("He has been
  dismissed/fired" — passive counterpart). Related: `säga till` ("to tell, inform"; `säga åt` as an
  alternative), `säga ifrån` ("to refuse, say no": `Han ville låna min häst, men jag sade ifrån`, "He
  wanted to borrow my horse, but I said no"), `det vill säga` ("that is to say," abbreviated `d.v.s.`
  — a written-register abbreviation), `så att säga` ("so to speak").

## Lesson IX (pp. 67-73) — idiom notes

- **`fruntimmer`** ("woman") — explicitly flagged by the source as usable when *speaking about*
  someone but **never in respectful direct address**; grammatically a neuter noun despite referring to
  a female person — a genuine register-restriction finding on a gendered-reference term.
- **`får jag lov att presentera`** ("may I introduce") — `förställa` is noted as a common alternative
  to `presentera`. Social-etiquette note: a gentleman being introduced bows but need not speak, though
  polite remarks are often exchanged anyway.
- **`hjärtans gärna`** ("certainly, I'll be glad to").
- **`komma överens om`** ("we agreed that...," connoting *mutual* agreement) vs. **`hålla med (om)`**
  (Lesson IV's point, here clarified as "of a one-sided nature" — one person agreeing with another's
  view). `komma överens` alone (no `om`) also means "to get along": `Vi kommer bra överens` ("We get
  along well").
- **`gå i päls`/`gå i hatt`** ("to wear a fur coat"/"to wear a hat" — `gå i` + garment as a wearing-
  state construction, distinct from the simple verb `bära`).
- **`rock` vs. `kappa`** (both "coat"): `kappa` is generally a woman's coat, `rock` a man's coat —
  though `kappa` extends to certain men's garments too: `prästkappa` ("clergyman's gown"),
  `militärkappa` ("military cloak").
- **`rätt så bistert`** ("rather severe") reconfirms Lesson III's `rätt (så)` intensifier; `ganska` and
  `rätt så` are noted here as fully synonymous.
- **`lägga sig till med`** ("to acquire, get," usually of a personal appearance trait or
  characteristic): `I somras lade han sig till med polisonger` ("Last summer he grew sideburns"); `Han
  har lagt sig till med glasögon` ("He has started wearing glasses"); `Han har lagt sig till med
  stockholmsdialekt` ("He has acquired a Stockholm dialect" — note: `stockholmsdialekt` implies
  distinct regional/urban dialects are a recognized, nameable social-marking feature). By contrast,
  bare `lägga sig` means "to go to bed" (`Han lägger sig tidigt`) or, with a following prepositional
  phrase, "to lie down" (`Han lägger sig på soffan`; `Hunden lägger sig under bordet`).
- **`en kusin till mig`** ("a cousin of mine") — a prepositional-possessive construction (`en X till
  Y`) used specifically for acquaintances/relatives of whom more than one could exist (as opposed to a
  simple possessive for a unique referent): `en skolkamrat till pappa`, `en moster till mig`, `en
  gammal bekant till henne`.
- **`hälsa på` — a second stress-shift-disambiguated idiom pair** (directly paralleling Croghan `008`
  Unit 7's `tycka om` stress-shift point): stressed `på` → `hälsa på` means "to visit, call on" (`Jag
  hälsade på honom när jag var hemma`, "I called on him when I was home"); unstressed `på` → `hälsa på`
  means "to greet" (`Jag hälsade på honom när vi möttes`, "I greeted him when we met").

## Lesson X (pp. 74-79) — idiom notes

- **`svartsjuk` vs. `avundsjuk`** (both loosely "jealous/envious," explicitly noted as **not**
  overlapping the way their English glosses might suggest): `svartsjuk` applies specifically to
  romantic jealousy (`Han är mycket svartsjuk på sin fru`, "He is very jealous of his wife");
  `avundsjuk` is used for envy of anything else (`Han är avundsjuk på alla, som har framgång`, "He is
  envious of everybody who is successful"). The deponent verb `avundas` ("to envy") is built from the
  noun `avund`.
- **`disputera`** ("to dispute," here in its specific academic sense "to defend a doctoral thesis") —
  the source describes the Swedish Ph.D. defense custom in detail: two appointed examiners
  (`opponenter`) publicly examine the candidate (`respondenten`); a third `opponent`, "really not an
  examiner," has the specific task of relieving tension by asking irrelevant/humorous questions — a
  genuinely distinctive academic-culture note.
- **`nation`** — at Lund and Uppsala universities, `nationer` are academic/social student
  organizations by home-district, dating to the 17th century, each with its own buildings/libraries;
  every enrolling student must belong to one. (`Nation` cross-confirms as a term of art rather than
  the ordinary "nation" sense covered in FSI `015b` Unit 11.)
- **`höll tal`** ("gave a speech") — `hålla föreläsningar över` ("deliver lectures about") is a
  parallel construction.
- **`det gör sitt till`** ("that helps, that makes a difference") — explicitly restricted to
  **positive** contexts only; the negative counterpart uses `hjälpa` instead (`Han är envis, och det
  hjälper inte att tala med honom`, "He is stubborn and it doesn't do any good to talk to him").
- **`god tid` vs. "good time"**: `god tid` means merely "ample/plenty of time" (`Vi har god tid ännu`,
  "We have lots of time yet") — **not** to be confused with English "a good time" (enjoyment), which
  instead requires `ha (det) trevligt`/`ha roligt`/`det är trevligt`/`det är roligt`.
- **`äpplet föll långt från trädet den gången`** — a deliberate, ironic *inversion* of the proverb
  `Äpplet faller ej långt från trädet` ("The apple doesn't fall far from the tree," i.e. "like father
  like son") — the inverted form means a child turned out *unlike* their parent.
- **`vara lagd`** ("to be inclined to"): `Han är lite lagd för att överdriva` ("He is inclined to
  exaggerate").

## Lesson XI (pp. 81-85) — idiom notes

- **`komma ihåg`** ("to remember, recall") — widely used; `minnas` is a synonym of equal commonness.
- **`lära` reflexive vs. non-reflexive split**: reflexive `lära sig` = "to learn" (`Han lärde sig
  läxan utantill`, "He learned the lesson by heart"); non-reflexive `lära` = "to teach" (`Han är
  anställd för att lära ungdomen fäkta`, "He is employed to teach the young people to fence");
  `undervisa` ("instruct") can substitute for the "teach" sense.
- **The `-lös` suffix** (= English "-less"), given as a productive derivational pattern: `hopplös`
  ("hopeless"), `tandlös` ("toothless"), `glädjelös` ("joyless"), `svarslös` ("without an answer"),
  `värnlös` ("defenseless"), `menlös` ("innocent, harmless").
- **Playing an instrument takes the indefinite noun** (contrasting with English "play *the* piano"):
  `Jag spelar fiol/orgel/dragspel/gitarr/flöjt/saxofon/klarinett` ("I play the violin/organ/accordion/
  guitar/flute/saxophone/clarinet") — the definite form is used only when referring to one specific,
  particular instrument (`Jag spelade på pianot hemma i går`, "I played the [that particular] piano at
  home yesterday").
- **`Gubben Noak`** ("Old Man Noah") — named as "a very simple but also very well known song" by Carl
  Michael Bellman (1740-1795), Sweden's most celebrated 18th-century song-poet — a genuine cultural
  reference with no equivalent elsewhere in this dataset.
- **`låta bli (att)`** ("to refrain from, leave alone"): `Låt bli att reta hunden, pojkar!` ("Don't
  tease the dog, boys!"); bare `Låt bli!` ("Don't!").
- **`i själ och hjärta`** ("at the bottom of your heart," lit. "in soul and heart").
- **`alltså`** ("then, therefore, accordingly") — lends "an element of affirmation" or may be pure
  filler, paralleling the discourse-particle family (`då, nog, ju, väl`) from Lesson I.

## Lesson XII (pp. 87-93) — idiom notes

- **`rent av`** ("actually, even, downright"): `Det var rent av bedrövligt, så hon sjöng` ("It was
  downright awful the way she sang"); `Det var rent av omöjligt att komma fram i buskarna` ("It was
  actually impossible to get through the bushes").
- **`känna på sig`** ("to have a feeling, sense intuitively"): `Jag känner på mig, att det blir regn`
  ("I have a feeling that it is going to rain") — distinct from plain `känna sig` ("to feel [a state]":
  `Jag känner mig trött/hungrig/glad/lycklig`).
- **`-dags` as a productive time-of-activity suffix**: `mjölkdags` ("milking time"), `kaffedags`
  ("coffee time"), `sängdags` ("bedtime"), `skoldags` ("time for school"), `kyrkdags` ("time for
  church") — `dags` also stands alone (`Hur dags kommer du?`, "What time do you come?").
- **Grain vocabulary**: `vete` (wheat), `råg` (rye), `havre` (oats), `korn` (barley); `majs` (corn) is
  noted as "not grown profitably in Sweden" — a period agricultural-geography fact.
- **`den skiner som solen i Karlstad`** ("it shines like the sun in Karlstad," meaning "it is very
  bright") — a regionally-anchored idiom naming Karlstad, "the largest city in the province of
  Värmland," commonly clipped to "Kalsta" in the saying itself.
- **`en kull`** ("a litter/brood," of animals): `en kull på nio stycken` ("a litter of nine"); `en
  andkull` ("a brood of wild ducks").
- **`tomtar`** ("brownies" — Swedish household/farmstead folklore spirits): "They inhabit the
  buildings of a farmstead and bring good luck. They are therefore catered to, especially at
  Christmas when a bowl of porridge (`gröt`) is set out for them" — a genuine, distinctive folklore/
  cultural-belief item with no equivalent elsewhere in this dataset, directly relevant to this
  project's broader interest in culturally specific lexical/belief items.
- **`pyssla (om)`** ("to putter about; coddle, pamper, trifle with," stressed `om`): `Hon pysslar om
  sina blommor` ("She is puttering about with her flowers"); `Hon pysslar om sina barn` ("She is
  coddling her children").
- **`i smyg`** ("furtively, on the sly"): `Han törs bara gå dit i smyg` ("He dares go there only on the
  sly"); related verb `smyga (sig)` ("to steal one's way, sneak"): `Han smyger sig genom skogen som en
  indian` ("He steals through the woods like an Indian" — a period simile, flagged for its dated
  framing rather than extracted as neutral vocabulary).

## Lesson XIII (pp. 94-99) — idiom notes

- **Language/nationality-adjective triples**: `ryss/ryska` ("Russian [man]/[language or woman]"),
  `spanjor/spanjorska` ("Spaniard/Spanish woman"), `tysk/tyska` ("German") — cross-confirms the
  male/female/language-noun syncretism pattern already documented from FSI `015b` Unit 11.
- **`rikssvenska`** ("standard Swedish," as contrasted with regional dialects) — explicitly defined as
  based on the dialects spoken around Lake Mälaren, i.e. the Stockholm region — a useful named term for
  "the prestige/standard variety" with no equivalent named elsewhere in this dataset.
- **`landskap`** ("province") — Sweden is stated to be divided into 24 `landskap`; the same word also
  means "countryside, landscape" generally.
- **`vänja sig vid`** ("to get used to") / **`vänja sig av med`** ("to break oneself of [a habit]"):
  `Jag vande mig av med att röka` ("I broke myself of smoking"); `Man vänjer sig vid allt` ("One gets
  used to everything").
- **`si och så`** ("so-so, after a fashion," of how something is going): `Det gick si och så med
  affärerna` ("The business did not go so well").
- **`i sinom tid`** ("in due time").
- **`för min del`** ("for my part") / **`för sin del`** ("for his/her part") — a possessive-marked
  "as for me/them" discourse construction.
- **`en del`** ("part, some"): `En del av pojkarna gingo barfota, en del hade skor` ("Some of the boys
  went barefoot, others wore shoes").
- **`för all del`** ("by all means" — affirmative — or "not at all" — dismissive, context-dependent):
  `Gå för all del inte ut, om du är förkyld` ("By all means, do not go outside if you have a cold");
  as a reply to thanks, "not at all."
- **`för den delen`** ("for that matter, as far as that goes") — cross-confirms Lesson III's `för
  resten` as a near-synonym discourse connector: `Han ljuger och det gör hon också, för den delen` ("He
  lies and she does, too, as far as that goes").
- **`tåla`** ("to tolerate, endure, bear"): `Jag tål honom inte` ("I can't stand him"); `Kärleken tål
  allt` ("Charity endureth all" — a biblical-register quotation); `Hon tål inte (vid) drag` ("She can
  not stand a draft").
- **`en annan` vs. `en till` — two distinct senses of "another," systematically contrasted**: `en
  annan` = "another [different one]" (`Nej, det var inte han, utan en annan som hjälpte mig`, "No, it
  wasn't him, but someone else who helped me"); `en till` = "another [additional] one" (`Det var goda
  äpplen; jag vill ha ett till`, "These were good apples; I'll have another [one]"). **This is a clean,
  genuinely useful semantic-distinction finding for conlang mechanics** — English collapses both senses
  into one word.
- **`det tjänar ingenting till`** ("it is of no use") / **`vad tjänar det till?`** ("what good is
  that? why do that?").

## Lesson XIV (pp. 102-107) — idiom notes

- **`så gott som`** ("almost, practically") — modifies an adjective, adverb, or indefinite pronoun
  (`dubbelt, ingenting, allt`): `Han förlorade så gott som alla sina ägodelar i eldsvådan` ("He lost
  practically all his belongings in the fire") — cross-confirms Croghan `008` Unit 1's identical
  phrase from a different angle (there used for "practically the whole country").
- **`en och annan`** ("an occasional [one]," of time or objects): `En och annan gång får han en fisk`
  ("Occasionally he catches a fish").
- **Collective-quantity idiom with `man` (singular, plural meaning)**: `ungefär två hundra man` ("about
  two hundred men") — used specifically for a group thought of as a unit (soldiers, a sports team):
  `ett lag på tio man` ("a team of ten men"). Separately, `man` also means "husband."
- **`smörgåsbord` defined explicitly**: "bread, butter, cold meats, fish, cheese, pickles, etc., which
  are served before the regular meal" — `smörgås` is the base word for "sandwich," usually open-faced.
  `allt annat än` ("anything but"): `Smörgåsbordet är allt annat än tomt` ("The smörgåsbord is anything
  but empty").
- **`helt enkelt`** ("simply"): `Han vill helt enkelt inte` ("He simply does not want to").
- **`ta på måfå`/`gå på måfå`** ("to take at random"/"to walk without goal or plan").
- **Cultural-culinary note**: `ärter och fläsk` (pea soup and pork) with `pannkaka` (Swedish pancakes)
  is "traditionally served on Thursdays" — a specific, named weekly-menu custom.
- **`rent ut sagt`** ("to speak bluntly, in plain language") — joins the `rent av` family (Lesson XII)
  of "actually/downright" intensifiers.
- **`slå igen butiken`** ("to close the shop") — also used figuratively to mean "terminate, end,
  conclude, quit" (`stänga butiken` as a synonym).
- **`så när`** (stressed `när`) ("almost," modifying a verb): `Hon hade så när trampat på ormen` ("She
  almost stepped on the snake") — a further, distinct "almost" construction alongside `så gott som`
  (which modifies adjectives/adverbs/pronouns, not verbs).
- **`ta för sig`** (stressed `för`, reflexive) ("to help oneself," e.g. at a meal) vs. unstressed `taga
  för` ("to consider, believe to be"): `Jag tog honom för engelsman, men han är tysk` ("I thought he
  was English [lit. 'took him for an Englishman'], but he is German").
- **`kalla på kyparen`** ("to call the waiter") — the source notes the actual Swedish etiquette: one
  attracts a waiter's attention by tapping a glass/plate edge with the knife, and addresses the head
  waiter as `hovmästarn`, not `kyparen`, when actually speaking to him.
- **`jag är hungrig som en varg`** ("I am hungry as a wolf, I am starved").
- **`Hungern är den bästa kryddan`** ("Hunger is the best sauce/seasoning") — explicitly labeled a
  proverb by the source.
- **`behagas det kaffe?`** ("Would you like coffee?") — a polite interrogative-passive request form;
  the active `behaga` can also be used (`Behagar herrarna...?`, "Would you gentlemen like...?").

## Lesson XV (pp. 108-113) — idiom notes

- **`husbonde`/`husse`** ("master," as in "master and servant"/"master and dog") — `husbonde` is
  generally pronounced "husbonn" in speech (a further spoken-vs-written pronunciation-reduction
  example, joining McClean `009`'s §73 catalog); `husse` is the specifically abbreviated form used in
  the "master and dog" (pet-owner) sense: `Gamla Karo viftade på svansen, när han såg husse komma` ("Old
  Karo wagged his tail when he saw his master coming" — `Karo` as a stock dog-name).
- **`gång`** — cataloged as covering two broad senses: (A) "motion" in a broad sense (`en långsam
  gång`, "a slow gait"; `Samtalet ville inte komma i gång`, "It was difficult to get the conversation
  started"; `Inom kort var slagsmålet i full gång`, "Before long the fist fight was in full sway"; `Allt
  går sin jämna gång`, "Everything goes as usual"); (B) "path" in a broad sense (`de grusade gångarna`,
  "the gravel-covered walks"; `en underjordisk gång`, "an underground passage").
