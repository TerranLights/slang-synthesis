**Gang-rivalry/mock-military register — CONFIRMED RECURRING from both prior books in this series.**
The "Vita Rosen" (White Rose) vs. "Röda Rosen" (Red Rose) gang rivalry and its "Rosornas krig" (War
of the Roses) framing is not only present in this third book but is the book's entire narrative
spine — it opens the first chapter and structures the plot throughout. The mock-military/mock-
chivalric vocabulary set recurs directly: **högkvarter** ("headquarters," used repeatedly, e.g. "De
vita återvände till sitt högkvarter"), **på fältet** ("in the field," used by the children themselves
of their gang operations), plus **riddare** ("knights," the gang members' self-styled rank, used on
the back-cover blurb itself: "riddarna Anders, Eva-Lotta och Kalle... mot de röda riddarna Sixten,
Benka och Jonte"), **trupper** ("troops"), **anfall** ("attack"), **order** ("order/command"),
**retirerade** ("retreated"), **ädling** ("nobleman," Sixten's self-styled title as "ädling och
ledare för Röda Rosen" on the book's own epigraph page), and a full mock-legal/mock-biblical
declaration-of-war parody register (see Notes below). This book does not use the exact terms
**spanare** ("scout") or **kringgående rörelse** ("flanking maneuver") within the sampled pages, but
the same stylistic device — elevated military/chivalric diction deployed for comic effect on a
children's gang war — is fully intact and, if anything, more structurally central here than in the
earlier two books, since this book's whole plot is literally titled around "rosornas krig."

## Grammar points

### Mock-elevated/parody register as a structural device
Confirmed again in this third book: Lindgren repeatedly switches into deliberately archaic, legal, or
biblical-sounding diction for comic effect when narrating the children's gang war — the war
declaration on p. 7 ("Härmed göres veterligt att..."), Sixten's self-styled epigraph title ("ädling
och ledare"), and Constable Björk's mock-oracular teasing ("Sök högt ovan jord... Fråga kajorna," p.
218) all draw on the same device: formal/archaic registers normally reserved for legal documents or
scripture, redeployed into a children's game. This is the clearest register-inversion pattern found
across the series so far and is worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase as a productive
source of slang-formation logic (cf. `register_inversion` in `00_Usage_Tier_Taxonomy.md`).

### Euphemistic exclamations in place of profanity
Several exclamations in the sample (sjutton, sabla, Du store Nebukanesar) substitute a numeral,
historical name, or softened intensifier for what would otherwise be a stronger oath — a
child-literature register convention keeping the books' profanity level mild while preserving the
rhythm and emotional force of real swearing. Worth comparing against the same device if it recurs in
later Swedish sources.

### Direct-address kinship titles for unrelated adults
"Farbror Björk" and "Storebror Klas" both use kinship terms ("farbror" = uncle, "storebror" = big
brother) as titles for non-relatives — a standard Swedish child-speech convention (cf. "farbror
polis" as a generic way children address any policeman) extended here to specific named characters,
including ironically to an antagonist.
