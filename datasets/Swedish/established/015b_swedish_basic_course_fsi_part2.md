## Unit 11 — Nations, Nationalities, Languages (p. 355)

A systematic point on Swedish's nationality-vocabulary morphology, genuinely distinct from anything
captured elsewhere in this dataset:

1. Language and nationality names are **never capitalized** in Swedish (already noted for days/months
   in Croghan; here extended explicitly to nationalities/languages).
2. Swedish has **separate words for female and male** nationals of a country (e.g., `fransyska`
   "Frenchwoman" vs. `fransman` "Frenchman").
3. The feminine nationality noun is **often identical** to the language name (e.g., `svenska` =
   both "Swedish (language)" and "Swedish woman").
4. Nationality nouns/adjectives decline by the ordinary noun/adjective rules — e.g. (nouns)
   `fransysk/a -an -or` ("Frenchwoman"), `svensk -en -ar` ("a Swede"), `fransman -nen, fransmän`
   ("Frenchman," irregular plural); (adjectives) `fransk -t -a`, `svensk -t -a`.
5. **No indefinite article** before nationality in predicate position (parallels the occupation-noun
   rule already documented from Croghan `008` Unit 1): `Erik är svensk` ("Erik is a Swede"), `John är
   amerikan` ("John is an American").

## Unit 12 — Social Gatherings: a three-tier formality register ladder (pp. 361-375)

**This unit is this file's single most valuable find** — FSI deliberately structures it as three
parallel invitation/visit scenarios of escalating formality ("Part I: An Informal Invitation to
Lunch," "Part II: An Invitation to a Dinner," "Part III: A Formal Dinner Party"), explicitly telling
the student it is "an introduction to Swedish social etiquette" with key phrases underlined for later
drill — a genuine, source-structured register contrast with no equivalent in this dataset's other
Swedish sources.

**Part I (informal, between friends on first-name/`du` terms):**
- `Hallå!` (telephone "Hello!"), `Är det [name]?` ("Is that [name]?"), `Ja, det är det` ("Yes,
  speaking").
- `Hur har ni det?` ("How are things with you?" — note `ni` used here as a plural "you," addressing a
  household, not the formal-singular `Ni`).
- `tacka för senast` ("thank [someone] for [the last time we met]") — cross-confirms Croghan `008`
  Unit 2's identical social convention.
- `göra sällskap` ("come/go together," lit. "make company") — `har du lust att göra sällskap ut?`
  ("would you like to come along?").
- `höra av sig` ("to get in touch, call back") — reflexive-verb idiom.
- `det passar [someone]` ("it suits/is convenient for [someone]").
- `Adjö då och välkomna!` ("Goodbye then, and welcome!" — `välkommen`/`välkomna` used here in its
  correct forward-looking "looking forward to your visit" sense, confirming Unit 2's false-friend
  note from `015a`).

**Part II (a dinner invitation — more vocabulary around arranging a mixed social evening):**
`skolkamrat`/`arbetskamrat` ("schoolmate"/"colleague," lit. "school-comrade"/"work-comrade"),
`försvarsattaché` ("defense attaché" — diplomatic-register vocabulary, fitting for an FSI course
written for US foreign-service trainees), `förresten` ("anyway, besides"), `det gör detsamma` ("it
doesn't matter," lit. "it does the same"), `försenad` ("late, delayed").

**Part III (the formal dinner party — the richest register-specific content):**
- `lägga bort titlarna` ("to drop the [formal] titles," i.e., switch to first names/`du`) — an
  explicit social ritual for transitioning from formal to informal address mid-acquaintance, directly
  paralleling Croghan `008` Unit 1's `vi säger väl du till varandra?` but showing the *verb-phrase*
  idiom for the same custom rather than a request-question form.
- `får jag skåla med er?` ("may I toast you?") — a formal toasting-permission formula, distinct from
  the bare `Skål!` exclamation already documented elsewhere.
- `hålla tacktalet` ("to give the thank-you speech") — names a specific structured Swedish dinner-
  party custom: a guest (traditionally a male guest) stands and gives a formal speech of thanks
  partway through the meal, addressing the `värdinna` ("hostess") by name and toasting her — the
  scripted content of Basic Sentences 48-53 enacts this custom in full, including `mina damer och
  herrar` ("ladies and gentlemen") as the speech's opening address and a closing group toast `Skål,
  skål, [hostess's name]!`.
- `värdinna` ("hostess") / implicit `värd` ("host").
- `förtjusande` ("lovely, charming," used of the hostess).
