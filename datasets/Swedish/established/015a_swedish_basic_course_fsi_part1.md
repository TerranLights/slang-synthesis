# Swedish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: FSI Swedish Basic Course, Part 1 (Units 1-8)

**Source:** Foreign Service Institute, U.S. Department of State, *Swedish* (Swedish Basic Course,
Student Text), public-domain government-produced course — Front matter (Preface, To the Student, To
the Teacher, Explanation of Grammatical Terms, Guide to Endings and Symbols), A Guide to Swedish
Pronunciation, and Units 1-8 ("Getting Around" through "Shopping for Clothes"), covering roughly
printed pp. iii-239 of a 16-unit, ~670-page course (this is the largest file in this wave; split into
three lettered parts — `015a` (this file, Units 1-8), `015b` (Units 9-12), `015c` (Units 13-16 +
appendices) — per this project's convention for long chunked sources).

**Rendering note:** vision-only scan, no usable text layer. Rendered via `pdftoppm -png -r 110-120`,
one printed page per PDF page. **Pagination offset confirmed empirically**: PDF page = printed page
number + 26 (verified against both the roman-numeral front matter and the Unit 1 opening, and again
against the grammar index near the end of the book) — used throughout this wave's FSI navigation
rather than assuming a fixed ratio.

**Non-redundant-supplement scope decision:** FSI's course structure (Basic Sentences → Notes on Basic
Sentences → Points to Practice, with numbered grammar points per unit) covers almost exactly the same
grammatical ground as Croghan's `008` and McClean's `009` already in this dataset — en/ett nouns,
present tense, word order, auxiliary verbs, the definite article, personal pronouns, telling time,
indefinite plural endings, the relative pronoun `som`, adverb placement, definite-plural forms,
adjective-noun agreement, the interrogative `vilken`, indefinite pronouns, rest/motion place adverbs,
and the second/third verb conjugations are **all** already documented from other sources. **This file
does not re-tabulate that overlapping grammar.** What it captures instead: (1) FSI's own distinctive
sociolinguistic commentary — notably a much more detailed, dated `du`/`ni` usage note than Croghan's
equivalent; (2) explicit false-friend/usage warnings not found elsewhere; (3) vocabulary items
genuinely not seen in this dataset's other Swedish sources. Given the scale of this single source
(~670 content pages), this file and its two companions are a **representative sampling pass, not
exhaustive page-by-page coverage** — gaps are noted explicitly rather than silently skipped. No
handwritten marginalia was found on any page read; all content read was clearly printed/typeset.

## Distinctive content: Unit 1 sociolinguistic note on du/ni (p. 39, Note 9)

**This is the single most valuable item found in Units 1-8 — a far more detailed and specifically
dated `du`/`ni` usage commentary than Croghan's parallel note (see `008` Unit 1).** FSI states the
informal `du` is "rapidly growing in Sweden and will most likely soon be the only form of addressing
a person"; most young people call everyone `du`; `du` is used between all employees and management in
many factories/offices. The source gives a specific period anecdote as evidence of how far the shift
had progressed while still being notable: **"eyebrows were raised when a young journalist
interviewing the king called His Majesty `du`."** The source's practical advice to a newcomer: be
cautious with conservative/older Swedes and let the Swede indicate their own preferred form of
address. A companion note (on `stå till` vs. `må`, both "to feel/be [well]") states `stå till` is more
formal than `må`, so `Hur står det till?` is the more appropriate greeting for someone one does not
address as `du`.

## Other distinctive notes (Units 1-8)

- **`välkommen` is explicitly flagged as a false friend** (Unit 2, Note 1): never used in the English
  sense of "you're welcome" (in reply to thanks) — that sense instead requires `Ingen orsak` ("no
  reason, don't mention it") or `Tack själv` ("thanks yourself"). `välkommen` is reserved for
  greeting someone's arrival or referencing an upcoming visit/invitation.
- **`om` as a future-time preposition** (Unit 2, Note 8): `om` translates "in" in future time
  expressions answering "when?" — `Om ett par månader` ("In a couple of months").
- **`eller hur?`** — a general-purpose tag question ("isn't that so?/don't you?/aren't you?"),
  appended regardless of the main clause's polarity or verb, functioning like a universal English tag
  question (Unit 8, Basic Sentence 1 & 3) — not previously captured as a named pattern in this
  dataset's other Swedish sources, though individual instances of similar tags may appear elsewhere.
- **Unit 3** (a dialogue about a Swedish naval officer's career) supplies period/occupational
  vocabulary with no clear Croghan/McClean counterpart: `tjänstgöra` ("to serve, be on duty," 4th-
  conj./irregular: `tjänstgör, tjänstgjorde, tjänstgjort`), `fartyg` ("ship," neuter, zero-plural),
  `ombord` ("aboard").
- **Unit 6** is a pure "True and False" listening-comprehension quiz (80 items, answers on pp.
  172-178) recycling Units 1-5's vocabulary with no new content — correctly skipped per the coverage
  rule (pure drill, no new vocabulary/grammar).

## Coverage gaps (Units 1-8)

Units 4-5 and 7 were not individually sampled in this pass beyond their TOC-listed grammar-point
topics (demonstrative expressions, possessives, word order, definite-form-plural, ligga/lägga-type
verb pairs, ordinal numbers) — all of which substantially overlap material already captured from
Croghan `008`. Flagged as a gap for a future pass if FSI's specific dialogue vocabulary for these
units (Unit 4 "Seeing the Sights," Unit 5 "A Place to Live," Unit 7 "At the Office") is wanted in
full; the grammar points themselves are very unlikely to add anything beyond what `008`/`009` already
cover, based on their TOC topic labels.
