# Swedish — Established Vocabulary: *Prismas Engelska Ordbok: Engelsk-Svensk och Svensk-Engelsk*,
Representative Sample

**Source:** *Prismas Engelska Ordbok: Engelsk-Svensk och Svensk-Engelsk* ("Prisma's English
Dictionary: English-Swedish and Swedish-English"), published by Norstedts Ordbok AB / Bokförlaget
Rabén Prisma. Colophon: © 1993, 1997 Norstedts Ordbok AB; "Första upplagan" (1st ed.) 1993,
"Andra upplagan andra tryckningen" (2nd ed., 2nd printing), printed by AiT Norbok, Norway, 1998;
ISBN 91-7227-043-5 (back cover) / 91-7227-043-8 (title-page colophon reads an adjacent digit — both
recorded as seen, not reconciled). Back cover advertises "Ca 85 000 uppslagsord och fraser" (approx.
85,000 headwords and phrases), "Ljudskrift till samtliga engelska uppslagsord" (phonetic
transcription for every English headword), and "Minigrammatik" (a mini-grammar appendix). PDF has
945 pages, no usable text layer (`pdftotext` returns zero bytes on a 20-page test range); vision-read
throughout via 150dpi page renders.

**Structure (confirmed by direct inspection, not assumed from a fixed-offset formula):** front
matter runs PDF pp. 1–21 (printed pp. i–xx, including a 3-page **"Förkortningar/Abbreviations"**
bilingual abbreviation key on PDF pp. 17–19 = printed pp. 16–18); the English-Swedish half
("Engelsk-Svenska delen") runs PDF pp. 22–455 (printed pp. 1–454, A "A 1a)" to "zucchini"); the
Swedish-English half ("Svensk-Engelska delen") has its own independent page numbering restarting at
1, running PDF pp. 459–~929 (printed pp. 1–~472, "aaa"-equivalent opening entries to "övad"); an
**"Engelsk grammatik"** (English Grammar) appendix — a contrastive mini-grammar with parallel
English/Swedish example-sentence pairs — begins by PDF p. 936 (printed p. 479) and runs to near the
book's end, followed by blank endpapers and the back cover at PDF p. 945. This two-level pagination
(continuous within each half, but each half restarting its own count) is a different convention from
the companion Norstedts dictionary already sampled in this project (`established/006`), which uses
one continuous page count across both halves.

**Coverage note — deliberately non-exhaustive sampling, not a full extraction.** Per this project's
convention for dictionaries over ~700 pages, entries were sampled from spread-out page ranges, not
transcribed exhaustively. **Ranges read:** (1) front matter PDF pp. 1–21 in full — cover, title
page, copyright/colophon, "Anvisningar" (user instructions, confirming the abbreviation list's own
location), the complete **Förkortningar/Abbreviations** key (PDF pp. 17–19), and verb-conjugation-
class notes; (2) EN-SV opening page (PDF p. 22 = printed p. 1, "A" through early entries); (3) EN-SV
"W"–"Z" sections, read densely after they proved (consistent with `established/006`'s finding on the
companion dictionary) to be the richest register-tagged stretch in the alphabet (PDF pp. 440, 450,
451, 452, 454, 455 = printed pp. 439, 449, 450, 453, 454); (4) SV-EN "I"/"J"/"K" sections, read in
a connected run specifically to pin down this dictionary's treatment of the `jätte-`/`jäkla`/`jävla`
taboo-intensifier family for direct comparison against `established/006`'s findings (PDF pp. 625,
628–631 = printed pp. 168, 171–174); (5) SV-EN "L" section (PDF p. 670 = printed p. 213); (6) SV-EN
"SK"/"SL"/"ST" sections, including a targeted check on the **skit-** cluster for direct comparison
against the same cluster already sampled in `established/006` (PDF pp. 795–796, 808, 825 = printed
pp. 338–339, 351, 368); (7) SV-EN "Ä"/"Ö" sections and the tail of the dictionary body (PDF pp. 915,
920, 922 = printed pp. 458, 463, 465); (8) the "Engelsk grammatik" appendix opening (PDF p. 936 =
printed p. 479) and the book's final pages (PDF pp. 940–945, confirmed blank endpapers + back
cover). This spans roughly 35 printed pages read across both halves out of ~926 total pages of
dictionary body — a sparse, register-tag-prioritized and comparison-targeted sample, not a census.

**No PDF text layer; vision-read throughout.** Print is sharp, clean, unwatermarked two-column
typeset text on every page inspected — **no handwritten marginalia of any kind was found** on any of
the ~35 pages read, including front matter and the final pages (one page bore a library barcode
sticker and "NBR Depotbiblioteket"/"98sd 14 108" accession-stamp text on the back cover — this is
printed library-processing matter, not handwriting, and was excluded from the vocabulary table as
non-dictionary content). Vision Reading Confidence is marked `plausible_unverified` throughout for
the same reasons given in `established/006`: individual headword readings were not independently
re-encountered a second time, though the register-tag abbreviations themselves were cross-checked
against this dictionary's own Förkortningar key.

**Register-tag taxonomy mapping (this dictionary's own abbreviation system, from its own bilingual
Förkortningar/Abbreviations key, PDF pp. 17–19 = printed pp. 16–18, read in full):** `vard.`
"vardagligt / colloquial[ly]" (by far the most frequent tag, as in `established/006`); `sl.` "slang
/ slang"; `vulg.` "vulgär stil / vulgar style"; `skämts.` "skämtsamt / jocular[ly]"; `neds.`
"nedsättande / derogatory"; `iron.` "ironiskt / ironical[ly]" — **a tag not present in the companion
dictionary's key**, a genuine cross-dictionary taxonomy difference worth flagging; `åld.`
"ålderdomligt, föråldra[d], -t / archaic"; `dial.` "dialektalt / dialectal[ly]"; `poet.` "poetiskt /
poetical[ly]"; `högt.` "högtidligt / formal[ly]" — **also not present in `established/006`'s key**,
a second genuine difference. `barnspr.` (child language) is **not listed in this dictionary's own
alphabetical Förkortningar entries as scanned** (its expected alphabetical slot, between `bank.` and
`BE.`, was not present), yet the tag demonstrably exists and is used in the body text (see `vove`
below, tagged `vard. o. barnspr.`) — flagged as an apparent gap between the printed key and actual
usage, or a key entry this sample's narrower abbreviation-list capture simply missed; not fully
resolved here. Mapped onto this project's `00_Usage_Tier_Taxonomy.md` as: `vard.` → `colloquial`;
`sl.` → `slang`; `vulg.` → `taboo`; `skämts.` → `colloquial.jocular`; `neds.` → `slang.derogatory`;
`iron.` → `colloquial.ironic` (new subcategory — language of origin: Swedish, this dictionary);
`åld.` → `archaic`; `dial.` → `regional`; `poet.` → `literary`; `högt.` → `formal`; `barnspr.` →
`colloquial.child_language` (consistent with `established/006`'s mapping). `AE.`/`BE.` (American
English / British English) are national-variety labels, not register tags, recorded in Attested
Region / Geographic Scope instead.

**Flag — genuine register-tagged content found, including direct cross-dictionary register
disagreements with `established/006` on the *same* headwords.** This is the single most valuable
finding from this sample: sampling the identical `skit`/`skita`/`skitsnack` cluster that
`established/006` found in the companion Norstedts dictionary turned up **different register
judgments for the same lexemes** — see the Grammar points section below for the full comparison.
Everything in the table is the dictionary's own lexicographic register annotation, making it
legitimately Phase 1 `established/` content per this project's content-type boundary, not raw
"in the wild" corpus material.

## Grammar points

### Two independently-compiled dictionaries disagree on register for the identical `skit-` cluster

The single most valuable finding from sampling this dictionary is a direct, headword-for-headword
comparison against `established/006` (the companion Norstedts *Lilla Engelska Ordbok*), made
possible because both samples happened to target the exact same `skit`/`skita`/`skitsnack`/`skitig`
cluster independently (this dictionary's sample re-targeted it deliberately once the overlap became
apparent). The results genuinely disagree:

| Headword | This dictionary (`established/007`) | `established/006` (companion dictionary) |
|---|---|---|
| `skit` (noun, "shit/muck") | `vard.` (colloquial) | untagged (treated as core/neutral) |
| `skita` (verb, "to shit") | `vard.` (colloquial) | `vulg.` (vulgar — the harsher tag) |
| `skitsnack` ("bullshit talk") | `sl.` (slang) | `vard.` (colloquial) |
| `skitig` ("dirty") | `vard.` (colloquial) | `vard.` (colloquial) — **agreement** |

Only one of four compared entries agrees exactly. This is a concrete, source-grounded illustration
of a point this project's own grammar-source methodology already treats as a working principle (see
`grammar_sources_describe_correlations` in project memory): **even a dictionary's own explicit,
formally-numbered register annotation is a lexicographer's judgment call, not a ground-truth
measurement of how taboo a word actually is in use.** Two professionally edited, roughly
contemporaneous (1993/1998 vs. 1998) general dictionaries from the same broad publishing tradition
(both ultimately Norstedts-affiliated) genuinely differ on where `skita` and `skitsnack` sit on the
colloquial-to-vulgar scale. For this project's later mechanics-analysis phase, this means
register-tier claims sourced from a single dictionary should be treated as one data point, not a
settled fact — exactly the cross-source-agreement caution already applied to grammar claims ought to
extend to lexicographic register tags too.

### `jävla` vs. `jäkla`: this dictionary omits the stronger taboo form entirely

`established/006` found `jävla` ("damn/fucking," from `djävul` "devil") only as a cross-reference to
a `djävla` main entry it didn't directly sample. This dictionary's sampled J-section run (printed
pp. 171–174, read as a connected block specifically to chase this comparison) found the adjacent
euphemistic form `jäkla` ("blasted, rotten, damn[ed]") listed as a full headword — but **did not
find `jävla` itself** anywhere in the `jäv-`/`jät-` alphabetical stretch where it should fall (the
dictionary goes directly from `jäva`/`jävig`, a legal-register false cognate meaning "challenge [a
witness]," to `jökel` "glacier," skipping the swear word entirely). Whether this is deliberate
editorial softening (a more conservative register policy than the companion dictionary, which did
at least cross-reference the word) or a coverage gap in this sample's specific read is not resolved
here — but if later Swedish corpus work turns up `jävla` as a high-frequency intensifier (plausible,
given it is one of Swedish's most common taboo-register words), its near-absence from this
dictionary's sampled range versus its presence in the companion dictionary is itself a data point
about how differently two general dictionaries from the same market handle the same taboo item.

### The `jätte-` intensifier prefix: tagged in one dictionary, untagged in the other

`established/006` found `jätte-` ("giant-") explicitly `vard.`-tagged on three separate compounds
(`jättekul`, `jätterolig`, `jättesuccé`). This dictionary's sample found five more `jätte-`
compounds on a single page (`jättebra`, `jättefin`, `jätteglad`, `jätteskön`, `jättetrevlig`) — but
**none carried a visible register abbreviation**, appearing as plain headwords. Since `jätte-` is
unambiguously a colloquial/informal intensifier in actual Swedish usage regardless of whether either
dictionary marks it as such, this is a second concrete example (alongside the `skit-` cluster above)
of two dictionaries drawing their register-marking line in different places for the same productive
derivational pattern — not individually tabulated as a discrepancy table row since the untagged
`jätte-` compounds don't meet this sample's register-tag-priority selection criterion on their own,
but worth flagging here as a pattern.

### A contrastive "Engelsk grammatik" appendix, not present in the companion dictionary

Unlike `established/006`'s source, this dictionary includes a dedicated grammar appendix (back
cover: "Minigrammatik"), confirmed beginning by PDF p. 936 (printed p. 479) and titled "Engelsk
grammatik" at the top of each page. Its format is a two-column contrastive layout: English example
sentences with Swedish translations in the left column, a paraphrased grammatical rule in the right
column explaining the construction (e.g. the `be + to + infinitive` periphrasis for a pre-arranged
future event, `shall` vs. `will` by grammatical person, impersonal "det" translated as `it`/`there`
depending on context). This is a genuinely distinct content type from the two dictionaries' shared
headword-list format and — per the dispatch's instruction to check "any grammar summary the
dictionary includes" — is flagged here as existing and worth a dedicated follow-up extraction pass
if this project ever wants a contrastive-grammar source for Swedish (most of Swedish's existing
`established/` grammar content so far comes from monolingual pedagogical grammars, not an
English-learner's-eye contrastive appendix bundled inside a dictionary).

### Dual compound-tagging recurs in this dictionary too

As in `established/006`, this dictionary stacks tags rather than using a single flat register
vocabulary: `wrath` is tagged `högt. el. åld.` (formal OR archaic) and `yonder` is tagged `åld.,
dial.` (archaic AND dialectal) — both dual-tag patterns directly comparable to the companion
dictionary's `amer. vard.` and `barnspr. el. vard.` compound tags. The `el.` ("or") vs. plain
juxtaposition (implicitly "and") distinction appears to track whether the dictionary considers the
two labels alternative characterizations of the same sense or simultaneously true of it — a
fine-grained distinction worth preserving if either dictionary's full tag corpus is ever mined
systematically.

## Coverage note (summary)

Approximately 35 printed pages sampled across both halves (front matter in full; EN-SV "A" opening
and the dense "W"-"Z" stretch; SV-EN "I"/"J"/"K", "L", "SK"/"SL"/"ST", and "Ä"/"Ö" sections; the
grammar-appendix opening; confirmed-blank final pages) out of roughly 926 total pages of dictionary
body plus grammar appendix. 33 vocabulary rows extracted, weighted toward register-tagged entries
and, in several cases, deliberately re-targeted at headwords `established/006` had already sampled
in the companion dictionary specifically to enable direct register-tag comparison — this produced
the sample's most valuable finding (documented in Grammar points above): two dictionaries from the
same broad publishing tradition give **different register judgments for the same Swedish taboo
vocabulary**, a genuine, source-grounded caution for any later mechanics-analysis work that treats a
single dictionary's register tags as authoritative. No handwritten marginalia found anywhere in the
sampled range (one page of printed library-accession/barcode matter on the back cover was noted and
excluded as non-dictionary content, not marginalia). This dictionary's own complete abbreviation key
was read in full and cross-checked against the companion dictionary's key, surfacing two Swedish-
register tags (`iron.` "ironical," `högt.` "formal") not present in `established/006`'s key, and one
apparent gap (`barnspr.` used in the body text but not located in this sample's capture of the
printed key). A follow-up pass specifically hunting for `djävla`/`jävla`'s full entry (cross-
referenced but not directly read in `established/006`, and apparently absent as a headword in this
dictionary's sampled range) remains the most promising specific target flagged across both
Wave 2 dictionary samples.
