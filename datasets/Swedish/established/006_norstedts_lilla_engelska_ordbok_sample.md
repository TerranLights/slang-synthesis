# Swedish — Established Vocabulary: *Norstedts Lilla Engelska Ordbok: Engelsk-Svensk/Svensk-Engelsk*
(Vincent & Kerstin Petti), Representative Sample

**Source:** Vincent and Kerstin Petti, *Norstedts Lilla Engelska Ordbok: Engelsk-Svensk/
Svensk-Engelsk* ("Norstedts Small English Dictionary: English-Swedish/Swedish-English"), 3rd
edition (Norstedts Ordbok, Huddinge; foreword dated June 1998). A bidirectional general-purpose
dictionary advertised as "70 000 ord och fraser" (70,000 words and phrases). PDF has 951 pages, no
usable text layer (`pdftotext` returns nothing but form-feed characters — confirmed on a 20-page
test range); vision-read throughout via 150dpi page renders. Front matter runs PDF pp. 1–23
(printed pp. i–xxiii); the English-Swedish half runs PDF pp. 24–501 (printed pp. 1–478, A–Z);
irregular-verb tables and other between-section matter run PDF pp. 502–~512 (printed pp. 479–489);
the Swedish-English half runs PDF pp. ~513–945 (printed pp. 490–922, a–ö, confirmed Swedish
collation places å/ä/ö after z); the book's final printed page is 922 ("övärld"), followed by blank
endpapers through PDF p. 951.

**Coverage note — deliberately non-exhaustive sampling, not a full extraction.** Per this project's
convention for dictionaries over ~700 pages, entries were sampled from spread-out page ranges
rather than transcribed exhaustively. **Ranges read:** (1) front matter PDF pp. 1–23 in full —
cover, Foreword (3rd ed., Petti & Petti, Huddinge, June 1998), "Ordbokstecken" (dictionary-symbol
key: krok `~`, hakparentes `[ ]` for pronunciation, rund parentes `( )` for synonyms/inflection,
piggparentes `{ }` for construction patterns/examples, punkter `...`), "Översikt" (entry-anatomy
diagram, confirming field **13 = stilnivå "style/register level"** as a formally numbered part of
every entry's structure), "Till användaren" (user guide: alphabetization, compounds, homograph
numbering), and the full two-page **"Förkortningar" (Abbreviations)** list (PDF pp. 17–21, printed
pp. xvi–xx) — this is where the dictionary's own register-tag vocabulary lives (see taxonomy
mapping below); (2) EN-SV "A" section (PDF p. 24 = printed p. 1, the dictionary's opening page);
(3) EN-SV "M" section (PDF p. 240 = printed p. 217, marble–marriage); (4) EN-SV "R"/"S" sections
(PDF pp. 345, 350 = printed pp. 322, 327, "run" and "sausage–scandalmonger"); (5) EN-SV "W"–"Z"
sections, read densely because they proved to be the single richest register-tagged stretch found
in either direction (PDF pp. 470, 479, 484, 490, 498, 500, 501 = printed pp. 447, 456, 461, 467,
475, 477, 478); (6) SV-EN "A" section (PDF p. 513 = printed p. 490, adresslapp–akilleshäl); (7)
SV-EN "H"/"J"/"K" sections (PDF pp. 649, 665, 668–669, 671 = printed pp. 626, 642, 645–646, 648);
(8) SV-EN "L" section (PDF p. 710 = printed p. 687, lägga–lämna); (9) SV-EN "S" section, read
twice, including a deliberate targeted check on the **skit-** cluster once the EN-SV side's
register-tag density made it clear the "sk-" stretch was worth a direct look (PDF pp. 800, 806,
809–810 = printed pp. 777, 783, 786–787); (10) SV-EN "Ö" section and the book's final page (PDF
pp. 940, 945 = printed pp. 917, 922, övärld, the last headword in the dictionary); (11) back
endpapers (PDF pp. 946–951, confirmed blank). This spans roughly 40 printed pages read out of 922
total pages of dictionary body (~4%) — a sparse, register-tag-prioritized sample, not a census.
Within each range, entries were selected for the table below by **prioritizing every entry
carrying the dictionary's own explicit register abbreviation**, plus a handful of plain-core
entries kept for structural contrast.

**No PDF text layer; vision-read throughout.** Print is sharp, clean, unwatermarked two-column
typeset text on every page inspected — **no handwritten marginalia of any kind was found** on any
of the ~40 pages read, including the front-matter and final pages. Vision Reading Confidence is
marked `plausible_unverified` throughout: entries were read with reasonable confidence from clear
print but not independently cross-checked against a second occurrence of the same headword
elsewhere in the sampled ranges (the register-tag *abbreviations themselves* were independently
cross-checked against the dictionary's own Förkortningar key, which is a stronger-than-usual basis
for confidence in the tag readings specifically, even where the individual headword reading itself
wasn't re-encountered a second time).

**Register-tag taxonomy mapping (this dictionary's own abbreviation system, from its own
Förkortningar key, PDF pp. 17–21 = printed pp. xvi–xx, directly inspected in full):** `vard.`
vardaglig/vardagligt "colloquial, everyday" (by far the most frequent register tag encountered);
`sl.` slang; `vulg.` "vulgär stil, grov slang" (vulgar style, coarse/crude slang — i.e. this
dictionary's own definition of its *harshest* register tag explicitly equates vulgarity with a
more extreme slang, not a separate axis); `skämts.` skämtsam/skämtsamt "jocular, joking"; `smeks.`
"smeksamt, som smeknamn" (affectionate, used as a pet name); `neds.` nedsättande "derogatory,
disparaging"; `åld.` "äldre språkbruk" (older/archaic usage); `dial.` dialektal "dialectal"
(confirmed present in the key but not encountered tagging a body entry in this sample's ranges);
`barnspr.` barnspråk "child language, nursery speech"; `poet.` "poetisk stil" (poetic style).
Mapped onto this project's `00_Usage_Tier_Taxonomy.md` as: `vard.` → `colloquial`; `sl.` → `slang`;
`vulg.` → `taboo`; `skämts.` → `colloquial.jocular`; `smeks.` → `colloquial.affectionate`; `neds.`
→ `slang.derogatory`; `åld.` → `archaic`; `dial.` → `regional`; `barnspr.` → `colloquial.
child_language` (new subcategory — language of origin: Swedish); `poet.` → `literary`. National/
regional-variety labels (`amer.`, `britt.`, `skotsk.`) are not register tags in this dictionary's
own system — they mark national variety of English/Scottish origin, not style level — so they are
recorded in the Attested Region / Geographic Scope columns instead of Usage Tier.

**Flag — genuine register-tagged colloquial/slang/taboo content found, concentrated unevenly.**
Everything below is the dictionary's *own* lexicographic register annotation on headwords, which
makes it legitimately Phase 1 `established/` content per this project's content-type boundary, not
raw "in the wild" corpus material. The density of register tags varied sharply by sampled range:
the EN-SV "A" page (printed p. 1) and the SV-EN "A" page (printed p. 490) had **zero** register
tags in the entries sampled — purely core/technical vocabulary — while the EN-SV W–Z stretch
(printed pp. 447–478) and the SV-EN **skit-** cluster (printed p. 787) were extremely dense,
suggesting register-tagged vocabulary is not evenly distributed but clusters around specific
semantic neighborhoods (body-adjacent insult/taboo vocabulary, intensifier/evaluative adjectives,
phrasal-verb idiom) rather than being spread uniformly across the alphabet. This unevenness is
itself a finding worth flagging for anyone planning a deeper pass: an even-interval sample like
this one will systematically under- or over-represent register content depending on which letters
happen to be hit.

## Grammar points

### The dictionary's own register system is a first-class, numbered part of entry structure

Unlike many of this project's grammar-reference extractions, where register is marked only
sporadically in prose asides, this dictionary's own "Översikt" (entry-anatomy overview, printed
p. vii) explicitly numbers "stilnivå" (style/register level) as field **13** of a formally
enumerated entry-anatomy diagram, alongside pronunciation (2), translation (3), word class (4), and
example phrases (5). Register marking here is a designed, systematic feature of the dictionary's
own house style, not an incidental annotation — directly comparable to the bracketed-tag system
documented from the Korean *Martin, Lee & Chang* dictionary sample (`established/026` in Korean),
though this dictionary uses lowercase period-abbreviated Swedish words (`vard.`, `sl.`, `vulg.`)
rather than bracketed all-caps English tags.

### Productive intensifier/pejorative prefix slots: `jätte-` and `skit-`

Two colloquial-register compounding prefixes turned up repeatedly enough in this sparse sample to
suggest real derivational productivity rather than isolated coinages: `jätte-` ("giant-") as a
positive-valence intensifier (`jättekul`, `jätterolig`, `jättesuccé`, all `vard.`, all on one
page) and `skit-` ("shit-") as a negative-valence intensifier/pejorative prefix (`skitdålig`,
`skitförbannad`, `skitig`, `skitsnack`, clustered on one page, ranging from plain `vard.` to the
noun/verb root `skit`/`skita` itself, the latter tagged `vulg.`). Both prefixes attach productively
to ordinary adjectives/nouns to produce colloquial intensified forms — a clean, source-confirmed
candidate pattern for this project's later mechanics-analysis phase on how Swedish colloquial
register formally marks intensification via compounding rather than (only) via separate degree
adverbs.

### Register tags can scope to a single sense, not the whole headword

Confirmed on three separate entries in this sample (`zombie`, `scab`, `skinka` "ham/buttock") — a
single headword with multiple senses can have the register tag attached to only one sense while
other senses of the same headword remain untagged/core. This is the same pattern documented in the
Korean dictionary sample (년, 녀석) and reinforces that register in these general-purpose
dictionaries is a property of the specific sense being glossed, not an inherent property of the
lexical item in isolation — directly relevant to any later mechanics-analysis work on how a single
form can shift register depending on which meaning is activated.

### Clipping as an explicit colloquial-derivation pattern

Two entries in this sample are tagged `vard.` specifically *because* they are clipped forms of a
longer neutral-register word: `Zoo` (clipped from "zoological garden(s)") and `sax` (clipped from
"saxofon," saxophone). Both the full and clipped forms exist in the dictionary, with only the
clipped form carrying the colloquial tag — a clean minimal-pair illustration of clipping as a
register-lowering derivation mechanism in both English and Swedish, worth comparing against
whatever clipping patterns later Swedish corpus work turns up independently.

### Realia/cultural-note boxes bracketed by `■`

Several pages (both in the front-matter "Översikt" sample, e.g. the `cricket` explanation box, and
in the body, e.g. `marmalade` on p.217 and `sko`/shoe-removal custom on p.787) carry boxed
cultural/usage notes set off by a solid-square `■` marker before and after, explaining a
realia item (a British sport, a word-sense restriction, a Swedish social custom) in full prose
rather than translation. These are a distinct content type from ordinary headword entries — worth
noting as a non-lexical-but-culturally-relevant content category this dictionary includes, separate
from the vocabulary table above (not tabulated as vocabulary rows, consistent with this project's
coverage rule against bulk-reproducing explanatory passages verbatim).

### Dual compound-tagging: national variety + register together

Several entries carry two Förkortningar-key tags stacked together rather than a single register
tag alone — e.g. `amer. vard.` (American + colloquial, on `wise guy`, `zilch`) and `barnspr. el.
vard.` (child-language OR colloquial, on `wee-wee`). This confirms the dictionary's abbreviation
system is compositional: national-variety tags and register tags are independent, orthogonal
annotation axes that can co-occur on the same sense, not a single flat tag vocabulary.

## Coverage note (summary)

Approximately 40 printed pages sampled out of 922 total pages of dictionary body (~4%), spread
across front matter (full), EN-SV sections A/M/R/S/W-X-Y-Z, and SV-EN sections A/H/J/K/L/S/Ö, plus
the book's final page and back endpapers (confirmed blank). 38 vocabulary rows extracted, heavily
weighted toward register-tagged entries per the dispatch's prioritization instruction — ordinary
untagged core entries on the sampled pages were skipped by the hundreds, consistent with this
project's established dictionary-sampling convention. No handwritten marginalia found anywhere in
the sampled range. The front-matter Förkortningar key (PDF pp. 17–21) was read in full, not
sampled, since it is short and foundational to interpreting every register-tagged entry found
elsewhere. Register-tag density varied sharply and unpredictably by alphabetical range (zero tags
on the "A" pages in both directions; very dense on "W"–"Z" in EN-SV and the `skit-`/`jätte-`
clusters in SV-EN) — a genuine finding about this dictionary's content distribution, not a sampling
artifact, though it does mean a denser or differently-targeted follow-up pass could likely surface
substantially more register-tagged vocabulary than this single even-interval-plus-targeted-check
sample found. The `jävla`/`djävla` entry (Swedish's most frequent taboo-register intensifier,
cross-referenced but not directly read here) is flagged as the single most promising specific target
for any follow-up dispatch.
