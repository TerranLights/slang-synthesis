# Serbian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Units 4-6 ("Ordering a drink," "Ordering food," "Going shopping")

**Source:** David Norris & Vladislava Ribnikar, *Teach Yourself Serbian* (Hodder Headline, 2003),
printed pages 33-70 (Units 4, 5, 6). PDF pages 25-44 of the scanned copy (two printed pages per
image spread; empirically verified — PDF page 25 shows printed pp. 32/33 with the Unit 4 title
page on the right-hand side, PDF page 44 shows printed pp. 70/71 with the Unit 7 title page
beginning on the right-hand side, confirming the assigned range's boundaries exactly).

**Coverage note:** This source has a garbled/unusable OCR text layer and was vision-read from
scanned page images throughout (no `pdftotext` used). Every grammar point and every distinct
vocabulary item from Units 4-6 is captured below; repeated drill/practice exercises that recycled
already-captured vocabulary and grammar without introducing anything new were skipped per the
coverage rule (e.g. most of the numbered "Practice" exercises at the end of each unit, which drill
already-tabulated vocabulary/case endings). This is the first *Teach Yourself Serbian* extraction
in `established/` for this project (no prior TYS chunks exist to cross-check against), and this
book is treated as independent of other Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian sources already extracted — no
redundancy-supplement narrowing was applied.

**Vision-reading guard:** No handwritten marginalia (pencil answers, underlines, previous-owner
notes) was found on any page in this range — all content read is genuinely typeset/printed. One
page (PDF p. 25 / printed p. 32) shows a *printed reproduction of the book's own handwritten-Cyrillic
teaching font* used deliberately in an exercise ("Здраво, Весна! Како си? Ја сам добро...") — this
is the book's own typeset facsimile of Serbian handwritten script (a pedagogical device, not a
reader's marginalia) and was excluded from the vocabulary table as it duplicates dialogue already
captured elsewhere in printed form, but is noted here for completeness.

## Grammar points

### Genitive case after "one" and "two"/"three"/"four"; genitive plural after 5+

The number "one" (један) is a true adjective agreeing in gender (masc. сок, fem. једна кафа →
acc. једну кафу, neut. једно пиво). "Two" (два masc./neut., две fem.), "three" (три) and "four"
(четири) are followed by the noun in the **genitive singular** (два сока, три кафе). Numbers
**5 upward (пет-двадесет)** are followed instead by the **genitive plural** (пет сендвича, осам
кафа). Which case a compound number above 20 requires depends on its *last* word: a number ending
in "one" (двадесет један) behaves like један and is followed by nom. singular agreeing with the
noun; one ending in "two/three/four" takes gen. singular; anything else (5-20, or numbers ending
in 5-9) takes gen. plural. p. 38-39, 50.

### Accusative case for direct objects

Serbian marks the direct object of a verb with the accusative case rather than relying on word
order alone (the way English distinguishes *he/him*, *she/her*). Masc. nouns are usually unchanged
in acc. singular, fem. nouns change final -а to -у, neut. nouns are unchanged. Applied throughout
the drink-ordering dialogues (е.g. "Ја бих једну кафу"). p. 37-38.

### Nominative plural formation and the extended monosyllabic stem

Regular nominative plural pattern: masc. add -и (сендвич → сендвичи), fem. -а → -е (кафа → кафе),
neut. -о → -а (пиво → пива). A subgroup of one-syllable masc. nouns (e.g. сок) lengthen their stem
with -ов- before adding the plural ending in *all* plural cases: сок → сокови (nom.), сокова
(gen.). Not every monosyllabic masc. noun does this — дан "day" simply pluralizes as дани. p. 48,
50.

### Numbers 5-100, and above 100

5-10: пет, шест, седам, осам, девет, десет. 11-19 are formed with -наест (једанаест...
деветнаест). Tens 20-90 (двадесет...деведесет) combine with units the same way English does
(21 = двадесет један). The same additive principle continues after сто "100" (125 = сто двадесет
пет). Above 100: двеста (200), триста (300), четиристо (400), петсто (500), шестсто (600),
седамсто (700), осамсто (800), деветсто (900), хиљада (1,000, often used in its accusative form
хиљаду when counting; takes gen. sg. or gen. pl. depending on the preceding number, same as
милион "million"). p. 39, 63.

### Types of regular verbs: the a/e/i groups

Nearly all regular Serbian verbs fall into one of three groups by the vowel used in their present-
tense endings: the **a-group** (like имам "I have"), the **e-group** (like пијем "I drink"), and
the **i-group** (like учим "I learn"). Each has six person-endings (three singular, three
plural); the plural endings differ slightly per group but the 1st-person-plural marker is always
-мо (имамо, пијемо, учимо — "we have/drink/learn"). Knowing a verb's infinitive plus its 1st
person singular present lets you generate every other form. p. 50-51, 60-61.

### Instrumental case with the preposition "sa" ("with"), and the soft-consonant spelling rule

The preposition "sa" (with) governs the instrumental case. Masc. nouns add -ом (сир → сиром),
fem. -а → -ом (шунка → шунком), neut. -о → -ом (месо → месом); neut. nouns ending in -е change to
-ем (позориште → позориштем). A **spelling rule** requires -ем instead of -ом after any of the
"soft" consonants ц, ч, ћ, џ, ђ, ј, љ, њ, ш, ж — for both masc. and neut. nouns/adjectives
(сендвич → сендвичем, чај → чајем) — but this rule does NOT apply to feminine endings (чаша →
чашом, not *чашем). p. 51.

### Third-person pronouns and mixed-gender plural default

Serbian has three 3sg pronouns by gender (он masc., она fem., оно neut. — all glossable as "he/
she/it") and three 3pl pronouns (они masc., оне fem., она neut.). When referring to a mixed-gender
group of people or nouns of different genders, the **masculine plural они is the default**, the
same convention this course itself uses for brevity in its own grammar explanations. p. 61-62.

### Demonstratives "this" and "that"

овај/ова/ово ("this," masc./fem./neut.) and тај/та/то ("that," masc./fem./neut.) are true
adjectives agreeing with the noun's gender: овај план "this plan," ова разгледница "this
postcard." p. 62.

### Nominative and accusative of adjectives (singular and plural)

Adjectives (model: добар "good") follow noun-like patterns across nom./acc., singular/plural,
and all three genders. Full paradigm tabulated by the source: nom. sg. добар/добра/добро, acc.
sg. добар/добру/добро; nom. pl. добри/добре/добра, acc. pl. добре/добре/добра. p. 63.

### Accusative plural

Regular acc. plural pattern for the drink/food-shopping vocabulary set: masc. -и → -е (сендвичи →
сендвиче), fem. and neut. plural forms are unchanged from nominative plural (кафе stays кафе,
пива stays пива). p. 62.

### The infinitive, and Serbian's strong preference for the да-construction

The Serbian infinitive is a single word usually ending in -ти (имати, учити) or -ћи (моћи). It is
used as the dictionary citation form, but Serbian **generally avoids using the infinitive in
actual speech**, preferring instead a construction with **да** ("that") + the present tense of the
verb, matching the person of the main verb: "Хоћу да купим..." ("I want that I buy..." = "I want
to buy...") rather than the grammatically possible but dispreferred "Хоћу купити...". This
construction is also used to say "I want you to do something": "Ја хоћу да ти учиш српски" (lit.
"I want that you learn Serbian"). This is flagged as a real register/preference distinction, not
just an optional stylistic variant — the да-construction is described as "generally preferred."
p. 67-68.

### Irregular verbs хоћу (want) and могу (can/able), and their one-word negatives

хоћу and могу are the two irregular verbs introduced in this unit (infinitives хтети, моћи), each
with a full six-person present-tense paradigm (хоћу/хоћеш/хоће/хоћемо/хоћете/хоће; могу/можеш/
може/можемо/можете/могу). Most Serbian verbs negate by simply prefixing не (не говорим "I don't
speak"), but **three verbs form their negative as a single fused word** rather than не + verb:
ја сам → нисам ("I am not"), хоћу → нећу ("I don't want," full paradigm нећу/нећеш/неће/нећемо/
нећете/неће), and имам → немам ("I don't have," full paradigm немам/немаш/нема/немамо/немате/
немају). p. 42, 68-69.

### Consonant-final feminine noun subgroup (ствар-type declension)

A subgroup of feminine nouns end in a consonant rather than -а, with their own separate declension
mostly in -и: ствар "thing" — nom./acc. sg. ствар, gen./ins. sg. ствари (with an alternative ins.
form ствaрjу), and nom./acc./gen. pl. all ствари. p. 69.
