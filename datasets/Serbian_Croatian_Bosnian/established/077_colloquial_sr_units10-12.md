# Serbian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Units 10-12 ("Putovanje autobusom" / "Porodični odnosi" / "Seoski život")

**Source:** Celia Hawkesworth with Jelena Ćalić, *Colloquial Serbian: The Complete Course for
Beginners* (Routledge, 2006). Units 10-12, printed pages 132-176 (PDF pages 71-93 of
`Colloquial Serbian.pdf`). Unit 10 = "Putovanje autobusom" (Bus journey, printed pp. 132-145);
Unit 11 = "Porodični odnosi" (Family relationships, printed pp. 146-162); Unit 12 = "Seoski život"
(Village life, printed pp. 162-176). This edition is Serbian-only and uses Cyrillic script
throughout the dialogue/vocabulary text (Latin transliteration given here per the project's
standard practice).

**Page-mapping note:** this PDF's page images are two-printed-page spreads (one PDF page =
two printed pages), so the file's stated "PDF pages 71-93" corresponds to 23 rendered images
covering printed pages 132-177. Boundary verified visually: PDF page 71 shows the Unit 10 title
page (printed 132/133); PDF page 93 shows printed page 176 (end of Unit 12 vocabulary) opposite
printed page 177 (Unit 13 "Muzika" title page) — confirming Units 10-12 end exactly at printed
page 176.

**Vision-reading note:** this source has no usable text layer and was read entirely by vision
(scanned book, `pdftoppm -r 150`). Every page in the range was checked for handwritten marginalia
(pencil/pen annotations, underlines, filled-in exercise answers) distinct from the book's own
typeset print. **No handwritten marginalia was found anywhere in this page range** — all pages
were clean, consistently-typeset printed source material (Latin-script main text with occasional
Cyrillic-script dialogue passages, both clearly print). Vision Reading Confidence is `verified`
where a term recurs elsewhere on the page or spread (e.g. in both a vocabulary box and the
dialogue/reading text) and `plausible_unverified` otherwise, as noted per-row; no `low_confidence`
rows were needed — print was consistently legible throughout.

**Non-redundant supplement note (per project pattern):** this book's own acknowledgements state
it "grew out of" *Colloquial Croatian and Serbian* (Hawkesworth, 1998), whose Units 10-12 are
already extracted in `established/068_colloquial_hr_sr_units10-12.md`. However, **the unit topics
in the two books do not align 1:1** — 068 covers "Obiteljski odnosi" (Family relationships),
"Putni planovi" (Travel plans), and "Na pošti" (At the post office); this book's Units 10 and 12
("Bus journey" and "Village life") have **no topical analog at all** in 068 and are extracted here
in full as genuinely new content. Only **Unit 11 ("Porodični odnosi" / Family relationships)**
overlaps in topic with 068's Unit 10. For that unit specifically:
- **Skipped as redundant:** the full personal-pronoun declension paradigm (ja/ti/on/ona/oni etc.,
  short/long forms across genitive-accusative/dative/locative/instrumental) — already fully
  tabulated in 068's "Declension of personal pronouns" grammar point, including the same "ju"
  alternative-accusative note. Also skipped: the enclitic word-order formula (li-ve-D,A/G-se-je)
  and the dative-of-pronouns-for-possession pattern ("On mi je brat") — both already captured
  verbatim-equivalent in 068.
- **Kept as genuinely new:** this book's family-tree diagram and vocabulary distinguish **strina**
  (paternal uncle's wife) from **ujna** (maternal uncle's wife) as separate lexemes, and explicitly
  notes **tetka**'s husband is **teča** — a fuller kinship-precision system than 068 captured (068's
  Croatian-dialect source used only "teta" without this stric/strina/ujak/ujna four-way split).
  This is flagged below as deeper/new content per the coverage rule.
- Unit 12's future-tense (**hteti**) paradigm table (full short/long/interrogative/negative/
  negative-interrogative columns) is a fuller, more systematic tabulation than 068's prose-style
  future-tense treatment (which came from Unit 12 of the *other* book, on a different topic) — kept
  as a deeper paradigm table per the coverage rule, with a note on where it overlaps.

**Coverage note:** every distinct vocabulary item from dialogues, explicit vocabulary boxes, and
reading passages ("Kulturni život u Beogradu" 10-12, "Ekipa" 10-12) in this range is captured.
Drill/translation exercises were skipped per the coverage rule — they recombine vocabulary already
captured rather than introducing new items, except where an exercise's own footnote glossed a new
word not otherwise listed (a few such footnoted glosses are included below, flagged in Notes).
This book is explicitly framed as "colloquial," so register/informality notes and the book's own
recurring hedging/discourse particles (**valjda**, **ajde/hajde**, **nema veze**) are called out
explicitly. A genuine explicit dialectal contrast was found and is flagged in Grammar points: the
recurring "Ekipa" reading-passage dialogue voices a Bosnian character (Sanja) using visibly
**ijekavian** forms (svijeta, uvijek, mjeseci) against the surrounding **ekavian** Serbian-standard
narration — a real within-book register/dialect juxtaposition, not previously captured from this
book's earlier units.

## Grammar points

### Formation of the past (perfect) tense: the active past participle

Pages 134-138. Serbian uses a compound perfect tense: present tense of **biti** + active past
participle. For the large majority of verbs (infinitive in **-ti**), remove **-ti** and add
gender/number endings: masc. sg. **-o**, fem. sg. **-la**, neut. sg. **-lo**; masc. pl. **-li**,
fem. pl. **-le**, neut. pl. **-la** (e.g. **spavati** → spavao/spavala/spavalo/spavali/spavale/
spavala). Verbs with infinitive in **-sti** drop the **s** (jesti → jeo/jela/jelo). Verbs with
infinitive in **-ći** (and all compounds of **ići**) form the participle irregularly and must be
learned case by case (stići → stigao; moći → mogao; reći → rekao; ići → išao; doći → došao;
ući → ušao). **biti**'s own past participle is regular (bio/bila/bilo/bili).

### Word order rules for past-tense enclitics (largely redundant with 068)

Pages 138-139, 156-157. Because the short forms of **biti** are enclitics, they must sit
immediately after the first stressed word/phrase (**Ja sam došla**) or, if no subject pronoun is
used, immediately after the participle (**Došli smo**). This chunk's fuller enclitic-cluster
formula — **li (interrogative) – ve (verbal enclitic) – D, A/G (pronoun clitics) – se – je** — is
the same rule and the same mnemonic formula already fully captured in
`068_colloquial_hr_sr_units10-12.md`'s "Word order rule for enclitic clusters" grammar point; not
re-extracted here beyond this brief confirmation that this Serbian-only source states it
identically.

### Past-tense questions and negation

Pages 138-139. Two ways of forming a past-tense yes/no question: (1) verb + **li** (**Jesi li
bila umorna?**), which requires the **long** form of **biti** when the verb *is* biti itself; (2)
**Da li** + verb, in which case the **short** form of the auxiliary follows **da li** (**Da li
ste spavali?**). Negation of the past tense is straightforward: negative **biti** (nisam, nisi,
nije...) + participle, with normal (non-enclitic) word order since the negative auxiliary is not
itself an enclitic (**Nisam zvao**; **Zar niste išli?**).

### Present tense to express English present perfect

Page 141. Serbian uses the simple present tense where English would use the present perfect for
an action begun in the past and continuing into the present: **U Beogradu sam već tri nedelje**
("I have been in Belgrade for three weeks already," lit. "I am in Belgrade already three weeks");
**Koliko dugo uči jezik?** ("How long has he been studying the language?").

### Kinship-precision vocabulary (Unit 11 family tree) — deeper than 068

Pages 146-148. This book's family-tree diagram and accompanying vocabulary distinguish
**strina** (father's brother's wife) from **ujna** (mother's brother's wife) as separate lexemes,
alongside the already-known **stric** (paternal uncle) / **ujak** (maternal uncle) pair. It further
notes explicitly that the *usual*, unmarked word for "aunt" in everyday speech is simply **tetka**
(father's or mother's sister), whose husband is **teča** — a fourth kinship-in-law term. 068's
source (a different, Croatian-dialect book) used only "teta" without this four-way stric/strina/
ujak/ujna precision system, so this constitutes a genuine deepening of the kinship-terminology
data already on file, per the coverage rule's "non-redundant supplement" guidance.

### The future tense (hteti + infinitive) — fuller paradigm table than 068

Pages 165-167, 170-172. Formed from infinitive (or **da** + present) + a new auxiliary **hteti**
("to want"), which — like **biti** — has a short/enclitic form (ću, ćeš, će, ćemo, ćete, će) and a
long form (hoću, hoćeš, hoće, hoćemo, hoćete, hoće), used when the auxiliary would otherwise carry
stress (answering a bare yes/no question, or for emphasis). This chunk gives markedly fuller
tabulation than 068's prose-style future-tense section: separate columns for **Affirmative**,
**Interrogative** (Xoće li... / Da li će...), **Negative** (Neće da...), and **Negative
interrogative** (Nećete li... / Zar nećete...) are laid out systematically with worked examples for
every person, which 068 did not tabulate this exhaustively. As in 068's already-flagged Croatian/
Serbian orthographic note, this source confirms independently (writing in Serbian throughout) that
with **-ti**-infinitives the final **-i** drops and infinitive+auxiliary fuse into one word when the
auxiliary follows with no preceding "prop" word: **biću, spavaćeš, videćemo** (vs. **ja ću biti, ti
ćeš spavati** when a pronoun precedes) — the same Serbian-standard fusion rule 068 already
documented, now attested from a purely Serbian (not comparative) source.

### Vocabulary building: animals and animal-simile idioms

Page 166. A basic domestic/wild-animal vocabulary set (pas, mačka, konj, magarac, tele, koza,
svinja, vo, kokoška, pile, guska, vuk, lav, tigar) is immediately followed by six fixed
animal-comparison similes — **umoran kao pas** (tired as a dog), **gladan kao vuk** (hungry as a
wolf), **tvrdoglav kao magarac** (stubborn as a donkey), **prljav kao svinja** (dirty as a pig),
**glup kao vo** / **glupa kao guska** (stupid as an ox / as a goose, gender-agreeing) — presented by
the book itself as a "work out what they mean" pedagogical exercise. These are genuine fixed
colloquial-register figurative expressions (predicate-adjective + kao + animal), a productive
simile template worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase as a real source of Serbian
figurative/slang-adjacent construction.

### Explicit ekavian/ijekavian dialectal contrast within the "Ekipa" reading passages

Pages 161, 175. The book's recurring "Ekipa" reading-passage dialogues (a running story thread
across units, independent of the main Dialogue 1-3 sequence) are narrated in standard ekavian
Serbian, but character Sanja's dialogue in the Unit 12 installment (p.175) uses visibly
**ijekavian** forms — **svijeta** (gen. of "svet/svijet," world), **uvijek** (always, vs. narration's
"uvek"), **mjeseci** (months, vs. "meseci") — consistent with a Bosnian-inflected voice for that
character. The book's own Unit 12 vocabulary entry for "always" is even given as the explicit pair
**uvijek/uvek**, acknowledging both forms side by side. This is a genuine, source-embedded
ekavian/ijekavian contrast used as a characterization device — worth flagging as register/dialect
content distinct from anything captured in 068, whose extracted units did not include this
recurring character thread.
