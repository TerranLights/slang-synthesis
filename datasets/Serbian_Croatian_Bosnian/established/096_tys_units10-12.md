## Grammar points

### Kinship register: тата/мама vs. отац/мајка, and баба/бака

The source draws an explicit register distinction within family vocabulary (p.123): **тата**
"dad" and **мама** "mum" are described as "commonly used by people of all ages," while **отац**
"father" and **мајка** "mother" are "somewhat more formal." Separately, **супруг/супруга**
"husband/wife" are marked as outright formal — "formal terms which you may find on official
documents rather than in everyday speech, rather like the English word *spouse*." The book also
gives **баба** as a regional/colloquial alternative spelling to the standard **бака**
"grandmother." This is a clean example of a teaching grammar directly marking a formal/informal
register split within one semantic field, exactly the kind of annotation the coverage rule
prioritizes.

### Vocative case wrap-up and spelling rule (carried over from p.94–95, immediately preceding Unit 9/10 boundary)

Not part of Units 10–12 proper but visible on the boundary page (p.94, Unit 8 end): masc. names/
nouns in the voc. singular undergo a spelling shift г→ж, к→ч, х→ш (e.g. **Предраг → Предраже**,
**Вук → Вуче**). Noted here only because it was encountered while locating the unit boundary; not
double-counted as Unit 10 content.

### Dative case (p.132–133)

The dative is the last of Serbian's seven cases, used after certain prepositions and for the
indirect object ("to/for" a verb). Its endings in the singular are identical to the locative:
masc. add **-у** (брат → брату), fem. **-а** → **-и** (сестра → сестри), neut. **-о/-е** → **-у**
(дете → детету). The usual к/г/х → ц/з/с spelling rule applies before **-и** (мајка → мајци).

### Dative, instrumental and locative plurals (p.133)

All three cases share identical plural endings across genders: masc./fem. add **-има**
(родитељ → родитељима, сестра → сестрама), neut. **-та** → **-има** (позориште → позориштима).

### Noun subgroups: тата and дете (p.124–125)

Some masc. nouns denoting people end in **-a** (тата, Влада — a man's name, short for
Владимир/Владислав); they decline like fem. nouns but all agreement (adjectives, verbs) stays
masculine: "Он је мој тата." Separately, дете "child" belongs to a small neut. subgroup that
inserts an extra syllable **-ет-** before case endings (gen. детета, ins. дететом, loc. детету);
дрво "tree" (gen. дрвета) follows the same pattern.

### Mixed noun agreements / omitting possessive adjectives (p.125)

When one adjective modifies two nouns of different genders, it takes masc. endings ("твоји мама и
тата"). Serbian also omits possessive adjectives more readily than English when the referent is
clear from context ("Дејан више не живи са родитељима" = "Dejan no longer lives with his
parents" — no word for "his" needed).

### Adverbs in time phrases: више не, још, још не, већ (p.126)

These four combine with present or past tense for different nuances: **више не** "no longer" (+
present, or + past for "no longer did X"), **још увек** "still," **још не** "not yet," **већ**
"already." Notably, where English uses present perfect continuous ("Maja has been studying English
for two years"), Serbian uses simple present with већ ("Маја већ две године студира енглески").

### Adjectives formed from personal names (p.126–127)

Man's name ending in a consonant or -о: add **-ов** (or **-ев** after a soft consonant) —
Дejaн → Дejaнов, Марко → Марков, Милош → Милошев. Name (masc. or fem.) ending in **-a**: add
**-ин** — Маja → Мajин, Весна → Веснин, Влада → Владин. The same pattern extends to kinship
nouns: мама → мамин, тата → татин.

### Flats and houses (p.132) — cultural/lexical note

Flat sizes in Serbian cities are named by total room count excluding bathroom/kitchen: **једно-**,
**дво-**, **тросoбан**, **четворосoбан** стан ("one-/two-/three-/four-roomed flat"). Fruit
brandies: **кajсиjeвача** (apricot), **крушковача** (pear), **jaбуковача** (apple), alongside
**шљивовица** (plum, "regarded by many as the national drink").

### Nouns человек/отац/сто (p.133–134)

**Човек** "person/man" has irregular plural **људи** (regular masc. plural endings except an
irregular gen. pl. **људи**, unchanged). **Отац** loses its final vowel and the moveable -a when
case endings are added, merging т+ц into а single ц-stem: gen./acc. **оца**, dat./loc. **оцу**,
ins. **оцем** (voc. **оче**); plural stem **очеви**. **Сто** "table" is a monosyllabic noun that
used to end in -л; the л reappears when endings are added: gen. **стола**, plural **столови**.

### Some, a certain — неки (p.134)

The adjective **неки** means "some" or "a certain": "Она учи неки страни језик" (she is studying
some/a certain foreign language).

### Forming the future tense (p.138)

The future uses the short form of **хтети** as auxiliary + infinitive (or the alternative "да +
present" construction): ја ћу, ти ћеш, он/она/оно ће, ми ћемо, ви ћете, они ће (negative нећу,
нећеш, неће...). When there's no introductory word before the auxiliary and the infinitive ends
in **-ти**, drop -ти and fuse the auxiliary onto the stem as one word (уhy + ħemo → **уhyheмо**
"we will take"); if the infinitive ends in **-hи**, keep it as a separate word.

### Verbal aspect in the future (p.139)

As with the past tense, aspect choice matters: perfective verbs signal a single, completed future
action ("Uzećemo autobus iz Beograda" — we'll take a bus, once), imperfective verbs signal
repeated/habitual future action ("Ponekad ćemo jesti u restoranu" — we'll sometimes eat out).

### Forming questions and negatives in the future (p.141)

Questions: either **да ли** + auxiliary + infinitive, or the long form of хтети + **ли** ("Хоћете
ли ићи саме?"). Negative statements simply use the negative of хтети (нећу, нећеш...) + infinitive
— these negative forms are not short forms.

### Using хтети "to want" (p.141)

When хтети means "to want" (not the future auxiliary), the long form is used, typically with the
"да + present" alternative infinitive construction: "Урош хоће само да игра фудбал."

### Accusative and genitive pronouns (p.141–142)

Personal pronouns have short and long acc./gen. forms (ме/мене, те/тебе, га/њега, je(ju)/nje,
нас/нас, вас/вас, их/njih). The short form is preferred but cannot start a sentence/clause; the
long form is used for emphasis and obligatorily after prepositions. **Je** is the preferred acc.
short form of она; **ju** is used only when biti's short form je (past tense) is already present
in the same clause, to avoid a clash ("Разумела ju je").

### There is/are... is not/are not... (p.142–143)

**Има** + gen. sg./pl. expresses "there is/are" for an undefined quantity ("има добрих
ресторана"); **постojaти** ("to exist") is used for a single definite item ("Постojи и други
пут"). **Нема** + gen. expresses "there is/are not" ("Нема проблема," "Нема воде на столу").

### Subgroups of nouns, adjectives, verbs (p.148)

**Кола** "car" is always neut. plural (agreeing verbs are plural too), like **врата** "door" and
**новине** "newspaper" — plural forms for singular referents. **Време** "time/weather" is neut.
with an inserted **-ен-** syllable before all case endings, singular and plural (full paradigm
given). **Цео** "whole" retains an old -л that resurfaces in some forms, parallel to
посao/послу, био/била.

### Verbal patterns (p.149)

Verbs in **-овати/-ивати** (путовати, посehивати) take **-yjeм** in the 1sg present
(путуjeм, посehyjeм). Verbs in **-исати** (borrowed, e.g. резервисати) take **-ишем**
(резервишем). Verbs in **-сти** (провести, jecти) take an irregular **-шhу** 1sg future
(провешhу, jeшhу).

### Accusative and genitive adjective endings (p.149, 161–163)

Full singular/plural adjective declension tables given for hard-stem (нов) and soft-stem (наш)
adjectives across masc./fem./neut., acc./gen. (Unit 11) and dat./ins./loc. (Unit 12). Masc. acc.
singular = nom. for inanimate nouns, = gen. for animate nouns.

### Points of the compass (p.153)

север/jyг/исток/запад with adjectival forms северни/jyжни/источни/зaпaдни. "In the X" uses
**на** + loc.; "to the X" uses **на** + acc.; "towards the X" uses **према** + dat. Compound
directions (jyгозaпaд "south-west") interpose **-o-** between the parts.

### Days of the week and case usage (p.158)

Days are written lower-case. **за** + acc. for "a ticket for [day]" ("карту за петак"). **y** +
acc. for "on [a day]" ("Враhaм се y недeљy увечe"). Instrumental singular for "on [day]s" (habitual)
— понедељком, срeдoм. As noted above, **недeљa** is polysemous (week / Sunday); context
disambiguates. Expressions of time often take the gen. case (ове недeљe "this week," прошле
недeљe "last week," идyhe недeљe "next week," jeднoг дана "one day," ове године "this year").

### Times of day (p.159)

Full paradigm of morning/evening/day/night forms and their "this X"/"in the X" variants; ноh
specifically denotes times after midnight; синоh is a dedicated irregular form for "last night."

### Dative, instrumental and locative pronouns (p.161)

Table of dat./ins./loc. personal pronoun forms; dat. and loc. share forms (loc. has no short
form, used only with prepositions); ins. forms have no short form either, sometimes gaining an
extra **-e** when used without a preposition (мном(e), њим(e), њом(e), etc.).

### Dative, instrumental and locative adjective endings (p.161–162)

Full singular/plural tables for hard (нов) and soft (наш) adjective stems across all three cases,
paralleling the acc./gen. tables from Unit 11.

### Dates (p.163)

Compound numbers use only the last word as an ordinal ("двадесет осми август" = 28 August). "On"
a date takes the gen. case ("двадесет првог августа"). "In" a month takes **y** + loc. ("y
фебруару"). Ordinal numbers in figures take a full stop. Years use the formula "(one)thousand
nine hundred ninety-fifth year," with acc. case for "thousand" (no word for "one" before
хиљаду); only the final numeral is ordinal and takes the gen. case for "in [year]."

### Monosyllabic masc. nouns and вече (p.167)

Most one-syllable masc. nouns take an extended plural stem (цeмoви, соkоvи), but a subset does
not — Чех→Чеси, Грк→Грци, дан→дани, and separately-named зyб, гoст, коњ. **Вечe** "evening" is
grammatically irregular: neut. in nom./voc./acc. singular, but declines like a fem. -и-stem noun
(стварtype) in all other cases — вечери (gen./dat./ins./loc. sg. and nom./voc./acc. pl.),
вечерима (dat./ins./loc. pl.).

### Endings for adjectives сав, овај, моj (p.167–168)

**Сав** "all" takes regular endings but is treated as soft in masc./neut. gen./dat. singular
(свег(a), свем(y)); **сви** (masc. pl.) = "everyone," **све** (neut. sg.) = "everything." **Овaj**
"this" has stem **ов-**; parallel demonstratives **тaj** (stem т-) and **oнaj** (stem он-). **Мoj**
"my" has a full singular/plural paradigm (твoj and свoj follow the same pattern), with shorter
gen./dat./loc. masc./neut. forms preferred.

### To say, tell: рећи vs. казати (p.168)

**Рeċи, рекao** is perfective and used only in past, future, and imperative — not present tense.
For present-tense "to say/tell," Serbian uses **казати, кажем** instead, which can function as
either imperfective or perfective depending on context.

### Reflexive verbs (p.169)

Reflexive verbs add **сe** to express an action done to oneself (**зовем сe** "I call myself" =
"my name is"). Aspect/meaning can shift with the reflexive: **враhaти/врaтити** "to give back" vs.
**враhaти сe/врaтити сe** "to go back." In past/future tenses, сe stays after the auxiliary
("Враħиħемо сe" = "we shall go back"), except the auxiliary je is dropped in 3sg past ("Вратиo
сe" = "he went back," not "*je вратиo сe").

### Names of countries (p.171)

Many country names are grammatically adjectives ending in **-скa/-шкa/-чкa** and decline with
adjective endings (Енглеска, Француска, Немачка, Хрватска). Exceptions decline as ordinary nouns
(Америка, Аустралиja, Jaпан, Русиja, Србиja). Compound state names combine adjective + noun
(Сjeдињeнe Америчке Државе, Beликa Британиja).

### Expressions of time (p.172)

**Током/y току** "during," **крajeм/на крajy** "at the end of," **почетком/на почетку** "at the
beginning of," **средином/y срeдини** "in the middle of" — all + gen. case, each with an
alternative preposition+noun-phrase form. **До** + gen. (temporal) means "by" a given time.

### Saying what you need: треба (p.172)

**Треба** + dat. (person) + nom. (thing needed): "Треба ми авионска карта" = "I need a plane
ticket" (lit. "a plane ticket is needed to me").

### Using код "at home"/"to see someone" (p.173)

**Код куhe** = "at home"; **куħи** = "(going) home." **Код** + gen. of a person = "at
[person]'s (house)" or "going to [person]'s"; the same construction extends to "going to see"
someone, including professionals ("Треба да идеш код лекара" = "you should go and see a
doctor").

### Saying "sorry" (p.172)

**Жao ми je** expresses regret/sympathy (inability to comply with a request, or sympathy for
someone's misfortune); "I am sorry for him" = **жao ми гa je**. **Извините** is "pardon
me/excuse me" for attracting attention or excusing a minor inconvenience; **пардон** is offered as
a further, informal alternative for the latter case.
