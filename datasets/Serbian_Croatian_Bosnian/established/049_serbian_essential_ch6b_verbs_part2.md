## Grammar points

### §6.14 Formation of the interrogative — the full inventory

Hammond gives nine distinct ways to form a question in Serbian, more systematically enumerated here
than the piecemeal treatment across the lesson files:

(a) **Intonation alone**, in casual speech, with no structural change: *Iz Beograda ste?* "You're from
Belgrade?"

(b) **Question-word first**, followed directly by the verb: *Gde ideš?* "Where are you going?"

(c) **The particle ли/li** in second position, preceded by the verb, when no question-word is used:
*Ideš li?* "Are you going?"

(d) **Да ли/da li** + verb (perhaps at a remove): *Da li stvarno tamo ideš?* "Are you really going
there?" — the most neutral/common yes–no question form.

(e) **Је ли/je li** + verb, present tense only: *Je li ideš?* "Are you going?" Hammond flags that in
this construction the verbal је/je is **not** an enclitic (unlike its ordinary auxiliary use), a
distinct behavior worth noting for enclitic-placement mechanics.

(f) **Зар/zar + negative verb** for a negative question: *Zar ne ideš?* "Are you not going?" — and
**зар не/zar ne** placed at the end of a statement functions as a confirmation tag equivalent to
English "..., aren't you?": *Ideš, zar ne?* "You are going, aren't you?"

(g) **Да/da + present tense**, used to phrase a suggestion or offer rather than a true question: *Da
idem?* "Shall I go?"

(h) **Зар/zar alone** (without negation) as an emphatic marker expressing surprise, roughly "really":
*Zar ideš?* "Are you really going?"

(i) **ли/li following a modal verb**, itself followed by да/da: *Mogu li da idem?* "Can I go?" — this
slots li after the modal rather than after the да-clause's own verb.

(Page 93–94.)

### §6.15 Formation of the negative — the "true double negative"

Beyond the already-documented obligatory double-negation rule (a negative pronoun/adverb in a sentence
forces the verb to also carry negation — see `038_grammar_ch4-6_supplementary.md` §56a), Hammond
distinguishes a separate, genuinely opposite phenomenon: the **true double negative**, where two
negative markers combine to produce an ordinary affirmative meaning, exactly as in English "I don't
want to *not* sleep" = "I want to sleep":

- *Neću da ne spavam.* "I don't want to not sleep." (i.e., I do want to sleep.)
- *Ne želi da ne zna.* "He/she doesn't want to not know." (i.e., wants to know.)
- *Nema osobe koja nije kupila taj proizvod.* "There isn't a person who hasn't bought that product."
  (i.e., everyone bought it.)

This is structurally distinct from the "double negative rule" already captured elsewhere and should
not be conflated with it: the earlier rule produces intensified *negation*; this one, an affirmative.
(Page 97–98.)

Also newly explicit: when existence is negated in the **past or future** tense, немати/nemati (present-
tense-only negated-existence verb, "there isn't") is replaced by the negated auxiliary of бити/biti,
taking a genitive complement: *Неће бити млека / Neće biti mleka* "there will be no milk" (future);
*Није било млека / Nije bilo mleka* "there was no milk" (past). The present-tense forms нема/nema
(there is not), future неће бити/neće biti, and past није било/nije bilo all govern the genitive case.
(Page 96–98.)

### §6.17 Ићи/ići derivatives — additional prefixed forms

The established motion-verb prefixation table (`038_grammar_ch4-6_supplementary.md` §54) documents seven
systematic pairs built on ићи/ići plus the imperfective replacement stem -лазити/-laziti. Hammond's
table in §6.17 adds five further prefixed derivatives not in that set — **проћи/proći** (pass by/
through), **прићи/prići** (come towards), **наћи/naći** (find), **наићи/naići** (come upon), **обићи/
obići** (go around, tour), and **заћи/zaći** (go behind) — each formed with the same -ђ-/-đ- present-
tense stem substitution pattern (e.g. *dođem, pođem, prođem, priđem, nađem, naiđem, obiđem, zaiđem*)
and the same -о/-ла/-ло past-participle pattern with an -ш-/-š- stem (e.g. *došao, pošao, prošao,
prišao, našao, naišao, obišao, zašao*). Hammond notes отићи/otići is the sole irregular case with
**two** competing present stems (*odem*/*otidem*), matching the note already flagged in `038`.

The consistent generalization across all these forms: preposition/prefix + ићи/ići → present tense in
-ем/-em with a -ђ-/-đ- stem consonant (rather than the -д-/-d- of bare ићи/ići), and a past tense in
-ao/-la/-lo built on a -ш-/-š- stem rather than -ao directly on ићи's own -шао/-šao root (which is
itself already -š-final, so the derivative pattern is actually the same base morphology inherited
unchanged). (Page 99–100.)

### §6.18 Modal verbs — треба(ти)/trebati's five constructions and the потребан/potreban alternative

Требати/trebati (to need) is the most productive Serbian source of modal-verb meaning ("need to/have
to/should/ought to"), and Hammond breaks it into five distinct да/da-clause constructions, each
mapping to a different English modal, all keeping требати/trebati itself in 3rd person singular
(neuter singular in past-tense forms) while the да-clause verb agrees with the logical subject:

1. **треба да/treba da** + present → "need to / have to / should": *Treba da idemo.* "We need to go."
2. **требаће да/trebaće da** + present → "will need to / will have to": *Trebaće da idemo.* "We will
   need to go."
3. **требало је да/trebalo je da** + past tense → "needed to / should have": *Trebalo je da smo
   otišli.* "We should have gone."
4. **требало би да/trebalo bi da** + present → "ought to": *Trebalo bi da razgovaramo s njom.* "We
   ought to talk to her."
5. **требало би да/trebalo bi da** + past tense → "ought to have": *Trebalo bi da smo razgovarali s
   njom.* "We ought to have talked to her."

Separately, требати/trebati also functions as an ordinary lexical verb meaning "to need something":
the person who needs is in the **dative** (logical subject), and the thing needed is the **nominative**
grammatical subject that the verb agrees with in gender/number across all tenses: *Njoj treba ključ.*
"She needs the key" (lit. "the key is needed to her"); *Trebali su joj ključevi za kola* (past, agreeing
plural); *Trebaće joj ključevi za kola* (future, with -ће/-će).

The adjective потребан/potreban ("necessary") can substitute for this lexical (non-modal) use of
требати/trebati, paired with бити/biti as an auxiliary that agrees in gender/number with the needed
object across tenses: *Potreban joj je ključ za kola* "She needs a key for the car" (lit. "necessary to
her is a key"); past *Potreban joj je bio ključ za kola*. (Page 101–103.)

Моћи/moći (can, be able to), when used modally, takes either да/da + present or a bare infinitive of
the main verb (*Mogu da kupe karte* / *Mogu kupiti karte*, "they can buy tickets") and has a full
regular present-tense paradigm: 1sg. *mogu*, 2sg. *možeš*, 3sg. *može*, 1pl. *možemo*, 2pl. *možete*,
3pl. *mogu*. It conjugates normally across past (*Mogli su da kupe...* "they could have bought...")
and future (*Moći će da kupe...* "they will be able to buy..."). (Page 103–104.)
