## Overall coverage summary (parts 1–8)

This 8-part extraction covers the complete "Croatian and Serbian-English glossary" from Celia
Hawkesworth's *Colloquial Croatian and Serbian* (Routledge, 1998), printed pp. 282–299 (PDF pages
290–307), letter A (`a`) through the final entry `žut` (letter Ž) — exhaustive, not a sample, per
the dispatch's dense-glossary coverage rule. Approximate total: **~640 headword entries** across the
8 files (part counts: part 1 ≈150, part 2 ≈95, part 3 ≈85, part 4 ≈85, part 5 ≈90, part 6 ≈90, part
7 ≈90, part 8 ≈70 — hand counts, not machine-verified).

**Usage tier finding:** every single entry in this glossary is `core` vocabulary — a beginner's
everyday-life wordlist (family, food, travel, weather, household items, basic verbs). **No
taboo/slang/archaic/literary-tier entries were found anywhere in this glossary** — this source is a
pedagogical beginner glossary, not a colloquial-register or slang dictionary, unlike some of this
dataset's other dictionary sources. The book's real usage-tier-diversity value lies elsewhere: its
systematic Ijekavian/Ekavian (regional) and Bosnian/Croatian/Serbian (national) variant tagging,
present on roughly a third of all entries, which is comparative sociolinguistic data of real value
for the mechanics-analysis phase even though every entry sits at the `core` register.

**Marginalia:** none found on any of the 18 pages (PDF 290–307) — all content is clean, consistently
typeset print. The one exception worth flagging again is the `grad` → "own (4)" entry in part 1
(PDF p. 292), which was confirmed via 300 DPI re-render to be a genuine print erratum in the source
book itself (not marginalia, not a vision-reading error on this extraction's part).
