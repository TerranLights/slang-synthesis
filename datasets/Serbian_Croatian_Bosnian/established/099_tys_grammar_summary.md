## Grammar glossary (English terminology, paraphrased — pp. 297–300)

Standard pedagogical glosses of English grammar terms for the benefit of the (assumed monolingual
English) learner, each illustrated with one short Serbian example sentence. Notable points beyond
the purely English-terminology content:

- **case**: states explicitly that Serbian has **seven cases** — nominative, vocative, accusative,
  genitive, dative, instrumental, locative (p. 297).
- **enclitics**: notes Serbian has two present-tense forms each of *biti* ("to be") and *hteti*
  ("to want") plus two case-form sets for personal pronouns (full and clitic), and that the shorter
  alternative forms are the enclitics, with rules governing when each is used (p. 297).
- **infinitive**: notes Serbian's alternative infinitive-replacement construction with *da* +
  present tense, e.g. *Ona voli da živi u Londonu* alongside *Ona voli živeti u Londonu* ("She likes
  to live in London") (p. 298).
- **verbal aspect**: imperfective (*pisati*, "to write," unfinished/habitual) vs. perfective
  (*napisati*, "to write," completed), illustrated with *Pisao je bratu svaki dan* ("He wrote to his
  brother every day," habitual) vs. *Juče je napisao pismo bratu* ("Yesterday he wrote a letter to
  his brother," completed) (p. 300).

No new Serbian vocabulary items beyond what's illustrated inline in these English-terminology
examples; not tabulated per dispatch instructions (explaining English grammar terms, not Serbian
language content).
