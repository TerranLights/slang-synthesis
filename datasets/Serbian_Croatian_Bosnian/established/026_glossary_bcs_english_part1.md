**Format of this glossary:** each entry gives the headword (with the book's own stress-accent marks
retained as printed), grammatical info (gender/POS, principal parts where the book gives them),
optional `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]` dialect tags (Bosnian/Croatian/Serbian regional variant) and/or `[E]`/`[J]`
tags (Ekavian/Jekavian pronunciation-variant, a sub-national split roughly correlating with
Serbia-vs-Bosnia/Croatia/Montenegro), an English gloss, and a lesson/section cross-reference code
(e.g. `16A1a`, `B.III`) which is retained in the Notes column as a citation back to the textbook, not
as frequency data. Per the extraction spec, only the headword/gloss/POS/dialect-tag information is
extracted — full inflectional paradigms and illustrative example sentences given in the source are
paraphrased or omitted rather than bulk-reproduced. `[B,C,S]` etc. tags are carried into the
**Attested Region** column exactly as marked; Geographic Scope is `national` for B/C/S-style tags
(distinguishing standard national varieties of the same pluricentric language) and `regional` for
E/J-style tags or explicit sub-national dialect labels (e.g. Čakavian, Montenegrin).

## Grammar points

This chunk is a pure alphabetical glossary (dictionary-style headword list), not a lesson with
discursive grammar explanations — there is no separate grammar-point prose to paraphrase here. The
one recurring grammatical pattern worth noting explicitly is the systematic **Ekavian/Jekavian (E/J)
split** that runs through a large fraction of these entries: many headwords appear as two separate
glossary lines differing only in their reflex of the Common Slavic yat vowel (e.g. `dete`/`dijete`
"child", `mleko`-type roots, `covek`/`covjek` "person", `vreme`/`vrijeme` "time"), each tagged `[E]`
or `[J]` respectively. Ekavian pronunciation is characteristic of Serbia (Serbian standard); Jekavian
is characteristic of Bosnia, Croatia, and Montenegro (Bosnian, Croatian, and Montenegrin standards).
This is distinct from the `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]` tags, which mark lexical (word-choice) differences between
the three/four national standards rather than a phonological alternation — the glossary applies both
tagging systems independently and sometimes stacks them on the same entry (e.g. `hljeb` marked both
`[J]` and `[B/S only]`). Two other minor grammatical items surfaced by full paradigm entries in this
range: the irregular verb `biti` "to be" (present clitic/full/negated forms, perfective `budem`-series,
aorist, and E/J-split imperfect), and the modal/future-auxiliary verb `hteti`/`htjeti` "want, will"
(full forms, future-auxiliary clitics `ću` etc., and negated forms split `neću` [B,S] vs. `ne ću` [C]).
Both are paraphrased above in their Notes cells rather than reproduced in full inflectional detail.
