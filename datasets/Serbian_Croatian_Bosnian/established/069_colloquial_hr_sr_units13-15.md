# Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Colloquial Croatian and Serbian, Units 13–15 ("U kazalištu" / "Pakiranje" / "Polazak")

**Source:** Celia Hawkesworth, *Colloquial Croatian and Serbian: The Complete Course for Beginners*
(Routledge, 1998), Units 13–15, printed pages 140–169 (PDF pages 148–177 of the scanned file; verified
by direct page inspection — Unit 13 title page is PDF p.148/printed p.140, Unit 16 "U kvaru" begins at
PDF p.178/printed p.170, confirming the boundary).

**Coverage note:** every grammar point and every distinct vocabulary item from these three units
(dialogues, explicit vocabulary-building boxes, reading-passage glossary lines), plus every explicit
register/idiom note the source itself flags. Repeated drill-exercise sentences (fill-in-the-blank
adjective/number agreement drills, translation exercises that reuse already-captured vocabulary) were
skipped per the coverage rule in `../../00_Reference_Extraction_Spec.md`. This source is scanned with
no usable text layer; every page was vision-read. **No handwritten marginalia was found on any page in
this range** — all content inspected was clearly typeset print consistent with the surrounding page,
so nothing was excluded on that basis.

This book is explicitly framed as "colloquial" — informal register and idiom notes embedded in the
dialogues (e.g. "Ubit će me stari" for parents, the informal parting "Bog", the explicit
formal-vs-informal contrast around office dress code) are flagged below even though the book itself
doesn't tag them with a formal usage-tier label the way a B/C/S-comparative source would.

## Grammar points

### Ordinal numbers

Ordinals are regular definite adjectives formed by adding `-i` (m.), `-a` (f.), `-o` (n.) to the
cardinal number: *prvi/prva/prvo* "1st", *drugi* "2nd", *treći* "3rd", *četvrti* "4th", *peti…
devedeset deveti* "5th–99th", *stoti* "100th". In compound ordinals only the last element is
declined (e.g. *dvadeset petog kolovoza* "25 August" — only *peti* takes the genitive ending). (p.
142–143)

### Dates, days, and time expressions

- "On Monday" etc. uses *u* + accusative (*u ponedjeljak*).
- A specific calendar date on which something occurs uses the genitive (*Stigli smo četvrtog
  kolovoza* "We arrived on 4 August").
- Other time-phrase expressions ("that day/week/year") follow the same genitive pattern
  (*slijedećeg dana*, *tog tjedna*, *te godine*).
- Duration of time is generally expressed by the accusative (*Pokušavam te dobiti telefonom cijeli
  dan* "I've been trying to get you on the phone all day").
- Habitual events tied to a specific day of the week use the instrumental (*Subotom uvijek nekuda
  idemo* "We always go somewhere on Saturdays"; *utorkom* "on Tuesdays"). (p. 145–146)

**Regional note (months):** the source explicitly distinguishes two parallel month-name systems —
Latin-derived names (*januar, februar…*) used "throughout the region," and a fully Slavonic set
(*siječanj, veljača…*) that is specifically "now the norm" in Croatian. This is one of the source's
few explicit dialectal/regional annotations in this chunk. (p. 144)

**Culture point (p. 148):** the source's own sidebar notes that in most Balkan countries the working
day starts very early (around 7 a.m.), people typically skip a substantial breakfast, and the main
meal of the day is lunch, eaten any time between 2 and 4 p.m.

### Comparative adjectives

Most comparatives add `-iji/-ija/-ije` to the indefinite adjective stem (*stariji, poznatiji,
gladniji*). Adjectives in `-o` derived from `l` add the ending to the retained `l` (*mio* →
*miliji*). Other adjectives take `-ji`, triggering regular consonant alternations at the stem
boundary: `k+j→č` (*jak*→*jači*), `g+j→ž` (*drag*→*draži*), `h+j→š` (*tih*→*tiši*), `t+j→ć`
(*ljut*→*ljući*), `d+j→đ` (*tvrd*→*tvrđi*). If the stem ends in `p`, `b`, or `v`, an `l` is often
inserted before the added `-ji` (*skup*→*skuplji*, *grub*→*grublji*). Note the exception *lijep* →
*ljepši*. Adjectives ending in `-ak/-ek/-ok` typically drop the final syllable before adding `-ji`
(*blizak*→*bliži*, *kratak*→*kraći*, *dalek*→*dalji*, *dubok*→*dublji*, *rijedak*→*rjeđi*,
*visok*→*viši*). Comparative adjectives decline like regular definite adjectives. (p. 152–154)

### Comparison of adverbs and irregular comparatives

Where an adjective's neuter singular form doubles as the adverb, the neuter singular comparative is
used the same way (*jako*→*jače*, *lako*→*lakše*, *blizu*→*bliže*, *drago*→*draže*,
*skupo*→*skuplje*, *teško*→*teže*, *rado*→*radije*). Several common adjectives/adverbs are fully
irregular: *dobar*→*bolji*, *zao/loš*→*gori*, *velik*→*veći*, *malen*→*manji*, *mnogo*→*više*,
*malo*→*manje*. (p. 155)

### Superlative

Formed by prefixing *naj-* to the comparative: *najstariji, najbliži, najbolje, najviše, najmanje*.
(p. 155)

### Comparison to an object or phrase

Comparing two objects normally uses *od* + genitive (*Moj brat je stariji od tebe*). When the
comparison involves a whole clause/phrase rather than a single noun, *nego* must be used instead
(*Kasnije je nego što sam mislila* "It's later than I thought"). *Nego* can also substitute for *od*
+ genitive even with a single noun (*On je stariji nego ti*). (p. 157)

### Structures with the comparative

Two fixed comparative constructions are introduced: `sve` + comparative = "more and more,
increasingly" (*Svakim danom sve bolje vladate našim jezikom* "You speak our language better and
better every day"); and `što` + comparative = "as … as possible" (*Dođite nam opet što prije!*
"Come again as soon as you can!"). (p. 157–158)

### Demonstrative pronouns

Full paradigm of *taj* "that" given (declined identically by *ovaj* "this" and *onaj* "that
[further/yonder]"):

| | Sg. m. | Sg. nt. | Sg. f. | Pl. m. | Pl. nt. | Pl. f. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| N | taj | to | ta | ti | ta | te |
| G | tog(a) | tog(a) | te | tih | tih | tih |
| D | tom(u) | tom(u) | toj | tim(a) | tim(a) | tim(a) |
| A | tog(a)/taj | to | tu | te | ta | te |
| P | tom(e) | tom(e) | toj | tim | tim | tim |
| I | tim(e) | tim(e) | tom | tim | tim | tim |

(p. 163)

### Possessive adjectives from proper nouns

Formed from proper nouns or nouns denoting people. Masculine nouns ending in a consonant or `-o`
add `-ov` (*Antun*→*Antunov*, *Marko*→*Markov*); if the final consonant is "soft" the suffix is
`-ev` instead (*prijatelj*→*prijateljev*, *Đorđe*→*Đorđev*, *Petrović*→*Petrovićev*). Feminine nouns
in `-a` drop the `-a` and add `-in` (*Marija*→*Marijin*, *sestra*→*sestrin*). These derived
possessives decline like regular definite adjectives, with endings added onto the new suffix
(*Vidjela sam Markovog brata*). Crucially, **a possessive adjective of this type cannot itself be
preceded by a qualifying adjective** — *putnikova torba* "the passenger's bag" is fine, but "that
other passenger's bag" must be rephrased as *torba onog drugog putnika* (genitive construction
instead). The source also notes indefinite endings are also found for masculine possessives of this
type (footnote, not further detailed in this chunk). (p. 163–164)
