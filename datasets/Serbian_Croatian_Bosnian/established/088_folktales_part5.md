# Serbian — Established Vocabulary: *Serbian Folk Fairy Tales* (Kreativni centar, 2000), pp. 117–144

**Source:** *Српске народне бајке* [Serbian Folk Fairy Tales], Kreativni centar, 2000 (Библиотека
Пустоловине / "Bajke" series). Printed pages 117–144 (PDF pages 121–148 of `29.Serbian Folk Tales.pdf`;
verified offset PDF = printed + 4, confirmed empirically against the printed page numbers visible at
the bottom of pp. 119, 123, 127, 133, 139, 141 before extraction proceeded).

**Coverage note:** this is a **scanned book with no usable text layer**, entirely in Cyrillic —
every page in range was vision-read directly (no `pdftotext`). This source is also **structurally
different from every other book in this project's extraction**: it is pure literary/folkloric
narrative (fairy tales), not a pedagogical grammar/vocabulary textbook, and it has no parallel
English translation. Per this dispatch's instructions, coverage is **vocabulary-only, no plot or
prose reproduction** — for each tale, only the genuinely interesting/archaic/regional/dialectal
vocabulary is extracted and glossed, using the book's own inline "+"-marked margin glosses wherever
present (these are folk-realia notes aimed at young readers — explaining obsolete objects, folk
beliefs, and regional/archaic words — and are frequently the highest-value content on a page).
Words without an inline gloss are given an original English gloss with Vision Reading Confidence
marked `plausible_unverified` rather than `verified`.

**Vision-reading guard:** every page in range was actively checked for handwritten marginalia
(pen/pencil notes, underlines, filled-in answers) per the extraction spec's mandatory guard. **None
was found.** All margin annotations across pp. 117–144 are consistently typeset in the book's own
small-caps "+"-bulleted glossary font, matching the surrounding printed page — genuine publisher
content, not a prior owner's handwriting. No exclusions were necessary.

**Tales covered in this range** (per the table of contents):
- End of an untitled preceding tale (a swan-maiden/enchanted-bird type story), pp. 117–118 —
  only the tail end and its closing illustration fall in range; no title page for it is present here.
- **Тица дјевојка** ["The Bird-Girl"] — a swan-maiden/enchanted-princess tale, pp. 119–122.
- **Правда и кривда** ["Truth and Falsehood" / "Justice and Injustice"] — an ethical-contest tale
  (the devil, disguised, repeatedly rules that "wrong" beats "right"; poetic justice restores the
  balance), pp. 123–126.
- **Међедовић** ["Bear's-son", from *међед*/*медвед* "bear"] — a Serbian variant of the
  international "Bear's Son" tale type (a boy born of a woman and a bear, superhuman strength,
  the buzdovan-testing motif, a "tall tale" story-within-a-story with the traditional closing lie
  formula), pp. 127–132.
- **Седам Влашића** ["The Seven Vlašići"] — a folk-astronomy etiological tale explaining the
  Pleiades constellation, pp. 133–137.
- **Ђаво и његов шегрт** ["The Devil and His Apprentice"] — a magician's-apprentice/shapeshifting-
  duel tale type, pp. 138–142.
- pp. 143–144 are full-page illustrations opening the *next* tale (not in this chunk's assigned
  range — no text yet appears on either page).
