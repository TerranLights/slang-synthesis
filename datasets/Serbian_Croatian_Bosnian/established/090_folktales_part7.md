# Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian — Established Vocabulary: *Serbian Folk Fairy Tales* (Part 7)

**Source:** *Српске народне бајке* (Serbian Folk Fairy Tales), Kreativni centar, 2000. Vision-read,
printed pages 173-200 (PDF pages 177-204; empirically-verified offset PDF page = printed page + 4,
held consistently across the whole range including unnumbered full-bleed illustration plates).

**Source type note:** unlike every other source extracted for this language so far, this is a pure
literary/folkloric narrative anthology (fairy tales), not a pedagogical grammar/textbook, and has no
parallel English translation. The book supplies its own inline "+"-marked margin glosses for
obscure/archaic/regional words; these are extracted directly and marked `verified` for Vision
Reading Confidence since the book's own gloss is the cross-check. A smaller number of additional
words judged genuinely interesting (archaic/dialectal/regional flavor, or notable
polysemy/false-friend risk) but *without* an inline "+" gloss are also included, self-glossed from
context and marked `plausible_unverified`.

**Coverage approach (per dispatch instructions):** vocabulary-only, no plot/prose reproduction.
Each tale gets a one-line orientation note; vocabulary tables follow. Skipped: retelling of plot,
reproduction of dialogue beyond single illustrative words/short phrases.

**Vision-reading guard:** every page in the assigned range was checked for handwritten marginalia.
**None found** — this copy shows only the book's own printed sidebar glosses (consistent typeset
sans-serif font in a shaded box, matching the rest of the print run) and printed illustrations/
photos. No pencil/pen marks, underlines, or handwritten answers anywhere in this range.

**Tale titles found in this range** (page numbers are printed-book pages; PDF = printed + 4):

1. **Дијете са девет чирака** ("The Child with Nine Candlesticks") — printed p.173-176, continuing
   from p.172 (outside this chunk's range; the tale's opening is not covered here).
2. **Милош и Дивоња** ("Miloš and Divonja") — printed p.179-183 (title page 179, plus two
   full-bleed illustration-only plates at unnumbered pp. ~183/184 before the next tale begins).
3. **Пепељуга** ("Cinderella") — printed p.185-189. **Not present in the dispatcher's TOC
   snapshot** — a genuine gap in the previously-known contents list.
4. **Ћела** ("The Bald Man") — printed p.191-193. This is the tale the sibling subagent flagged
   as an unidentified title around printed page ~193; confirmed here as its own distinct tale,
   titled "Ћела." Notably written in a mixed/more ekavian-leaning register (е.g. "је" not "ђе/ђе",
   "леп" not "лијеп") compared to the surrounding (I)jekavian tales — worth flagging as a possible
   different collector/region of origin within the same anthology.
5. **Ђевојка бржа од коња** ("The Girl Faster than a Horse") — printed p.195-198. Also **not in
   the TOC snapshot.**
6. **Тамни вилајет** ("The Dark Land/Realm") — printed p.199-200, a short complete
   parable-like tale finishing on a single page. Also **not in the TOC snapshot.**

Printed page 200 (PDF 204) is followed by a blank/unnumbered verso page, which is the end of this
chunk's assigned range.
