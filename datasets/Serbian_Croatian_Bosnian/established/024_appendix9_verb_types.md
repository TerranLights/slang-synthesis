# Serbian, Croatian, Bosnian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Appendix 9 — Verb Types

**Source:** Ronelle Alexander & Ellen Elias-Bursać, *Bosnian, Croatian, Serbian, a Textbook: With
Exercises and Basic Grammar* (2nd ed., 2010), Appendix 9 ("Verb types"), pp. 359–366 (PDF pages
380–387).

**Coverage note:** Appendix 9 is a reference re-sort, not a new vocabulary-presentation section —
it takes every verb already introduced across Lessons 1–20 (each tagged only with the lesson
code it was originally introduced in, e.g. `16A1a`, `B.III`) and regroups them by conjugation
type, so a learner can predict a verb's present-tense form from its infinitive. The appendix gives
**no English glosses anywhere** — only the model verb pairs (infinitive + 1st singular present)
and bare lesson-citation codes for roughly 500 member verbs. Per this dispatch's specific
instruction ("extract every verb-type grammar point and representative example verbs per type")
and the extraction spec's copyright-discipline rule against bulk-reproducing a source's own
vocabulary listing, this file extracts **every grammar point in full** (all 16 numbered types, the
two mixed-pattern subtypes, and the irregular class, plus the accent-shift rule that governs all of
them) but gives only a **representative sample** of member verbs per type rather than the
complete ~500-verb list. English glosses given below (for the model verbs and the representative
samples) were supplied independently from general BCS lexical knowledge, since the source itself
provides none in this appendix — flagged here so it's clear these are not source-verbatim
translations.

This appendix also carries the source's own `[E]`/`[J]` (Ekavian/Jekavian) dialect-reflex tags on
a number of verbs whose *jat* vowel reflex differs between the two pronunciation norms (e.g.
`videti [E]` / `vidjeti [J]`) — a different tagging axis than the `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]` lexical/script
tags used elsewhere in the book, and one the extraction spec requires capturing wherever the
source marks it explicitly. Source has a clean embedded text layer (`pdftotext` extracted it
directly, verified against a manual re-check of the raw output); no vision-reading was required
and no handwritten marginalia was present.

## Grammar points

### Overview: purpose and structure of the verb-type system

The verb-type numbering was first introduced in Lesson 10 (p. 142) to help learners of BCS
memorize conjugational forms and predict, with reasonable confidence, the forms of verbs they
haven't yet encountered. These same type numbers are reused in later grammar sections to explain
the past tense (p. 143), aspect relationships (p. 146), and passive participles (p. 160), and are
elaborated further in the companion volume *BCS, A Grammar with Sociolinguistic Commentary*.
Appendix 9 (pp. 359–366) collects every verb introduced in the textbook and sorts it by type. Each
type is represented by a **model verb**, cited as an infinitive + 1st person singular present pair
(e.g. `igrati, igram`); the relationship between those two forms *is* the type definition, and
lets a learner predict the rest of a new verb's present-tense paradigm once they know which type
it belongs to. The 1st singular present form is only given for a member verb when it isn't
unambiguously predictable from the model.

### Accent-shift rule (italicized infinitives)

When an infinitive is printed in italics in the appendix's lists, that signals the word's accent
falls on a different syllable in the present tense than in the infinitive. The shift moves
**toward the beginning of the word** for Types 1–2, 6–9, 11, and 16, and **toward the end of the
word** for Types 10 and 13–15. This applies even to words that don't carry a visible accent mark
in print — the source's guidance is to mentally place an invisible accent mark to the left of such
a word and apply the same shift rule. Illustrative pairs given by the source (p. 359):

- Leftward shift: `govòriti` → `gòvorim` (Type 2); `krènuti` → `krenem` (Type 7, accent mark
  present only on the infinitive); `dàvati` → `dajem` (Type 9, same pattern).
- Rightward shift: `dòvesti` → `dovèdem` (Type 13); `prati` → `pèrem` (Type 10, accent mark
  present only on the present-tense form); `tresti` → `trèsem` (Type 14, same pattern).

### Type 1 — igrati, igram

By far the largest and most productive type in the appendix, spanning roughly a page and a half
of member verbs. Infinitives end in `-ati`; the present tense is formed with `-am` endings, and
(per the model-verb footnote) the accent does not shift in the 3rd person plural. Representative
members (p. 359): `čekati` "to wait," `čitati` "to read," `gledati` "to watch/look," `imati` "to
have," `pitati` "to ask," `pričati` "to talk, tell," `slušati` "to listen," `spavati` "to sleep,"
`kuvati`/`kuhati` "to cook," `znati` "to know."

### Type 2 — staviti, stavim

The second-largest type. Infinitives end in `-iti`; present tense in `-im`. Representative members
(pp. 360–361): `raditi` "to work, do," `govoriti` "to speak," `misliti` "to think," `nositi` "to
carry, wear," `platiti` "to pay," `praviti` "to make," `kupiti` "to buy (P)," `učiti` "to learn,
teach," `voziti` "to drive," `roditi se` "to be born."

### Type 3 — videti [E] vidjeti [J], vidim

A small, closed class of roughly two dozen verbs. Infinitives end in `-eti`/`-jeti` (with the
same E/J dialect-reflex alternation seen throughout the book) and the present tense is formed with
`-im`, matching Type 2's present-tense pattern despite the different infinitive ending.
Representative members (p. 362), most themselves carrying E/J tags: `vòleti`/`vòljeti` "to love,
like," `žèleti`/`žèljeti` "to want, wish," `žìveti`/`žìvjeti` "to live," `sèdeti` "to sit
(be seated, I)," `svideti se`/`svidjeti se` "to be pleasing (to someone)."

### Type 4 — držati, držim

A small closed class of about 15 verbs. Infinitives end in `-ati` on a root whose stem-final
consonant is one that keeps an `-im`-type present rather than the `-am` of Type 1. Representative
members (p. 362): `bòjati se` "to be afraid," `lèžati` "to lie (be lying down)," `stàjati` "to
stand," `tr̀čati` "to run," `zvìždati` "to whistle."

### Type 5 — pisati, pišem

Infinitives end in `-ati`, but the stem-final consonant "softens" (palatalizes) before the
present-tense endings, per a chart given earlier in the book (p. 163, not reproduced here); stems
ending in `-r-` or `-j-` are the exception and don't change. Representative members (p. 363):
`pisati` → `pišem` "to write," `plakati` → `plačem`-type pattern "to cry" (listed as `plakati`
without an explicit present, i.e. predictable), `vikati` "to shout," `kazati` "to say, tell,"
`igrati`-adjacent forms like `slagati` "to lie / to stack" (a homograph pair, both Type 5),
`smejati se`/`smijati se` "to laugh."

### Type 6 — piti, pijem

A smaller type with short infinitive stems; present tense formed with `-ijem`. Representative
members (p. 363): `piti` "to drink," `čuti` "to hear," `dobiti` "to get, receive (P)," `popiti`
"to drink up (P)," `sakriti` "to hide (P)," `ubiti` "to kill (P)."

### Type 7 — krenuti, krenem

Infinitives end in `-nuti`; present tense in `-nem`. A footnote on this type notes that some of
its member verbs have alternate infinitives that instead belong to Type 15b (e.g. `dignuti`
alongside `dići`, both meaning "to lift," sharing one present-tense paradigm). Representative
members (p. 363): `krenuti` "to set out," `spomenuti` "to mention (P)," `prekinuti` "to interrupt,
break off (P)," `stignuti`/`stići` "to arrive (P)," `okrenuti` "to turn (P)."

### Mixed Type 5(inf.)/7(pres.) — stati, stanem

A small named sub-class whose members take a Type-5-style infinitive but a Type-7-style present.
Members given in full by the source (p. 364, only four verbs): `nestati` "to disappear/run out
(P)," `ostati` "to remain, stay (P)," `postati` "to become (P)," `prestati` "to stop, cease (P),"
and `stati`/`ustati` "to stand up/come to a stop; to get up."

### Type 8a — kupovati, kupujem

Infinitives end in `-ovati`; present tense in `-ujem`. Representative members (p. 364):
`kupovati` "to buy (I, imperfective counterpart of `kupiti`)," `putovati` "to travel," `verovati`/
`vjerovati` "to believe," `poštovati` "to respect," `stanovati` "to reside, live (in a place)."

### Type 8b — kazivati, kazujem

Infinitives end in `-ivati` instead of `-ovati`, but share the same `-ujem` present-tense pattern
as Type 8a, which is why the source distinguishes 8a/8b rather than merging them. Representative
members (p. 364): `kazivati` "to tell, narrate," `istraživati` "to investigate, research,"
`očekivati` "to expect," `pokazivati` "to show (I)," `zahvaljivati` "to thank (I)."

### Type 9 — poznavati, poznajem

Infinitives end in `-avati` (with a long stem vowel); present tense in `-ajem`. Representative
members (p. 364): `poznavati` "to know, be acquainted with," `davati` "to give (I)," `prodavati`
"to sell (I)," `priznavati` "to admit, acknowledge (I)," `predavati` "to lecture, teach; to hand
over (I)."

### Type 10 — brati, berem

Short infinitive stems with a present-tense vowel change (an `e`-grade alternation) and a
rightward accent shift. Members given nearly in full by the source (p. 364, a small type):
`brati`/`berem` "to pick, gather," `prati`/`perem` "to wash," `slati`/`šaljem` "to send,"
`zvati se`/`zovem se` "to be named/called," `poslati`/`pošaljem` "to send (P)."

### Type 11 — uzeti, uzmem

Infinitives end in `-eti`; present tense in `-mem`. Representative members (p. 365): `uzeti` "to
take (P)," `zauzeti` "to occupy (P)," `početi`/`počnem` "to begin (P)," `umreti`/`umrijeti` "to
die (P)," `popeti se` "to climb up (P)."

### Type 12 — smeti [E] smjeti [J], smem [E] smijem [J]

A very small closed class of only five verbs (several derived from the same root by prefixation),
infinitives in `-eti`/`-jeti`, present in `-em`/`-ijem`. Full member list (p. 365): `smeti`/
`smjeti` "to be allowed, dare," `razumeti`/`razumjeti` "to understand," `uspeti`/`uspjeti` "to
succeed (P)," `dospeti`/`dospjeti` "to reach, arrive at (P)," `izumeti`/`izumjeti` "to know how to
(archaic/regional sense noted by the source's own citation)."

### Type 13 — jesti, jedem

Infinitives end in `-sti`; present tense in `-dem`, with a rightward accent shift. A footnote
notes some members share their present-tense pattern with Type 15b instead. Representative members
(p. 365): `jesti` "to eat," `pojesti` "to eat up (P)," `pasti`/`padnem` "to fall (P)," `sesti`/
`sjesti` "to sit down (P)," `sresti`/`sretnem` "to meet (P)."

### Type 14 — tresti, tresem

Infinitives end in `-sti`; present tense in `-sem`, rightward accent shift. A small type.
Representative members (p. 365): `tresti` "to shake," `dovesti`/`dovezem` "to bring (by vehicle,
P)," `odvesti`/`odvezem` "to take away (by vehicle, P)," `prevesti`/`prevezem` "to transport;
to translate (P)," `prerasti`/`prerastem` "to outgrow (P)."

### Mixed Type 12(inf., long)/14(pres.) — doneti [E] donijeti [J], donesem

A named sub-class combining a long-vowel Type-12-style infinitive with a Type-14-style present.
Full member list (p. 365), all prefixed on the same root and all carrying E/J tags: `doneti`/
`donijeti` "to bring (P)," `izneti`/`iznijeti` "to carry out; to present, put forward (P),"
`odneti`/`odnijeti` "to carry away (P)," `poneti`/`ponijeti` "to bring along (P)," `preneti`/
`prenijeti` "to transfer, convey (P)," `prineti`/`prinijeti` "to bring near, offer up (P),"
`pridoneti`/`pridonijeti` "to contribute (P)."

### Type 15a — teći, tečem, teku

Infinitives end in `-ći`; present tense in `-čem`, with an irregular 3rd person plural in `-ku`
(`teku`, not the expected `-e/-u` pattern). A footnote notes some members have an alternate present
that instead follows Type 15b. Full member list (p. 366, a small type): `teći` "to flow," `moći`
"to be able to (irregular present `mogu, možeš...`)," `tući` "to beat, hit," `izvući` "to pull out
(P)," `steći` "to acquire (P)," `reći`/`izreći` "to say/pronounce (P, irregular present `reknem,`
rarely used)."

### Type 15b — pomoći, pomognem

Infinitives end in `-oći`/`-ći`; present tense in `-gnem`. A footnote notes some members have
alternate infinitives that also fit Type 7 (e.g. `dići`/`dignuti` "to lift," `podići`/`podignuti`
"to raise, pick up," `stići`/`stignuti` "to arrive," `sleći se`/`slegnuti se` "to shrug"). Full
member list (p. 366): `pomoći` "to help (P)," `dići`/`podići`/`postići` (achievement-type
compounds), `leći` "to lie down (P)," `pobeći`/`pobjeći` "to flee (P)."

### Type 16 — doći, dođem

Infinitives end in `-ći`; present tense in `-đem`. Every member of this type is a directional
prefix attached to `ići` "to go" (itself listed here rather than under "Irregular"), and the
accent shifts leftward. Representative members (p. 366): `doći` "to come, arrive (P)," `ići` "to
go (I)," `izaći`/`izići` "to go out, exit (P)," `naći` "to find (P)," `otići` "to leave, go away
(P, irregular present `odem`)," `ući` "to enter (P)," `proći` "to pass (P/I)."

### Irregular verbs — biti, hteti [E] htjeti [J]

Two verbs stand entirely outside the 16-type system. `biti` "to be" has the irregular
future/conditional-stem form `budem` (used in forming the future tense and certain subordinate
constructions) rather than a predictable Type-pattern present. `hteti`/`htjeti` "to want" is fully
suppletive in the present tense (`hoću, hoćeš, hoće...`, not derivable from the infinitive by any
of the 16 rules) and carries the book's E/J dialect-reflex tag pair like many of the type-3/12/14
verbs above.

### The `[E]`/`[J]` dialect-reflex tagging convention in this appendix

Throughout Appendix 9, many verbs (especially in Types 3, 12, and the mixed 12/14 class, and the
irregular `hteti`/`htjeti`) are given as an Ekavian/Jekavian pair rather than a single form — e.g.
`videti [E] vidjeti [J]`, `smeti [E] smjeti [J]`, `doneti [E] donijeti [J]`. This marks the *jat*
vowel reflex difference between the Ekavian pronunciation norm (predominant in Serbia) and the
(i)Jekavian norm (predominant in Croatia, Bosnia, and Montenegro) — a phonological/orthographic
split that cuts across, and is independent from, the book's separate `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]` tags used
elsewhere for lexical or standard-specific wording choices. Both reflex forms share the same
1st-singular present-tense form and the same conjugation type; the appendix lists both spellings
purely so a reader following either pronunciation norm can locate "their" form of the verb.
