## Notes on textbook footnotes

### Syllable-switching slang (see table above)

The footnote (p. 354/PDF 375) characterizes this as a genuine, historically attested colloquial word
game in Bosnian/Croatian speech — switching a word's syllables, comparable in mechanism to English
pig-Latin-style play — distinct from Belgrade's own (non-syllable-switching) slang system. Captured
as vocabulary above rather than quoted; the mechanism itself (not the book's exact wording) is
paraphrased.

### *Kum*/*kuma* wedding terminology

A short cultural footnote (p. 356/PDF 377) glosses *kum* and *kuma* (already present in the
Appendix 8 Vocabulary VIII box as "best man" / "maid or matron of honor") as broader than those
English equivalents: the terms cover not only a baby's baptismal sponsor (whose godchild is called a
*kumče*) but also a wedding's witnesses. The footnote notes a *kum*/*kuma* need not be a blood
relative but is treated as close to kin through participation in family ceremonies, that a *kum* is
traditionally expected to give the most significant wedding gift (historically, per the footnote, a
refrigerator), and that the *kum* customarily delivers the wedding's principal toast — the
*kumova zdràvica* term already present in the Vocabulary VIII box. Paraphrased here, not quoted.
