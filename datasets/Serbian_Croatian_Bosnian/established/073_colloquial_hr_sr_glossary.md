# Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian — Established Vocabulary: Croatian and Serbian-English Glossary, Part 1

**Source:** Celia Hawkesworth, *Colloquial Croatian and Serbian: The Complete Course for Beginners*
(Routledge, 1998), "Croatian and Serbian-English glossary," printed pp. 282–299 (PDF pages 290–307).
This file (part 1) covers PDF pages 290–293 (printed pp. 282–285), letters A through K (`kćer`).

**Source has no text layer — vision-read from page scans.** Pages were rendered at 150 DPI (and
300 DPI for a few ambiguous words) via `pdftoppm` and read visually. Per the extraction spec's
vision-reading guard: every page in this range was checked for handwritten marginalia (pencil/pen
annotation, underlining, previous-owner notes) distinct from the printed typeface. **None was found
on any page in this file's range** — all content is clean, consistently-typeset print.

**Format of this glossary (per the book's own header note, reproduced/paraphrased, not quoted in
full):** brackets show both Ekavian and Ijekavian variants of a headword (e.g. `bijeli (E beo, bela)`
= Ijekavian `bijeli`, Ekavian `beo`/`bela` for "white"). Words tagged `(C)` are specifically Croatian,
`(B)` Bosnian, `(S)` Serbian — these are regional-variant tags carried into the Attested Region
column below, with Geographic Scope `national` (distinguishing standard national varieties of the
same pluricentric language, matching the convention already established from the Alexander textbook
glossary in this dataset). Bracketed numbers after a gloss are the book's own lesson cross-references
and are retained in the Notes column as citations, not as frequency data (per the extraction spec:
lesson-ordering is not real frequency data).

**Columns held constant for every row below** (per dispatch instructions, matching the convention
used in files 026–028 for this dataset's other dictionary source): Weight/Frequency `—`; Attested Era
`contemporary (source published 1998)`; Source Type `dictionary`; Transcription Confidence `n/a`.
Vision Reading Confidence is set per-row; `verified` is used for words cross-checked either against
their own appearance elsewhere in the book's numbered lessons or against unambiguous print, and
`plausible_unverified` for entries read with reasonable confidence but no independent cross-check.
Only rows with genuine reading difficulty get `low_confidence`, called out explicitly.

**Note on a likely print erratum:** the entry `grad` (p. 284 / PDF p. 292) is printed with the gloss
**"own (4)"**. This was cross-checked at 300 DPI and the print is unambiguous — it genuinely says
"own," not "town." Since `grad` means "town/city" in Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian (not "own"), this
gloss is almost certainly a source-book erratum (a dropped leading "t"), not a mis-vision-read on
this extraction's part. Extracted as printed, with the correction noted in the Notes column, per the
principle of recording what the source actually says while flagging the discrepancy.
