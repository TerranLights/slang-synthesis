## Grammar points

### More about reflexive verbs (Unit 19, p. 271)

The reflexive pronoun `se` can give a verb an impersonal or general meaning ("one does this"/"you
do this"), sometimes conveying a passive-like sense ("how is this said?"). Source examples:
*Tamo se samo jede i pije* — "There one just eats and drinks"; *Tamo se slušaju starogradske pesme*
— "There one listens to old-time songs" / "You can listen to old-time songs there"; *To se zna* —
"That is known"; *Kako se kaže...?* — "How do you say...?"; *Ovde se govori engleski* — "English is
spoken here."

### Other tenses: aorist and pluperfect (Unit 19, pp. 271–272)

The **aorist**, formed from perfective verbs to express a completed past action, is now heard
infrequently but survives in **colloquial usage** with a restricted set of verbs — the source's own
example is *reći* "to say/tell," giving *rekoh* "I said" and *reče* "s/he said," used in conversation
in place of the longer, more conventional *rekao sam* / *rekao je*. This is an explicit
source-flagged register distinction (aorist forms marked as colloquial survivals vs. the standard
perfect-tense forms).

The **pluperfect** is usually expressed simply by adding *već* "already" to a perfective verb in the
past tense. A separate, dedicated pluperfect tense form exists (built from the verbal adjective of
*biti* plus the past tense) but the source notes it is "nowadays only rarely used." Example: *Jovan
je već stigao...* and *Jovan je bio stigao...* both translate as "Jovan had already arrived..." (the
second being the rarer, dedicated pluperfect construction).

### Three verbs meaning "to meet" (Unit 19, p. 272)

Serbian distinguishes three verbs for English "to meet" by context: *naći se* — to meet/have a date
(informal gathering); *sastati se* — to meet, convene (formal or business occasion); *sresti* /
*sresti se* — to meet, bump into (an accidental meeting).

### Extended usage of the preposition `kod` (Unit 19, p. 272)

*Kod* ("at the house of," + gen.) extends beyond its literal sense: *Naći ćemo se kod pozorišta* —
"We'll meet at the theatre" (kod = beside/in front of); *Šta ima novo kod tebe?* — "What's new with
you?" (kod = in your life/situation); *Nemam kod sebe njegov broj telefona* — "I don't have his
telephone number with me" (kod sebe = on my person).

### Reflexive verbs meaning "each other"/"one another" (Unit 19, p. 272)

Beyond the impersonal-reflexive use above, *videti se* and *pozdraviti se* are used reflexively to
mean reciprocal action: *Videćemo se* — "We'll see each other"; *Pozdravili smo se* — "We said our
goodbyes (to each other)."

### More about comparatives — attenuative use (Unit 20, p. 278)

Comparative adjective forms in Serbian are used not only to mean "bigger/more X" in a strict
comparison but also to convey "somewhat X" (an attenuative/approximative sense). Source examples:
*veći stan* — "a biggish flat"; *manji stan* — "a smallish flat"; *u užem centru* — "in the very
centre" (lit. "in the narrower centre"); *u širem centru* — "in the wider centre"; *na duži period*
— "for a long let, for a lengthy period."
