same spread as PDF page 52 (printed pp.86/87) — content identical, confirmed by direct comparison —

## Grammar points

### Locative case for "in" and "on" (Unit 7, p.75)

To say *in* or *on* somewhere, Serbian uses **u** or **na** with the locative (loc.) case. Regular
masc. nouns add **-u** (park → parku); fem. nouns in **-a** change **-a** to **-i** (ulica → ulici);
neut. nouns in **-o/-e** change to **-u** (pozorište → pozorištu).

### Spelling rule: k, g, h → c, z, s (Unit 7, p.76)

A noun cannot end its stem in **k, g, h** immediately before an **-i** ending. These three
consonants change to **c, z, s** respectively when a case ending beginning with **-i** is added:
apoteka → **u apoteci** (loc. sg.), banka → u banci, Amerika → u Americi. Exception: personal
names keep -k-/-c- shift too (Branka → Branki) but the rule is stated as applying broadly, with
proper names as ordinary instances rather than true exceptions. The same shift governs masc. nom.
plural in -i: jezik → jezici, kiosk → kiosci, Grk → Grci.

### Monosyllabic masculine nouns add a syllable in the plural (Unit 7, p.76)

park → parkovi, trg → trgovi — an epenthetic **-ov-**/**-ev-** is inserted before the plural
ending for most one-syllable masc. nouns.

### Three uses of case (Unit 7, p.77)

Cases serve three functions in Serbian: (1) marking a purely grammatical role, such as acc. for the
direct object; (2) carrying a meaning that would be a preposition in English, such as gen. meaning
"of"; (3) appearing after a preposition (the nominative never follows a preposition), such as loc.
after u/na to mark location. A single phrase can combine more than one case use — e.g. *u centru
grada* "in the centre of town" has loc. (after u) and gen. (grada, "of the town") simultaneously.

### Irregular verb *ja sam* "to be" (Unit 7, p.77)

Full present-tense paradigm, short/long/negative forms:

| Person | Short | Long | Negative |
|---|---|---|---|
| ja | sam | jesam | nisam |
| ti | si | jesi | nisi |
| on | je | jeste | nije |
| mi | smo | jesmo | nismo |
| vi | ste | jeste | niste |
| oni | su | jesu | nisu |

The short form cannot begin a sentence or clause. The long form is used for one-word answers to
questions (Da, jeste.) and for asking questions (Jesmo li blizu škole?), except in 3rd sg. where the
short form je is used even in questions (Je li to gospođa Petrović?).

### Question formation: da li / li (Unit 7, p.78)

Questions can be formed either by putting **da li** before the verb, or by putting **li** directly
after the long form of the verb.

### Possessive adjectives (Unit 7, p.78)

Full paradigm, agreeing in gender with the noun they describe: moj/moja/moje ("my"), tvoj/tvoja/tvoje
("your," sg.), njegov/njegova/njegovo ("his"), njen/njena/njeno ("her"), naš/naša/naše ("our"),
vaš/vaša/vaše ("your," pl./formal), njihov/njihova/njihovo ("their"). Neuter ending is **-o**, or
**-e** after a soft consonant (soft consonants: c, č, ć, dž, đ, j, lj, nj, š, ž).

### Demonstrative *onaj* "that" (far away) vs. *taj* "that" (Unit 7, p.78)

onaj/ona/ono refers to something/someone far from *both* speaker and listener, distinct from taj/ta/
to (introduced earlier, nearer to the listener).

### Reflexive verbs with *se* (Unit 7, p.79)

Some verbs take the particle **se** ("oneself"), which can translate as myself/yourself/etc.
depending on person: Kako se zoveš? (lit. "How do you call yourself?"), Zovem se... ("My name
is...," lit. "I call myself...").

### Noun subgroup: *posao* "work, job" (Unit 7, p.84)

Some masc. nouns ending in **-o** historically ended in **-l**; the **-l** reappears when a case
ending is added and the **-a** ("moveable a") drops out: nom./acc. sg. posao, gen. sg. posla, ins.
sg. poslom, loc. sg. poslu; nom./acc. pl. poslovi/poslove, gen. pl. poslova.

### Going to somewhere: acc. vs. loc. with u/na (Unit 8, p.91)

u/na + loc. = location ("I am in/on"); u/na + acc. = motion toward ("I am going to"). Ja sam u
restoranu ("I am in the restaurant") vs. Idem u restoran ("I am going to the restaurant"). **kuda**
is used only to ask about direction of motion (with acc.); **gde** can mean both "where are you" and
"where are you going."

### Use of *na* beyond simple "on" (Unit 8, p.92)

**na** (rather than u) is used for the post office, points of the compass, bus/railway station,
university/faculty buildings, and for meals/drinks: na pošti, na fakultetu, na doručku/ručku/večeri,
na kafu/piće.

### Imperatives (Unit 8, p.93)

Formed from the 3rd person plural present tense (they-form) minus the final letter, giving the stem.
If the stem ends in **-j**, the informal (ti) form is the stem itself unchanged (pogledaj); if it
ends in a consonant, add **-i** (idi, skreni). The formal (vi) form adds **-te** to the informal form
(pogledajte, idite, skrenite).

### Ordinal numbers (Unit 8, p.94)

Ordinals are adjectives agreeing in gender with their noun: prvi/prva/prvo, drugi, treći/treća/treće*
(*treći has a soft ending, taking -e in neuter), četvrti, peti, šesti, sedmi, osmi. Higher ordinals
are formed by adding adjective endings directly to the cardinal number (deveti "ninth," etc.); masc.
nom. sg. ending is -i.

### Vocative case (Unit 8, p.95)

Used when addressing someone directly; in practice mostly limited to personal names and nouns for
people. Masc. nouns add -e (Roberte), or -u after a soft consonant (Gospodine Jovanoviću) — р is
treated as soft for this purpose (конобару! "waiter!"). Fem. nouns change -a to -o (gospođo), or
-a to -e for nouns in -ica (gospođice); many fem. personal names stay unchanged (Vesna → Vesna,
voc.). Neut. nouns don't change. Plural voc. = plural nom. for all genders. Spelling shift for
masc. names/nouns in the voc. sg.: -г → -ж, -к → -ч, -х → -ш (Predrag → Predraže, Vuk → Vuče).

### More prepositions of place with genitive (Unit 8, p.95)

pored, preko puta, između, ispod, iznad, blizu, daleko od (already introduced), plus newly added
**iza** ("behind") and **ispred** ("in front of"). Expressions: sa desne/leve strane, na kraju
ulice, na ćošku, iza ćoška.

### Instrumental case with transport (Unit 8, p.100)

The ins. case expresses "by means of": ići autobusom "to go by bus." Instrumental forms of transport
nouns: tramvajem (soft ending), trolejbusom, autom, vozom, taksijem. Taksi is a borrowed foreign word
and doesn't follow the normal masc. pattern — it's masc. but adds -j- before case endings for ease of
pronunciation (nom./acc. taksi, gen. taksija, ins. taksijem, loc. taksiju).

### Negative commands (Unit 8, p.100)

Formed with **nemoj**/**nemojte** (informal/formal) + infinitive, or (more often) with the
alternative da + present-tense construction: nemoj ići = nemoj da ideš; nemojte ići = nemojte da
idete.

### Modal verbs (Unit 8, p.100)

Verbs that precede and govern another verb, which usually takes the alternative da + present-tense
infinitive form: hoću da... ("I want to"), mogu da... ("I may/am able to"), moram da... ("I must,
have to"), treba da... ("I should, ought to"; treba is impersonal/invariant, only ever this one
form).

### Animate masculine nouns (Unit 8, p.101)

The acc. sg. ending for masc. nouns normally matches the nom. But when the noun refers to a human or
animal (any animate being — people, animals, fish, insects), the acc. sg. instead matches the gen.
sg. This applies only in the singular; plural acc. is unaffected. Compare: Vesna vidi park
(inanimate, acc. = nom.) vs. Vesna vidi Roberta (animate, acc. = gen.).

### More about genitive plurals (Unit 8, p.101–102)

Fem. nouns generally form gen. pl. by taking the nom. pl. and changing -e to -a (kafe → kafa,
established in Unit 5). When the resulting stem (word minus case ending) ends in two consonants, an
extra **-a-** is inserted between them in all genders: Amerikanac → Amerikanaca, banka → banaka,
pismo → pisama. Fem. nouns like stvar ("thing") that already end the stem in a consonant take gen.
pl. in **-и**: stvari. Some fem. nouns in -a with a two-consonant stem end also take this -и gen.
pl. ending rather than inserting -a-: radnja → radnje (nom. pl.) → radnji (gen. pl.).

### Forming the past tense: infinitives in -ti (Unit 9, p.106)

The past tense uses a verbal adjective (from the infinitive) plus the auxiliary verb biti. For
infinitives ending in -ti, remove the final two letters to get the stem, then add gender/number
endings: putovati → putova- → putovao (masc. sg.), putovala (fem. sg.), putovalo (neut. sg.);
plural putovali (masc.), putovale (fem.), putovala (neut.). Mixed-gender groups of people take the
masc. plural ending. All verbs ending in vowel + ti follow this pattern (dobiti → dobio, biti → bio,
čekati → čekao, kupiti → kupio).

### Forming the past tense: infinitives in -ći (Unit 9, p.107)

Verbs with infinitives in -ći have stems not directly predictable from the infinitive and must be
learned individually — e.g. ići "to go" → išao/išla/išlo, išli/išle/išla; stići "to arrive" → stigao/
stigla/stiglo, stigli/stigle/stigla; proći "to pass, go through" → prošao/prošla/prošlo, prošli/
prošle/prošla. Endings are the same as the -ti pattern, but the penultimate -a- of the stem
disappears in some forms.

### Auxiliary verb agreement and omission (Unit 9, p.108)

The auxiliary (biti) form matches the subject's person/number. When a sentence has two or more verbs
sharing the same subject, the auxiliary can be omitted from the second verb's past-tense form (only
stated once).

### Word order: short forms of *biti* cannot open a sentence/clause (Unit 9, p.108)

Because the short (clitic) forms of biti cannot come first, sentences whose logical subject is the
verbal adjective must restructure — e.g. Marko Pavlović je putovao... (subject-first, aux mid-
clause) vs. Dobio je... (verbal adjective first, since there is no other word to open the clause).
Serbian word order is governed by case marking, not fixed subject-verb-object order, so a
noun's syntactic role must be read from its case ending rather than its position: Na izlazu je čekao
Stevan (Stevan, nom., subject) vs. Na izlazu je Marko čekao Stevana (Stevana, acc., object).

### Use of *svoj* (Unit 9, p.108–109)

English "his/her/their" can refer either to one's own possession or to someone else's; Serbian
disambiguates with **svoj**, used when the possessor is the same as the sentence's subject (refers
back to the subject): Dobio sam svoj kofer ("I collected my own suitcase") vs. Dobio sam njen kofer
("I collected her [someone else's] suitcase"). svoj/svoja/svoje declines like other possessive
adjectives.

### Past-tense questions (Unit 9, p.110)

Formed either with **Da li...** + short form of biti, or **li** placed after the long form: Da li
ste dobro putovali? / Jeste li dobili moje pismo? A bare "yes" answer uses the long form alone: Da,
jesam.

### Agreement with formal *vi* (Unit 9, p.110)

When addressing one person formally as vi, verb and adjective agreement is always masc. plural, even
when addressing a woman: Kada ste poslali pismo? / Da li ste vi gladni?

### Past-tense negation (Unit 9, p.110)

Formed with the negative form of biti (nisam, nisi, nije...) plus the verbal adjective. Unlike the
short affirmative forms, the negative aux forms are not clitics and normally precede the verbal
adjective: Nisam dobio vaše pismo.

### Verbal aspect (Unit 9, p.111)

Most Serbian verbs come in imperfective/perfective pairs. Imperfective verbs indicate an action in
progress or done habitually/frequently; perfective verbs indicate a completed (or to-be-completed)
action. Example pair piti/popiti "to drink": Pili su kafu i razgovarali ("They were drinking coffee
and chatting" — ongoing/simultaneous) vs. Popili su kafu i krenuli ("They drank their coffee up and
set off" — completed, sequential). Adding **već** ("already") to a perfective past-tense verb
produces an English-pluperfect-like meaning: već su popili... "they had [already] drunk...". Aspect
choice matters across the past, future, imperative, and other forms, not just the simple past.

### Telling the time (Unit 9, p.115–116)

sat = "hour, o'clock" (gen. sg. sata, gen. pl. sati). Minutes past the hour are added directly
(Jedan i pet = 1.05); the word for "minute" (minut) is usually omitted. Half past is expressed as
"halfway to [next hour]": pola sedam = 6.30. Minutes before the hour count down using **do**:
dvadeset pet do sedam = 6.35, deset do deset = 9.50.

### 24-hour clock (Unit 9, p.116)

Used for timetables and official announcements. In this context "hour/o'clock" is **čas** rather
than sat (gen. sg. časa, gen. pl. časova): Let je u četrnaest časova i trideset minuta ("The flight
is at 14.30").
