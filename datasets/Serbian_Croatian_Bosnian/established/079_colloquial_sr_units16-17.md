(Routledge, 2006). Units 16–17, printed pages 217–245 (PDF pages 113–128 of `source_reference/languages/

The actual, verified mapping is `left printed page = 2×(PDF page) − 10`, confirmed against three

start; PDF p128 = printed 246/247, Unit 18 start). Unit 16 begins on the right-hand page of PDF p113
(printed 217) and Unit 18 begins on the left-hand page of PDF p128 (printed 246); this chunk covers PDF
pages 113–127 inclusive (printed 217–245), stopping before Unit 18 ("Долазак у Котор" / "Dolazak u
Kotor" / Arrival in Kotor) as instructed.

**Non-redundant supplement note.** This chunk overlaps heavily with `070_colloquial_hr_sr_units16-18.md`
(Hawkesworth's earlier *Colloquial Croatian and Serbian*, 1998, same Units 16–18 by topic: breakdown,
shopping, road accident/arrival). Per the extraction spec's non-redundant-supplement pattern:

- **Grammar is almost entirely redundant** — this later solo-Serbian edition reuses the same possessive-
  pronoun paradigms (moj/tvoj/svoj, naš/vaš, njegov/njen/njihov), the same relative/interrogative pronoun
  material, the same negative-compound-pronoun-plus-preposition rule (even the same worked example, "Не
  виђа се ни с ким" / "Ne viđa se ni s kim"), and — most strikingly — the **same Aspect 2 worked example
  sentence verbatim** ("Већ шест месеци купују нову кућу, и сад су је коначно купили" = 070's "Već šest
  mjeseci kupuju novu kuću; sad su je konačno kupili"), confirming the two books share a direct textual
  lineage. Grammar points below are therefore kept brief, citing only what's genuinely new or different,
  not re-explaining paradigms 070 already covers in full.
- **Vocabulary is NOT redundant** despite topic overlap: this is a different, single-language Serbian
  edition, printed entirely in **Cyrillic** and consistently **ekavian** (e.g. `решење`/rešenje, `сервис`,
  `седиште`), where 070 is a bridging Croatian/Serbian edition in Latin script with mixed/ijekavian forms
  (e.g. `sjedalo`, `riješenje`-type forms). The lexical choices, example sentences, and even some
  terminology differ (e.g. this book's car-parts diagram gives `седиште` for "seat" where 070 gives
  Croatian `sjedalo`; this book's interrogative pronoun is `ко/шта` where 070's Croatian-leaning edition
  used `tko/što`). The full vocabulary is extracted below as genuinely new data, with the ekavian/
  Cyrillic vs. 070's ijekavian/Latin contrast flagged inline where directly comparable.
- **One genuinely new lexical item not seen in 070:** the car-parts diagram (p226) glosses the boot/trunk
  as `пртљажник/гепек` — offering **`гепек`** as an unmarked colloquial-register alternative (from German
  *Gepäck*), not present in 070's car-vocabulary list at all. Also notable: this book explicitly supplies
  **British/American English dual glosses** for two car parts — `хауба` "bonnet **[hood]**" and
  `пртљажник/гепек` "boot **[trunk]**" — a small but real difference in translation register from 070,
  which glossed only British terms.
