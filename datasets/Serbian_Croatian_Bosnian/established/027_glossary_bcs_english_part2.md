# Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian — Established Vocabulary: BCS-English Glossary, Part 2 of 5

**Source:** Ronelle Alexander, *Bosnian, Croatian, Serbian, a Textbook: With Exercises and Basic
Grammar* (2nd edition, 2010), BCS-English Glossary, PDF pages 424–435 (printed pages 403–414),
headwords *hvala* (continuation) through *napasti* (partial, cut off at page break).

**Coverage note:** every distinct headword entry in this page range is extracted exhaustively,
including cross-reference entries ("see X", "see under X") since the source glossary lists these
as separate alphabetized entries. This source has a real PDF text layer (confirmed via `pdftotext`)
— no vision-reading was needed, so the vision-reading marginalia guard does not apply; all content
is genuinely printed/typeset. One extraction caveat: the PDF's own metadata records it as processed
through a third-party "Advanced PDF Repair" tool, and the two-column glossary layout produced a
handful of column-reconstruction artifacts when converting to plain text (a few lines' worth of
text momentarily displaced between columns). These were cross-checked against multiple independent
extraction passes and resolved by hand; no content was lost, but flagging this per the extraction
spec's confidence-tracking discipline.

A few entries carry an apparent gender-marking inconsistency as printed in the source (e.g. *ispit*
marked "f" though the word is ordinarily masculine; *jogurt* marked "n"; *kuvar* marked "f" despite
its own gloss reading "(m., general)"; *lešinar* marked "f"; *luk* "onion/garlic" marked "n" despite
an *-ovi* plural typical of masculine nouns). These are reproduced as printed and flagged in the
`Notes` column rather than silently corrected — they may be print/OCR artifacts in this particular
copy rather than errors in the original edition.

Dialect/register tags reproduced exactly as marked by the source: `[B]` Bosnian, `[C]` Croatian,
`[S]` Serbian, `[B,C]`/`[B,S]`/`[C,S]`/`[B,C,S]` combinations, `[E]` Ekavian, `[J]` (I)jekavian.
Where a headword carries no tag, the term is presumed common to all three standards.
