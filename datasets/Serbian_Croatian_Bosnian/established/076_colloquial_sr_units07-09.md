# Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Colloquial Serbian, Units 7-9
("U pošti" / "U restoranu" / "U turističkoj agenciji")

**Source:** Celia Hawkesworth with Jelena Ćalić, *Colloquial Serbian: The Complete Course for
Beginners* (Routledge, 2006). Units 7-9, printed pages 90-131 (PDF pages 50-70 of
`source_reference/languages/Serbian, Croatian, Bosnian/Colloquial Serbian/Colloquial Serbian.pdf`).
Unit 10 ("Putovanje autobusom" / "Bus journey") begins exactly at printed page 132 (PDF page 71)
and is excluded from this chunk. **Note on PDF-page mapping:** this PDF's pages are two-page-spread
scans (one PDF page image = two printed pages side by side), and the front matter has ~10 unnumbered
pages before printed page 1 begins, so the offset is non-constant — verified directly against
printed page numbers visible on each scanned image rather than assumed from a fixed arithmetic
offset.

**Non-redundant-supplement check (per `00_Reference_Extraction_Spec.md`):** checked
`established/067_colloquial_hr_sr_units07-09.md` first (Hawkesworth's earlier *Colloquial Croatian
and Serbian*, 1998). Important finding: **the unit numbering/topic assignment does not line up
between the two books.** 067's Units 7-9 cover "U restoranu" (restaurant) / "Na ulici" (street) /
"Posjet prijateljima" (visiting friends). This book's Units 7-9 cover "U pošti" (post office) /
"U restoranu" (restaurant) / "U turističkoj agenciji" (tourist agent's) — a *different* three-unit
slice of the same overall course structure. Only this book's **Unit 8 (restaurant)** genuinely
overlaps in topic with 067's Unit 7 (restaurant); this book's Unit 7 (post office) and Unit 9
(tourist agency) have **no topical overlap with 067 at all** — 067's own Units 8-9 covered street
navigation and a friends' visit, not the post office or a travel agency.

**Skipped as redundant** (Unit 8 only, matched directly against 067's Unit 7 grammar/vocabulary):
full re-derivation of the genitive/accusative/dative-locative/instrumental adjective declension
tables (identical endings: masc./neut. def. `-og(a)`/`-eg(a)` genitive, `-u` fem. accusative,
`-om`/`-oj` dative-locative, `-im`/`-om` instrumental, `-ih` genitive plural, `-im(a)` dative-
locative-instrumental plural); the partitive genitive rule (`Imate li hleba/kruha?`); the locative-
vs-instrumental preposition lists (`k/ka, prema, pri, o, u, na` for locative; `među, nad, pod, za,
pred` for instrumental, with the same note that `između, iznad, ispod, iza, ispred` + genitive are
preferred in practice); and the reflexive-impersonal "one does X" construction (`Kako se ide...?` /
`Ovde/Ovdje se ulazi...` — near-identical example sentence in both books). These are all covered in
067 with equivalent examples; re-extracting them here would add no new information.

**Kept as genuinely new:** (1) **the entire cardinal-number system** (0 to 5,000,000) and the
number-agreement rules (1 declines as an ordinary adjective and can function as an indefinite
article; 2/3/4 + adjective-and-noun-in-genitive-singular; 5+ + genitive plural; verb agreement
patterns) — this content does not exist anywhere in 067's chunk at all; (2) **the entire post office
unit** (Unit 7) — vocabulary, dialogues, and a real telephone-card realia item — with zero topical
precedent in 067; (3) **the entire tourist-agency unit** (Unit 9) — street-direction vocabulary, a
real Belgrade street-map realia item, and a substantial Belgrade-geography reading passage — again
with zero topical precedent in 067; (4) a real "Restoran Balkan" menu realia item with specifically
Serbian dish names (ćevapi, pljeskavica, karađorđeva šnicla, špricer) not present in 067's Croatian
"Trocadero" menu; (5) a set of **Serbian-Ekavian/Croatian-Ijekavian lexical doublets** surfaced by
direct comparison with 067's Croatian-sourced forms (e.g. this book's `sećati se`/`vreme`/`uveče` vs.
067's `sjećati se`/`vrijeme`/`navečer`), and a set of **Serbian/Croatian synonym pairs** for the same
referent (`paradajz`/`biber`/`sirće`/`kajsija` here vs. 067's `rajčica`/`papar`/`ocat`/`marelica`) —
directly useful comparative data for this project even though the underlying concepts are already
covered; (6) one **explicit source-marked colloquial-register annotation** (`escajg`, marked
"(coll.)" by the source itself) not present in 067; (7) an **explicit dialectal annotation**: the
source directly notes that two characters (Sanja and Dado) "speak the Bosnian Ijekavian dialect,
using 'j' and 'ije' where Belgrade speech has 'e'" — a genuine `[B]`-vs-Belgrade-speech register/
dialect flag of exactly the high-value kind this spec prioritizes.

**Vision-reading guard:** this source has no usable text layer (scanned book) and was fully
vision-read at 150dpi, page by page, PDF pages 50-70 (printed pages 90-131) in sequence with no
gaps. Every page was actively checked for handwritten marginalia (pencil/pen notes, underlines,
filled-in exercise answers in a different hand from the printed type). **No marginalia was found on
any page in this range** — all content is clean, consistently typeset printed material (Cyrillic
dialogue/vocabulary text and Latin-script explanatory prose are both uniform throughout, matching
this book's dual-script presentation). Accordingly, no content was excluded on marginalia grounds.

**Coverage note:** every grammar point and essentially every distinct vocabulary item in Units 7 and
9 is captured below (post office and tourist-agency content, entirely new). For Unit 8 (restaurant),
only genuinely new/distinct vocabulary items (new dishes, Serbian-specific spellings/synonyms not in
067, explicitly source-flagged register items) are captured; the large food-vocabulary-building
list that duplicates 067's near-identical list is intentionally not re-listed except where the
Serbian form differs lexically or orthographically from 067's Croatian form. Drill/exercise
vocabulary was captured only where it introduced items not already listed from dialogue/reading
content.

## Grammar points

### Cardinal numbers (Unit 7, p.92-93) — genuinely new content, no precedent in 067

Full cardinal-number system given: 0 `nula`; 1 `jedan/jedna/jedno`; 2 `dva/dve`; 3 `tri`; 4 `četiri`;
5 `pet`; 6 `šest`; 7 `sedam`; 8 `osam`; 9 `devet`; 10 `deset`; 11-19 formed with `-naest`
(`jedanaest`...`devetnaest`); 20 `dvadeset`; tens 30-90 formed with `-deset` (`trideset`,
`četrdeset`, `pedeset`, `šezdeset`, `sedamdeset`, `osamdeset`, `devedeset`); compound numbers may
optionally insert `i` (`dvadeset jedan` or `dvadeset i jedan`); hundreds 100-500 have two forms
(`sto`/`stotina`, `dvesto`/`dve stotine`, etc.); thousands use `hiljada`/`hiljade`; millions use
`milion`/`miliona`. p.92-93.

**Number 1 (`jedan`):** declines as an ordinary adjective (like `umoran`) and can function as an
indefinite article (`jedan čovek` "a man", `jedna žena` "a woman"). It also has plural forms used
with pluralia tantum or collective nouns: `jedni ljudi` "some people", `jedne cipele` "a pair of
shoes", `jedna vrata` "a door" (`vrata` is neuter plural). p.93.

**Numbers 2, 3, 4** (and any compound ending in 2, 3, or 4, e.g. 22, 23, 24, 92, 104): followed by
the **genitive singular** of the noun (`dva mladića`, `dvadeset tri putnika`, `četiri sestre`); any
accompanying adjective takes the same genitive-singular-matching ending as the noun. The number 2
itself has distinct masculine (`dva`) and feminine (`dve`) forms, plus a neuter form for mixed-gender
groups (`dvoje dece`, `dvoje zaljubljenih`) — the book notes true declined forms of 2/3/4 exist but
are "hardly used in the modern language." p.93-94.

**Numbers 5 and above:** followed by the **genitive plural** (`pet mladića`, `šest devojaka`, `sedam
sestara`, `osam sinova`, `devet pisama`, `deset maraka`). The verb accompanying such a numbered
subject is normally **singular** (`Dvadeset šest putnika čeka autobus` "Twenty-six travellers are
waiting for the bus" — literally singular verb agreement despite the plural sense). p.94, p.97.
