# Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: BCS-English Glossary, Part 3 of 5

**Source:** Ronelle Alexander, *Bosnian, Croatian, Serbian, a Textbook: With Exercises and Basic
Grammar* (2nd edition, 2010), BCS-English Glossary, PDF pages 436–447 (printed pages 415–426),
covering headwords from **nàpadati** through **posvètiti** (the *na-* stretch of N through the
early *pos-* stretch of P).

**Coverage note:** this is a dense two-column alphabetical dictionary glossary with essentially
zero pedagogical padding — every distinct headword entry in the assigned page range was extracted
exhaustively (791 entries), not sampled. This source has a genuine embedded text layer
(`pdftotext` produced clean, complete text), so no vision-reading was required and the
vision-reading/marginalia guard in `../../00_Reference_Extraction_Spec.md` does not apply — no
handwritten annotations were encountered or excluded. The glossary is printed in two columns per
page; the source text was extracted column-by-column (not raw reading order) to preserve correct
alphabetical sequence, and cross-checked against the raw combined extraction to confirm no entries
were dropped or duplicated at column/page boundaries. The final entry, **posvètiti**, is truncated
mid-entry at the end of PDF page 447 (the boundary of this chunk); its continuation falls on the
next PDF page, outside this dispatch's assigned range, and is left for the next chunk (part 4).

**Dialect/register tag legend** (every tag is reproduced exactly as marked in the source, in the
`Attested Region` column below): `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]` mark a headword or sense as specific to the
Bosnian/Croatian/Serbian standard variety; `[B,C]`, `[B,S]`, `[C,S]`, `[B,C,S]` mark combinations
of those; `[E]`/`[J]` mark the Ekavian vs. (I)jekavian reflex of the Common Slavic yat vowel — a
phonological/regional split that in practice correlates with geography (Ekavian centers on Serbia;
Jekavian covers Bosnia, Croatia, and Montenegro) rather than with the B/C/S standard-language split
itself, which is why both an `[E]` and a `[J]` headword can each also carry a `[B,S]`-type tag.
Untagged entries carry no explicit regional marking in the source and are left as `—` in both the
`Attested Region` and `Geographic Scope` columns per the extraction spec (no regionality should be
inferred where the source itself does not mark it). Where the source stacks two tags on one entry
(e.g. `nèdjeljni adj. [J] [B/S] weekly`), both are reproduced together.

**Cross-reference entries** ("see under X") are retained as their own table rows exactly as they
appear as separate headwords in the glossary (this is how the source itself lists aspectual verb
pairs and alternate forms), with the gloss column noting the cross-reference rather than a
translation.

## Grammar points

N/A — this chunk is a dictionary glossary section (BCS-English Glossary, part 3 of 5), not
grammar-point instructional content from a lesson chapter. The source's own dialectal/register
annotation system (the `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]` national-standard tags and the `[E]`/`[J]`
Ekavian/Jekavian reflex tags) is captured per-entry in the `Attested Region` and `Geographic
Scope` columns of the Vocabulary table above, per the extraction spec's instruction to never skip
explicit dialectal/regional annotations. Two recurring morphological patterns worth noting for
later synthesis work, though they are lexical rather than grammar-chapter content:

- **Aspectual pairing.** The overwhelming majority of verb entries in this range are listed as
  imperfective/perfective pairs (e.g. `napùstiti (P) / napùštati (I)` "to abandon"), or as one
  member of the pair with a cross-reference to the other (e.g. `nàstavljati` "see under
  nàstaviti"). Several biaspectual loan-derived verbs (`organizìrati`/`orgànizovati` "to
  organize", `operìrati`/`opèrisati` "to operate") are marked `(I/P)` rather than split.
- **Systematic E/J lexical doubling.** Because Ekavian and (I)jekavian reflect the same
  underlying yat vowel differently, a large fraction of headwords in this range appear as
  near-duplicate pairs distinguished only by that vowel and tagged accordingly (e.g.
  `nèdelja`[E]/`nèdjelja`[J] "Sunday, week"; `posle`[E]/`poslije`[J] "after"; `òsetiti`[E]/
  `òsjetiti`[J] "to feel"). This is not free variation — it is the single most systematic
  regional-tagging pattern in the whole glossary and should be treated as a structural feature of
  the lexicon, not noise, in any later cross-reference or frequency-comparison analysis.
