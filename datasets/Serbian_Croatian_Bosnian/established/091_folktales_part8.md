# Serbian — Established Vocabulary: Serbian Folk Fairy Tales, "Пустоловни додатак" (Adventure
Appendix / Afterword Essay), printed pp. 201–222

**Source:** *Српске народне бајке* [Serbian Folk Fairy Tales], Kreativni centar, Belgrade, 2000.
Vision-read from PDF pages 205–226 (printed pages 201–222; empirically confirmed offset PDF = printed
+ 4, matching the predicted offset). Scanned book, no text layer — read entirely by vision.

**Coverage note / important structural correction.** This dispatch was framed as covering "the final
narrative content before the glossary," expecting more fairy-tale titles in the manner of the earlier
chunks. **That premise did not hold for this specific page range.** The narrative tales in this book
end before printed p. 201. What occupies printed pp. 201–222 is a distinct, non-narrative back-matter
essay section titled **"Пустоловни додатак"** ("Adventure Appendix," the same "Пустоловне" library
branding as the book's marginal illustration of a coated traveler figure that runs down the outer
margin of every page in this section) — an expository essay *about* fairy tales as a genre (their
origin, Vuk Karadžić's collecting methods, dialect variants, folklorists' and writers' quotes about
fairy tales, a suggested Proppian plot-structure exercise, and a bibliography), not more fairy tales
themselves. **No tale titles exist in this range** — see the "Section headers found" list below
instead. Two boundary findings worth flagging to whoever owns the neighboring chunks:
- A new tale title, **"ТАМНИ ВИЛАЈЕТ"** ("The Dark Vilajet/Realm"), appears at PDF p. 203 (≈ printed
  p. 197–199, just *before* this dispatch's range) with its own "+"-marked margin glosses (`вилајет`,
  `оставити ждребад од кобила`, `драго камење`) in the book's usual tale-page format — this belongs to
  whichever chunk covers printed pp. ~197–200, not this one. Flagging since the original TOC didn't
  list it and a sibling subagent's territory ended around "Ћела"/p.193–197.
- The actual glossary **"Мање познате речи и изрази које се помињу у овој књизи"** does **not** begin
  at printed p. 223 as estimated. Printed p. 223 (PDF p. 227) is instead a **promotional excerpt from
  a different book** in the same publisher's series ("Српске народне приповетке," Библиотека
  Пустоловине) — a full short tale, "ЧОБАНЧЕ ПРЕВАРИЛО ПОПА" ("The Shepherd Boy Outwitted the
  Priest"), printed as an ad for that other title, not part of *this* book's own content. The real
  glossary begins at **printed p. 224 (PDF p. 228)** — and turns out to be a bare page-reference index
  (word → page number only, no definitions), not a defining glossary. Both of these are outside this
  dispatch's assigned upper bound (printed p. 222) and are left for whichever chunk is assigned that
  territory.

**Coverage approach.** Per instructions, no plot/prose reproduction — this section is expository essay
prose anyway, not narrative, so the same discipline applies: vocabulary + a one-line orientation note
per subsection, not paraphrase of the essay's arguments. This section carries no "+"-marked margin
glosses of its own (that convention belongs to the tale pages, not this essay); its own inline
word-glosses (found on printed p. 219, where the book explicitly defines several archaic/dialectal
fairy-tale words in-line as illustrative examples) are captured below using the book's own given
definitions. Remaining vocabulary is drawn from the essay's own archaic/regional prose and from short
illustrative quotations it reproduces (an 1853 Vuk Karadžić dialectal recording of "Пепељуга," a
Grujo Mehandžić manuscript excerpt, Vuk's own correspondence) — quoted minimally and selectively per
copyright discipline, never bulk-reproduced.

**Vision-reading guard:** every page in this range was checked for handwritten marginalia. **None
found** — all content on every page (PDF 207–226) is consistent typeset print, matching the
surrounding printed material. No exclusions needed.
