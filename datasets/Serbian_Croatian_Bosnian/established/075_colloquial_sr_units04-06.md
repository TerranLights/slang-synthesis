# Serbian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Colloquial Serbian, Units 4–6

**Source:** Celia Hawkesworth with Jelena Ćalić, *Colloquial Serbian: The Complete Course for
Beginners* (Routledge, 2006). Unit 4 ("Odlazak u grad" / Going into the city), Unit 5 ("U Beogradu"
/ In Belgrade), Unit 6 ("Razgovor o stanu" / Conversation about a flat). Printed pages 51–89.

PDF page image is a scanned two-page spread. Verified formula: `printed_left_page = 2×pdf_page − 10`
(checked at multiple points, e.g. pdf p.30→printed 50/51, pdf p.47→printed 84/85, pdf p.50→printed
90/91). Unit 4 title page = PDF p.30 (printed p.51, right side of spread showing printed 50/51).
Unit 7 ("U pošti") title page = PDF p.50 (printed p.90), confirming Unit 6 ends at PDF p.49

**Coverage note — non-redundant supplement dispatch.** This book's own acknowledgements state it
"grew out of" *Colloquial Croatian and Serbian* (Hawkesworth, 1998), already extracted as
`established/065-073`. Units 4–6 of *this* book overlap partially with Units 4–6 of that one
(`066_colloquial_hr_sr_units04-06.md`):

- **Unit 6 ("Razgovor o stanu" / "Conversation about a flat")** has the *identical title* to the
  Croatian/Serbian book's Unit 6 and is heavily redundant: the adjective grammar (definite/indefinite
  masculine, mobile *a*, neuter/feminine nominative, adverbs-from-adjectives), the flat/furniture
  vocabulary-building lists (jednospratnica/dvospratnica, zid/krov/vrata/prozor/dimnjak,
  dušek/jorgan/posteljina/ćebe/jastuk, sporet/rerna/frižider, etc.), and the floor-plan diagram labels
  are essentially the same lesson content already captured in `066`. **These were skipped as
  redundant** except where this book's version differs lexically or adds new material (see below).
- **Units 4 and 5 ("Odlazak u grad" / "U Beogradu")** cover different ground from the Croatian book's
  Units 4–5 ("Svatko ide na svoju stranu" / "Dolazak u hotel" — arrival, hotel check-in) and are
  **genuinely new**: Belgrade-specific toponymy (Kalemegdan, Knez Mihailova, Zeleni venac, Dorćol,
  Zemun), the accusative/genitive/dative/locative/instrumental case system built around Belgrade
  city-tour dialogues rather than airport/hotel ones, and functional real-estate/rental register not
  present in the earlier book at all. **Kept in full.**
- Within Unit 6, genuinely new items were kept: lexical variants not in `066`'s furniture/building
  list (**kauč** "couch/sofa" — absent from the Croatian book's list; **bašta** "garden", the Serbian
  form used on this book's own floor-plan diagram, vs. the Croatian **vrt** used in `066`'s
  floor-plan), an explicit archaic-vs-current register flag for a word already listed in `066`
  (**kovčeg** flagged here as archaic, "you should use **kofer**" — `066` listed kovčeg without this
  flag), a functional real-estate classified-ad dialogue (renting a flat: kirija, oglas, izdavati,
  nenamešten, centralno grejanje, kvadratni metar) entirely absent from `066`, and a Belgrade
  neighborhood name (Dorćol) not previously attested.

**Vision-reading guard: this PDF has no usable text layer (scanned book) — every page in this range
was vision-read directly.** Every page was actively checked for handwritten marginalia (pencil/pen
annotation, underlines, filled-in exercise answers from a previous owner). **None was found** — all

page. No exclusions were necessary. Coverage rule followed: every distinct vocabulary item and
grammar point in the non-redundant portions was extracted; repeated drill exercises reusing already-

## Grammar points

Note: the case-system grammar in Units 4–6 of this book (accusative formation, accusative-governing
prepositions, genitive formation and its use after ~80% of prepositions, `nema`+gen./`evo`+gen.,
dative formation and dative-governing verbs, the locative/dative-identical endings and k+i→ci /
g+i→zi alternations, country-names-as-adjectives in -ska/-čka/-ška, the instrumental case and its
`s`/`sa` preposition, masculine adjective definite/indefinite forms, "mobile a", and adverbs derived
from neuter adjectives) is **substantively the same system already captured in full** in
`066_colloquial_hr_sr_units04-06.md`'s Grammar points section — same rules, same endings, largely
overlapping example sentences translated into the Ekavian Serbian forms this book uses. Rather than
re-transcribe that material, only points genuinely new or differently framed here are recorded below.

### Register flag: kovčeg (archaic) vs. kofer (current)

Unlike `066`, which lists **kovčeg** "suitcase" as a plain vocabulary item with no register note, this
book explicitly flags it: "**kovčeg** is an archaic word, you should use **kofer**" (p.55). This is a
genuine current-usage-vs-archaism data point for a lexical item this project had previously only
recorded as unmarked `core`.

### Dialect variation shown within a single dialogue: живели vs. живјели

In the toast scene at p.76, the Ekavian-speaking hosts (Filip, Jelena — implicitly Belgrade-based)
say **живели!** ("cheers", lit. "long live"), while the Bosnian-heritage characters Dado and Sanja
reply **живјели!** — the Ijekavian reflex of the same jat-derived root. The source presents this
without commentary, embedded naturally in dialogue, making it a directly observed (not merely
described) sociolinguistic contrast between Ekavian Serbian and Ijekavian Bosnian/Serbian speech —
useful primary evidence distinct from the socioling. chapters already extracted in `031`–`036`.

### Belgrade name etymology (cultural/historical note, not strictly grammar)

Reading passage 1 of Unit 5 (p.71) explains that Beograd received its name because its white color
is visible from a distance ("grad je dobio ime po tome što se bela boja grada vidi izdaleka"),
tying the adjective **beo/bela** "white" directly to the city name — a piece of source-stated
folk/historical etymology worth preserving as cultural context alongside the vocabulary.

### Telephone-conversation formulas

Two distinct fixed telephone idioms recur across Units 5–6: the caller/answerer self-identification
formula **"Halo, [Ime] Prezime na telefonu"** ("Hello, this is [Name] speaking") and the responder's
**"Ovde [Ime]"** ("This is [Name]", lit. "here [Name]") — both are register-specific to phone
etiquette, paralleling but distinct from the single **halo** interjection already flagged in `066`
(p.45, "used only on the telephone").

### Real-estate/rental functional register (new domain, not present in 066)

Unit 6's second half (pp.87–89) introduces an entire functional vocabulary set around renting flats
— classified-ad structure (**oglas**, **izdavanje i potražnja**), pricing/terms (**kirija**,
**mesečno**, **kvadratni metar**, **nenamešten**/**nameštet**, **centralno grejanje**), and a modeled
phone-call dialogue for inquiring about a listing. This functional/transactional register (distinct
from the descriptive "what furniture is in which room" register that dominates the redundant portion
of Unit 6) has no counterpart in the Croatian/Serbian book's equivalent unit and represents genuinely
new material for the corpus.
