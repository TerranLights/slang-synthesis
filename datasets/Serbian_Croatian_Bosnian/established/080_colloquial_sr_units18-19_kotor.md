marginalia was found on any page in this range** — this appears to be a clean, unmarked scan; all
content extracted is genuinely printed/typeset source material.

**Montenegro relevance:** these two units are set in Kotor, Budva, Cetinje, and on Mount Lovćen —
all in Montenegro — making this the first vocabulary-lesson content in this project's Serbian corpus
with a sustained Montenegrin setting. Despite the setting, the dialogues and narration are written in
standard **ekavian** Serbian (e.g., `nisam`, not `nijesam`; `posle`, not `poslije`), consistent with
the book being a Belgrade-standard teaching grammar. However, **two explicit dialectal/regional
annotations were found** in the vocabulary boxes themselves (see Vocabulary table, rows `плесати`
and `посв(ј)етити се`) — both flagged per the coverage rule. The `посв(ј)етити се` entry is
particularly notable: the source's own dictionary-style headword spells the vowel alternation
directly into the entry as `е/(ј)е`, i.e., it registers the ekavian/ijekavian isogloss (посветити
vs. посвјетити) as a live variant even in a book that otherwise teaches ekavian throughout — this is
the same је/ије axis that established/035_socioling_ch25_serbian_montenegro.md documents at length
as the central marker separating Montenegrin/Bosnian/Croatian ijekavian speech from Belgrade-standard
ekavian. No other tokens matching established/021_lesson19.md's or established/035's specific
Montenegrin dialect-marker list (đe, nijèsi, viđet', pređe, starijem, ćerati, đèvojka, sjutra,
zboriti) were found in this chunk's dialogue text — the Kotor/Budva dialogue characters (Nena, Nemanja
and friends) all speak standard ekavian Serbian, not dialect Montenegrin, in this textbook's
presentation.

## Grammar points

### Formation of the imperative (Unit 18, Language points 1, p. 248-249)

Take the 3rd person plural present, remove the final vowel. If the resulting stem ends in **-j**,
add nothing (2sg), **-jmo** (1pl), **-jte** (2pl) — e.g. чекају → чекај!/чекајмо!/чекајте!. If the
stem ends in a consonant, add **-и/-имо/-ите** — e.g. говоре → говори!/говоримо!/говорите!. Verbs
with infinitive in **-ћи** build the imperative from the infinitive stem, not the present stem, and
must be learned individually (рећи → реци, помоћи → помози). бити has a fully irregular imperative:
буди/будимо/будите. Imperatives for other persons are periphrastic: 1sg uses да + present (Да
видим! "Let me see!"); 3rd person (sg. and pl.) uses нека + present (Нека дође! "Let him come!").
A very common colloquial substitute for иди/идимо is хајде(мо) (also spelled ајде(мо)), used for
"let's ..." constructions (Хајдемо у биоскоп! "Let's go to the cinema!").

### Negative imperative (Unit 18, Language points 2, p. 255)

Two methods: (i) **не + imperative** — used for blunt/abrupt prohibitions and required with the
imperfective (Не брини! "Don't worry!"); (ii) **немој(мо)/немојте + да + present** — a "softer"
prohibition, usable with either aspect (Немој да то једеш! "Don't eat that!"). For 1sg/3rd person,
simply insert не before the verb (Да не закасним! "Let me not be late!").

### English adjective phrases expressed by a verb (Unit 18, Language points 3, p. 256)

Many English "to be + adjective" constructions correspond to a single Serbian verb rather than a
copula+adjective phrase — e.g. каснити/закаснити "to be late", љутити се/наљутити се "to be angry",
стрпети се "to be patient", ћутати "to be quiet".

### Aspect 3 (Unit 18, Language points 4, p. 258-259)

Deepens the aspect discussion from earlier units. Perfective verbs express a single, indivisible
finite action with no reference to beginning/middle/end (Појео сам сладолед "I ate up the ice
cream" — describes totality, not process). Imperfective is vaguer and can be gradual (Једем сладолед
и гледам море "I am eating ice cream and looking at the sea"), repeated (Долазимо често на ову
плажу "We often come to this beach"), general (Воле да читају стрипове "They like to read comic
strips"), or open-ended/incomplete (Неколико дана се одлучивао да отпутује "He had been deciding to
leave for several days" — contrasted with the perfective Већ се неколико пута одлучио да отпутује
"He has already decided several times to leave", where the decision was actually made). Verbs
referring specifically to a phase of a process — почети "to begin", наставити "to continue",
завршити "to finish" — must be followed by an imperfective infinitive/да-clause.

### Formation of the conditional (1) and (2) (Unit 19, Language points 1-2, p. 265-269)

The conditional ("would") is a compound tense: the active past participle (l-participle, same form
used for the perfect tense) + the aorist of бити (бих, би, би, бисмо, бисте, би). The auxiliary can
precede or follow the participle depending on clause position (enclitic placement rules, same as
other auxiliaries). Negative: не is placed immediately before the auxiliary (Не бих никад од њега
купио кола. "I'd never buy a car from him."). Interrogative: question word + auxiliary + participle
(Где би (они) ручали? "Where would they have lunch?"). Beyond true conditionals, бих etc. also
express habitual past actions (Прочитао би по пет књига сваке недеље. "He would read some five
books each week.") and "softened" requests/statements (Да ли бисте отворили прозор? "Would you open
the window?").

### Conditional clauses (1) and (2): real, unreal, and realizable conditions (Unit 19, Language
points 2-3, p. 268, 271-272)

Real conditions use ако + the same tenses as English (Ако нађемо смештај, преноћићемо овде.). Unreal
conditions (the condition was/will not be met) put the main-clause verb in the conditional, and the
"if"-clause can be introduced by да + appropriate tense instead of ако when the English "if" could
idiomatically be dropped for "were you/had it" inversion (Да си овде, видео би га. "Were you here,
you would see him."; Да није падала киша, изашли бисмо. "Had it not been raining, we would have gone
out."). Realizable conditions ("if he were to come...") use кад or ако + conditional in the
subordinate clause (Кад би дошао на време, могли бисмо да га видимо.).

### Reflexive passive (Unit 19, Language points 4, p. 273)

One of the main ways Serbian expresses passivity: the object of an otherwise-transitive verb becomes
the subject of its reflexive form. Овде се не говори енглески. "English is not spoken here." Где се
купују карте? "Where are tickets bought?" Како се каже...? "How is it said?" (i.e., "How do you
say...?").

### Expression of "whoever," "whatever," etc. (Unit 19, Language points 5, p. 273-274)

The particle било + a pronoun or adverb conveys English "-ever": било ко "whoever", било шта
"whatever", било где "wherever", било кад "whenever", било какав "whatever kind", било који "any...
whatever".

### Aspect 4: features of English as a guide to choosing aspect (Unit 19, Language points 6, p.
274-277)

A practical guide for English-speaking learners: (i) the inherent nature of some verbs suggests one
aspect over the other (rest/sleep/listen/watch/study/walk/talk tend imperfective; say/learn/switch
on-off/glance/catch sight of tend perfective). (ii) Sense-impression verbs ("to see", "to hear")
followed by a participle in English require како + present in Serbian and the imperfective (Чуо сам
га како прилази. "I heard him approaching."), while an infinitive construction in English tends to
indicate the perfective (Чини ми се да сам чуо како је ушао. "I think I heard him come in."). (iii)
English progressive tenses must be rendered by the imperfective (Седим крај прозора и размишљам. "I
am sitting by the window thinking."). Certain adverbs reliably signal one aspect (imperfective:
непрестано, стално, редовно, дуго, цео дан, неко време; perfective: коначно, одједном, изненада,
одмах, нагло), and some conjunctions depend on context — кад can trigger either aspect depending on
meaning, while чим ("as soon as") tends to trigger the perfective (Телефонираћу чим стигнем.).
Time phrases with "for" emphasize the process and take the imperfective (Писали смо писма три сата.
"We wrote letters for three hours."), while phrases with "in" emphasize totality and take the
perfective (Написали смо писма за три сата. "We wrote the letters in three hours.").
