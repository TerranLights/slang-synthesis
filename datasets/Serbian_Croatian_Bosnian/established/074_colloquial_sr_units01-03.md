# Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Colloquial Serbian, Units 1-3
("Upoznavanje" / "Putnici idu u hotel" / "Dolazak u hotel")

**Source:** Celia Hawkesworth with Jelena Ćalić, *Colloquial Serbian: The Complete Course for
Beginners* (Routledge, 2006). Units 1-3, printed pages 8-50 (PDF pages 9-30 of
`source_reference/languages/Serbian, Croatian, Bosnian/Colloquial Serbian/Colloquial Serbian.pdf`).
Unit 4 ("Odlazak u grad" / "Going into the city") begins exactly at printed page 51 (PDF page 30,
right-hand side of the same page-image) and is excluded from this chunk.

**Non-redundant-supplement check (per `00_Reference_Extraction_Spec.md`):** checked
`established/065_colloquial_hr_sr_units01-03.md` first (Hawkesworth's earlier *Colloquial Croatian
and Serbian*, 1998, same author, same Units 1-3 topics: greetings, hotel arrival, nationalities,
`biti`, question formation, nominative/vocative case, reflexive verbs, verb aspect, `ići`-prefix
word family, adjectives from proper nouns, double negatives, letter-register examples). This
Serbian-only book's own acknowledgements state it "grew out of" the 1998 volume, and the overlap is
substantial and often near-identical in structure (the `stvarati`/`stvoriti` aspect example, the
`da li`/`li` Croatian-vs-Serbian question-marker note, the three-tier letter-formality scale, the
double-negative examples, and the core grammar-point sequence all recur almost beat-for-beat).
**Skipped as redundant:** full re-extraction of the `biti` paradigm, personal-pronoun table,
nominative/vocative case rules, reflexive-verb mechanics, verb-class endings (`-am`/`-im`/`-em`),
and the double-negative rule — all already fully captured in 065 with equivalent or near-identical
examples. **Kept as genuinely new:** (1) this is a Serbian-only textbook that presents nearly every
dialogue and many vocabulary boxes in **both Cyrillic and Latin script side by side** — a
structural feature 065's Croatian-focused source does not have, and directly relevant to a Serbian
slang corpus that will encounter both scripts; (2) different character names and a different
narrative thread ("Ekipa" — a group of young Belgrade friends — rather than 065's "Ljubavna priča"/
"business traveller" threads), yielding genuinely distinct example vocabulary even where grammar
overlaps; (3) Serbian-specific lexical items confirmed directly from a Serbian source rather than
inferred from a Croatian source's contrastive footnotes (`šta` not `što`, `ko` not `tko`, `sto` not
`stol`); (4) a hotel-accommodation vocabulary-building box and Serbian dinar currency material not
present in 065's range; (5) one colloquial/slang-adjacent item of real value — `super` used as an
informal exclamation — surfaced only in this book's "Ekipa" reading passages, not in 065.

**Vision-reading guard:** this source has no usable text layer (scanned book) and was fully
vision-read at 150dpi, page by page, PDF pages 9-30 (printed pages 8-51) in sequence with no gaps.
Every page was actively checked for handwritten marginalia (pencil/pen notes, underlines, filled-in
exercise answers in a different hand from the printed type). **No marginalia was found on any page
in this range** — all content is clean, consistently typeset printed material (both the Latin and
Cyrillic typesetting are uniform throughout). Accordingly, no content was excluded on marginalia
grounds, and Vision Reading Confidence is `verified` or `plausible_unverified` throughout — no

## Grammar points

### Overlap disclosure

The following grammar territory is covered by this book's Units 1-3 in essentially the same
sequence, scope, and (often) the same worked examples as `065_colloquial_hr_sr_units01-03.md`, and
is **not** re-extracted here: the `biti` paradigm (short enclitic vs. long stressed forms, negative
forms), personal pronouns and pro-drop, enclitic word order, the four question-formation patterns
(intonation / interrogative-word / verb+`li` / `da li`) including the explicit Croatian-favours-`li`
vs. Serbian-favours-`da li` register note, the nominative case's two core uses, the vocative case's
two endings-sets (masculine hard/soft, feminine `-a`/`-ca`), reflexive verbs and the placement rule
for `se`, verb aspect (imperfective/perfective) illustrated with the `stvarati`/`stvoriti` pair, the
`ići`-prefix verb-of-motion family (`otići`/`odlaziti`, `ući`/`ulaziti`, etc.), adjectives formed
from proper nouns (lower-case even from a capitalized source noun), the double-negative rule, and
the three-tier letter-formality register scale (informal `Dragi X` / mid `Dragi gospodine X` +
`Srdačan pozdrav` / formal `Poštovana gospođo X` + `S poštovanjem`).

### Dual-script presentation (genuinely new structural feature)

Unlike 065's source, this book presents a large share of its dialogues and reading passages in
**both Cyrillic and Latin script**, sometimes with the Latin transliteration given as a direct
parallel column (Unit 1, Dialogue "Vi ste Srbin, zar ne?") and sometimes as Cyrillic-only "extra
challenge" reading passages with a Latin-script vocabulary gloss beneath (the three-part "Ekипа"/
"The gang" reading strand running through Units 1-3, explicitly framed by the book itself: "It is
printed in the Cyrillic script to offer you an extra challenge... Good luck!"). This is directly
relevant to any downstream slang-synthesis work that needs to model Serbian's live biscriptality —
065's Croatian-leaning source has no equivalent parallel-script content anywhere in its Units 1-3.

### Ekavian reflexes (genuinely new observation)

Because this book is Serbian-only and written in the Ekavian standard, several verbs/nouns that
appear in (i)jekavian form in 065's Croatian-sourced material show their Ekavian counterparts here
directly from source rather than by inference: `sedeti` (cf. 065 `sjediti`), `dete`/`deca` (cf.
`dijete`/djeca`-type Croatian forms), `odneti` (cf. `odnijeti`), `predsednik` (cf. `predsjednik`),
`grešiti` (cf. `griješiti`). None of these pairs were independently confirmed from a Serbian-only
source in the dataset before this chunk — worth flagging as a systematic ekavian/(i)jekavian
contrast set for later mechanics analysis, since it is a genuinely productive phonological
correspondence (jat-reflex) rather than a set of unrelated lexical variants.

### "Ekipa" reading strand: colloquial/informal-register value

The three-part "Ekipa" ("The gang") reading passages, following a group of young Belgrade friends
(Maja, Luka, Jelena, Filip, Sanja, Dado) rather than the tourist-couple/business-traveller threads
used elsewhere in the unit, are the book's own explicitly informal/colloquial register strand —
comparable in function to 065's "Ljubavna priča" thread, but yielding distinct vocabulary: casual
invitations (`Ajdemo u klub!`), affirmation (`Važi!`), the loanword exclamation `Super!`, treating/
paying for a round (`Ja častim!`), and casual seating requests (`Ima li mesta do tebe?`). This is
the highest-density colloquial-register material in this chunk and the primary reason the
"non-redundant supplement" pass was worth doing in full rather than skipping this book's Units 1-3
as pure overlap.

### Hotel-accommodation and currency vocabulary (new, not in 065's range)

Unit 3 includes a dedicated "Hotel accommodation" vocabulary-building box (`smeštaj`, `jednokrevetna
soba`, `dvokrevetna soba`, `apartman`, `polupansion`, `punpansion`) and reproduces a 500-dinar
banknote as realia (`Narodna banka Srbije`) alongside the hotel-registration dialogue — neither
appears in 065's Units 1-3, which do not include a comparable accommodation-vocabulary box in that
range.

### Grammatically feminine collective "deca" (children)

`Deca` ("children") is a grammatically feminine singular collective noun (singular `dete`, genitive
`deteta`) despite referring to a plural group of people — an irregular gender/number mismatch not
covered in 065's Units 1-3 grammar points, surfaced here via the "Kulturni život u Beogradu (3)"
reading passage vocabulary box (p.44-45).
