# Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: BCS-English Glossary, part 5 of 5 (sta- through end, Ž)

**Source:** Alexander & Elias-Bursać, *Bosnian, Croatian, Serbian, a Textbook with Exercises and Basic
Grammar* (2nd ed., 2010), BCS-English Glossary, PDF pages 460–473 (printed pages 439–452, the final
pages of the glossary).

**Coverage note:** exhaustive — every headword entry in the assigned page range is captured, per the
dense-glossary exception to the normal sampling rule (see `00_Reference_Extraction_Spec.md`). This
source has a clean PDF text layer (`pdftotext` produced usable text), so no vision-reading was needed;
Vision Reading Confidence is `n/a` throughout. The glossary is a two-column layout; `pdftotext -layout`
run on the full page width interleaves the two columns onto shared physical lines, which would corrupt
alphabetical reading order, so each page was re-extracted as two separate crops (left half, right half)
and concatenated left-then-right per page, matching the method used for part 4. This range picks up
mid-letter-S at `stalo [mu je]` (the entry immediately after part 4's last complete entry, `stalan`) and
runs to the literal end of the glossary at `žut` (page 452) — confirmed as the final entry by the printed
page footer and the absence of any further glossary pages in the source.

**Dialect/aspect tag conventions used below (same as part 4):** `[B]`/`[C]`/`[S]` mark Bosnian/Croatian/
Serbian-specific forms (Geographic Scope `national`); `[E]`/`[J]` mark Ekavian/Ijekavian pronunciation
variants (Geographic Scope `regional`, since these track a phonological reflex distributed by dialect
region rather than by national standard). A handful of entries are explicitly marked "(Montenegrin)" in
the source rather than with a bracket tag — these are recorded with Attested Region "Montenegro" and
Geographic Scope `regional`. Where the source pairs a perfective/imperfective (P)/(I) verb form under one
headword, that pair is treated as a single vocabulary item (one row). Where the source gives a bare
cross-reference stub as its own alphabetized headword (e.g. `stavljati see under staviti`), that stub is
still given its own row (gloss "(cross-reference)"), matching part 4's practice, since the source itself
treats it as a distinct alphabetized entry. Where the source gives two separate headwords for the same
lexeme differing only by dialect tag, each headword is its own row. Grammatical case-paradigm sub-listings
printed under pronoun headwords (`sva`, `sve`, `svi`, `tko`/`što`/`šta`, `ti`, `to`, `taj`, `vi`) are
summarized briefly in that headword's Notes column rather than given their own rows — these are inflected
forms of one lexeme, not separate vocabulary items. One printed duplication was found and is flagged: the
adjective `strog` "strict" (6A3) appears twice, once at the bottom of the left column and once again at
the top of the right column of the same page (page 460); it is recorded once here. A second oddity: the
entry `tjerati (I); pòtjerati (P) [E] to drive away, to chase` prints an `[E]` (Ekavian) tag on what is
morphologically the Ijekavian form (paired earlier on the facing page as `terati (I); pòterati (P) [E]`
for the actual Ekavian form) — transcribed exactly as printed and flagged in that row's Notes rather than
silently corrected, per the extraction spec's guidance to flag rather than "fix" apparent source
inconsistencies.
