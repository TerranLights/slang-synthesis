# Serbian — Established Vocabulary: Serbian Folk Fairy Tales, part 6 (printed pp. 145–172)

**Source:** *Српске народне бајке* (Serbian Folk Fairy Tales), Kreativni centar, 2000 ("Библиотека
Пустоловине" / "Bajke" series). Vision-read (scanned, no text layer, Cyrillic). Printed pages
145–172 = PDF pages 149–176 of "29.Serbian Folk Tales.pdf" (offset +4, verified empirically by
reading the printed page number stamped at the bottom of PDF page 153, which reads "149").

**Coverage note.** This source is pure literary/folkloric narrative (fairy tales), not a pedagogical
grammar — it carries no parallel English translation. The book supplies its own inline "+"-marked
margin glosses for obscure/archaic/regional words, which are extracted directly below (marked
`Vision Reading Confidence: verified` where the book's own gloss text was legible and unambiguous).
A handful of additional archaic/mythological terms that appear in the running narrative without an
inline "+" gloss are also captured, with the agent's own gloss and `plausible_unverified` or
`low_confidence` as appropriate. Per the extraction spec, this file captures **vocabulary only** —
no plot/prose reproduction. A one-line orientation note is given per tale for context.

**Tales covered in this chunk** (in page order):
1. **Виловска кочија** ("The Fairy Coach") — tail end only, pp. 145–146 (opening was covered by an
   earlier chunk, not this one).
2. **Змија младоженя** ("The Snake Bridegroom") — pp. 149–153 in full. A Cupid-and-Psyche-type tale:
   a queen bears a snake-son who is secretly a man by night; her meddling undoes the marriage, and
   the wife must journey to Sun, Moon, and Wind to find him again.
3. **Биберче** ("Peppercorn," a Tom-Thumb-sized hero) — pp. 155–157 in full. A tiny hero defeats a
   nine-headed *ала* to rescue a princess, then must prove it was him and not an impostor.
4. **Усуд** ("Fate/Destiny," personified) — pp. 159–165 in full. Two brothers split their inheritance;
   the industrious one grows poor and travels to question Усуд and Срећа (Fortune) about the
   workings of fate, luck, and proper ancestral/guest-rite observance.
5. **Дванаест мрва** ("Twelve Crumbs") — pp. 167–171 in full. A prince exiled by a jealous stepmother
   survives by a magic *свирала* (pipe), slays a fire-dragon (*огњени змај*) menacing a kingdom, and
   is reunited with his true love.
6. The chunk ends at the unnumbered full-page illustration on printed p. 172 (PDF 176), which opens
   the *next* tale, "Дијете са девет чирака" ("The Child with Nine Candlesticks") — that tale's own
   text begins on printed p. 173 (PDF 177) and is **out of scope** for this chunk.

**Vision-reading guard.** All pages in this range (PDF 149–176) were inspected for handwritten
marginalia. **None was found** — every mark on every page is consistent, typeset print (the book's
own "+"-prefixed sidebar glosses, in a distinct but clearly typeset sans-serif font, plus captioned
photographs/illustrations of period ethnographic objects). No pencil/pen annotation, underlining, or
filled-in exercise answers were observed anywhere in this range.
