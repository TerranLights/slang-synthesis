## Grammar points

### Nouns and gender

Nouns in BCS are masculine, feminine, or neuter. Masculine nouns (e.g. `pas`) usually end in a
consonant; feminine nouns (e.g. `mačka`) usually end in `-a`; neuter nouns (e.g. `pìtanje`) end in
`-e` or `-o`. Possessive pronominal adjectives (`moj`, `tvoj`, `njihov`) take endings agreeing with
the noun: `moj pas` (masc.), `mòje pitanje` (neut.), `mòja mačka` (fem.).

### Pronouns

Subject pronouns are used for emphasis or first mention; otherwise omitted, since the verb form
alone carries the necessary information. Third-person pronoun choice is determined by the gender of
the referent — the plural masculine `oni` covers masculine-only, mixed, or general "they" groups,
while `one`/`ona` are reserved for exclusively feminine/neuter groups respectively.

### Questions

Yes/no questions are formed two ways, both meaning the same thing: `da li` placed before the verb
(Serbian's most frequent pattern) or `li` placed after the verb (Bosnian and Croatian's most
frequent pattern). Other questions begin with a question word ("what," "who," etc.), as in English.
The question marker always stands at the sentence's start.

### Adjectives

Adjectives usually precede a noun, but many adjectives — including all possessive pronominal
adjectives — can also follow a form of "to be" (`pas je moj`, `mačka je tvòja`), with the adjective's
ending agreeing with the noun either way. Neuter adjectives end in `-o` or `-e` depending on the
preceding consonant (`njihovo` but `naše`).
