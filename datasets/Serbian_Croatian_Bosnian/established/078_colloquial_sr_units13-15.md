# Serbian — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Colloquial Serbian, Units 13–15 ("Muzika" / "Pakovanje" / "Polazak")

**Source:** Celia Hawkesworth with Jelena Ćalić, *Colloquial Serbian: The Complete Course for Beginners*
(Routledge, 2006), Units 13–15, printed pages 177–216 (PDF pages 93–113 of the scanned file — each
scanned PDF page is a full two-page spread, not a single printed page; verified by direct page
inspection: Unit 13 "Muzika" title page is the right-hand page of PDF p.93/printed p.177 [left-hand
page of that same spread is p.176, the tail end of Unit 12 "Seoski život" — not part of this chunk],
and Unit 16 "U kvaru" begins on the right-hand page of PDF p.113/printed p.217, with printed p.216
(left-hand page of that spread, the end of Unit 15) being the last page of this chunk). This edition
is **printed entirely in Cyrillic** (unlike the Latin-script "Colloquial Croatian and Serbian" already
extracted as `established/069`) — all vocabulary below is transliterated to Latin script for
consistency with the rest of this dataset's established files, per the convention already used in
`established/050`.

**Coverage note:** Unit 13 ("Muzika"/Music) has no analog in the earlier-extracted *Colloquial
Croatian and Serbian* (whose Unit 13 was "U kazalištu"/At the theatre) and was extracted thoroughly
and normally — every grammar point, every distinct vocabulary item, and every cultural/register note.
Units 14 ("Pakovanje"/Packing) and 15 ("Polazak"/Departure) cover the **same grammar territory** as
`established/069`'s Units 14–15 (comparative/superlative adjectives and adverbs, demonstrative
pronouns, possessive adjectives from proper nouns) — this book's own acknowledgements state it "grew
out of" that earlier work, and the paradigm tables and rule statements are essentially identical
(down to the same example-sentence patterns in places). Per the non-redundant-supplement protocol:
**grammar rule statements for Units 14–15 are only briefly summarized below with a pointer to 069**
rather than re-derived in full, since 069 already tabulates them completely. However, **the
vocabulary, dialogues, and dedicated reading passages for Units 14–15 are a substantially different,
independently-written text** (different characters, different plot — a family driving from Belgrade
to Novi Pazar/Sopoćani rather than the earlier book's Zagreb-set story) and were extracted in full,
since they are new lexical and cultural content even where the grammar scaffolding repeats. Two
reading passages in this chunk (on Novi Pazar's geography/history and on a Belgrade literary evening)
have no equivalent at all in 069 and are flagged as genuinely new cultural content.

**No handwritten marginalia was found on any page in this range** — all content inspected across all
21 scanned spreads was clearly typeset print consistent with the surrounding page, so nothing was
excluded on that basis.

This book is explicitly framed as "colloquial" — several dialogues in this chunk (the flirtatious
"Ekipa" conversations, a sibling argument about packing, casual discourse markers) carry strong
informal/spoken register and are flagged accordingly below.

## Grammar points

### Ordinal numbers (Unit 13)

Regular definite adjectives, formed by adding `-i` (m.), `-a` (f.), `-o` (n.) to the cardinal, with a
handful of irregular low forms to memorize: *prvi/prva/prvo* "1st", *drugi* "2nd", *treći* "3rd",
*četvrti* "4th", *peti…* regular through *devedeset deveti* "99th", *stoti* "100th", *dvestoti*
"200th", *devetsto devedeset deveti* "999th". In compound ordinals, only the final element is
declined (e.g. *dvadeset petog avgusta* "25 August" — only *petog* takes the genitive ending). (p.
179)

### Dates, days, months, seasons, and time expressions (Unit 13)

- Days of the week are **not capitalized**; the week is counted starting from *nedelja* "Sunday":
  *nedelja, ponedeljak, utorak, sreda, četvrtak, petak, subota*.
- Months are the Latin-derived set only (*januar, februar, mart, april, maj, juni, juli, avgust,
  septembar, oktobar, novembar, decembar*) — **unlike the Croatian-focused 069, this Serbian-only book
  gives no parallel Slavonic month-name set**, since that system is specifically a Croatian
  standardization choice not used in Serbian.
- Seasons: *proleće, leto, jesen, zima* (Ekavian; cf. Croatian *proljeće, ljeto*).
- "On Monday" etc. = *u* + accusative (*u ponedeljak*). A specific calendar date on which an event
  occurs = genitive (*Stigli smo četvrtog avgusta* "We arrived on 4th August"; *Mojoj ćerki je rođendan
  drugog septembra*).
- Duration of time = accusative (*Pokušavam da te dobijem telefonom ceo dan* "I've been trying to get
  you on the phone all day"; *Provešćemo mesec dana u Beogradu* "We shall spend a month in Belgrade";
  *Oni su stanovali godinu dana u ulici do naše* "They lived in the next street to ours for a year").
- Habitual events tied to a specific day of the week = instrumental (*Subotom uvek nekuda idemo u
  šetnju* "We always go somewhere for a walk on Saturdays"; *Obično idu u klub utorkom* "They usually
  go to the club on Tuesdays").
- A "useful series of adverbs": *jutros* "this morning", *večeras* "this evening", *noćas* "tonight",
  *sinoć* "last night". (p. 181–187)

### Telling the time (Unit 13)

*Koliko je sati?* "What time is it?"; hour + gen. pl. minuta pattern for "past" times (*devet sati i
pet minuta* "five past nine", *deset i petnaest* "quarter past ten"); *pola* + next hour for "half
past" (*jedanaest i trideset / pola dvanaest* "half past eleven" — explicitly flagged by the source as
a false-friend risk for English speakers used to colloquial "half twelve" meaning 12:30, whereas
Serbian *pola dvanaest* means 11:30); "to" times use the falling-count pattern (*dvadeset pet do jedan*
"25 to 1", *petnaest do dva* "quarter to 2", *deset do tri* "10 to 3"). (p. 187)

### Comparative adjectives (Unit 14)

**Substantially the same rule set as `established/069`'s "Comparative adjectives" section** — most
comparatives add `-iji/-ija/-ije` to the indefinite stem (*stariji, poznatiji, gladniji*); `-o`
adjectives derived from `-l` add the ending to the retained `l` (*mio*→*miliji*, *veseo*→*veseliji*);
other adjectives take `-ji` with the same regular consonant alternations at the stem boundary (`k+j→č`
*jak*→*jači*; `g+j→ž` *drag*→*draži*; `h+j→š` *tih*→*tiši*; `t+j→ć` *ljut*→*ljući*; `d+j→đ`
*tvrd*→*tvrđi*); an inserted `l` before p/b/v stems (*skup*→*skuplji*, *grub*→*grublji*); the same
*lep*→*lepši* exception (Ekavian form of 069's *lijep*→*ljepši*). Adjectives in `-ak/-ek/-ok` drop the
final syllable before `-ji` (*blizak*→*bliži*, *kratak*→*kraći*, *dalek*→*dalji*, *dubok*→*dublji*,
*redak*→*ređi*, *visok*→*viši*). (p. 192–195)

### Comparison of adverbs and irregular comparatives (Unit 14)

Same pattern as 069: neuter-singular-as-adverb doubles for the comparative (*jako*→*jače*,
*lako*→*lakše*, *blizu*→*bliže*, *drago*→*draže*, *skupo*→*skuplje*, *teško*→*teže*, *rado*→*radije*).
Irregular forms: *dobar*→*bolji*, *zao/loš*→*gori*, *velik*→*veći*, *malen*→*manji*, *mnogo*→*više*,
*malo*→*manje*. (p. 196)

### Superlative (Unit 14)

*naj-* prefixed to the comparative: *najstariji, najbliži, najbolje, najviše, najmanje*. Identical rule
to 069. (p. 196)

### Comparison to an object or phrase (Unit 14)

Two objects: *od* + genitive (*Moj brat je stariji od tebe*). A whole clause/phrase: *nego* required
instead (*Kasnije je nego što sam mislila* "It's later than I thought"). *Nego* can also substitute
for *od* + genitive even with a single noun (*On je stariji nego ti*; *Tvoj sin je viši nego tvoj
muž*). Same rule as 069, independently re-illustrated with different example sentences. (p. 198–199)

### Structures with the comparative (Unit 14)

`sve` + comparative = "more and more, increasingly" (*Svakim danom sve bolje vladate našim jezikom*
"You speak our language better and better every day"; *Moram da priznam da sve ređe pišem pisma* "I
must confess I write letters increasingly rarely"; *Vreme sve brže prolazi!* "Time passes increasingly
quickly!"). `što` + comparative = "as … as possible" (*Dođite nam opet što pre!* "Come again as soon as
you can!"; *Sedi što bliže meni!* "Sit as near me as possible!"; *Rekao je da će da piše što češće* "He
said he'd write as often as possible"). Same construction as 069. (p. 199)

### Demonstrative pronouns (Unit 15)

Full declension of *taj* "that" given (also covers *ovaj* "this" and *onaj* "that, further away",
declined identically):

| G | tog(a) | tog(a) | te | tih | tih | tih |
| D | tom(e) | tom(e) | toj | tim(a) | tim(a) | tim(a) |

### Possessive adjectives from proper nouns (Unit 15)

Same rule as 069: masculine nouns ending in a consonant or `-o` add `-ov` (*Vojin*→*Vojinov*,
*Branko*→*Brankov*); if the final consonant is "soft," `-ev` is used instead (*prijatelj*→
*prijateljev*, *Đorđe*→*Đorđev*, *G. Petrović*→*G. Petrovićev*). Feminine nouns in `-a` drop the `-a`
and add `-in` (*Marija*→*Marijin*, *sestra*→*sestrin*); the same rule applies to men's names with a
feminine surface form (*Nikola*→*Nikolin*). These possessives decline like regular definite adjectives
(*Videla sam Markovog brata*; *Da li si to rekla profesorovoj sestri?*). A footnote again notes
indefinite endings are also found for masculine possessives (not detailed further). Crucially, **the
same restriction as 069**: a possessive of this type cannot itself be preceded by a qualifying
adjective — *Ovo je putnikova torba* "This is the passenger's bag" is fine, but "that other
passenger's bag" must be rephrased with a genitive construction: *To je torba onog drugog putnika*.
(p. 208–209)

### Regional/dialectal note: "krajnje vr(ij)eme" (Unit 15)

At the very end of Unit 15, the source itself gives a parenthetical spelling *vr(ij)eme* for "time" in
the fixed phrase *krajnje vreme* "high time" — flagging, within an otherwise consistently Ekavian
(*-e-*) book, that the Ijekavian (*-ije-*) variant is also current/expected for at least some readers
or speech contexts. This is one of the few explicit dialectal annotations in this chunk, on a par with
the "pola dvanaest" false-friend note and the Novi Pazar Muslim-population note as places where the
text steps outside pure grammar drilling to flag real sociolinguistic variation. (p. 216)
