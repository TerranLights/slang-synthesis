# Korean — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: *Korean (한국어 2)*, 2nd Edition — Seoul National University Language Education Institute (33-lesson intermediate course)

**Source:** Language Education Institute (Language Research Institute), Seoul National University, *Korean 2* (한국어 2), 2nd edition, Moonjin Media (published with The Korea Foundation's support; series preface dated June 1993). 184 scanned PDF pages, no text layer (`Image2PDF Command Line Software`), scanned as two-printed-pages-per-image landscape spreads; printed pagination runs pp. 1–367 (lessons 1–33 span pp. 19–337; glossary pp. 338–357; listening transcript from p. 358). This is Level 2 of the same SNU series whose Level 1 volume is `037` (extracted in parallel; not cross-checked against this file per dispatch instructions).

**Vision-reading guard:** entire source has no text layer — fully vision-read from rendered page images. No handwritten marginalia (pencil answers, underlining, reader's notes) was found anywhere in the pages inspected; the scan shows only minor printing/dust artifacts (small specks, faint smudges) that never obscured legible print. All extracted content below is genuine typeset source material.

**PDF↔printed-page mapping (for anyone re-deriving page ranges):** verified empirically (not assumed) against three independent anchor points — PDF p.10 right=printed p.19 (Lesson 1 opener), PDF p.165 right=printed p.329 (Lesson 33 opener, matches the book's own table of contents), PDF p.170=printed pp.338–339 (glossary opener, matches TOC). Formula confirmed consistent across that whole span: `printed_right = 2×pdf_page − 1`, `printed_left = 2×pdf_page − 2`.

## Coverage note — non-redundant-supplement scope (this is a 184pp/33-lesson book; full page-by-page vision-reading of all 33 lessons was not undertaken)

This project already has 35 established Korean reference files (~23,500 lines) covering basic through intermediate grammar extensively (Basic/Intermediate/Continuing/Using Korean series, College Korean, Korean Grammar for International Learners, Colloquial Korean ×3, Elementary Korean, dictionaries, honorifics, etc.). Before vision-reading, I extracted this book's own curriculum table (교재 구성, pp. 7–9) — which lists every one of the 33 lessons' topic, function, and target grammar pattern — and grep-checked the existing established corpus for each listed grammar pattern.

**Decision rule applied:** grammar patterns already attested in multiple established files (e.g. `-게 되다` [5 files], `-아지다/어지다` [6], `-지 말다` [4], `-다가` [11], `-네요` [8], `-기 전에` [7], `-겠-`, `것 같다` [5, incl. a full paradigm table], `-(으)면 되다`, `N에 대해서` [4]) were treated as redundant and **not** re-extracted in depth. Grammar patterns that returned **zero hits** in the established corpus, or only 1 thin hit, were vision-read in full from their lesson's 문법 (grammar) box. This identified **18 of the 33 lessons** (3, 8, 9, 11, 12, 15, 17, 18, 21, 23, 25, 26, 27, 28, 30, 31, 32, 33) as containing genuinely new grammar territory, and those 18 lessons' 발음/문법/어휘와 표현 sections were vision-read page-by-page. The remaining 15 lessons (1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 10, 13, 14, 16, 19, 20, 22, 24, 29) were **not** vision-read beyond the curriculum table — their listed grammar points (`-게 되다`, `A-아지다/어지다`, `V-지 말다`, `A/V-(으)ㄹ 것 같다`, `S-기 때문에`, `A/V-아야/어야 되다`, `V-기 전에`, `V-거나`, etc.) are already well-represented elsewhere, and their 연습 (drill) content is by the spec's own coverage rule low-value padding. Lesson 1's dialogue/vocabulary box was read in passing while calibrating the page-mapping formula and is included below as a bonus.

**Vocabulary scope:** the book's own back-of-book glossary (찾아보기, printed pp. 338–357, alphabetized, ~700 core words + supplementary ★-marked words with lesson/page citations and English+Japanese glosses) was located and confirmed but **not transcribed in full** — bulk-reproducing a ~700+-entry glossary that overlaps heavily with this project's existing core-Korean-vocabulary coverage would be low marginal value for high token cost. Instead, the vocabulary table below draws every content word that appeared in the worked examples of the 18 vision-read lessons' grammar/vocabulary boxes — this is a genuine, non-arbitrary sample (not cherry-picked) but is **not** a complete enumeration of this book's ~700-word core list. A future targeted pass could mine the glossary directly (pp. 338–357 = PDF pages 170–179) if finer vocabulary coverage of this specific book is ever wanted.

**Repeated drill exercises** (연습 1 / 연습 2 sections) were skipped throughout per the spec's coverage rule — they recombine already-captured vocabulary/grammar into new sentence slots without introducing new material, with the exception of a few drill sentences quoted briefly above as grammar illustrations where the 문법 box's own examples were thin.

## Grammar points

*Lessons not listed here (1 excepted for its headline point, 2, 4–7, 10, 13, 14, 16, 19, 20, 22, 24, 29) were judged redundant against this project's existing 35 established Korean files and were not vision-read beyond the curriculum table — see the coverage note above.*

### V-게 되다 — "to come to V, to end up V-ing" (Lesson 1, p.22)

Marks a change of state or circumstance arriving independent of the subject's own volition/plan — "it came about that I V," distinct from a plainly intentional V. 매운 음식을 못 먹었는데 한국에 와서 먹게 되었어요 "I couldn't eat spicy food before, but now that I live in Korea I've come to be able to." A related pattern noted on the same page, N은/는 N이/가 되다 "N becomes N" (김 선생님의 아들은 중학교 선생님이 되었습니다 "Mr. Kim's son has become a middle-school teacher"), is the plain change-of-state copula counterpart.

### V-(으)ㄴ N — past-tense verbal attributive/relativizer (Lesson 3, p.38)

Verb stem + -(으)ㄴ modifies a following noun, marking the action as completed relative to the noun it modifies — a true relative-clause-forming attributive, contrasted on the same page with the book's own worked present-tense form (지금 읽는 책 "the book [I'm] reading now," using -는, already covered elsewhere) and future/intentive form (이번 주말에 읽을 책 "a book [I intend] to read this weekend," using -(으)ㄹ). Example: 지난 달에 읽은 책을 다시 읽어야 해요 "I have to re-read the book that I read last month." The three-way tense split (-(으)ㄴ/-는/-(으)ㄹ) across the same slot is presented as one paradigm in-source.

### A-(으)ㄴ데요 / V-는데요 — predicative/mild-interruption sentence ending (Lesson 3, p.38)

A descriptive or action predicate ending that presents the clause as background information, a mild objection, or an opening for the listener to respond — softer and more conversational than a flat declarative. 이 책이 아주 재미있는데요. 한번 읽어 보세요 "This book is very interesting — you should give it a read." A nominal counterpart N(이)ㄴ데요 is given too: 김철수 씨 계세요? — 네, 전데요 "Is Mr. Kim there? — Yes, this is he" (literally "yes, it's [I who am] he").

### N(으)로 — instrumental "by means of N" (Lesson 3, p.39)

Marks the means/method/vehicle of an action — by mail class (배로/비행기로 "by boat/by air"), by direction (학교로 "to school"), or destination-of-departure (호주로 여행을 떠나셨어요 "left on a trip to Australia"). Distinguished from the N(으)로 하다 "will have N" choice-marking sense documented separately below (Lesson 17).

### N이/가 아니고 N이다 — "not N, but N" (Lesson 8, p.84)

A corrective copula construction naming what something is NOT before stating what it IS, used to correct a mistaken assumption. 여기는 1급 교실이 아니고 2급 교실이에요 "This is the level-2 classroom, not level 1."

### N인지 알다[모르다] / V-는지 알다[모르다] — embedded (indirect) question with "know/not know" (Lesson 8, p.84)

Embeds a full question (copular or verbal) as the object of 알다/모르다 "to know / not know," using the same -ㄴ지/는지 "whether/what" complementizer across noun and verb predicates. 2급반 학생이 몇 명인지 아세요? "Do you know how many students there are in level 2?"; 그 사람이 어디에 갔는지 알아요? "Do you know where he went?" This is a genuinely productive embedded-question system — no prior established file in this project's Korean corpus covers it.

### V-(으)려고 (V) — "in order to V, intending to V" (Lesson 9, p.93)

Marks the purpose/intention behind a following action — the two clauses need not share a subject requirement as strictly as some purpose connectives, and the matrix clause is typically a motion or acquisition verb. 내 동생은 역사 공부를 하려고 중국에 갔어요 "My younger brother went to China [in order] to study history."

### V-아/어 오다[가다] — directional-resultative compound, "V and bring/take it" (Lesson 9, p.93)

A serial-verb-like compound where 오다/가다 as an auxiliary marks that the main verb's result is carried toward (오다) or away from (가다) the speaker's reference point — distinct from the plain motion-verb use of 오다/가다. 사진을 세 장 찍어 와야 해요 "You need to take three photos and bring them [back]"; 이름을 써 오세요 "Write your name and bring it." The same directional-compound mechanism recurs with 뛰다 in Lesson 28 (뛰어가다/뛰어오다, "to run away from/toward" the reference point) — a reusable pattern worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase as a productive family, not a one-off idiom.

### A-게 — adverbializer suffix (Lesson 11, p.110)

Converts a descriptive adjective into a manner adverb modifying a following verb, with a small paradigm table given in-source (좋다→좋게, 쉽다→쉽게, 늦다→늦게, 싸다→싸게, 짧다→짧게; 맛있다→맛있게, 바쁘다→바쁘게, 재미있다→재미있게, 비싸다→비싸게, 정확하다→정확하게). 이야기를 길게 못 합니다 "I can't make a long conversation" — formed directly from 길다 "to be long."

### N처럼 A/V — "like N" (Lesson 11, p.111)

A comparison particle marking a noun as the standard of resemblance/manner for the predicate. 윌슨 씨는 한국 사람처럼 한국말을 잘해요 "Wilson speaks Korean well, like a Korean [would]." (The project's existing corpus had zero prior hits for this exact particle despite its frequency, so the full set of in-source examples is captured here for the first time.)

### A/V-(으)면 A/V-(으)ㄹ 거예요 — "will be/do A/V if A/V" (conditional-future) (Lesson 11, p.111), plus A/V-았/었을 거예요 — past conjecture (p.110, marked ★ supplementary)

The plain conditional-future (이 옷을 입으면 예쁠 거예요 "You'll look pretty if you wear this outfit") is paired in-source with a conjectural-past variant stacking the same -(으)ㄹ 거예요 future/probability ending onto a past-tense stem: 10년 전에는 그 여자도 예뻤을 거예요 "She must have been pretty 10 years ago." The two together show -(으)ㄹ 거예요 functioning as a general epistemic-probability marker independent of temporal reference, not only as a plain future.

### V-(으)려면 S — "if (one) intends to V, S" (Lesson 12, p.120)

A conditional connective specifically framing the condition as the addressee's own intention/goal, answered with what must be done to achieve it — functionally "if you want to V, then S." 광화문에 가려면 어떻게 해야 합니까? "What should I do if I want to go to Gwanghwamun?"

### V-(으)면 N이다 — "N is there if V" (Lesson 12, p.120)

A locative-result conditional: performing the action V reveals/arrives-at the noun N. 곧장 가면 서점입니다 "The bookstore is there if you go straight [ahead]"; a resultative variant with 나오다/나가다 "to come out, emerge" is given alongside it (곧장 가면 서점이 나옵니다, same meaning), showing the two verbs (이다 vs. 나오다) as interchangeable in this route-direction frame.

### V-(으)ㄹ 수는 없다 — "cannot V" (contrastive negative ability) (Lesson 12, p.121)

The ability-negation V-(으)ㄹ 수 없다 with the topic/contrast particle -는 inserted, implying a contrast with some other ability that does hold. 중국어를 말할 수는 없지만 한자를 쓸 수는 있어요 "I can't speak Chinese, but I can write Chinese characters."

### V-기를 좋아하다[싫어하다] — "to like/dislike V-ing" (Lesson 15, p.148)

Nominalizes a verb with -기 and marks it as the object of 좋아하다/싫어하다. 내 동생은 공부하기를 싫어합니다 "My younger brother dislikes studying."

### N만 — "only N" (Lesson 15, p.148)

Exclusive-focus particle. 제 친구들만 불렀어요 "I only invited my friends."

### N이/가 모자라다 — "to lack/not have enough N" (Lesson 15, p.148)

돈이 이천 원 모자랍니다 "I'm two thousand won short."

### A/V-(으)ㄹ 테니까 — "will be/will do A/V, so..." (Lesson 15, p.149)

States the speaker's own intention or a confident prediction as the basis/reason for an instruction or suggestion that follows — functionally "I'll do X, so (you should) Y" or "it'll be X, so Y." 깎아 드릴 테니까 다음에 또 오세요 "I'll cut the price, so please come again next time" (also the lesson's own title). Zero prior coverage in the established corpus.

### A-(으)ㄴ가요? — descriptive-predicate interrogative ending (Lesson 15, p.149)

A softer/more conversational question ending for adjectives, parallel to the V-나요? verbal interrogative documented below (Lesson 18). 책이 그렇게 비싼가요? "Are books really that expensive?"

### A/V-았/었군요 / -었군요 — past exclamatory sentence ending (Lesson 17, p.168)

Marks a realization/discovery the speaker has just arrived at, about something already completed — "oh, it turns out that..." 벌써 비행기가 떠났군요 "Oh, the plane has already left [I see]."

### N이/가 되다 (availability sense) — "can we have/get N" (Lesson 17, p.168)

A distinct sense of 되다 from the ordinary change-of-state use documented under Lesson 1 above: used of menu items or services to ask/state whether something is available to be prepared/provided. 냉면은 빨리 돼요? — 미안합니다. 빨리 안 되는데요 "Can the naengmyeon be ready quickly? — Sorry, it can't be done quickly."

### N(이)나 N — "N or N" (Lesson 17, p.168)

A disjunctive particle linking two alternatives. 영어나 불어를 할 줄 알아요? "Do you know how to speak English or French?"

### N(으)로 하다 — "will have N" (choice-marking, e.g. ordering) (Lesson 17, p.168)

Distinct from the instrumental N(으)로 documented under Lesson 3: here the particle marks a chosen option, typically in an ordering/selecting context. 저는 인삼차로 하겠어요 "I'll have ginseng tea."

### V-나요? — verbal interrogative sentence ending (Lesson 18, p.178)

A softened question ending, parallel to A-(으)ㄴ가요? above. 받아쓰기 시험도 보나요? "Is there a dictation test too?"

### V-도록 하다 — "be sure to V, make it so that V happens" (Lesson 18, p.178)

An other- or self-directed instruction/resolution marker, stronger than a plain imperative in framing the outcome as something to be ensured. 한국어를 빨리 배우려면 책을 많이 읽도록 하세요 "If you want to learn Korean quickly, be sure to read a lot of books."

### N밖에 — "nothing but N, only N (with negative predicate)" (Lesson 21, p.206)

An exclusive particle that obligatorily co-occurs with a negated predicate, distinguishing it from the plainly-exclusive 만 (Lesson 15): 돈이 천 원밖에 없어요 "I have nothing but 1,000 won" (literally "...don't have [anything] besides 1,000 won"). The established corpus already had 11 prior hits for 밖에, but none captured this negative-concord co-occurrence requirement explicitly, so it is included here.

### N 대신(에) — "instead of N" (Lesson 21, p.206)

케이크 대신 꽃을 샀어요 "I bought flowers instead of cake."

### A-아/어 보이다 — "to look/appear A" (Lesson 23, p.225)

An evidential/perceptual marker: the speaker reports a visual impression of a quality, not an assertion of the quality itself. 이 책은 어려워 보입니다 "This book looks difficult [to me]." Zero prior coverage in the established corpus despite this being a core intermediate pattern.

### N을/를 닮다 (also N와/과 닮다) — "to resemble, look like N" (Lesson 23, p.225)

Both an object-marked frame (저는 어머니를 닮았어요 "I look like my mother") and a comitative-marked frame (동생은 저와 많이 닮았어요 "My younger sibling looks a lot like me") are attested for the same verb on the same page — worth flagging for the mechanics-analysis phase as a verb with genuine case-frame flexibility rather than a single fixed government pattern.

### A/V-지 않아요? — negative tag question (Lesson 25, p.244)

A rhetorical-leaning negative question seeking agreement/confirmation, paired in-source with its affirmative-tag counterpart already covered elsewhere (-지요?). 일찍 자야 하지 않아요? "Don't you have to go to bed early?"

### 덜 / 훨씬 덜[더] — "less" / "much less[more]" (Lesson 25, p.244–245)

덜 is a plain comparative-reduction adverb (이 길이 그 길보다 덜 복잡합니다 "This road is less crowded than that one"); 훨씬 is an intensifier that can stack onto either 덜 or 더 to mean "much less/much more."

### N을/를 믿다 — "to trust/believe N" (Lesson 25, p.245)

그 사람의 말을 믿지 마세요 "Don't believe what that person says."

### 서두르다 — "to hurry" (르-irregular verb) (Lesson 25, p.245)

Full paradigm given in-source: 서두릅니다/서두르니까/서두르면/서두를 거예요/서둘러요/서둘렀어요/서둘러서/서두르세요 — showing the 르→ㄹㄹ stem-doubling irregularity before a vowel-initial ending (서두르 + 어 → 서둘러, not *서두러). Zero prior established-file coverage of this specific 르-irregular verb's full paradigm.

### V-(으)ㄴ 적이 있다[없다] — experiential aspect, "to have[not have] V-ed before" (Lesson 26, p.256)

설렁탕을 먹어 본 적이 있으세요? "Have you ever tried seolleongtang?" Zero prior coverage in the established corpus; this is a core intermediate experiential-aspect marker.

### V-는 것을 보다 — "to watch/see (someone) V-ing" (Lesson 26, p.256)

Nominalizes an ongoing action with -는 것 and takes it as the object of 보다. 어머니는 아이가 노는 것을 보고 있습니다 "A mother is watching her child play."

### V-아/어 있다 — resultative state, "to be [in the state of having] V-ed" (Lesson 27, p.268)

Marks a durative state resulting from a prior (often inherently telic/posture) verb, distinct from the ongoing-action -고 있다 already covered elsewhere in the project. Full verb-pair table given in-source: 앉다⇒앉아 있다 (sit⇒be seated), 서다⇒서 있다 (stand⇒be standing), 눕다⇒누워 있다 (lie down⇒be lying), 걸리다⇒걸려 있다 (be hung [as an event]⇒be hanging [as a state]), 놓이다⇒놓여 있다 (be placed [as an event]⇒be in a placed state). This posture/result-verb class and its systematic contrast with -고 있다 had zero prior coverage in the established corpus.

### ㅎ-irregular color adjectives (파랗다 and class) (Lesson 27, p.268)

Full conjugation table for 파랗다 "to be blue": 파랗습니다/파랗고/파라니까/파란데/파래요/파랬어요/파래서/파란 — showing the ㅎ-final stem dropping ㅎ and contracting before a vowel-initial ending (파랗 + 아 → 파래, not *파라아). The four other basic color adjectives (하얗다 white, 노랗다 yellow, 까맣다 black, 빨갛다 red) are explicitly grouped with it as the same irregular class. Zero prior established-file coverage of this ㅎ-irregular paradigm.

### V-ㄴ다/는다, A-다 — plain/narrative sentence-final endings (평서형 어미) (Lesson 28, p.278)

The plain written/narrative style's full declarative paradigm, contrasted with the polite -아/어요 and formal -습니다 styles already covered extensively elsewhere: verbs take -ㄴ다 (vowel stems: 공부한다) or -는다 (consonant stems: 읽는다), adjectives and the copula take bare -다 (아름답다, N(이)다: 이것은 책이다). The source also tabulates the full tense/aspect range in this same plain style on one page: past A/V-았다/었다 (어제 나는 음식을 만들었다), and future/presumptive A/V-(으)ㄹ 것이다 / A/V-겠다 (곧 겨울 방학이 될 것이다; 올해는 공부를 열심히 하겠다). This is the first established-file extraction of the plain style's complete paradigm in one place — directly feeds the speech-level/register system (see Lesson 32 below).

### A/V-(으)ㄹ 때(에)(는) A/V — "when A/V" (Lesson 30, p.296)

날씨가 따뜻할 때에는 기분이 좋아요 "I feel good when the weather is warm." A bare-noun variant N 때 "at N-time" is given alongside it (점심 때 만납시다 "let's meet at lunchtime").

### V-ㄴ다고/는다고 (말)하다[듣다], A-다고 (말)하다[듣다] — indirect/reported speech, declarative (Lesson 30, p.296)

Embeds a plain-style declarative clause (built on the -ㄴ다/-다 endings from Lesson 28 above) under a speech/perception verb to report what was said or heard, with the quoted clause's original tense preserved. 학생들은 시험이 어렵다고 말해요 "The students say the test is difficult"; 수미는 그 일이 정말 힘들었다고 했는데 토니는 별로 힘들지 않았다고 했어요 "Sumi said the work was really hard, but Tony said it wasn't that hard" — showing two independently-reported, contrasting quotes in one sentence. Zero prior established-file coverage of indirect quotation as a system.

### N(이)라고 하다 — indirect quotation of a copular statement (Lesson 30, p.296)

The copular counterpart to the verbal/adjectival indirect-quote pattern above. 이 꽃을 한국말로 뭐라고 해요? — 장미라고 해요 "What do you call this flower in Korean? — It's called 'jangmi.'"

### N이/가 (N에게) V-(으)라고 하다 — indirect quotation of an imperative (Lesson 31, p.309)

Reports a command given to someone, built on the imperative connector -(으)라고 rather than the declarative -다고 documented above — completing the indirect-quotation system across all three sentence types (declarative, imperative, and — by extension of the same -자고 pattern not separately captured here — propositive). 어머니가 아이에게 뛰지 말라고 했다 "The mother told her child not to run" (negative imperative quote, -지 말라고).

### A/V-아서/어서 걱정이다 — "to worry because A/V" (Lesson 31, p.308)

한국의 여름 날씨가 너무 더워서 걱정이에요 "I'm worried because Korean summer weather is too hot."

### 짓다 — "to build" (ㅅ-irregular verb) (Lesson 31, p.309)

Full paradigm given in-source: 짓습니다/짓는데/지어요/지었어요/지어서/지으면/지으니까/지을 거예요 — showing the ㅅ-final stem dropping ㅅ before a vowel-initial ending (짓 + 어 → 지어, not *짓어). Zero prior established-file coverage of this ㅅ-irregular verb's paradigm.

### 해체 (반말/intimate) speech-style paradigm — full system (Lesson 32, pp.318–321)

The lesson systematically introduces the intimate/casual speech level (해체, commonly called 반말) across every sentence type, directly complementing this project's existing honorifics file (`031_korean_honorifics_and_politeness.md`) by supplying the *casual* end of the register scale with a full in-source paradigm rather than scattered mentions:
- **Declarative/interrogative A/V-아/어(?)** — same form serves both, disambiguated by intonation/context: 지금 집에 가? "Are you going home now?" / 응, 지금 집에 가 "Yeah, I'm going home now."
- **Interrogative A/V-니/냐?** and **A/V-지?** — alternate casual question forms, the latter seeking confirmation: 숙제 다 했지? "You finished your homework, right?"
- **Copula N(이)야(?)** — the intimate-style copula, replacing 이에요/예요: 저 사람은 누구야? — 우리 형이야 "Who's that? — He's my older brother."
- **Probability A-(으)ㄹ까? / A-(으)ㄹ 거야** and **future V-(으)ㄹ 거야(?)** — intimate-style counterparts of -(으)ㄹ까요?/-(으)ㄹ 거예요.
- **Imperative V-아/어** — 내 말을 잘 들어 봐 "Listen carefully to what I'm saying."
- **Propositive V-자 / negative propositive V-지 말자** — 시간 없으니까 빨리 가자 "There isn't much time, let's go quickly"; 오늘부터 담배 피우지 말자 "Let's not smoke starting today."
- **Exclamatory A-다! / V-ㄴ다/는다!** — reuses the Lesson 28 plain-style stems with an exclamatory reading: 아주 잘한다! "You're doing really well!"

Together with Lesson 28's plain-narrative paradigm, this gives a near-complete map of Korean's non-honorific registers in one source — high value for any later register/slang-synthesis mechanics work, since slang and intimate registers are overwhelmingly built on exactly this 해체 base rather than the polite -요 style.

### "……" 하고 V — direct-quotation marker (Lesson 33, p.330)

하고 following a quoted string marks direct (verbatim) speech or sound, distinct from the indirect-quotation -다고/-라고 system documented under Lessons 30–31. 고양이가 "야옹" 하고 울었습니다 "The cat cried, 'meow.'" The same lesson's folk-tale text is explicitly built around onomatopoeia (찍찍, 멍멍) quoted this way — the TOC lists "의성어 익히기" (mastering onomatopoeia) as one of this lesson's functions, and this 하고-quotation frame is the grammatical vehicle for it.

### V-아/어 버리다 — completive aspect with emphasis/regret nuance (Lesson 33, p.330)

Marks an action as completed entirely/irrevocably, frequently with a nuance of regret, relief, or finality depending on context. 기차를 놓쳐 버렸어요 "I (went and) missed the train [completely]"; 돈을 다 써 버렸어요 "I used up all my money." Two fully lexicalized forms built on this same auxiliary are given alongside it: 잃어버리다 "to lose [an object]" and 잊어버리다 "to forget" — both have become fixed compound verbs rather than productive V+버리다 combinations. Zero prior established-file coverage of this aspectual auxiliary.
