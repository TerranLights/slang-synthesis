### Grammar points

- **-게 되다** — attached to a verb stem, marks a change of state or capability arising from someone
  else's action or an external circumstance ("to come to/end up ~ing"), not the subject's own
  volition: 한국에 와서 매운 음식을 먹게 되었어요 "After coming to Korea I came to be able to eat
  spicy food" — notably extends to a *capability* change, not just a passive circumstantial one.
  Attached to N+이/가 되다, it marks a change of status/identity: 동생이 고등학생이 됐어요 "My
  younger sibling became a high-schooler."
- **~을/를 소개하다 / 자기 소개를 하다** — "to introduce X (to someone)" / the set phrase "to
  introduce oneself."
- **부터** — "starting from X," attached to a noun; also marks priority/order rather than pure time:
  선생님부터 드세요 "please eat first, teacher" (not "starting from the teacher").
- **-(이)라고 하다** — "to be called/named ~," attached to a noun: 저는 김철수라고 합니다 "I am
  called Kim Cheolsu."
- **~에게 ~을/를 가르치다** — "to teach someone something."
- **V-게 되어(서) 반갑다/기쁘다** — a fixed greeting idiom combining -게 되다 with 반갑다/기쁘다,
  "glad to come to ~" (used specifically in introduction-greeting contexts).
- **자기 + N** — "one's own N" (reflexive possessive).
- **동안** — "for (a duration of) ~," attached after a time-count noun: 삼 년 동안 한국에서
  살았어요 "I lived in Korea for 3 years."

- **때문에** — "because of ~," attached after a noun to mark cause/reason.
- **-아지다/-어지다** — "to become ~," attached to an adjective stem, marking a change of state:

  this clothing decent/okay?"
- **~을/를 조심하다** — "to be careful of ~."

### Grammar points

- **V-지 말다** — prohibitive, "don't do V" (지 마십시오/마세요/맙시다 forms).
- **V-지 말고 V2** — "don't do V, (do V2 instead)": 놀지 말고 공부하세요 "don't play, study."
- **잘/잘못 + V** — "do V well / do V wrongly": 잘못 거셨어요 "you dialed the wrong number."
- **번** — counter for "number/times" (phone numbers, bus routes): 몇 번에 걸었어요?, 148번 버스.
