- *(deeper)* **Instrument-specific "play (an instrument)" verb collocation system** (p. 252, a usage
  note not tied to a single numbered G-point): Korean uses a different verb depending on how the
  instrument is played — 치다 "to hit" for keyboard/percussion instruments played with a striking
  motion (기타를 치다, 피아노를 치다, 드럼을 치다— note 기타 'guitar' is grouped with 치다 here,
  i.e. strum-as-strike), 켜다 "to play (a bowed/stringed instrument)" for bowed strings (바이올린을
  켜다, 첼로를 켜다), and 불다 "to blow" for wind instruments (클라리넷을 불다, 플룻을 불다); 하다 can
  substitute generically for any of these (피아노를 하세요? / 바이올린을 할 줄 아세요?) and is also
  the only option for non-instrument activities like sports (테니스를 하세요?) and habitual actions
  (아침마다 조깅을 하세요?). This is a genuinely systematic sub-lexical verb-choice pattern not found
  tabulated this explicitly in the Korean established files grepped before this dispatch.

- **Appendix 1 — Copula, Adjective, and Verb Conjugations master table (p. 284-296, excerpted at p.
  286). (deeper)** A cross-lesson conjugation reference spanning Lessons 2-14 of the whole two-level
  series: rows are individual grammatical patterns (e.g. "~(으)면서 'while ~ing', clausal connective",
  "~었/았는데 background information", "~(으)세요 honorific polite", "~(으)십니다 honorific
  deferential", "~어/아 보다 'try doing'", "~어서/아서 causal/sequential", "~어/아야 되다/하다
  obligation"), columns are five predicate classes (이다, 아니다, 있다, 계시다, 되다, plus a
  worked example pair 깨끗하다/공부하다 for adjective-vs-verb conjugation). This is explicitly a
  study-reference synthesizing the whole book's grammar into one navigable grid — a genuinely more
  systematic cross-lesson resource than any single lesson's own grammar box, and worth noting as a
  structural finding for the mechanics-analysis phase even though each individual pattern is already
  documented elsewhere.
- **Appendix 2 — Honorific and Humble Expressions (p. 297). (deeper)** A unified plain-form/
  honorific-form/humble-form three-column grid spanning nouns (나이→연세; 이름→성함; 생일→생신; 말→
  말씀[hon.]/말씀[humble, same surface form — note the honorific/humble syncretism]; 집→댁; 밥→진지;
  사람/명→분), pronouns (그/사람→그 분[hon.]; 나는/내가→저는/제가[humble]; 내→제; 우리→저희[humble]),
  verbs (보다/만나다→보시다/만나시다[hon.]→뵙다[humble]; 있다→계시다[hon.]; 죽다→돌아가시다[hon.];
  자다→주무시다[hon.]; 먹다/들다→잡수시다[hon.]/드시다[hon.]→드리다[humble, as 'give to']; 주다→
  주시다[hon.]/드리다[humble]; 말하다→말씀하시다[hon.]/말씀드리다[humble]; 물어 보다→여쭈어 보다
  [humble]), particles (이/가→께서[hon.]; 은/는→께서는[hon.]; 한테/에게→께[hon.]), and suffixes (~님
  for titles: 교수→교수님, 부모→부모님, 선생→선생님). This is more systematic than the scattered
  honorific-vocabulary items individual lessons introduce piecemeal, and is a useful cross-check
  against `031_korean_honorifics_and_politeness.md`'s own treatment.
- **Appendix 3 — Kinship Terms (p. 298). (deeper)** Two tables: §1 nuclear family with explicit
  plain-vs-honorific pairs and male-speaker-vs-female-speaker sibling-term splits (오빠/언니 for a
  female speaker's older brother/sister vs. 형/누나 for a male speaker's); §2 extended relatives with
  an unusually granular paternal/maternal and older/younger uncle-aunt system (큰아버지/큰어머니 =
  father's older brother/his wife; 작은아버지/작은어머니 = father's younger brother/his wife; 삼촌/
  숙모 = a less formally specified version of the same; 외삼촌/외숙모 = mother's brother/his wife;
  고모 = father's sister; 이모 = mother's sister) — more granular than this same book's own Lesson 10
  "More Family Terms" vocabulary box, worth a direct cross-reference for any future
  mechanics-analysis pass on Korean kinship-term morphology.
- **Appendix 4 — Color Terms (p. 299). (deeper)** A 5-column paradigm (dictionary form → color-noun
  form → noun-modifier form → -아/어요 conjugated form) for the five basic native-Korean color
  adjectives (노랗다/하얗다[희다]/까맣다/빨갛다/파랗다), plus a 7-item "Additional Colors" list of
  Sino-Korean-based color nouns (초록색, 갈색, 밤색, 회색, 보라색, 주황색, 분홍색) that don't
  participate in the native-adjective paradigm. This is a cleaner systematic treatment of Korean
  basic color terms than the scattered individual-lesson mentions (Lesson 10's 까맣다/빨갛다/노랗다)
  found elsewhere in this corpus.
- **Appendix 5 — Numbers (p. 300). (deeper)** Full Sino-Korean and native-Korean numeral tables, 0
  through 10,000, with the native-Korean counter-bound allomorphs (하나→한, 둘→두, 셋→세, 넷→네,
  스물→스무) shown as a fourth column — plus a "Large Numbers" sidebar illustrating Korean's
  myriad-based (10,000-unit, 만/억/조) grouping convention, a structural fact about the number system
  worth flagging for any typological comparison.
- **Appendix 6 — Counters (p. 302). (deeper)** Explicitly split into "A. With Sino-Korean Numbers"
  (층 floors, 분 minutes, 과 lessons, 년 years, 월 months, 일 days, 달러 dollars, 원 won, 마일 miles,
  학년 school-years, 번 serial numbers, 주일 weeks) and "B. With Native-Korean Numbers" (명/사람
  people, 과 generic items) — a clean systematic statement of the Sino-Korean-vs-native-Korean
  counter-choice rule that individual lessons only ever illustrate with one or two counters at a
  time.
