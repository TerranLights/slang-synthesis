## Grammar points

### Lesson 28

**28.1 Retrospective Aspect (-더-/-습디다/-데요/-더라 family).** Korean marks a distinct
evidential/retrospective aspect: the speaker has direct (usually sensory) evidence for a past
event, contrasted with a plain matter-of-fact past statement. Meaning ranges over "I saw/noticed
that...," "I understand/heard that...," or simple "was doing [when I observed it]." Forms differ
by speech style and base tense:

| Style | Statement | Question | Apperceptive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formal | -습디다/-ᄆ디다 | -습디까?/-ᄆ디까? | -더군요! |
| Polite | -데요 (-던데요/-더라고요) | -던가요? | -더군요! |
| Plain | -더라 | -더냐?/-디? | -더군! |

**Register note (explicit in the source, flagged as a 주의 "caution" box):** the Formal-style
retrospective forms in -습디까/-습디다 are now used primarily by speakers in their 50s-60s. The
book explicitly advises learners not to use this form themselves even though they may hear it from
older speakers — a directly marked generational-register constraint, not a formality-only
distinction.

**28.1.2 Apperceptive retrospectives** (-더군/-더구먼) mark a "why... / what do you know... / I
see!" realization, built by attaching -더군/-더구먼 to any base (cf. non-retrospective apperceptive
-군/-구먼, taught in section 21.9, attached via the processive modifier -는 for processive verbs).

**28.2 Retrospective modifier -던 on past bases.** Previously (§19.5) -던 attached only to plain
bases (progressive "was doing" sense). Attached to a past base (-었더) it implies the action
continued for a time, then stopped. Fixed idiom: 갔던 길에 "on the way back (from having gone)"
vs. plain 가는 길에 "on the way (going)" — the retrospective-past form specifically encodes the
return leg.

**28.3 -던데(요).** Combines retrospective -더- with imminent-elaboration -(으)ᄂ데(요): "[I
observed that] X happened/happens/will happen, and there's more I could tell you."

**28.4 Retrospective conditionals in -더라면 / -다면.** Attaching -더- to a past base plus
conditional -라면 (했더라면) or simply -다면 to a past base (했다면) yields "if [X] had
happened" — a past-counterfactual conditional. The book notes there is a separate, less-common
past-conditional form in -었으면 (Lesson 21) with the same meaning, but -더라면/-다면 is the one
"most commonly used." §28.4.2 reviews compounded past-past/past-future/past-past-future verb forms
(했었을 거예요 etc.) used in the "then" clause of such conditionals, with a note that the future
marker -겠- carries either a definite-future or an evidential "must have been" sense, and that
sense compounds when -겠- attaches after -었을.

**Morpheme breakdown example** (agglutinative stacking, illustrative): 했었더라면 = 하 (do, verb
stem) + -었- (past) + -었- (remote/past-past) + -더- (retrospective) + -라면 (conditional "if").
Four grammatical morphemes stacked on one lexical stem — a clear case where treating the surface
form as an atomic vocabulary item would hide real compositional structure.

**28.5 Extended uses of the -고 quotation pattern.**
- **28.5.1** -더라고(요): since Plain-Style retrospective -더라 cannot itself take the Polite
  particle 요 (no Plain-Style ending can), Korean speakers instead *quote* -더라 and round it off
  with 요, yielding a colloquial Polite-register retrospective. Presented as a workaround for a
  genuine morphological gap in the paradigm.
- **28.5.2** Quotation repeats: 요 attached to a quoted form is used specifically for
  asking-for/giving a repeat ("What? — [I said,] do you have any money?"), e.g. 돈이 있(느)냐고요.

**28.6 Errand reporting.** Korean errand-reporting sentences often mention only the "do something"
+ "come/go back" legs of a three-part errand (go / do / return), omitting the "went" leg that
English keeps — e.g. 시계 찾아왔어요? "did you go and get the watch [and bring it back]?" This
pattern is restricted to verbs where an object is taken away or brought as a result. Directionally,
가- ("go[es]") and 오- ("come[s]") in Korean are always anchored to the speaker's location *at the
time of speaking*, which can invert the English go/come mapping (e.g., promising over the phone
"I'll come to your house" is 갈게요 "I'll go," not 올게요, since the speaker is not yet there).

**28.7 -(으)ᄅ 정도(로): "to the extent that...".** 정도 ("extent") as postmodifier after the
prospective modifier -(으)ᄅ yields "is/does to the extent that VERB," e.g. 배꼽이 빠질 정도로
재미있게 웃었다 "we laughed so hard our belly buttons nearly fell out." Register note (주의 box):
in objective written-prose Plain Style (한다/하다 endings), the honorific marker -(으)시- is
considered inappropriate even with a nominally honorific-worthy subject — register and grammatical
person/politeness marking are explicitly decoupled in formal written registers.

**28.8 -고 나서: "after VERBing".** Plain base + -고 나서 = "after doing [it]"; restricted to
processive verbs only.

**28.9 NOUN(을/를) 위해(서) / VERB-기(를) 위해(서): "for the sake of NOUN" / "in order to VERB".**
The verbal form attaches the summative nominalizer -기 (itself a noun-forming device) before 위해서.

### Lesson 29

**29.1 Summative-form (-기) auxiliary patterns.** Three constructions built on the -기 nominalizer
plus common particles:
- **-기도 하-** (I): "really does/is," typically paired with mild-exclamation -네요.
- **-기도 하-** (II): repeated as "does/is both X and Y" when used twice in succession (for verbs);
  for verbal nouns the 도 particle instead attaches directly to the noun (운동도 하고 산보도 가요).
- **-기는 하지만**: "does/is, to be sure, but..." — tense/mood marking attaches only to the -지만
  clause, never to the -기는 clause, which stays invariant.
- **-기만 하-**: "does nothing but..." Can drop 하- with verbal nouns (공부(하기)만 해요) and with
  cognate objects (잠만 자요 alongside 잠을 자기만 해요), for "does nothing but sleep."

**29.2 -는/-(으)ᄂ 편이- : "is on the ...ing side; is fairly/pretty...".** 편 ("side, direction")
as postmodifier after the processive modifier -는 or descriptive/past modifier -(으)ᄂ, plus the
copula, yields a hedge/mitigation pattern roughly equivalent to English "pretty/fairly ADJ" or
"tends to VERB fairly ADVERB." An adverb modifying the underlying verb is typically present and
important to the construction's natural use.

**29.3 Postmodifier -(으)ᄂ지: "[TIME] since...".** Past modifier + 지(가) + a time word + one of
이에요/돼요/넘어요/지났어요 ("is/becomes/exceeds/has passed") yields "it's been [TIME] since...".
A negative-modifier version of this clause paraphrases the same situation from the other side (안
만난 지 = "it's been [TIME] since I *haven't* met" = "I haven't met [him] for [TIME]") rather than
denying the construction's core meaning.

**29.4 Postmodifier -지: oblique (embedded) questions.** Modifier + 지 forms an embedded
question used inside a larger sentence containing an information verb (알아요/몰라요, 기억(을)
해요, 생각(이) 나요, 잊어버려요, 궁금해요, 가르쳐요) or a verb of asking/saying (물어봐요, 말해요).
Shapes: descriptive base + -(으)ᄂ지; processive base + -는지; past base + -는지 (uniformly, for all
verb types); future/prospective base + -(으)ᄅ지 or -(으)ᄅ 것인지 (abbreviated -(으)ᄅ건지). With
알아요/몰라요 specifically, a *content*-question-word oblique question needs no negative
counterpart, but a *yes/no*-type oblique question is normally paired with its negative ("...는지 안
...는지 알아요?" "do you know whether or not..."). With other information/asking verbs (선생님이
왜 그렇게 거만하신지..., etc.), no such negative-pairing restriction applies. A further extension:
oblique question + polite particle 요 alone, without any following verb, means "I wonder..." — the
book notes this is a favorite construction of TV interviewers using honorific-marked oblique
questions (예: 언제 하셨는지요? "I wonder/may I ask when you did [it]?").

**29.5 Polite questions in -(으)ᄂ/는가(요)? and -나(요)?.** Two additional polite-question
formations beyond the plain -어/아요:
- **-(으)ᄂ/는가요?**: modifier + 가, more natural with descriptive verbs/copula (occasionally
  processive). Two special future/past modifiers -겠는 and -었는 are restricted to positions before
  certain postmodifiers (가, 야, 지, 데) only.
- **-나(요)?**: attaches to processive bases and to 있-/없-, and to any past or future base; more
  natural for processive verbs than -(으)ᄂ가요?. The book's practical rule of thumb: use -(으)ᄂ가요?
  with *present descriptive* verbs, and -나요? for everything else.

Both are noted as generally more common in practice than their "theoretical" full paradigm
counterparts (e.g. 먹었나요? is more natural than the fully regular 먹은가요?).

### Lesson Thirty / Review 5 — book-wide structural summary (30.2-30.4)

This is not new grammar per se but a systematic cross-reference the book itself compiles at the
end — genuinely valuable as a compact map of the whole grammar system, so it is captured here
rather than skipped as "review":

**30.2.1 Verb base classification.** Korean verb bases are exhaustively classified by their
phonological shape and the alternations they undergo before consonant- vs. vowel-initial endings:
vowel-base verbs; 으-dropping vowel-base verbs (고프-, 끄-, 슬프-, etc. — the 으 drops before a
vowel-initial ending); ㄹ-doubling vowel-base verbs (게으르-, 마르-, 바르-, 조르-, etc. — the final
syllable doubles its ㄹ before a vowel ending, e.g. 몰라); 르-extending vowel-base verbs (길-,
다-ᄅ- "be sweet," 부르- "blow," 빠르- "wash [clothes]," etc. — insert an extra ᄅ before certain
endings); plain consonant-base verbs; w-irregular verbs (귀엽-, 덥-, 아름답-, 뜨겁- etc. — final
ᄇ becomes w before a vowel-initial ending: 돕다 → 도와요, traditionally called "irregular
ᄇ-verbs"); ᄅ-N verbs (e.g. 깨닫- "realize," an ㄷ-irregular where final ㄷ becomes ㄹ before a
vowel-initial ending, traditionally "irregular ᄃ-verbs" — contrasted explicitly with regular
ᄃ-verbs like 닫- "close," which keep ᄃ throughout, and with ᄅ-extending 드르-/들- pairs that look
similar in some forms but diverge in others, e.g. conditional 들면 "if it costs" vs. 들으면 "if
[one] hears"); ᄉ-ᄋ verbs (나ᄉ-, 붓-, 짓- — final ᄉ drops before a vowel-initial ending); and
ᄒ-irregular verbs (그렇-, 까맣-, 빨갛-, 하얗-, etc. — color/manner descriptives whose final ᄒ
interacts with following consonants in specific ways, detailed next).

**30.2.1 (cont.) Consonant sound-change rules at verb-ending boundaries** — a genuinely dense
phonological summary:
- A one-shape ending beginning with ᄃ/ᄌ/ᄀ attached to a ᄒ-final consonant base is pronounced with
  aspiration: ᄒ+ᄃ → [ᄐ], ᄒ+ᄌ → [ᄎ], ᄒ+ᄀ → [ᄏ] (e.g. 좋지 → [조치], 좋고 → [조코]). Same for ᄒ
  clusters 많 and 싫.
- When such an ending instead begins with ᄂ (only common case: -네요), a base-final ᄒ after a
  vowel is realized as [ᄂ] (좋네요 → [존네요]); clusters ᄡ and ᄚ reduce to [ᄂ]/[ᄅ] respectively
  (많네요 → [만네요], 싫네요 → [실레요]).
- A base-final w (the "irregular ᄇ" class) becomes ᄇ before a one-shape ending (돕지 → [돕찌], 돕고
  → [돕꼬]) — distinguishing these bases from ordinary ᄇ-final bases like 입- only via their other
  forms (도와, 도우면 vs. 입어, 입으면).
- A base-final irregular-ᄃ ᄅ (from 듣- "hear," etc.) is pronounced ᄃ before a one-shape ending
  (듣지 → [드찌], 듣고 → [드꼬]), again distinguished from ᄅ-extending bases only by other forms.
- General consonant-cluster reduction: base-final consonants/clusters other than ᄇ/ᄃ/ᄀ/ᄆ/ᄂ/ᄅ are
  reduced toward one of those six in pronunciation (ᄡ/ᄑ → ᄇ-like; ᄁ/ᄀ + ᄉ → ᄀ-like; ᄐ → ᄆ-like
  [as printed]; ᄌ → ᄂ-like; ᄉ/ᄊ/ᄌ/ᄎ → ᄃ-like).

**30.2.2 Verb endings, classified two ways:**
1. **By finality** — FINAL endings (complete a sentence: e.g. Plain-Style statement -다/-는다,
   Retrospective forms, Polite questions -나요) vs. NONFINAL endings (do not complete a sentence,
   many connective: -고, infinitive -어/아, nominalizer -기, modifiers -(으)ᄂ/는/던, conditional
   -(으)면, intentive -(으)려(고), -느라(고), -더라면, -고 나서, etc.).
2. **By number of alternate shapes** — one-shape endings (attach unchanged regardless of a
   preceding consonant/vowel: -고, -기, -게, -자, -네요, etc. — with the irregular-base sound
   changes above applying at the boundary); two-shape endings (a vowel-final-base form vs. a
   consonant-final-base form with 으 inserted: -(으)면, -(으)려고, -(으)라, etc. — attach to the
   *extended* base of ᄅ-extending verbs, e.g. 놀- → 놀며/놀면/놀려고/놀려다가/놀려면/놀라); and
   three-shape endings (a further three-way split by descriptive-vowel / descriptive-consonant /
   processive-or-past-or-future base, covering the -데(요) family, -는/은/ᄅ데도, -(으)ᄂ/는가(요)?,
   -(으)냐/느냐?, and the -다/-는다 Plain-Style statement family).

**30.3 Particle/postnoun/suffix inventory** for the whole book: NOUN네, NOUN보다, NOUN말고, NOUN에
관해서/대해서 ("about, concerning" — two synonymous forms), NOUN으로, NOUN(을/를) 위해(서), NOUN에
의하면 ("according to..."), NOUN에다(가), and a diminutive suffix -이. The book also explicitly
classifies 하고 and -(이)라고 (verbatim-quotation markers) and -고 (indirect-quotation marker), as
well as 도 and 서 in -어야/-어도/-어서, as belonging to the particle category rather than being
ordinary verb endings — a taxonomic point about how this course's own grammar treats quotation
marking.

**30.4 Auxiliary verb inventory**: 가- (compound verbs, e.g. 돌아가-), 말- (prohibition, -지
말아요/마세요), 보- (exploratory -어 봐요), 싶- ("want to," -고 싶-), 주- (favor, -어 줘요), 않-
(negation, -지 않아요), 오- (compound verbs, 돌아오-), 있- (resultant state, -어 있어요), 지-
(inchoative "get/become," -어져요), 하- (twice, in two distinct functions: 3rd-person sensory
-어해요, and the -기도/-기만/-기는 하- family from §29.1).
