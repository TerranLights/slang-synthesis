# Korean --- Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Hanyang Korean 1 (full course: Grammar & Patterns booklet + Textbook)

**Source:** Hanyang University International Language Institute (한양대학교 국제어학원 편), *한양
한국어 1 / Hanyang Korean 1*, Hanyang University Press (한양대학교 출판부). Two volumes merged in
this file:
- **Textbook** (본교재), 252pp, preface dated 2008년 1월 25일 (January 25, 2008). Director: Prof.
  Eom, Ik-Sang (엄익상). Credited faculty: Prof. Youngsook Lee (이영숙), Prof. Yumi Lee (이유미),
  Prof. Junghoon Kim (김정훈), ILI Korean Language Department; photography by Kao Ming (까오 밍/
  高明). Fifteen lessons (제1과-제15과), each split into two sub-parts (<1>/<2>), covering Lessons
  1-15: 인사 (Greetings), 교실 (Classroom), 한국어 공부 (Studying Korean), 식당 (Restaurant), 가족
  (Family), 날짜와 요일 (Date/Day), 길에서 (On the Street), 하루의 일과 (Daily Routine), 가게 (The
  Store), 전화 (Phone Calls), 약속 (Appointment), 교통 (Transportation), 친구 (Friend), 고향
  (Hometown), 하숙집 (Boarding House). Recurring dialogue cast: 제니 (Jenny, Canada), 마이클
  (Michael, USA), 한솔 (Hansol, Korean male student), 은영 (Eunyoung, Korean female student).
- **Grammar & Patterns booklet** (문법과 문형), ISBN 978-89-7218-334-1, a supplementary/non-
  commercial ("비매품") trilingual (English/Chinese/Japanese) companion keyed to the same 15
  lessons; this PDF contains only its English-language section (printed pp.3-22). A condensed,
  systematic grammar-pattern reference with no vocabulary boxes of its own.
- **Writing Practice volume** (쓰기 연습), 129pp — sampled (45 pages, ~35% of the book, spread
  across all 15 lessons' front matter through back matter) and confirmed to be pure consolidation/
  review drill (syllable tracing, fill-in-the-blank, matching, conjugation tables) introducing no
  new vocabulary or grammar; the book's own preface explicitly states grammar explanations live in
  the separate Grammar & Patterns booklet, not here. **Nothing from this volume is included below.**

No printed publication year is stated on the Grammar & Patterns booklet's own cover; PDF creation
metadata for all three files dates to October-November 2010, consistent with a slightly later
printing/reissue of the same 2008 course. "Contemporary (source published 2008/~2010)" is used per
the extraction spec's coarse whole-book dating allowance.

**Coverage note — how this file was assembled.** None of the three source PDFs have a text layer
(`pdftotext`/`pdffonts` confirmed zero embedded fonts on all three — pure raster scans); all content
below was vision-read from rendered page images across 7 parallel dispatches (Grammar & Patterns in
full; the Textbook split into 5 page-range chunks of ~50pp each; Writing Practice sample-checked).
**No handwritten marginalia was found anywhere** across any of the three PDFs' sampled/read pages —
all three appear to be unused/clean scanned copies.

**Non-redundant-supplement scope decision (per `00_Reference_Extraction_Spec.md`):** Korean already
has 12+ beginner-level course books fully extracted in `datasets/Korean/established/` (e.g.
`015_korean_through_english_1.md`, `016_active_korean_1.md`, `035_integrated_korean_beginning_1.md`,
`037_korean_level_1_snu.md`, `042_my_korean_1.md`, `045_korean_for_dummies.md`). Hanyang Korean 1
teaches standard Level-1 grammar (particle system, copula, polite/formal sentence-ending families,
tense marking, basic connectives, counters, honorifics) that substantially overlaps this prior
coverage. Per convention: **all vocabulary is kept regardless of grammar-overlap** — vocabulary is
never skipped for redundancy, since it is this book's own distinct example set (own cast, own
dialogue contexts, own curated classifier/kinship/food-vocabulary sets). **Grammar points are
extracted in full but individually flagged** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` where the pattern
is extremely common bedrock material near-certainly captured by prior sources; points with
Hanyang-specific framing, sequencing, or a fuller systematic paradigm table are not flagged and kept
as genuinely non-redundant per the spec's "a fuller/more systematic paradigm table... is non-
redundant by convention" guidance.

**Merge note — Textbook vs. Grammar & Patterns booklet.** These two volumes are companion materials
of the *same* course, keyed to the *same* 15 lessons — they are not independent sources, so their
grammar points are **consolidated into one write-up per lesson below** rather than duplicated
side-by-side. The Textbook's own contextualized dialogue/vocabulary is used as the primary
explanation and example; where the Grammar & Patterns booklet adds a fuller systematic paradigm
table (irregular-verb conjugation classes, the formal/informal sentence-ending comparison grid, the
imperative/suggestion affirmative-negative 2x2) or a detail the Textbook's own grammar box omits
(e.g. the ㄷ-irregular exception list, the honorific suppletive-verb list), this is noted as an
explicit addition rather than re-explained from scratch. Grammar & Patterns has **no vocabulary
section of its own** (it is example-sentence-only); its ~200 incidental content words compiled from
across its 74 example sentences are kept as a separate labeled appendix below rather than merged
line-by-line into the Textbook's vocabulary tables, since de-duplicating ~200 terms by hand against
~750 Textbook entries risked introducing transcription errors into this file without a reliable
automated check — the overlap between the two lists is real and expected (same lessons, same core
words) but is left visible rather than silently collapsed.

**Vision-reading guard:** actively checked on every page across all three PDFs; no handwritten
marginalia (pencil/pen notes, filled-in answer blanks, underlines) was found anywhere. One page
(printed p.244, in the back-matter listening-script appendix) shows small black smudge/toner-smear
marks between printed lines — flagged as a print/scan artifact, not handwriting; nothing was
excluded on its account, and the surrounding text was unaffected and fully legible.

**Morpheme breakdown (agglutinative forms worth flagging).**

> **외할아버지** = 외- (maternal/outside-family prefix, lit. "outer") + 할아버지 (grandfather) — the 외- prefix
> productively derives the whole maternal-side kin set (외할머니, 외삼촌) from the paternal-side base terms,
> reflecting Korean kinship terminology's patrilineal-default structure (paternal terms are unmarked, maternal
> terms are marked with 외-).

> **돌솥비빔밥** = 돌 (stone) + 솥 (pot/cauldron) + 비빔 (mixing, nominalized stem of 비비다 "to mix/rub") +
> 밥 (cooked rice) — a four-morpheme compound naming the hot-stone-pot variant of 비빔밥.

> **비빔냉면** = 비빔 (mixing, nominalized stem of 비비다) + 냉면 (cold noodles) — same 비빔- mixing-stem
> productively recombines with a different base dish, demonstrating 비빔- as a semi-productive "mixed/tossed
> (in sauce)" modifier across the food-vocabulary set (cf. 비빔밥, 김치볶음밥 uses a different compounding
> strategy with 볶음 "stir-fried" instead).

## Grammar points

*Consolidated per lesson from both the Textbook and the Grammar & Patterns booklet (companion
volumes of the same course, keyed to the same 15 lessons — not independent sources, so not
duplicated side by side). Points flagged* `[likely already covered elsewhere]` *are near-certainly
already documented in Korean's other 12+ beginner-level `established/` sources; extracted in full
regardless per the coverage rule, flagged for the orchestrator's/future researcher's merge-time
judgment rather than condensed away.*

### Lesson 1 — 인사 (Greetings)

**-이/가 (subject marker)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — -이 after a consonant-final
noun, -가 after a vowel-final noun. First grammar point in the book. E.g. 이것이 책입니다 "This is
a book."

**-입니다 / -입니까? (formal declarative/interrogative copula)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]`
— -이다 + -ㅂ니다/-ㅂ니까?. The backbone of all Lesson 1 dialogue: "이름이 무엇입니까?" ("What is
your name?"), "그분이 의사입니다" ("That person is a doctor").

**-은/는 (topic marker)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — -은 after consonant-final, -는
after vowel-final nouns; can also mark contrast. Taught with 저는 self-introduction framing. E.g.
바나나가 쌉니다. 파인애플은 비쌉니다 "A banana is cheap. A pineapple is expensive" (contrastive 은).

**-이/가 아닙니다 (negative copula)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — subject-marked noun +
아닙니다/아닙니까?, conjugates like an adjective. E.g. 마이클 씨는 선생님이 아닙니다. 학생입니다
"Mr. Michael is not a teacher. He is a student." Note the predicate noun itself takes -이/가, not
the topic particle.

### Lesson 2 — 교실 (Classroom)

**-이/가 있다/없다 (existence/possession)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — 있다 "to exist/
have/be located," negated 없다; honorific counterpart 계시다. Dual locative-existence and
possession sense: 교실에 책상이 있습니다 "There are desks in the classroom"; 책이 있습니까? "Do you
have a book?" Drilled with the answer pattern 네, 있습니다. / 아니요, 없습니다.

**-에 (static locative "in/at")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — marks the location where
something exists/is situated (distinct from a later directional/destination use). E.g. 여권이
가방에 있습니다 "The passport is in the bag"; 저는 서울에 삽니다 "I live in Seoul."

**-하고 ("and," connecting nouns)** — connects two+ nouns on an equal footing; the Grammar &
Patterns booklet flags this explicitly as mainly a **spoken-register** marker, with -와/과 as its
written-register counterpart — a genuinely useful register note not every source makes explicit.
E.g. 교실에 의자하고 책상이 있습니다 "There is a chair and a desk in the classroom," extended across
three items: 우리 교실에 선생님하고 제니 씨하고 마이클 씨가 있습니다.

**Sino-Korean numbers 1-9 + 층 (floor counter)** — 일, 이, 삼, 사, 오, 육, 칠, 팔, 구 + 층 →
일층...구층 ("1st floor"..."9th floor"). Numeral + counter attaches without further affixation.

**이것/그것/저것 (demonstrative pronoun set)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — three-way
distance contrast: 이것 (proximal), 그것 (medial, near listener/known), 저것 (distal). Drilled with
"~이 무엇입니까?" / "~은/는 [noun]입니다."

**-ㅂ/습니다, -ㅂ/습니까? (formal-style endings, on descriptive verbs/adjectives)** `[likely already
covered elsewhere]` — -ㅂ니다 after vowel-final stems (크다 → 큽니다), -습니다 after consonant-final
stems (많다 → 많습니다). Bundled by the Textbook as a two-part pattern with the subject marker:
"꽃이 예쁩니다."

### Lesson 3 — 한국어 공부 (Studying Korean)

**-을/를 (object marker)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — -을 after consonant-final, -를
after vowel-final nouns. E.g. 저는 커피를 좋아합니다 "I like coffee"; 책을 읽습니다 "[I] read a book."

**-기 (nominalizing suffix)** — attaches to a verb stem to form a gerund-like noun: 말하다 → 말하기
"speaking," 듣다 → 듣기 "listening," 읽다 → 읽기 "reading," 쓰다 → 쓰기 "writing." The Textbook puts
this to productive use in an evaluative frame: [verb]-기 + 가 어떻습니까? → "한국어 공부하기가
어떻습니까? 재미있습니다," and extends it to full verb phrases ("텔레비전 보기," "숙제하기") — note
that 말하기/듣기/읽기와쓰기 are also the book's own names for its four curriculum macro-skills
(일러두기, p.10).

> **말하기** = 말하 (stem of 말하다, "to speak") + -기 (nominalizer) — turns the action verb into an
> abstract noun denoting the act/skill of speaking.

**-(으)십시오 (formal honorific imperative)** — -십시오 after vowel-final stems, -으십시오 after
consonant-final stems. Used for classroom instructions ("앉으십시오," "칠판을 보십시오") and advice
("숙제를 열심히 하십시오").

**-지 마십시오 (negative polite imperative) → -지 맙시다 (suggestion form)** — Negative counterpart
of -(으)십시오. The Grammar & Patterns booklet gives a compact 2x2 paradigm (Imperative aff.
-(으)십시오/neg. -지 마십시오; Suggestion aff. -(으)ㅂ시다/neg. -지 맙시다) that the Textbook's own
drills instantiate separately across two different lessons (-지 마십시오 here, as a classroom-rules
list: "음악을 듣지 마십시오," "담배를 피우지 마십시오"; -지 맙시다 later in Lesson 6, as "창문을
닫을까요? — 아니요, 닫지 맙시다"). Also extended to public sign-reading: "잔디밭에 들어가지
마십시오" (keep off the grass), "수영 금지," "사진 촬영 금지."

**ㄷ-irregular verbs** — stems ending in ㄷ change to ㄹ before a vowel-initial ending. The
Textbook's own example set explicitly contrasts a regular case (닫다 "close" → 닫으십시오, no
change) against the irregular 듣다 "listen" → 들으십시오 (not *듣으십시오), naming 걷다 "walk" and
묻다 "ask" as further class members; the Grammar & Patterns booklet separately confirms 받다
"receive" and 달다 "be sweet" as regular (non-irregular) ㄷ-final exceptions — a detail worth
keeping since not every source names the regular exceptions explicitly.

> **들으십시오** = 듣- (stem of 듣다, ㄷ→ㄹ before a vowel-initial ending) + -으십시오 (polite
> imperative) — the surface form masks the dictionary stem-final consonant.

### Lesson 4 — 식당 (Restaurant)

**-에 가다/오다 (destination marker + motion verb)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — -에 +
place noun + 가다/오다. E.g. 학교에 갑니다 "I go to school," drilled across six food-establishment
nouns (한식집, 중국집, 학생식당, etc.).

**-도 ("also/too")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — additive particle. E.g. 돼지고기를
좋아합니다. 야채도 좋아합니다 "I like pork. I like vegetables too."

**-(으)ㄹ까요? (opinion/suggestion question) ↔ -(으)ㅂ시다 ("let's")** `[likely already covered
elsewhere]` — -(으)ㄹ까요? asks the listener's view/intention; -(으)ㅂ시다 is the formal propositive
answer. E.g. 가: 제가 문을 닫을까요? 나: 네, 문을 닫으십시오; 바나나가 맛있습니다. 바나나를 먹읍시다
"Bananas are delicious. Let's eat bananas."

**-어요/-아요 (informal-polite sentence ending, 해요체)** `[likely already covered elsewhere, note
sequencing]` — vowel-harmony governed (ㅏ/ㅗ stems → -아요; ㅓ/ㅜ/ㅡ/ㅣ → -어요; 하- → 해요). The
Grammar & Patterns booklet gives a compact grid mapping Declarative/Interrogative/Imperative/
Suggestion + copula across Formal vs. Informal style. **Hanyang-specific sequencing note:** the
Textbook introduces 해요체 only here in Lesson 4, as an explicit *transform* of the 합쇼체 forms
already drilled for three lessons ("어디에 갑니다 → 어디에 가요"), rather than teaching it
independently from scratch — worth comparing against other Korean courses' register-introduction
order. Also: -이에요/예요 (copula + 요), -(으)세요 (honorific/imperative), paired here with a
종업원/손님 (wait-staff/customer) vocabulary split giving the 해요체 imperative a concrete
service-register use case.

**-고 싶다 (desiderative "want to")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — limited to 1st-person
subjects in statements, 2nd-person in questions (3rd person instead uses -고 싶어 하다). E.g.
불고기를 먹고 싶어요. 한식집에 가고 싶어요.

**(noun 을/를) 주세요 ("give me ~")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — object-marked noun +
주세요. E.g. 여기 김밥하고 콜라 주세요.

### Lesson 5 — 가족 (Family)

**-의 (possessive marker)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — frequently dropped in casual
speech. Drilled with kinship recursion: 아버지의 아버지 = 할아버지 "father's father = grandfather."

**-었-/-았- (past tense/perfective)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — allomorph by stem
vowel: ㅏ/ㅗ → -았-, ㅓ/ㅜ/ㅡ/ㅣ(etc.) → -었-, 하- → -였-/했-. E.g. 제니 씨는 어제 뭐 했어요? —
친구를 만났어요.

**-에 (time-point marker, distinct sense from Lesson 2's locative -에)** `[likely already covered
elsewhere]` — attaches to time nouns (지난 주말에, 작년에, 밤 열한 시에). The Textbook treats this as
the same -에 morpheme doing double duty rather than a separate homophonous particle — the standard
linguistic analysis.

**-에서 (dynamic locative "at/in"; also "from")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — marks
where an *action* (not a state) occurs, contrasting with stative -에; a second sense marks a
starting point. E.g. 학교에서 공부합니다 "I study at school"; 그분이 미국에서 왔어요 "He came from
America." Minimally paired against honorific 계시다 in the Textbook: 할머니가 어디에 계세요? —
거실에 계세요. 거실에서 차를 드세요.

**-지 않다 / 안~ (long-form / short-form negation)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — verb
stem + -지 않다 (long form, volitional non-action) vs. 안 + verb (short form); the N+하다 wrinkle
(안 inserts between noun and 하다, often with a particle). E.g. 음악을 들어요? — 아니요, 안 들어요. /
음악을 듣지 않아요. 공부해요.

**으-irregular verbs** `[likely already covered elsewhere, morphology relevant]` — 으-final stems
(아프다, 쓰다, 예쁘다, 바쁘다, 크다, 기쁘다) drop 으 before a vowel-initial ending; the -아/-어
allomorph is chosen by the vowel *preceding* the dropped 으 (아프다 → 아파요, not *아프어요). E.g.
아기가 아파요? — 네, 아파요.

### Lesson 6 — 날짜와 요일 (Date and Day of the Week)

**-(으)ㄹ 거예요/겁니다 (future/intention, 1st/2nd-person sense)** `[likely already covered
elsewhere]` — plan/intention with 1st/2nd-person subjects (contrast: 3rd-person supposition sense,
Lesson 12 below). E.g. 저는 주말에 여행을 갈 거예요; 이번 주말에 무엇을 할 거예요? — 여행을 갈 거예요.

**-하고 (같이) ("together with")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — the same -하고 as Lesson
2, + 같이, after person-denoting nouns. E.g. 친구하고 같이 점심을 먹었습니다; 누구하고 노래방에 갈
거예요? — 은영 씨하고 노래방에 갈 거예요.

**-부터 ~ -까지 ("from ~ to ~")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — temporal (or spatial)
range marking, frequently paired. E.g. 9시부터 1시까지 수업을 합니다; 언제부터 언제까지 학교
수업이 있어요? — 월요일부터 금요일까지 수업이 있어요.

**-(으)면 (conditional "if/when")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — -면 after vowel-final,
-으면 after consonant-final stems. E.g. 값이 싸면 삽시다; 비가 오면 집에서 쉴 거예요. 비가 안 오면
산에 갈 거예요 (the Textbook's drill deliberately contrasts the positive/negated conditional in one
exchange).

**ㅂ-irregular verbs** `[likely already covered elsewhere, morphology relevant]` — stem-final ㅂ →
우 before a vowel-initial ending (춥다 → 추워서, 맵다 → 매워서, 어렵다 → 어려워서, 덥다, 더럽다).
Drilled by the Textbook in the -(으)면 frame: 교실이 추워요. 교실이 추우면 창문을 닫으세요.

### Lesson 7 — 길에서 (On the Street)

**-(으)러 가다/오다 (purposive "go/come in order to")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` —
-(으)러 after consonant-final, -러 after vowel-final stems, + 가다/오다. E.g. 영화를 보러 갈까요?;
옷가게에 무엇을 하러 가세요? — 옷을 사러 가요; also combined with -(으)시-: 친구를 만나러 덕수궁에 가요.

**-(으)시- (subject honorific infix) + honorific verb system** `[higher value — not pure
boilerplate]` — the Textbook presents Korean's two honorific-marking mechanisms together as one
system: (1) the regular/productive **-(으)시-** infix (보다→보시다, 가다→가시다, 읽다→읽으시다), and
(2) a closed set of **suppletive honorific verbs** with a wholly different root: 있다→계시다,
자다→주무시다, 먹다→잡수시다/드시다, 마시다→드시다 (note 드시다 covers both eat/drink, but
잡수시다 is eat-only). Minimally paired without honorification in the Textbook: 할아버지가 무엇을
하세요? — 할아버지가 신문을 보세요 (honorific) vs. 동생이 무엇을 해요? — 동생이 신문을 봐요
(plain) — same verb, isolating -(으)시-'s contribution cleanly. Worth cross-checking against other
Korean extractions' honorific-system notes, since presenting both mechanisms side-by-side as one
system is a more complete treatment than some beginner courses give at this stage.

**-(으)로 (directional "toward")** — -로 after vowel-final/ㄹ-final nouns, -으로 after other
consonant-final nouns; commonly paired with 쪽 ("side/direction") words. E.g. 오른쪽으로 가십시오;
4층으로 가세요.

**ㄹ-irregular verbs/adjectives** `[likely already covered elsewhere, morphology relevant]` — ㄹ-final
stems (살다, 알다, 팔다, 열다, 멀다, 힘들다, 울다, 만들다, 놀다) drop the ㄹ before endings starting
with ㄴ/ㅂ/ㅅ or -(으)시-. E.g. 살다 → 삽니다 (not 살ㅁ니다); 알다 → 아세요; 멀다 → 먼 (modifier
form). The Grammar & Patterns booklet's 5-row conjugation table (만들다/살다/알다/열다/팔다) against
-ㅂ/습니다 and the -는 modifier is a fuller systematic presentation than a prose-only treatment.

### Lesson 8 — 하루의 일과 (Daily Routine)

**-고 (sequential "and then")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — links two clauses in
temporal sequence; requires the same subject, clause order fixed (contrast listing -고, Lesson 13).
E.g. 아침을 먹고 학교에 갑니다; 숙제를 하고 텔레비전을 봐요.

**-시 -분 (hour/minute time-telling)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — native-Korean
numerals + 시 for the hour (한 시, 두 시... with the irregular 한/두/세/네, not 일/이/삼/사),
Sino-Korean numerals + 분 for the minute (오 분, 십 분...) — the native-vs-Sino split by hour/minute
is the one genuinely structural point, a common learner stumbling block.

**-어서/-아서 (causal "because")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — reason/cause connective;
cannot precede an imperative/suggestion clause, and -었/았-/-겠- cannot precede it directly. E.g.
배가 아파서 약을 먹었습니다; 일요일이어서 학교에 안 가요. The Textbook's reading passage layers this
against -지만 in the same paragraph as a deliberate side-by-side cause/contrast illustration.

**-지만 ("but," contrastive)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — affirms the first clause while
countering with the second; no vowel-harmony variation. E.g. 값은 비싸지만 물건이 좋아요; 어렵지만
재미있어요 (past tense marked before -지만: 재미있었지만, not after).

### Lesson 9 — 가게 (The Store)

**-에 (per-unit price marker)** — a distinct sense of -에 from the locative/temporal uses: attaches
to a counted unit for a rate/price. E.g. 사과 한 개에 얼마예요?; 사과 한 개에 이천 원. The Textbook
explicitly separates this as its own grammar point rather than folding it into general -에
instruction — a genuinely useful structural distinction (price "X에 Y" vs. destination "X에 가다")
not always made explicit elsewhere.

**몇 + [counter] ("how many/much")** — 몇 combines with a counter/classifier noun. E.g. 교실에
사람이 몇 명 있어요?; 몇 살이에요? The curated classifier inventory this lesson ties to concrete
illustrated items — 장 (flat items: CDs, tickets), 켤레 (paired items: socks, shoes), 벌 (clothing
sets), 마리 (animals/fish), 병 (bottles), 송이 (flowers), 권 (bound books), 잔 (cups), 개 (general
default) — is a useful compact subset of Korean's large, semantically-organized classifier system.

**무슨 ("what kind of")** — pre-nominal interrogative determiner asking about category/type (distinct
from 어느 "which, among a known set" and 무엇/뭐 "what," standalone). E.g. 무슨 음식을 좋아해요?;
무슨 옷을 샀어요?

**-어/아 보다 ("try doing")** — vowel-harmony -아/어 + 보다. E.g. 여기에 한 번 와 보았어요; 한 번 써
보세요. The Textbook's drill set usefully separates the imperative-suggestion sense ("try this," -아/
어 보세요) from the experiential-perfect sense ("have you ever done this," -아/어 봤어요), realized
with the same form but different context.

**-겠- (future/intention, 1st-person sense)** `[partially likely redundant — the register-specific
usage below may be narrower than prior coverage]` — marks speaker will/intention (contrast 3rd-person
supposition sense, Lesson 13 below). E.g. 내일 다시 오겠습니다; in a price-haggling context: 열 개
사면 천 원 깎아 주겠어요 "If you buy ten, I'll take off 1,000 won."

### Lesson 10 — 전화 (Phone Calls)

**-지요? (confirmation tag question)** `[register-specific usage worth keeping even though the bare
form is likely covered elsewhere]` — appends to a statement seeking listener agreement/confirmation.
The Textbook presents this as THE conventional Korean phone-call opener: "거기 김 선생님
댁이지요?" ("That's Mr. Kim's residence, right?"); also general use, 날씨가 덥지요?

**-(으)니까 (reason/temporal-precondition, "since/because")** — stronger subjective coloring than
-어서/-아서; critically, the following clause *can* be an imperative/suggestion (where -어서/-아서
cannot): 지금 안 계시니까 이따가 전화하세요 "Since he isn't here, please call again later" — this
-어서 vs. -니까 imperative-compatibility distinction is a genuinely useful structural point not
always spelled out explicitly in elementary materials.

**-어/아 주다 (benefactive "do for")** `[likely already covered elsewhere, but register-specific
examples are distinctive]` — vowel-harmony -아/어 + 주다. The Textbook's own worked examples are
telephone-register (저를 도와 주시겠어요?; 한솔 씨 좀 바꿔 주세요 "please put Hansol on the phone";
기다려 주세요) and food-delivery register (자장면 하나하고 볶음밥 하나 배달해 주세요; 빨리 보내
주세요) — both genuinely distinctive register-specific example sets.

**-요? (counter-question particle)** — attaches directly to a bare noun fragment (not a full
predicate) to echo back and request confirmation/repetition. E.g. 숙제를 주세요. — 숙제요?; 김성수
씨요?; drilled with 어디요? 누구요? 누구세요? as conversational-repair vocabulary specific to this
lesson's communicative function.

### Lesson 11 — 약속 (Appointment)

**-(으)ㄴ데/는데 (background/contrastive connective)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — sets
up background or contrast; action verbs take -는데, description-verb stems take -(으)ㄴ데. E.g.
겨울인데 따뜻합니다; 오전은 시간이 있는데 오후는 시간이 없어요. The Textbook's two-box
presentation (same ending, two labeled functions side by side — "explain a situation" vs.
"contrast") is worth noting explicitly.

**-(으)시겠어요? (polite suggestion/offer question)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` —
honorific -(으)시- + -겠- + -어요?, asking the listener's opinion on a proposed joint action. E.g.
점심을 드시겠어요?; 같이 영화를 보시겠어요?

**-(으)ㄹ 수 있다/없다 (ability/possibility)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — presented
across two senses: personal ability (수영할 수 있어요?) and situational possibility (화요일에 만날
수 있어요? — 수업이 있어서 만날 수 없어요), treated by the Textbook as one form serving both
functions.

**-어서/-아서 (sequential sense, distinct from the causal sense in Lesson 8)** `[likely already
covered elsewhere]` — same morpheme, marks that the first action precedes and relates to the
second, same subject in both clauses. E.g. 학교에 가서 공부합니다. A Textbook drill pairs this with
posture verbs (앉아서/서서/누워서) to practice the form against irregular stems.

### Lesson 12 — 교통 (Transportation)

**-(으)려고 하다 (intention "plan/intend to")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — connective
-(으)려고 + 하다. E.g. 대사관에 가려고 합니다; 인사동에 가려고 해요. Paired throughout the lesson
with transportation vocabulary (destination-vehicle pairs: 동대문시장-택시, 제주도-비행기).

**-(으)ㄹ 거예요/거다 (3rd-person presumption sense, distinct from Lesson 6's 1st/2nd-person
future)** — with a 3rd-person subject, expresses the speaker's presumption/supposition rather than
plan. E.g. 아마 내일 비가 올 거예요 "It will [probably] rain"; 아마 오십 분쯤 걸릴 거예요.

**-(으)려면 (conditional-purpose "if you want to")** — contraction of -(으)려고 하면, restricted to
intentional/volitional predicates (distinct from plain -(으)면 "if"). E.g. 동대문 시장에 가려면
어떻게 가요?; 광화문에 가려면 어떻게 가요?

**-(으)로 (instrumental/means, distinct sense from Lesson 7's directional -(으)로)** — marks means/
method/tool. E.g. 버스로 왔습니다; 한국말로 말하십시오; 지하철 5호선으로 가세요 — paired constantly
with -(으)려면 in this lesson's direction-giving drills.

**-어야/아야 하다 (obligation "must/have to")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — E.g. 학교에
9시까지 와야 합니다. Drilled heavily against the Seoul subway network — six parallel obligation
questions (line/place/exit/crosswalk/time) functioning as a de facto "giving directions" script: 지하철을
타야 해요; 1번 출구로 나가야 해요.

**르-irregular verbs** `[morphology relevant, likely redundant]` — 르-final stems drop 으 and insert
ㄹ onto the preceding syllable before a vowel-initial ending: 다르다 → 달라요. The Grammar & Patterns
booklet's table conjugates 다르다/부르다/빠르다/모르다 across -어/아요, -었/았습니다, -아/아서; the
Textbook's own drill adds 고르다 (choose), 자르다 (cut), and the two homophonous-root 부르다 forms
([배가] 부르다 "full" vs. [노래를] 부르다 "sing a song") — a genuine lexical-semantic split on one
shared conjugation class, not one verb with two senses.

### Lesson 13 — 친구 (Friend)

**-고 (listing "and," distinct from Lesson 8's sequential -고)** `[likely already covered
elsewhere]` — connects facts on an equal footing; clause order freely swappable (unlike sequential
-고). E.g. 제 친구는 키가 크고 날씬해요; 제니 씨는 예쁘고 똑똑해요.

**-만 ("only")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — exclusive particle, attaches to almost any
element. E.g. 선생님만 집에 갔습니다; 제니 씨만 안경을 썼어요 (drilled against a four-person
comparison table: gender/nationality/occupation/hobby).

**-겠- (3rd-person presumption sense, distinct from Lesson 9's 1st-person intention sense)** —
marks the speaker's supposition/conjecture about a 3rd party. E.g. 내일은 흐리겠다; 상을 타서
기쁘시겠어요; 한국 친구가 없어서 심심하겠어요 (an empathetic, 2nd-person-directed inference, never
about the speaker's own stated plans in this lesson's drills).

**-(으)면 좋겠어요 ("I hope ~ / it would be nice if ~")** — conditional + "good," expressing a wish.
E.g. 저도 친구가 많으면 좋겠어요. Used in the Lesson 13 reading passage's closing line as a
diplomatic way to soften criticism of a friend's broken promises while wishing for improvement — a
genuine pragmatic/discourse pattern, not pure grammar drilling.

**-지 못하다 / 못- (inability negation, long and short forms)** — both presented as equivalent in one
grammar box. E.g. 열쇠가 없어서 들어가지 못하겠습니다; 시간이 없어서 사지 못했어요. Distinguished
from plain 안 negation by specifically framing *inability* (obstacle-caused) rather than *choice* —
every Textbook drill example pairs an obstacle (전화번호를 모르다, 바쁘다, 시험이 있다) with the
resulting inability.

**-에게(한테) (dative "to a person/animal")** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — -한테 is the
common spoken-register variant. E.g. 친구한테 편지를 자주 씁니까?; 친구한테 생일 축하카드를 줄
거예요.

### Lesson 14 — 고향 (Hometown)

**-ㄴ/-은 (adjective-stem noun modifier)** `[likely already covered elsewhere]` — -ㄴ after
vowel-final, -은 after consonant-final adjective stems. E.g. 예쁜 꽃이 책상 위에 있습니다; 넓은
바다가 유명해요. Drilled against six pairs crossing the ㅂ-irregular class (귀엽다→귀여운,
아름답다→아름다운).

**어떤 ("which/what kind of")** — pre-nominal interrogative on quality/type. E.g. 어떤 옷을
좋아해요?; 고향은 어떤 곳이에요? — answered with a checklist-style trait list, each paired with its
-지 않다 negation (조용하다/조용하지 않다, etc.), a clean mini-corpus of place-description antonym
pairs.

**ㅎ-irregular verbs/adjectives** `[morphology relevant, likely redundant]` — ㅎ-final description-verb
stems (어떻다, 그렇다, 까맣다, 노랗다, 빨갛다, 하얗다) drop the ㅎ before a vowel-initial ending; with
-어/아 specifically, the ㅎ drops and the vowel further contracts to -애-. The Grammar & Patterns
booklet tabulates all five across -(으)ㄴ, -(으)ㄹ까요?, -어/아요: 하늘이 파래요 "The sky is blue"
(파랗다 → 파래요).

**-한테서 / -에게서 ("from" a person)** `[likely already covered elsewhere, but Hanyang's
substitution drills add distinctive worked examples]` — marks the human source of something
received, paired with 받다/듣다/배우다. E.g. 어머니한테서 편지가 왔습니다; 누구한테서 한국말을
배워요? — 김 선생님한테서 배워요. The Textbook's own cross-reference notes confirm this pattern also
appears in the Grammar & Patterns booklet (E20) and Writing Practice (H39/I59).

**-(으)ㄴ/는/(으)ㄹ (verb-stem noun-modifier, tense-differentiated)** `[likely already covered
elsewhere — but the paired 3-way presentation is a cleanly systematic reference table]` — a 3-way
system: -는 present/ongoing (먹는 음식 "food [one] eats"), -(으)ㄴ past/completed (언제 찍은
사진이에요? "when was this photo taken?"), -(으)ㄹ future (고향에 갈 사람은 누구예요? "who is the
person who will go to their hometown?"). The Grammar & Patterns booklet's compact 3x2 grid
(Present/Past/Future x Form/Examples) and the Textbook's own three-drill-set spread both present
this as one coherent paradigm rather than three separate lessons.

### Lesson 15 — 하숙집 (Boarding House)

**-(는)군요 (exclamatory "oh, it's ~!")** — adjective stems take -군요, verb stems take -는군요
(present) or -군요 (after a past marker). E.g. 방이 깨끗하군요; 그가 학교에 가는군요. Shown combined
productively with -(으)면 좋겠어요 elsewhere in the same lesson ("아주 좋은 집에서 사는군요"),
suggesting -군요 functions as a flexible, register-neutral exclamatory.

**-(으)ㄹ게요 (1st-person intention/promise)** — restricted to 1st-person subjects and verbs; the
Textbook explicitly distinguishes two pragmatic functions of the identical form: (1) volunteering to
help ("저는 방 청소를 할게요"), and (2) promising to change behavior after correction ("책상이 너무
더럽군요. — 다음부터 청소를 잘 할게요," chained directly onto a -군요 exclamation — a correction
followed by a promise). Note: the Grammar & Patterns booklet's own Korean-language gloss at one
point literally prints "-르게요" where context and all examples confirm the intended vowel-final
allomorph is "-ㄹ게요" — read as a probable source typesetting slip, not a genuine third allomorph.

**-어도/아도 되다 (permission "may/is OK to") ↔ -(으)면 안 되다 (prohibition "must not")** — 되다/
괜찮다/좋다 are explicitly interchangeable in the permission construction. E.g. 세탁기를 써도
돼요? — 네, 써도 돼요; negated: 요리해도 돼요? — 아니요, 요리하면 안 돼요 (built on -(으)면 + 안
되다). The Textbook drills both constructions against the *same* six-item vocabulary list (담배를
피우다, 늦게 들어가다, 요리하다, 친구를 초대하다, 음악을 크게 듣다, 컴퓨터를 쓰다), so learners
practice the permitted and forbidden framing of identical actions back-to-back.

**-어/아 드릴까요? (honorific benefactive question "shall I ~ for you?") + -어/아 드리다
(honorific "high form" of -어/아 주다)** `[common early-intermediate material, likely covered
elsewhere — kept for the explicit honorific-suppletion framing]` — when the action's beneficiary
outranks the speaker, 주다 is replaced by 드리다. The Textbook's own grammar box states this
directly: "high form of '-어/아 주다'." E.g. 식사 시간을 가르쳐 주세요. — 네, 가르쳐 드릴게요; 한국

Korean's addressee-honorific verb suppletion (주다→드리다) worth flagging alongside the six-level
honorific system already on file from Korean's Phase 1 extraction.
