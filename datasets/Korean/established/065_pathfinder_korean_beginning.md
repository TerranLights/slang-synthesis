# Korean — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Pathfinder in Korean Beginning Student Book (Lessons 1-15, complete)

**Source:** Ewha Language Center (Ewha Womans University), *Pathfinder in Korean Beginning Student Book*
(말이 트이는 한국어, 1999, 10th printing 2009), all 159 PDF pages (= printed pages 1-159, confirmed 1:1,
no offset, no two-page spreads, no out-of-sequence binding anywhere in the book).

**Vision-reading note:** This PDF has no usable text layer at all (`pdftotext` probe returns ~0-3 bytes
per page) — the entire book was vision-read from 150dpi page renders, dispatched in 5 page-range passes
(pp.1-35, 36-70, 71-105, 106-140, 141-159) that are merged into this single file. **No handwritten
marginalia (pencil/pen notes, filled-in answers) was found anywhere in the book** — every pass confirmed
a clean, unused/lightly-used copy. Two genuine printed artifacts were correctly identified as book design
rather than reader annotation and extracted normally: a stylized handwriting-style typeface simulating an
addressed envelope (p.14), and the publisher's own convention of printing comprehension-question answer
keys upside-down at the foot of several pages (pp.44, 55, 145, 154) so students don't see them
accidentally. Vision Reading Confidence is marked `verified` for items read from a dedicated boxed
vocabulary list (often cross-checked against the book's own back-of-book index, see Lesson 15 section)
and `plausible_unverified` for items read once from smaller-font dialogue/running-text context.

**Non-redundant-supplement note (grammar only — does not apply to vocabulary):** This project already has
extensive basic-to-intermediate Korean grammar coverage from many prior beginner courses
(`established/001-002, 014, 020-025, 035-038, 042-043, 045, 056-059`, among others). Throughout this book,
elementary topics with no new angle (basic particles, present/past/future tense, basic negation, basic
imperatives/propositives, basic counters) are explicitly skipped as full grammar subsections — each
lesson's section below states what was skipped and why. Every distinct vocabulary item is still captured
regardless of grammar-redundancy status, per the extraction spec's vocabulary coverage rule.

**Companion Workbook — assessed, not extracted (see final section):** *Pathfinder in Korean Beginning
Workbook* (179pp, same authors/year) was separately assessed against this Student Book and confirmed to
be **pure drill material with no new vocabulary or grammar** — the same outcome as the Hanyang Korean 1
Writing Practice booklet precedent elsewhere in this corpus. No extraction file was produced for it; see
the "Companion Workbook" section at the end of this file for the full assessment.

**Morpheme breakdown.**

> **스무 살** = 스무 (bound allomorph of 스물 "twenty," used only before a counter) + 살 (counter: years of
> age) — a small closed class of native-Korean numerals (20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90) take a shortened
> bound form before the age-counter 살; only 스물→스무 is confirmed clearly within pp.1-35.

> **안녕히 가십시오** = 안녕히 (adverb, "in peace/safely") + 가(다) (verb stem "to go") + -십시오 (formal
> polite imperative ending) — structurally distinct from 안녕히 계세요, which swaps the verb stem to
> 계시(다) ("to stay/be," honorific) + -세요, reflecting whether the addressee is leaving or staying.

> **연세가 어떻게 되셨습니까?** = 연세 (honorific noun "age") + 가 (subject particle) + 어떻게 (adverb "how")
> + 되(다) (verb stem "to become") + -시- (honorific infix) + -었- (past tense) + -습니까 (formal polite
> question ending) — substitutes an honorific noun (연세) for the plain 나이/살 frame *and* layers the
> honorific infix -시- on the verb, i.e. double honorification (lexical + verbal).

### Grammar points

**Honorific lexical-substitution set for referring to people (p.33).** Beyond the familiar -(으)시- verbal
honorific infix (already well documented elsewhere in this corpus), this lesson presents a parallel-column
table of honorific *noun* substitutions that swap out the plain-register word entirely: 아버지→아버님,
어머니→어머님, 이 사람→이분, 그 사람→그분, 저 사람→저분, 몇 살입니까?→연세가 어떻게 되셨습니까?,
누구입니까?→누구십니까?. The genuinely new angle is the explicit side-by-side plain/honorific pairing
presented as a discrete memorizable system, and that the age-question pair stacks a lexical substitution
(나이→연세) on top of the verbal honorific (-시-) — double honorification within a single phrase.

**우리 as a default collective/group possessive, not just "we/our" (p.30 cultural note).** The book states
Koreans say 우리 아버지 ("our father") rather than 내 아버지 ("my father") even when speaking of one's own
individual father, extending the same pattern to 우리 나라/선생님/학교 even where an English speaker would
mark singular ("my teacher"). 우리 functions as a default collective-affiliation marker for family members
and group-affiliated institutions, regardless of whether the referent is singular — distinct from a simple
plural/possessive gloss.

**Kinship-term address convention: never address an elder relative by name (p.30 cultural note).** A
younger family member addresses an older sibling by the relational term itself (형, 누나, 오빠, 언니) rather
than by name — the kinship term doubles as a second-person vocative for elders within the family. This
explains the fixed four-way, speaker-gender-conditioned 오빠/언니 vs. 형/누나 set, with no single-word
English equivalent — a plausible site for later register/address-term slang innovation.

**Graduated three-tier politeness register ladders shown in parallel (pp.15, 23).** Two "단어와 표현" boxes
present the *same* speech-act phrased at three politeness levels side by side: "how much is it?" as
얼마입니까?(formal)/얼마예요?(polite)/얼마죠?(casual-polite); "welcome" as 어서 오십시오/어서 오세요/어서 와
(plain/panmal); "here it is" as 여기 있습니다/여기 있어요/여기 있어; "it's all done" as 다 됐습니다/다
됐어요/다 됐어. The genuinely new content is the explicit three-way contrast including the plain/panmal
register (와, 있어, 됐어) presented as a legitimate labeled register tier rather than omitted as "impolite."

**Graduated politeness formulas for "excuse me" (pp.20-21).** 저, 실례합니다 / 저, 실례지만~ / 저, ~
escalate in brevity/directness from the full phrase down to a bare hesitation particle 저 followed
directly by the request — i.e. 저 alone can function as a minimal, context-dependent "excuse me" opener
once the fuller 실례합니다 framing is established (ellipsis of the politeness-marking content while
retaining the attention-getting particle).

**Subtractive clock-time construction with 전 (p.10).** Alongside the standard additive "hour + minutes"
construction, this lesson shows the subtractive alternative "[next hour] [N minutes] 전" ("N minutes
before [next hour]"), e.g. 아홉 시 십오 분 전 = 8:45. Presented as a fully interchangeable alternate reading
for the same clock position (여덟 시 사십오 분 = 아홉 시 십오 분 전), not merely a different vocabulary item.

**Skipped as redundant:** -(스)ㅂ니다 formal present (p.11, pervasive); -(으)세요 polite imperative (p.3,
pp.19-23); dual Sino-Korean/native-Korean numeral systems and basic counters 개/명/장 (explicitly named as
skippable in the extraction spec; the number-word vocabulary itself is still captured above); -의
possessive particle (p.26, only the irregular 나의→내/저의→제 contraction kept); 은/는, 이/가, -에서/-에,
-아요/어요 in entirely ordinary usage throughout.

**Morpheme breakdowns.**

> **걸어서** = 걷- (walk, ㄷ-irregular stem) + -어서 (connective "and so") — surfaces as 걸어서, not
> `걷어서`, because the stem-final ㄷ becomes ㄹ before a vowel-initial ending (same class as 듣다→들어요).

> **인천행** = 인천 (Incheon) + -행 (bound suffix "bound for/departing to") — a productive
> destination-marking suffix, not limited to this one place name.

> **부모님께** = 부모 (parents) + -님 (honorific nominal suffix) + -께 (honorific dative particle, the
> elevated counterpart of 에게/한테) — three honorific-register morphemes stacking in one surface word;
> contrast with plain 친구에게/친구한테 elsewhere in this lesson range.

### Grammar points

**전에/후에 — -기 전에 vs. -(으)ㄴ 후에 ("before" vs. "after") (p.47).** Korean marks "before doing X" and
"after doing X" with two different, asymmetric nominalization strategies rather than a symmetric pair.
"Before" always uses -기 전에 (nominalizer -기, tense-neutral since the preceding event hasn't happened
yet); "after" always uses -(으)ㄴ 후에 (the completed-aspect modifier -(으)ㄴ). Example: 식사 전에 샤워를
합니다 vs. 식사 후에 출근합니다 — 전에/후에 attach directly to a bare noun when the preceding element is
nominal, but take the verbal forms -기 전에/-(으)ㄴ 후에 when attaching to a full clause.

**Consolidated irregular-verb paradigm table (p.40, "동사 활용").** A single table juxtaposes the -(스)ㅂ니다
and -어/아요 forms of ten verbs chosen to showcase different irregularity patterns side by side: 자다→자요
(vowel-stem contraction), 살다→삽니다 (ㄹ-final stem drops before -ㅂ니다 but not before -아요), 오다→와요 and
주다→줘요 (vowel-glide contractions), 쓰다→써요 (으-stem drop), 듣다→들어요 (ㄷ-irregular), 모르다→몰라요
(르-irregular), 이다→예요/이에요 (copula allomorphy). Each class individually is near-certainly already
documented elsewhere; the value here is the single consolidated cross-reference table.

**약 ...쯤 — doubled approximation marking (p.63).** The example ("약 ___쯤 걸립니다") marks approximation
**twice** in one phrase: 약 (pre-posed, Sino-Korean 約) and -쯤 (post-posed suffix). Both are individually
sufficient for "approximately" alone, but the dialogue specifically pairs them — worth noting as a fixed
collocation pattern for approximate durations in spoken Korean.

**Three register ladders for formulaic spoken exchanges (pp.64-65).** "Please wait a moment": 잠시만
기다리십시오 (most formal) → 잠깐만 기다리세요 (polite) → 잠시만요 (clipped) → 잠깐만 (bare, most casual).
"May I have your name?": 성함이 어떻게 되십니까? (honorific noun + honorific verb) → 성함을 말씀해
주십시오 → 성함은요? → 이름은요? (plain noun, most casual) — the contrast between 성함 (elevates the
addressee) and plain 이름 is the key lexical-honorific distinction, independent of verb-ending register.
Send-off expressions: 안녕히 다녀오십시오/즐거운 여행 되십시오 (formal) → 잘 다녀오세요/즐거운 시간 보내세요
(polite) → 잘 갔다 와/재미있게 놀다 와 (반말/casual imperative) — the only one of the three ladders that
explicitly reaches into 반말, a useful anchor for where the politeness scale bottoms out in this book.

**-에게 / -한테 / -께 — three-way dative-particle register system (p.69, with p.42/45 context).** Korean's
"to (a person)" dative has three register-distinguished forms: -에게 (neutral/written), -한테
(colloquial/spoken counterpart, referentially identical), -께 (honorific, used when the recipient outranks
the speaker socially) — e.g. 부모님께 "to [one's] parents" vs. plain 친구에게/친구한테 "to a friend." The
three-way plain-written/plain-spoken/honorific system recurring on the same particle is the point worth
flagging, beyond "에게 means to."

**Cultural/historical notes (쉼터 sidebars).** Hangeul's creation (p.51): before Hangeul, Korea used 한자
(Chinese characters), too difficult for ordinary people; King Sejong (세종대왕, r. within Joseon, personal
dates given as 1397-1450) had officials devise a native script despite opposition, completed in 1443 as
훈민정음 with an original inventory of 17 consonants/11 vowels — four letters (ㅇ "old eung," ㅸ, ㅿ, ㆍ
arae-a) have since dropped from the modern 19-consonant/21-vowel inventory the book lists. 가마 palanquin
custom (p.62): before modern transportation, Koreans used the 가마, a covered sedan chair with a closable
window, carried by 2-4 men; the book notes a gender/class pattern — gama use was typically associated with
양반집 여자 (noble-household women) going out, and brides specifically rode a gama to the groom's house on
their wedding day (시집을 가다, lit. "go to [husband's] house"); high-status yangban men also rode gama
occasionally, but theirs were specifically roofless — a visible material distinction marking gendered
expectations about being seen in public.

-(으)로/-어서 means-of-transport particle and connective (p.56, except 걸어서's irregular surface form,

**Morpheme breakdown (agglutinative/compositional forms).**

> **연락 드리겠습니다** = 연락 ("contact") + 드리- (humble-honorific "give to a superior," substitutes for
> 주다) + -겠- (future/intention) + -습니다 — p.85. A humble-honorific benefactive verb stacks with
> ordinary tense/mood/formality suffixes exactly like a plain verb.

> **전자 수첩** = 전자 ("electronic") + 수첩 ("small notebook") — p.87, Sino-Korean compound.

> **손세탁을 하다** = 손 ("hand") + 세탁 ("laundering") + -을 + 하다 — p.89. 손세탁 is lexicalized as a unit
> on wash-care labels, distinct from a free combination of 손 + 세탁.

> **횡단보도** = 횡단 ("crossing") + 보도 ("walkway") — p.91, fully Sino-Korean compound.

> **우회전하다/좌회전하다** = 우/좌 ("right"/"left") + 회전 ("rotation") + 하다 — p.92, both built on the
> same -회전하다 template, contrasting with the native-Korean paraphrases 오른쪽으로 가다/왼쪽으로 가다.

> **식기 세척기** = 식기 ("tableware") + 세척기 (세척 "washing" + -기 "device" nominalizer) — p.98. The -기
> "device" suffix is productive across this vocabulary set (녹음-기, 청소-기, 건조-기), except for loanwords
> (오디오, 비디오, 컴퓨터, 에어컨, 팩시밀리) which keep their borrowed forms intact.

> **전자 레인지** = 전자 ("electronic") + 레인지 (loanword "range") — p.98, a hybrid Sino-Korean +
> English-loanword compound for "microwave oven."

### Grammar points

**Honorific system (condensed; likely redundant with prior established coverage) — p.71.** The source
presents a full four-part honorific paradigm: (1) verb-stem + -(으)시- for regular verbs (가다→가시다,
읽다→읽으시다, 듣다→들으시다, 만들다→만드시다); (2) suppletive honorific verbs replacing the plain verb
outright (있다→있으시다/계시다 — two options, 계시다 for physical presence, 있으시다 for possession-type
existentials; 자다→주무시다; 먹다→드시다/잡수시다, two options; 죽다→돌아가시다); (3) honorific nouns
(밥→진지, 생일→생신, 나이→연세, 집→댁, 말→말씀); (4) honorific particles (는/은→께서는, 이/가→께서,
에게→께, 에게서→께, i.e. 에게 and 에게서 collapse to the same honorific form). Treated as condensed per the
non-redundant-supplement rule; the items most likely missing from a terser prior treatment are the
two-way suppletive doublets (있으시다 vs. 계시다; 드시다 vs. 잡수시다) and the 에게/에게서→께 merger.

**-(으)ㄹ 수 있다/없다 (ability/possibility) — pp.79-87.** "Can/cannot (do)." The source threads this
through an entire lesson (제9과): drilled first with sports/instrument ability, then combined with the
connectives 그리고/그렇지만/그래서 to build reason-giving chains ("돈이 없습니다. 그리고 시간도 없습니다.
그래서 친구를 만날 수 없습니다"), then recombined with -도 for listing abilities, finally tested in a job
interview register ("외국어를 할 수 있습니까?"). One usage nuance: a split-ability answer pattern, "읽을
수는 있지만 말은 잘 못 합니다" ("I can read it but can't really speak it") — the topic particle -는/-은
attached after 수 marks a contrastive sub-ability.

**Sentence-initial connectors: 그리고/그렇지만/그래서 — pp.80-82, 86.** Distinct from clause-final
connective endings (-고/-아서/-지만, already covered elsewhere): these are independent sentence-initial
adverbs linking two separate sentences. 그리고 = "and" (additive), 그렇지만 = "but" (adversative), 그래서 =
"so" (consequential). Drilled as a discourse-level toolkit for reason-giving narratives — the
desire→obstacle→consequence three-connector chain is a reusable discourse template, not just three
isolated vocabulary items.

**-지 마십시오 (prohibitive, "please do not...") — pp.88-89.** Formal negative imperative. Introduced via
real-world prohibition signage then drilled against a word bank, with the model answer "담배를 피우지
마십시오." Explicitly paired with -(으)십시오 as a permission/prohibition minimal pair in one combined
grammar box.

**-(으)면 안 되다 ("must not / is not allowed to") — pp.92-93.** A second, semantically distinct
prohibition strategy, built on conditional -(으)면 + 안 되다 rather than a dedicated prohibitive ending.
Modeled on a traffic-police scenario ("여기에 주차하면 안 됩니다") then drilled against a museum/library
scenario word bank. Reads as describing a rule/state-of-affairs, while -지 마십시오 is a direct command —
the book's choice of scenario (impersonal signage vs. a person directly addressing someone) tracks this
distinction consistently.

**-번째 (ordinal-number counter) — p.90.** Native-Korean ordinals attach -번째 (번 "number/time" + 째
ordinal suffix) to the native cardinal stem: 첫 번째...열두 번째, ending 마지막. Two irregularities: "first"
uses the suppletive root 첫- (not ×하나 번째), and from "eleventh" on, compound-cardinal stems truncate
(열하나→열한, 열둘→열두) before -번째, mirroring the shortening native cardinals undergo before any native
counter. Used productively both as sequence-ordinal and spatial-position-ordinal (왼쪽에서 두 번째에
있습니다; 오른쪽 세 번째 집입니다).

**-지요? (confirmation-seeking tag question) — p.103.** Attaches to a verb/adjective stem (or noun +
-이지요?) to ask confirmation of something the speaker already believes — close to an English tag
question. Modeled first inferentially ("중국 사람이지요?") then drilled against a Jeju Island fact bank. The
sample answer ("네, 섬이에요. 그래서 배나 비행기로 가야 돼요") shows a -지요? question typically gets a plain
네/아니요 followed by elaboration, not a tag-matching echo.

**-어/아 주시겠습니까? vs. -어/아 주세요 (two-register polite-request pair) — pp.104-105.** Both attach via
the -어/아 주다 benefactive, differing in formality: -어/아 주시겠습니까? (more deferential, -시- + -겠- +
-습니까?, used asking a stranger/delivery worker, e.g. "문 좀 열어 주시겠습니까?") vs. -어/아 주세요 (plainer,
used with friends, e.g. "연필 좀 빌려 주세요"). Placed in back-to-back grammar boxes with near-identical
example sentences (both use 사진을 찍다) specifically to let the learner contrast the two register levels
for the same request-content.

**Pronoun/demonstrative contractions — pp.75-76.** 나는→난, 저는→전, 우리는→우린 (topic-marked first-person
pronoun contracts to a single syllable); 이것은→이건, 이것이→이게, 이것을→이걸 (이것 fuses with each
particle). Both patterns are productive beyond these examples (그것/저것 undergo the same contraction,
though not spelled out here) and represent a systematic colloquial phonological reduction frequent enough
in casual speech to function as the default rather than a marked variant.

**Skipped as redundant:** -겠- (future/intention/epistemic-guess, seen in 모르겠어요, 알겠어요, 연락
드리겠습니다, 걸겠습니다) — no new angle found beyond ordinary usage.

**Morpheme breakdowns.**

> **자리에 안 계시다** = 자리 (seat/place) + -에 (locative) + 안 (negator) + 계시다 (honorific "to be,"
> suppletive for 있다) — the honorific negative-existential used of a superior away from their desk;
> ordinary-register equivalent would be 자리에 없다.

> **들어오다** = 들다 (to enter) + 오다 (to come) — serial-verb compound, "to come in/return" (deictically
> anchored to the speaker).

> **하늘색** = 하늘 (sky) + 색 (color) — transparent noun-noun compound, the most common productive
> pattern for color-term coining in this chart (cf. 밤색 "chestnut-color").

> **운동화** = 운동 (exercise/sport) + 화 (shoes, Sino-Korean 靴) — a Sino-Korean compound meaning "sport
> shoes," contrasting with the native/loanword items elsewhere in the shoe chart (구두, 샌들, 부츠).

> **단발머리** = 단발 (short hair, Sino-Korean 短髮) + 머리 (hair, native) — a hybrid construction where a
> whole Sino-Korean compound is re-suffixed with the native word for "hair," effectively restating the
> head noun.

> **가르쳐 드리다** = 가르치다 (teach) + -어 (connective) + 드리다 (humble benefactive auxiliary) — the
> humble-benefactive construction, structurally parallel to -어 주다 but substituting the humble auxiliary
> when the beneficiary outranks the speaker.

> **더위(를) 먹다** = 더위 (heat, from 덥다 + nominalizing -이 with vowel contraction) + 먹다 (eat) —
> idiomatic "object-incorporation" collocation; 먹다 here means "to suffer/be afflicted by," paralleling
> other Korean "eat + abstract noun" idioms (e.g. 겁을 먹다 "to become scared," lit. "to eat fear").

### Grammar points

**Numeral-homophone wordplay as a lexical phenomenon (pp.108, 112) — new finding, slang-adjacent
mechanism.** The 쉼터 sidebar (p.108) and the reading "재미있는 전화 번호" (Fun Phone Numbers, p.112)
document a genuinely productive Korean lexical-play mechanism directly relevant to this project's
slang-mechanics interest: **Korean phone numbers are commonly chosen and advertised because their
digit-by-digit readings sound like a real word or phrase relevant to the business.** Worked examples
(paraphrased): **2424** → 이사이사 "move, move" (moving companies); **5252** → 오이오이 "cucumber,
cucumber" (produce shops, since 오이 = cucumber); **8282** → approximates "빨리빨리" "quickly, quickly"
(errand-running services); **2875** → approximates "이빨 치료" "tooth treatment" (dentists); **7788** →
칠칠팔팔 phonetically echoes 칙칙폭폭, the onomatopoeia for a steam train (Seoul Station); **8949** →
approximates "팔고 사고" "selling and buying" (secondhand shops); **1004** → "천 사" (1000=천, 4=사) = 천사
"angel" (a famous, widely-recognized pun, used by churches); **1009** → approximates 천국 "heaven" (also
churches). This is the same cognitive mechanism underlying numeronym/leetspeak-style slang elsewhere
(digits standing in for syllables by sound), grounded in Korean's dual native/Sino-Korean numeral-reading
system, which gives it more flexibility than a single-reading numeral system — a concrete,
textbook-documented example of phonetic/numeral wordplay productivity, distinct from (and a useful
complement to) morphological-recombination-based slang mechanisms, since this operates on numerals read
aloud.

**-을/를 위해서 (benefactive purpose) — brief note, likely redundant (p.106).** Seen in 여러분은 다른
사람을 위해서 무엇을 해 줄 때가 있지요? Marks "for N's sake," distinct from the plain purpose-of-motion
connective -(으)려고. Standard A2-level pattern; flagged only so its presence in this book is on record.

**-에서요, -에요 (elliptical/fragment answer forms) — kept, pragmatic-economy nuance (p.107).** p.107
explicitly frames this as "how do you answer without repeating the whole sentence": given 어디에서 영화를
봤어요? → 서울극장에서 봤어요, the book trains learners to compress a repeated question into a bare
fragment: 어디에서요?/무슨 영화요? This is an explicit pragmatics lesson in Korean conversational economy —
dropping everything but the new information and relevant particle, closing with polite -요 — directly
relevant background for how colloquial/slang registers compress utterances further.

**-(으)ㄴ/는 N — descriptive-verb attributive form, with its irregular-conjugation set (pp.116-118).** The
plain rule is extremely basic; worth keeping is the specific irregular set the drills surface: ㅂ-irregular
stems drop ㅂ and add -운 (뜨겁다→뜨거운, 귀엽다→귀여운, 어지럽다→어지러운); ㄹ-final stems drop ㄹ before -ㄴ
(길다→긴); 있다/없다-stem descriptive words take -는 rather than -(으)ㄴ, patterning with action verbs
(맛있다→맛있는, 멋있다→멋있는); regular stems simply add -(으)ㄴ (예쁘다→예쁜, 넓다→넓은, 뚱뚱하다→뚱뚱한,
시원하다→시원한).

**-고 있다 extended to clothing/accessory "wearing" states, with body-part-specific wear-verbs
(pp.118-119).** -고 있다 itself is basic. Genuinely worth a full write-up: Korean has no one general verb
for "to wear" — the verb is selected by *what kind of item* is being worn. This lesson's suspect/lost-child
description task forces the full set into view: **입다** (torso clothing: 옷을 입고 있습니다), **쓰다**
(head/eyes: 모자를/안경을 쓰고 있습니다), **신다** (footwear: 운동화를 신고 있습니다), **매다** (tied around
neck/waist: 넥타이를/스카프를 매다), **끼다** (fingers/hands: 장갑을 끼다), **메다** (shoulder straps: 배낭을
메다), **하다** (miscellaneous, e.g. 허리띠를 하다). A concrete, textbook-attested example of fine-grained
lexical categorization by how an item sits on the body (surrounds/slots-onto/drapes-over/misc) —
informative for how any later register/slang substitutions in this domain would need to respect the same
partitioning.

**-겠습니다 in weather-forecaster register (p.126).** -겠습니다 is common grammar generally, but this
lesson explicitly frames it as *the* register marker of Korean weather-broadcast delivery ("내일은 비가
오겠습니다. 그리고 바람도 많이 불겠습니다"), contrasted against the more neutral -(으)ㄹ 겁니다 used in
ordinary conversational answers to the same question — a grammatical form whose primary associative
register (broadcast formality) is itself socially marked.

**-(이)나, -거나 (alternative connective: noun vs. verb forms) (p.128).** -(이)나 attaches to nouns ("N or
N": 책이나 잡지를 보다); -거나 attaches to verb/adjective stems for the same meaning (친구를 만나거나 영화를
보다). Recorded as a connective-form-inventory entry (noun/verb split via two different particles rather
than one covering both word classes).

**-에서/-에게서 (source marker: place vs. person), equivalence to -한테서 (p.129).** Korean marks "from"
differently for a place (-에서: 신문에서 읽었다) vs. a person (-에게서: 어머니에게서 들었다). The book glosses
-에게서 = -한테서 (the more colloquial spoken equivalent), paralleling the general -에게/-한테 split
elsewhere. A source-marking distinction split along the animate/inanimate axis.

**그러니까 (causal/justificatory connective triggering a correction) (p.136) — brief note.** Used to
justify overriding a previous proposal: 오늘은 토요일입니다. 그래서 길이 막힐 겁니다. 그러니까 버스를 타지
맙시다. 지하철을 탑시다. Functions almost as "which is why" — drawing a conclusion that licenses rejecting
the interlocutor's first suggestion, a slightly different pragmatic shade than 그래서's plain "and so."

**바람맞다 — idiom: "to be stood up" (p.139).** 바람 ordinarily means "wind" (바람이 많이 불어요). But
바람맞다 (lit. "to be struck by wind") has drifted to a figurative social meaning: to be stood up by
someone, waiting at an agreed meeting place while the other person never shows — "as if a cold wind had
passed through an empty chest." A clean example of a weather-domain word undergoing semantic extension
into the social/relationship domain via a bodily-sensation metaphor, directly relevant to this project's
interest in how ordinary vocabulary develops idiomatic/slang-adjacent secondary senses. Colloquial
register, not neutral/formal.

**Folk weather lore (p.130) — cultural-linguistic note, not grammar.** Before mass media, people predicted
weather from animal behavior and bodily sensation: swallows (제비) flying low meant rain; frogs (개구리)
croaking heavily meant rain; elderly relatives' joint pain (허리가 아프다/무릎이 쑤시다) meant rain; morning
fog or visible stars at night meant clear weather ahead. A ready-made source of "folk prediction"
idiom/collocation material for this semantic domain.

**우산 장수와 짚신 장수 ("The Umbrella Seller and the Straw-Sandal Seller") — folk tale (p.134).** An old
woman has two sons, one selling umbrellas and one selling straw sandals; she worries on every kind of
day, since clear weather hurts one son's business and rain hurts the other's — until a neighbor points
out she could instead be glad every day. The Korean cultural touchstone for a "silver lining" reframing
idiom pattern, analogous to but distinct from the English cliché.

**백의민족 and the color-preference survey (p.119) — cultural note tied to the color vocabulary table.**
Koreans are traditionally called "백의민족" ("the white-clad people," lit. "white-clothes people"). A
cited (unattributed) survey found Koreans' favorite clothing color is still white, versus dark green for
Europeans; both groups favored white/black as colors that pair well with others, but Europeans leaned
toward dark saturated colors while Koreans leaned toward bright, light colors. The book does not cite its
survey source, so this is the textbook's own unverified claim rather than attested fact.

**Skipped as redundant, noted only:** -(으)면 plain conditional (pp.127-128); -(으)ㄹ까요?/-(으)ㅂ시다
suggestion (pp.135-136); -어/아야 하다, -지 않아도 되다 obligation (p.137); 그러면 plain consequence
connective (p.138).

### Grammar points

**Nominalizers -기 and -는 것 (pp.146-147). Skipped as a full write-up — redundant.** Both nominalizers are
ubiquitous basic-grammar content already covered extensively elsewhere. The book teaches -기 first for
simple hobby nouns and -는 것 second for the fuller "V-는 것을 좋아하다/싫어하다" frame — standard pedagogical
sequencing, not a new grammatical fact.

**Intention: -(으)려고 하다 / -(으)려고 했다 (pp.148-149).** Skipped for the core present-intention pattern
(extensively covered elsewhere). **One genuinely worth flagging:** the past-tense form -(으)려고 했다 is
taught specifically for its "intended to, but didn't end up happening" nuance. The book's own example
makes the contrast explicit: "김민호 씨는 어렸을 때 야구 선수가 되려고 했습니다. 그런데 지금은 회사에
다닙니다" ("Kim Min-ho intended to become a baseball player as a child. But now he works at a company") —
-(으)려고 했다 carries an implicature of a thwarted/abandoned plan, reinforced by a following 그런데 clause
in every drill example on p.149 (eye trouble ending a pilot dream, etc.).

**Trip-duration counter N박 M일 and sports-score reading (pp.142-143). Skipped — redundant.** Both already
documented elsewhere in this project (박 in `established/041_korean_course_snu_vol4.md`; 대 in
`established/011_intermediate_college_korean_part1.md`). These pages only supply additional worked
examples (1박2일/3박4일/4박5일; a baseball score read with Sino-Korean numerals) rather than a new
grammatical fact.

**쉼터 cultural-note sidebar, p.150.** A dated, sourced statistic on Korean leisure-time habits, cited to
동아일보 (Dong-a Ilbo), July 6, 1997: television watching 41.2%, sleep/housework 29.5%, sports/travel
14%, with an age breakdown (10s-20s skew toward drawing/calligraphy, 30s-40s toward sports/travel,
50s-60s toward sleep/housework). Genuine attested survey data with a specific date, more precise than the
whole-book "contemporary (source published 1999/2009)" era tag used elsewhere in this file.

**Reading passages (pp.144-145, 154).** "아기도 사람?" ("Is a baby not a person too?"): 이수영 travels to
Hawaii to introduce her 5-month-old baby to family who emigrated there; at check-in she's told the infant
needs its own passport and protests ("그럼 이 아기는 사람이 아닙니까?"). "귀여운 이구아나?" ("A cute
iguana?"): a high-school friend, 경미, keeps a pet iguana (up to ~1.6m long, tail two-thirds of body
length, venomless, fed mostly insects/leaves/moss) — the narrator finds it not at all cute, unlike 경미.

## Companion Workbook — assessed, not extracted

*Pathfinder in Korean Beginning Workbook* (Ewha Language Center, 1999/2009, 179pp) was assessed against
this Student Book per the project's non-redundant-supplement rule, rather than extracted.

**Method:** Unlike the Student Book, this PDF has a genuine (if messy) OCR text layer — `pdftotext`
(plain and `-layout`) across all 179 pages produced readable body text (cover/colophon pages render as
CJK/Latin mojibake, but exercise content and Korean prose came through legibly), so no vision-reading
fallback was needed.

**Verdict: pure drill — no extraction performed**, the same outcome as the Hanyang Korean 1 Writing
Practice booklet precedent elsewhere in this corpus. The workbook covers all 15 lessons, mapping 1:1 to
this Student Book's own 15-lesson structure (confirmed via recurring "문법 및 구조/Grammar Focus" and
"상황 표현/Function Focus" section headers and TOC fragments for 제4과, 제7과, 제8과, 제11과, 제12과, 제14과,
제15과). Every "Grammar Focus" section sampled opens with a one-line restatement of a grammar point the
Student Book already teaches, then moves straight into drill tables (number-reading drills, particle
fill-ins, irregular-verb conjugation paradigms, noun-formation drills). "Function Focus"/"Sample Dialogs"
sections are short model dialogues plus comprehension questions on short bio paragraphs recombining
lesson vocabulary — recap, not new teaching. **Zero hits** for 새 단어 (new-word list headers), 문화
(culture notes), or 복습 (review chapter) anywhere in the book; no appendix glossary (the tail of the book
is publisher colophon/author-bio/ISBN material). This structural signature — one-line grammar recaps
immediately followed by drills, dialogue recaps followed by comprehension questions built from known
vocabulary, and the total absence of any new-word-list or culture-note section — confirms the workbook is
pure drill/recombination material, not an independent content source. This is an expected, not a failed,
outcome per the project's non-redundant-supplement rule.
