# Danish — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Danish Course Book — "Reading Lessons" appendix (Units Two–Ten)

**Source:** *Danish Course Book* (vision-read, PDF filename as cataloged by the project's triage). 12
PDF pages, each a two-printed-page spread (confirmed by reading the printed page numbers directly),
giving ~21 printed pages of actual content. **Correction to the original dispatch brief**: this is
not part of the "Univerb Danish Basic Course" bundle as described — the booklet's own text identifies
it as a **Pimsleur Language Programs** product booklet (the generic "Dr. Paul Pimsleur and His Unique
Method" / "Principle of Anticipation" / "Graduated Interval Recall" marketing-and-methodology material
that accompanies every Pimsleur language course, not Danish-specific) **followed by** a Danish-specific
"Reading Lessons" appendix (Table of Contents, p. "11": Introduction, Unit Two through Unit Ten, with
Unit Seven marked "No Readings," plus an Acknowledgments page not captured here). The general shape
the dispatch brief described — "English-medium how-to-use booklet followed by graded Danish
reading-lesson word/phrase lists" — does hold, just for the wrong publisher/bundle name.

**Coverage note:** the generic Pimsleur-method pages (printed pp. 1–9: "Pimsleur Language Programs,"
"How to Use the Program," "Guidelines for Success," "Check Your Progress," "Dr. Paul Pimsleur and His
Unique Method," "Principle of Anticipation," "Graduated Interval Recall," "Core Vocabulary," "Organic
Learning," "A Note on Regional Language Differences") contain **zero Danish-language content** —
confirmed by reading all of them — and are correctly out of scope for this extraction; they are
boilerplate shared across every Pimsleur language product, not Danish teaching material. Every
genuinely Danish-language item in the booklet (the "Reading Lessons" appendix, printed pp. "1"–"10" of
that appendix, i.e. PDF pages 7–12) is captured below in full — this is a short, fully-covered source,
not a partial sample.

**Vision-reading guard:** this is a clean, well-printed scan with no marginalia of any kind found on
any page — no handwritten annotations to exclude.

**A structural note on the source itself:** the "Reading Lessons" are explicitly a **phonics/decoding
drill**, not a vocabulary-building exercise — the Introduction page states plainly that meaning
"resides in the sounds of the spoken language" and that the printed reading lists exist only to connect
already-orally-learned words to their written form; **no English translation is printed anywhere in the
source** (translations were originally conveyed only via the accompanying audio, which this project does
not have access to). Every Gloss below is therefore supplied from general Danish-English dictionary
knowledge, not transcribed from the source itself — flagged accordingly in Vision Reading Confidence
(`plausible_unverified` for isolated single words; `verified` only where a full example sentence in the
same list confirms the word unambiguously, e.g. numerals, or "Jeg er amerikaner" confirming
*amerikaner*). Each unit's word list is also a deliberate **phonetic minimal-pair/rhyme-family
progression** (e.g. Unit Two drills the short *-ej*/*-eg* rhyme family *sej/leg/jeg/bleg* before moving to
*-en* words *sen/sten*; Unit Four drills doubled consonants and the ø-sound; Unit Five drills æ-sounds
and -tyve/-ykke words; Unit Six drills å-sounds) — flagged here as a pedagogical pattern rather than
extracted as a grammar point, since it is a pronunciation-teaching device, not a grammatical rule.

## Grammar points

### Graded phonetic reading-lesson structure (not a grammar rule, but a structural pattern worth noting)

Each numbered Reading Lesson unit is built as a **minimal-pair/rhyme-family phonics drill**, not a
thematic vocabulary list: Unit Two chains together the short *-ej*/*-eg*/*-en* rhyme families
(*sej, leg, jeg, bleg* → *sen, sten* → *mene, let, pen, ven*); Unit Four drills doubled-consonant /
ø-vowel words (*lakken, frøken, søle, sølle, mølle, dømme*); Unit Five drills æ-vowel words
(*sæbe, glæde, væg, pære, æble, tænke, bænk, ægte*) then pivots to *-tyve/-ykke* words
(*tyve, hytte, lykke*); Unit Six drills å-vowel words (*blå, må, går, forstår, måtte, på, blåt*).
Each unit ends by shifting from isolated drill words into one or two full example sentences that
recombine the unit's own vocabulary with material from earlier units — e.g. Unit Five's drill
culminates in the real Copenhagen street name *Mikkel Bryggers Gade*, and Unit Ten's numeral drill
(1–24) resolves into a sequence of complete travel-survival sentences. This progression — phonology
first, lexicon/syntax recombination second — is the opposite emphasis from Colloquial Danish or Teach
Yourself Danish, both of which lead with dialogue/vocabulary and treat pronunciation as a secondary
practice track.

### Du vs. De, directly drilled as a minimal pair (Unit Eight)

Unlike Colloquial Danish, which introduces *du*/*De* gradually across Unit 1's grammar point and later
usage notes, this source drills the contrast directly as adjacent near-identical sentences: *"Forstår
du dansk?"* immediately followed by *"De. Forstår De dansk?"* — isolating the pronoun swap as the only
variable between the two sentences, a deliberate minimal-pair pedagogy technique.
