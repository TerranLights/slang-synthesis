# Tagalog — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Basic Tagalog for Foreigners and Non-Tagalogs, Appendices I–III (Foods, Cooking Terms, Popular Filipino Dishes)

**Source:** Paraluman S. Aspillera (revised/updated by Yolanda Canseco Hernandez), *Basic Tagalog for
Foreigners and Non-Tagalogs* (Tuttle Publishing, 2nd/revised ed., 2007), Appendices "Foods," "Cooking
Terms," and "Popular Filipino Dishes and Delicacies," pp. 189–192. Continuation of
`007`–`007h_basic_tagalog_for_foreigners.md`; see those files for this source's overall coverage note
and vision-reading method. No handwritten marginalia found in this chunk.

**Coverage note:** The "Foods" appendix (p. 189–190) is a large, dense bilingual glossary (~150+
individual food-item translations across vegetables, fish/shellfish, noodles, meats, poultry, spices/
condiments) presented as a pure word-for-word reference list with essentially no grammatical content
or illustrative example sentences — structurally closer to a dictionary glossary than to ordinary
lesson vocabulary. Per the coverage rule's intent (comprehensive-but-not-exhaustive, representative
depth for low-information-density repeated-pattern content) and the copyright-discipline rule against
bulk-reproducing a vocabulary box/glossary, this file captures a **representative sample across every
category** (roughly a quarter of the full list, chosen to span the full range of categories and any
items with notable etymological/cultural interest) rather than all ~150 entries verbatim. The "Cooking
Terms" (20 numbered verbs) and "Popular Filipino Dishes" (prose-described named dishes) sections are
fully captured, since each entry there carries real distinct content (a culinary technique or a named,
culturally-specific dish description), not a flat word-list.

## Grammar points

*(This appendix is primarily a vocabulary/glossary/culinary-description resource rather than a
grammar-point lesson; no new grammar rules are introduced in this chunk beyond what's already
documented in `007`–`007h`. A few recurring morphological patterns from earlier lessons surface again
in the Cooking Terms list — the **-in-/-hin/-an/i-** object-trigger affix choices for each cooking verb
exactly follow the per-root allomorphy rules already fully documented in Lessons 15/32/33 — and are not
re-explained here to avoid redundancy; see `007c_basic_tagalog_for_foreigners.md` and
`007e_basic_tagalog_for_foreigners.md` for those rules.)*

**Lexical-gap and loanword notes worth flagging explicitly**: the source volunteers several genuine
etymological/cultural asides across this appendix beyond the bare glossary function — the **turò-turò**
"point-point" folk-etymology for roadside eateries; **hálo-hálo**'s dish-name-as-reduplicated-verb
construction ("mix-mix"); the Luzon-vs-Visayas/Mindanao regional variation in how **lechón** is served;
and **litsón**'s proposed Spanish *leche* etymology. These are all genuine instances of the kind of
culturally-grounded word-formation and regional-variation evidence this project's later mechanics-
analysis phase will want to draw on, distinct from the purely pedagogical grammar content of the main
44 lessons.
