**Morpheme breakdown.**

> **pangalawa** = pang- (ordinal-forming prefix) + dalawa ("two") → first consonant of the base
> dropped on prefixation (da- elided). **pangatlo** = pang- + tatlo ("three") → same elision
> pattern (ta- elided). The book states the general rule explicitly (p.183): `pang-` is the
> ordinal-forming prefix, assimilating to `pam-` before a labial-initial base (b, p) and to `pan-`
> before a base beginning with d, t, s, l, or r (nasal place-assimilation), with the 2nd/3rd
> ordinals additionally eliding the base's own initial syllable on prefixation — a genuinely
> morphophonological rule stated by the source itself, not inferred.

## Grammar points

### Imperative vs. completed-aspect pairing: mag- vs. nag-
p.173 gives a systematic ten-item table pairing each `mag-` household-chore verb's **basic/command
form** (`magpakain`, `magpunas`...) against its **completed-aspect form** (`nagpakain`,
`nagpunas`...) — the clearest single-page illustration in the course of the mag-/nag- actor-trigger
aspect alternation applied consistently across an entire semantic set (domestic chores) rather than
piecemeal across scattered examples.

### Existential-negation constructions for reporting problems ("X ran out/broke/is missing")
Several emergency-report sentences share a construction type not previously systematized in this
course: `nawalan ng X` ("ran out of X," lit. "lost-having X") applied to both electricity
(`nawalan kami ng kuriyente`) and water (`nawalan kami ng tubig`), alongside `nasira` ("broken"),
`nasusunog` ("on fire"), `nasagasaan` ("run over"), and `nanakawan` ("robbed") — all `na-`-initial
stative/completed forms describing an involuntary event happening *to* the topic rather than an
action performed *by* it. Worth flagging for Phase 3 mechanics analysis: this `na-` family appears
to be this language's dedicated grammatical slot for "misfortune happened to me" reporting,
distinct from the ordinary actor-trigger verb system used for intentional action throughout the
rest of the course.

### Ordinal numeral formation
`pang-` + cardinal number forms ordinals, with nasal place-assimilation (`pang-` → `pam-` before
labials, → `pan-` before coronals) and elision of the cardinal's own initial syllable for "second"
and "third" specifically (`pangalawa`, `pangatlo`) — an idiosyncratic extra irregularity on top of
the regular assimilation rule, flagged explicitly by the source. (p.183)
