# Tagalog — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Diccionario Ingles-Español-Tagalog, Part III (sampled) — Letters O–W

**Source:** Sofronio G. Calderón, *Diccionario Ingles-Español-Tagalog (Con partes de la oracion y
pronunciacion figurada)*, Part III (1915), 706 pp. total, covering English headwords O through
Zymology. Sampled pages: pp. 25–27 (PDF 29–31, Orient–Other), pp. 185–187 (PDF 189–191,
Purge–Pus), pp. 365–367 (PDF 369–371, Sidle–Silly), pp. 535–537 (PDF 539–541, Tilt–Tippet), pp.
685–687 (PDF 689–691, Word–Wound).

**Coverage note — representative sampling, not exhaustive extraction.** Same sampling discipline as
`011_diccionario_sampled.md` (see that file's header for the full rationale): 5 three-page zones
spread evenly across Part III's 706 pages (landing on O, P, S, T, and W), with every distinct
headword entry on those pages extracted in full. The Spanish middle column is again out of scope
per the dispatch and not extracted — only its presence is noted. Real text layer confirmed via
clean `pdftotext -layout` output; `Vision Reading Confidence` is `n/a` throughout. Same pre-1940
orthography (`ng̃` digraph, accent marks) as Parts I–II — see `004`'s Grammar/orthography points
section for the full writeup, not repeated here. (This sample did not happen to reach the letters X,
Y, or Z within the 3 pages drawn near Part III's end; the final ~20 pages of Part III, covering the
tail of W through Z, were seen in passing during the initial triage read confirming Part III's
alphabetical range but were not separately re-sampled as their own zone here — Zodiac, Zoology,
Zymology etc. were spot-checked only to confirm the part's endpoint.)

**Morpheme breakdown (illustrative examples).**

> **Nagagayakan** = naka- (allomorph napa-/naga- family, stative-passive prefix, "already in the
> state of having been...") + gayak ("adorn, dress up") + -an (locative-trigger suffix) — "ornate,
> already adorned." Compare the plain AT form **maggayak** ("to adorn," `mag-` + `gayak`) — the same
> root takes a simple actor-trigger verb form or a stative-passive adjectival form depending on
> whether the entry glosses the action or the resulting state.

> **Ipamulsa** = i- (instrumental/conveyance-trigger prefix) + pa- (causative) + mulsa ("pocket," a
> Spanish-derived loan root, cf. bulsá "purse/pocket" elsewhere in this file) + implied -an — "to
> pocket (something)." A compact illustration of the i- trigger combining with a causative pa- layer
> on a loanword root.

> **Manggagawà** = mang- (actor-trigger prefix) + full reduplication of the root's first syllable
> (ga-) + gawâ ("work, make") — "worker." The reduplicated mang- form here derives a habitual-
> agent noun directly, without a separate taga- or nag- layer — a third strategy (alongside taga-
> and nag-) for deriving "one who does X" nouns in this dictionary, seen already in `004b`'s
> house-breaker (magnanakaw) entry.

## Grammar/orthography points

No new orthography points beyond `004`'s write-up. Two further observations from this sample:

### A fourth "one who does X" agentive strategy: bare reduplicated mang-/mag- agent nouns

`004` recorded `taga-` agentive nouns and `004b` recorded `nag-`-headed relative-clause phrases as
two distinct strategies for deriving agent nouns. This sample adds a third/fourth pattern:
reduplicated `mang-`-prefixed nouns functioning directly as agent nouns without any further
derivation (**manggagawà** "worker," **maglalatá** "tinker/tinman" — the latter actually an `mag-`
reduplicated form rather than `mang-`, so this is genuinely two closely related but distinct
sub-patterns: reduplicated-`mang-` and reduplicated-`mag-` agent nouns). Across `004`/`004b`/`004c`,
this dictionary shows at least four distinct morphological strategies coexisting for the same broad
semantic function ("one who habitually does X"): `taga-` + verb stem, `nag-`-headed relative clause,
reduplicated `mang-` noun, and reduplicated `mag-` noun. Worth a focused look once a dedicated
Tagalog grammar reference (Wave 1/2) is extracted, to see whether these track distinct semantic/
register distinctions (e.g. profession vs. momentary doer) or are genuinely free variation.

### Bracketed glosses as the source's own disambiguation device

Several entries in this sample carry a bracketed parenthetical directly in the Tagalog gloss itself
— not an editorial addition by this extraction, but the source's own practice: **Lagaslas [ng̃
batis]** ("purl, [of a brook]"), **Palalò, [dahil sa kayamanan]** ("purse-proud, [because of
wealth]"), **Táyuan (sa pintuan)** ("sill, (at the doorway)"). This is a compact period-accurate
disambiguation convention worth noting for anyone citing these particular glosses, since the
bracketed material is load-bearing for sense-disambiguation, not incidental.
