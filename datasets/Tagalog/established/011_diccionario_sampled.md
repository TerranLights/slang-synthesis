# Tagalog — Established Vocabulary/Grammar: Diccionario Ingles-Español-Tagalog, Part I (sampled) — Letters A–D

**Source:** Sofronio G. Calderón, *Diccionario Ingles-Español-Tagalog (Con partes de la oracion y
pronunciacion figurada)*, Part I (1915), 628 pp. total, covering English headwords A through
Dy- (Dyspeptic). Sampled pages: pp. 26–28 (PDF 30–32), 146–148 (PDF 150–152), 296–298 (PDF
300–302), 446–448 (PDF 450–452), 596–598 (PDF 600–602).

**Coverage note — representative sampling, not exhaustive extraction.** This dictionary (across
its 3 parts) is explicitly flagged by the dispatch for representative sampling given its combined
~1,989-page scale, rather than exhaustive page-by-page extraction. This file samples 5 three-page
zones spread evenly across Part I's 628 pages (landing on the letters A, B, C, Co-, and D
respectively) rather than reading sequentially from the start, so the sample spans Part I's full
alphabetical range rather than clustering on one letter. **Within each sampled zone, every distinct
headword entry on those pages is extracted in full** per the coverage rule's "every distinct
vocabulary item" requirement — no entries are skipped inside a sampled zone itself; the sampling
discipline operates at the level of *which* pages are read, not *what* is kept from the pages read.

**Trilingual-source scope note.** The source gives each headword as English / Spanish / Tagalog in
that order (e.g. "Adjourn, v. [adchérn] / Diferir, prorogar. / Magpaliban."). Per the dispatch's
explicit instruction, **the Spanish middle column is out of scope as a metalanguage and was not
extracted** — only its presence in the source is noted here. The `Term` column below holds the
Tagalog gloss(es); the `Gloss` column holds the English headword's meaning. The bracketed text after
each English headword (e.g. `[adchérn]`) is an English-pronunciation respelling aimed at Tagalog-
speaking readers learning English — not Tagalog content, and not reproduced per-row.

**Real text layer confirmed.** `pdftotext -layout` on this single-column, one-entry-per-block
dictionary format produced clean, correctly-ordered output with no column-scrambling or
interleaving (verified against the printed page numbers at the foot of each page). `Vision Reading
Confidence` is `n/a` throughout this file — no vision-reading was needed.

**Orthography note (see Grammar/Orthography points below).** This 1915 source predates the
standardized modern Filipino orthography (the *Balarila*, 1940) and consistently uses the older
Spanish-influenced spelling convention: `ng̃` (the letters n-g with a combining tilde over the g) for
the velar nasal /ŋ/, distinct from a plain `ng` sequence, plus grave/acute/circumflex accent marks
(`à`, `á`, `â`, `ô`, `í`, etc.) marking stress and/or the glottal stop. This is preserved exactly as
printed in the `Term` column below.

**First Austronesian-family language in this project.** Per this language's extraction-checklist
convention, verbal entries whose Tagalog gloss carries an identifiable trigger/focus affix are
flagged in the `Notes` column (AT = actor trigger, OT = object trigger, LT = locative trigger, IT =
instrumental/conveyance trigger — see the Grammar points section below for the fuller system
overview compiled from patterns observed across this sampled file).

**Morpheme breakdown (illustrative examples).**

> **Mang̃asiwà** = mang- (actor-trigger prefix, with homorganic nasal assimilation of the root's
> initial consonant) + asiwa ("manage, supervise") — "to administer." Compare **pang̃asiwaan** =
> pang- (nominalizing, derived from the same mang- AT form) + asiwa + -an (locative-trigger suffix,
> "the place/domain of managing") — "administration." The two forms share one root but show the
> trigger system's verb→noun derivational productivity.

> **Tagapang̃asiwà** = taga- (agentive, "one who habitually does X") + pang̃asiwa (itself mang-/
> pang- + asiwa) — "administrator." Illustrates taga- stacking on top of an already-affixed AT verb
> stem rather than attaching directly to a bare root.

> **Matatanggap** = ma- (potentive/abilitative prefix) + reduplicated first syllable (ta-) + tanggap
> ("receive") — "receivable, able to be received." Contrast **tinatanggap** = root tanggap + -in-
> infix (object-trigger, inserted after the first consonant) + reduplicated first syllable (present/
> incompleted aspect) — "being received/accepted." Same root, two different trigger/aspect
> combinations, both glossing the single English headword "admissible."

## Grammar/orthography points

### Pre-1940 Spanish-influenced Tagalog orthography

This dictionary (1915) predates Lope K. Santos's standardized *Balarila* orthography (1940, the
basis of modern Filipino spelling). Two features recur throughout the sampled pages and are worth
flagging for anyone cross-referencing this source against a modern-orthography Tagalog text:

- **`ng̃` (n-g digraph with a combining tilde over the g)** renders the velar nasal /ŋ/ — e.g.
  `mang̃asiwà`, `ng̃umiyaw`, `ang̃awng̃aw`. Modern orthography writes this simply as `ng` and relies
  on context to distinguish /ŋ/ from a genuine /n/+/g/ sequence; this source instead marks the
  distinction with the tilde.
- **Accent marks** (grave `à`/`è`/`ò`, acute `á`/`é`/`í`/`ó`, circumflex `â`/`ô`) mark stress and, in
  several cases, the glottal stop — a distinction modern informal Filipino writing frequently drops
  entirely (stress/glottal-stop placement is instead left to the reader's existing knowledge of the
  word). This source's systematic accent-marking is itself a useful resource for reconstructing
  period-accurate stress placement.

### Observed trigger/focus (voice) system patterns (compiled from this sampled file)

Tagalog's defining grammatical mechanism — not explained explicitly anywhere in this dictionary
(it is a word-list, not a grammar), but the pattern is clearly recoverable across many entries in
this sample and is recorded here since it is the first Austronesian-family language in this
project:

- **Actor Trigger (AT)**, several allomorphs conditioned by the root: `-um-` infix (humatol,
  tumanggap, umubó), `mag-` prefix (magpaliban, maglapat, magbigay), `mang-`/`maN-` prefix with
  nasal assimilation of the root-initial consonant (mang̃asiwà, manumpâ, manggilalas), and `ma-`
  prefix for a smaller stative/involitional subset (mamanghâ, matuto — the latter in a later
  sample).
- **Object Trigger (OT)**: `-in` suffix in the infinitive/dependent form (hulihin, dakpin, purgahín,
  linisin) and `-in-` infix in the completed/incompleted aspect (tinatanggap).
- **Locative Trigger (LT)**: `-an` suffix (pagsabihan, pang̃usapan, pang̃asiwaan).
- **Instrumental/Conveyance Trigger (IT)**: `i-` prefix (isuot → "don/put on," ipaunawà in a later
  sample).
- **Causative**: `magpa-`/`pa-...-an` layered on top of the base trigger (magpasukò, paamuin,
  pabang̃uhán).
- **Nominalization**: `pag-`, `pagka-`, `ka-...-an`, and `pang-` derive event/state/abstract nouns
  from the same verb roots (pagpapaliban, pagkakalapat, kaamuan, pang̃asiwaan).
- **Agentive**: `taga-` derives "one who habitually does X" nouns, sometimes stacked on an already-
  affixed verb stem (tagapang̃asiwà, tagapaglaan).

This is a dictionary-derived, pattern-recognition summary rather than the source's own explicit
grammatical exposition — treat it as a working observation to refine once a dedicated Tagalog
grammar reference (Wave 1/2's other sources) is extracted, not as an authoritative account of the
full trigger system.
