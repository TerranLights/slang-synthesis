## Grammar points

### Lesson 40 — Noun affixes (nine distinct patterns)

A systematic catalogue of noun-forming affixes beyond the general ang/ng/sa nominalization (Lesson
31) and the verb-specific gerund affixes (Lesson 38), each tied to a specific derived meaning:
**-an/-han** forms (1) a storage/location noun (aklát→aklátan "library"), (2) a season noun
(áni→anihán "harvest time"), (3) an instrument/article noun (inóm→inúman "something to drink from"),
or, when the *root itself is reduplicated and hyphenated* before the suffix, (4) a miniature/toy
version (báhay→baháy-baháyan "playhouse") or (5) an "imitator/copycat" sense (iná→iná-ináhan
"pretending to be a mother") — the source explicitly notes **o→u** and **d→r** sound changes recur
across these patterns whenever a root ends in o or d respectively. **Ka- + -an** forms either (1) an
abstract idea/concept noun (butí→kabutíhan "goodness") or (2) a collective-group noun
(bahay→kabahayán "place of many houses"; Tagálog→Katagalúgan "place of the Tagalogs," an explicit
named-ethnolinguistic-region derivation) — genuinely the same affix template producing two
semantically distinct outputs depending on whether the root is abstract or concrete, paralleling the
identical abstract/concrete split already documented for adjective-forming **ma-** (Lesson 12/41).
**Mag-** forms either (1) a reciprocal-relationship noun (iná→mag-ina "mother and child";
kapatíd→magkapatíd "siblings," note the irregular non-hyphenated contraction) or (2) a
profession/trade noun with reduplication (bigás→magbibigás "rice merchant"). **Mang-/man-** (the
same d/l/r/s/t-initial allomorphy already documented for comparative kásing-/kásim- in Lesson 11, now
recurring in a wholly different grammatical category) forms profession nouns with reduplication
(gamót→manggagamot "doctor"; langóy→manlalangoy "swimmer," l-initial root taking **man-**). **Pag-**
forms gerunds as already fully documented in Lesson 38, cross-referenced here rather than re-taught.
**Sang-/san-/sam-** (the same allomorphy pattern as the Lesson 13 numeral prefix **labíng-**, with
**san-** before d/l/r/s/t and **sam-** before b/p) forms either (1) a "whole of" totalizing noun
(pulô→sangkapuluán "archipelago") or (2) a "one [unit]" noun (gabí→sanggabí "one night"). **Tag-**
forms a season/time-of noun (araw→tag-aráw "dry season"), genuinely overlapping in function with
the -an "season" sense above (áni→anihán "harvest time" vs. áni→tag-ani "harvest time" — two
distinct affixes both independently productive for the identical semantic slot on the same root, a
real case of morphological redundancy/free-variation the source doesn't explicitly flag as such but
which emerges clearly from comparing the two affix sections). **Taga-** forms either (1) a demonym
("native/resident of," taga- + a place name: taga-Batángas, taga-Mindanáo — the latter illustrated
with an explicit real-world ethnoreligious reference to Muslim Filipinos from Mindanao) or (2) a
habitual-agent noun ("one who [frequently does]," taga- + action root: dalá→tagadalá "one who
brings," illustrated with real occupational nouns kartéro "postman" and maybahay "housewife"). **Tala-
+ -an** forms a list/catalogue/record noun (tinig→talátinígan "dictionary"; aklát→taláaklatán
"catalogue of books"; áraw→taláarawán "diary/journal"). **The plurality rule** (first introduced
Lesson 4) is recapped and consolidated into four concrete strategies: plural articles (siná/ang mga),
numeral/quantity-adjective + noun (ilán, marámi), the productive ma- + reduplicated-first-syllable
plural-adjective rule (magagandáng dalaga "pretty young women," recapping Lesson 1's adjective
pluralization), and bare whole-root reduplication of the noun itself (baháy-baháy "house to house,"
bágay-bágay "various things") — this last strategy is a genuinely distinct plural-marking device from
every other rule in the book, since it reduplicates a noun root (not an adjective) with a
distributive/variety sense ("from house to house," "several different things") rather than simple
numeric plurality.

### Lesson 41 — Formation of adjectives (five distinct affix patterns)

**Ma-** (recapping and formally systematizing the pattern already used informally since Lesson 1)
splits by noun-root type: with an **abstract** noun root it means "having the quality of X"
(gandá→magandá "beautiful," explicitly glossed as equivalent to the periphrastic **may gandá**
"has beauty" — the same ma-as-contracted-may etymological link the source drew out explicitly back in
Lesson 12, now reconfirmed as the general rule rather than a one-off observation); with a **concrete**
noun root it means "having much/many of X" (bulaklák→mabulaklák "many flowers," táo→matáo "many
people/crowded"). **Ka-** forms a reciprocal-relationship adjective (balát→kabalát "of the same
skin," sama→kasáma "accompanied/companion"). **Kay-** forms an exclamatory adjective meaning "how
[quality]!" (gandá→kaygandá! "how beautiful!") — a dedicated exclamation-forming affix, distinct from
the ordinary interjection vocabulary catalogued in Lesson 35. **Maka-** forms an "in favor of/inclined
toward" adjective, notably **productive on proper names** (Rizal→maka-Rizal "in favor of/pro-Rizal," a
genuine real-world political/ideological-stance derivation directly parallel to English "pro-X") as
well as ordinary roots (bágo→makabágo "modern," lumà→makalumà "old-fashioned") — this maka- is a
distinct lexical entry from the ability/capability maka- of Lesson 18 and the causative-adjacent
magpa-/paki- family, a further addition to this book's running inventory of form-identical-but-
functionally-distinct affix homographs. **Pang-/pam-/pan-** forms a "specific use of X" adjective, with
a fully regular three-way allomorphy conditioned by the root's initial sound: **pang-** before
vowels and k/g/h/m/n/ng/w/y (with a hyphen before vowel-initial roots), **pam-** before b/p, **pan-**
before d/l/r/s/t — the identical place-of-articulation-driven conditioning pattern recurring for a
*third* time in this book (after kásing-/kásim- comparative prefixes in Lesson 11 and mang-/man- noun
profession-affixes in this same lesson's own Section IV), strongly suggesting this three-way nasal-
nasal-assimilation allomorphy is a genuinely pervasive, cross-cutting phonological rule of the language
rather than a quirk specific to any one affix family. **Mapag-** and **pala-** both form a "habitually/
frequently does X" adjective and are **interchangeable for action roots** (tawa→palatawá/mapagtawá
"laughs frequently") **but not for descriptive/quality roots, where only mapag- works**
(mahál→mapagmahál "loving/affectionate," no *palamahál* form given or implied) — an explicit,
source-stated asymmetric-interchangeability rule between two near-synonymous affixes, itself a further
example (alongside the i-/-in- per-root restriction from Lesson 32, and the pala-/mapag- restriction
just noted) of this language's trigger/derivation system having real, root-specific productivity limits
that a learner (or, speculatively, a slang-innovating speaker) must track rather than freely generalize.
