## What was asked and what came back

**Q: Is Okinawan even present in contemporary Hawaii, or is it just Japanese?**

Answer given (general knowledge, not sourced from a specific citable reference — flag it as such if
you fold it into any canon document):

- **The Okinawan *community* is real and historically distinct from mainland ("Naichi") Japanese
  immigration** — a separate wave starting ~1900, historically facing discrimination from mainland
  Japanese immigrants too, not just from the plantation system generally. That distinct identity
  persists today in organized form (Hawaii United Okinawa Association, the annual Okinawan
  Festival — one of the larger ethnic-heritage festivals in the state). A real, separately-tracked
  share of Hawaii's "Japanese-American" population is specifically of Okinawan descent.
- **The Okinawan *language* (Uchinaaguchi) is not a living spoken community in Hawaii.** It wasn't
  passed down as a home language past the immigrant generation — English and Hawaii Creole English
  took over within a generation or two, the same pattern as most other plantation-era immigrant
  languages there. This mirrors Uchinaaguchi's own severe-endangerment status back in Okinawa
  itself. What actually survives in Hawaii is **lexical/cultural residue inside Hawaii Creole
  English's substrate mix** (alongside Hawaiian, Cantonese, Portuguese, Japanese, Ilocano, Korean
  features), not an ongoing speech community to document. Small-scale diaspora revival classes may
  exist via cultural organizations, but that's active language-preservation effort responding to
  near-extinction, not a thriving source.

## Why this matters for your roster

Your own framing of the Okinawan want (`SOURCE_ACQUISITION_TODO.md`) is "short grammar and Hawaii
contact notes" — i.e., the value proposition was specifically the *contact* angle, the same
justification that got Ilocano added (D-12). The difference: Ilocano has a real, substantial,
living contemporary Hawaii-Ilocano-speaking population and an easy, well-documented contact trail
(which the Ilocano extraction already surfaced concretely — Aloha/Haway/kanaka entries, the "one
fifth of Hawaii's population is Ilocano-descended" claim). **Okinawan's contact trail is the same
historical category but without a living contemporary community backing it in Hawaii, and without
any source material found anywhere yet** (checked against `source_reference/`, the external drive,
and the LibGen-sourced unsorted stashes during this project's recent sweeps — no hits).

This doesn't mean Okinawan contributed nothing real to Hawaii Creole English's history — it likely
did, in the same way Cantonese, Portuguese, and the others did. It means the *practical case* for
treating Okinawan as a priority acquisition — over, say, spending that effort on a source that's
better-documented or easier to find — may be weaker than when the line was first written. It's
also possible a worthwhile source exists (an academic contact-linguistics paper documenting
specific Okinawan-origin Hawaii Creole English lexical items, for instance) without a full grammar
being necessary at all, given the "vocabulary only" framing in your own Q-3.
